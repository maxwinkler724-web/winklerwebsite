Stainless Steel Special Gas Vent for Category III and IV Boilers

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A condensing or positive-pressure boiler cannot use ordinary gas vent. Its exhaust is wet, acidic and pushed out under fan pressure. Stainless steel special gas vent, usually AL29-4C or 316L, is one of the answers. This lesson covers the alloys, the joints, the slope and drains, and why one maker's parts never mix with another's.

The manual decides

The manual decides. Every number here comes from a maker document we read in 2026, and each maker's rules differ. The boiler manual names the allowed vent, and the vent maker's instructions govern the joints, supports and clearances. This page is general education, not a design. For what Category III and IV mean, start with Furnace and Boiler Vent Categories I to IV Explained.

Why these boilers need special vent

Category IV exhaust runs below its dew point, so water condenses inside the vent, and the fan pushes it out under positive pressure. A Department of Energy tip sheet says condensed water vapor in a condensing economizer is acidic and has to be neutralized before it goes to a sewer. A boiler maker, Patterson-Kelley, puts the condensate pH of its Category IV boiler between 3.0 and 5.0. Plain Type B vent is not built for that, and Patterson-Kelley says its manual does not allow it on that boiler. A positive-pressure vent also has to hold its joints tight so exhaust cannot leak into the room.

AL29-4C and 316L

Both are stainless steels chosen to resist acid. Heat-Fab says its Saf-T Vent SC inner wall is super-ferritic AL 29-4C and its SC 316 version uses 316L. Metal-Fab says its Corr/Guard flue is AL29-4C or an equivalent superferritic stainless. DuraVent lists 316L for its smaller single-wall sizes and a 29-4C inner flue in some double-wall models. Patterson-Kelley says its boiler needs "listed Category IV vent pipe (316L or AL29-4C Stainless Steel)." The choice between them is not yours to make from memory. The boiler manual lists what it accepts, and the listed product for that appliance is what goes in.

Listing: UL 1738

These systems are tested and listed to UL 1738, the standard for venting systems for gas-burning appliances. DuraVent says UL 1738 covers special gas vent for Category I through IV appliances, and that ULC S636 applies in Canada. Heat-Fab states its product is listed to UL 1738 and ULC S636. A listing is a package: the pipe, fittings, seals and terminations tested together. Nothing here links the standards themselves, because only the maker's listing tells you what was tested.

Single wall or double wall

Single-wall vent is bare stainless and needs more clearance. DuraVent's table lists 2 in clearance to combustibles for single wall in sizes 3 in to 12 in, rising to 4 in for 14 in to 24 in. Double-wall vent has an outer jacket with an air space, which allows much less clearance. Heat-Fab says its double-wall design may be enclosed in combustibles at zero or 1 in clearance, depending on orientation and flue gas temperature. Both makers say not to put insulation in the required clearance space. Heat-Fab adds that support hangers alone do not keep the needed clearance.

Joints and sealing

Joints differ by maker, which is the main reason for the mixing rule below. DuraVent's DS line uses an O-ring on the male end, a thin film of silicone lubricant, and self-locking clips. Its larger sizes use V-bands and a bead of Viton caulk on horizontal joints. Heat-Fab's SC line has an integral seal that "does not require sealant," and a snap ring on the outer jacket, and it asks you to point the female ends (with the red seal) away from the appliance unless the appliance maker says otherwise. Metal-Fab offers a joint sealant rated for continuous temperatures up to 500°F. Follow the system's own steps and nothing else.

Slope and condensate drains

Condensate has to go somewhere, and the pitch decides where. Several makers cite a minimum of 1/4 in per foot: Aerco says horizontal runs slope upward from the boiler to the termination at no less than that. DuraVent and Metal-Fab also cite it. Heat-Fab adds an exception for positive-pressure Category III or IV systems that terminate horizontally with no drain: they may pitch downward at 1/4 in per foot instead. Patterson-Kelley says its larger models have a 4 degree slope toward the boiler, so read the exact boiler manual.

For drains, DuraVent and Heat-Fab both say to provide one drain for every 30 ft of horizontal vent and at the base of a vertical stack, placed as close to the flue collar as possible. DuraVent sizes the trap loop at no less than 3 in and at least four times the appliance stack pressure in inches of water column, and says a dry trap can let exhaust into the building. Heat-Fab says to prime the trap with water before final assembly and check it at least annually. DuraVent adds that in cold climates the drain should not be on the building exterior.

Because the condensate runs pH 3 to 5, the drain line goes to a neutralizer where the local code or the boiler maker requires it. See Condensate Neutralizers for High-Efficiency Furnaces and Boilers. Patterson-Kelley calls for PVC or CPVC for its condensate piping.

Termination basics

DuraVent and Metal-Fab agree that a sidewall termination sits at least 12 in above grade, and above the snow line where snow collects. DuraVent also gives 3 ft from building openings and 7 ft above walkways. Metal-Fab says a vertical vent ends at least 3 ft above the roof. Patterson-Kelley says at least 3 ft above the roof or 2 ft above any structure within 10 ft. These are examples. The boiler maker's termination table and local code win. Combustion air is its own topic; see Combustion Air for Furnaces, Boilers and Water Heaters.

Never mix vent brands

Heat-Fab says, "Do Not Mix Pipe, Fittings, or Joining Methods from Different Manufacturers," and DuraVent says the same. The reason is listing: a system was tested as a set, with its own seals and bands, and a part from another maker is untested. Heat-Fab does say its SC and SC 316 parts can be combined with each other, which shows that mixing is judged within one maker's listed system. Heat-Fab also forbids galvanized pipe, PVC and Type B sections in the system.

Common venting

Common venting of Category II, III and IV appliances needs maker approval. DuraVent says a Category II, III or IV appliance may not be common-vented with Category I, and that multiple connections need manufacturer approval. Aerco allows its AM Series units on a common vent under conditions, such as a WYE connection instead of a tee, but not with other boiler types. Patterson-Kelley says multi-boiler stacks must be designed by qualified professionals and verified by the stack manufacturer, and must prevent backflow through an idle boiler. If the boiler manual is silent, do not common vent. Compare the chimney liner rules for the masonry case.

Worked example: slope, drains and equivalent length

Assumptions (Our own, for practice): a Category IV boiler, 4 in stainless vent, 10 ft of vertical rise, 20 ft of horizontal run, two sweep 90 degree elbows and an exit cone. We use the Aerco AM Series values as a stand-in: a sweep 90 counts 5 ft, the exit cone 10 ft, and the maximum is 60 equivalent ft. Slope is 1/4 in per foot, as the makers above cite.

  1. Slope rise over 20 ft: 20 ft × 1/4 in per ft = 5 in. The high end of the run sits 5 in above the low end.
  2. Drains: 20 ft is under 30 ft, so the 30 ft rule needs one drain, at the base of the stack near the flue collar.
  3. Trap loop, assuming 0.5 in W.C. stack pressure: 4 × 0.5 = 2 in, which is below the 3 in minimum, so 3 in governs.
  4. Equivalent length: 10 + 20 = 30 ft straight, plus 2 × 5 = 10 ft for the elbows, plus 10 ft for the cone = 50 ft.
  5. Check: 50 ft is under the 60 ft limit, leaving 10 ft of margin. A third sweep elbow would use 5 ft of it. A sharp 90 at 10 ft would use all of it.

For your own boiler, replace every assumed value with the numbers in its manual.

Field card: Stainless Special Gas Vent

Same maker, listed to UL 1738, sloped to a drain.

  • Category III and IV need listed special gas vent. The boiler manual names the allowed one.
  • Alloys cited: AL29-4C and 316L. Condensate cited at pH 3 to 5.
  • Do not mix pipe, fittings or joints from different makers.
  • Slope at least 1/4 in per foot per the makers cited. A 20 ft run gives 5 in.
  • Drain every 30 ft of horizontal and at the base of a vertical stack. Keep traps primed.
  • No insulation in clearance spaces. Single wall needs more clearance than double.
  • Common vent only if the boiler maker allows it.
