Heat Strip Size for a Heat Pump: kW, Breakers and Staging

Last updated: 2026-10-16

The backup heat in a heat pump air handler is a box of electric resistance elements, sold in kilowatt sizes. Picking the size, and finding the wire and breaker that go with it, is a table-reading job. This lesson walks the arithmetic and then reads real maker tables.

Check the maker's tables

Check the maker's tables. Kit sizes, amps, breaker limits and airflow rules differ by brand and by air handler model. The numbers below come from the documents listed under Sources and are examples of how to read them. Your unit's installation manual and data label always win.

From kW to Btu/h

Resistance heat is simple: every watt of electricity becomes a watt of heat. The U.S. Energy Information Administration lists 1 kilowatt-hour of electricity as 3,412 Btu, so a heater running at 1 kW puts out 3,412 Btu/h. A 5 kW kit is about 17,060 Btu/h and a 10 kW kit about 34,120 Btu/h. There is no efficiency curve and no outdoor-temperature effect, which is the reason strips fill the gap in the cold (see Heat Pump Balance Point and Low-Temperature Capacity).

Choosing the kit: the gap method

The strips cover the gap between what the house loses and what the heat pump delivers. The method has three steps. Take the heat loss from the load calculation at the design temperature. Subtract what the heat pump delivers at that temperature, using the maker's low-temperature capacity data (the balance point lesson covers where that data comes from, and Cold-Climate Heat Pumps explains why some models hold more capacity). Convert the remainder to kW by dividing by 3,412, then pick the next kit size up. Sizing the strips for more than the gap is a design decision, and it means a bigger kit with bigger wire and breaker.

Worked example (made-up numbers)

The load and capacity figures here are invented for practice and are labeled as such. Only the kit data comes from a maker.

  1. Design heat loss at 5 F: 36,000 Btu/h (made up).
  2. Heat pump output at 5 F: 17,000 Btu/h (made up; read yours from the maker's capacity table).
  3. Gap: 36,000 − 17,000 = 19,000 Btu/h.
  4. Required kW: 19,000 ÷ 3,412 = 5.57 kW.
  5. On a 240 V supply, the next size up is a 6 kW kit (about 20,472 Btu/h).

Now the maker's table. The Goodman heater kit specification sheet lists, for the AVPTC25B14AB air handler on 240 V, the HKSX06XC kit at 6.0 kW with 25 A of heater current, a minimum circuit ampacity (MCA) of 36.1 A and a maximum overcurrent protection (MOP) of 40 A. The sheet's MCA note reads (Heater Amps + Motor Amps) × 1.25, so the MCA already counts the blower and the 125 percent factor.

Suppose the building is fed at 208 V instead. Heating output falls with the square of voltage, because a fixed resistance draws current proportional to voltage and power is voltage times current. The ratio is (208 ÷ 240)² = 0.751. Goodman's sheet prints a 0.75 factor for 208 V (0.92 at 230 V) and Trane's wall mount air handler data lists 4.8, 7.68 and 9.6 kW kits at 240 V as 3.6, 5.76 and 7.2 kW at 208 V, the same 75 percent. A 6 kW kit gives only 6.0 × 0.75 = 4.5 kW at 208 V, which is short of 5.57. Work backward: 5.57 ÷ 0.751 = 7.42 kW of rating at 240 V, so the next size up is the 8.0 kW kit. At 208 V it delivers 8.0 × 0.75 = 6.0 kW, about 20,472 Btu/h, which covers the 19,000 Btu/h gap.

The same Goodman table, 208 V section, lists the HKSC08XC (breaker version) with 28.9 A heater current, MCA 41 A and MOP 45 A. The arithmetic checks: 6,000 W ÷ 208 V = 28.8 A. The 240 V row for the same kit shows 33.3 A, MCA 46.5 A and MOP 50 A. Notice that the kit pulls fewer amps at 208 V, but the heat is also lower, so this is not a free saving.

Single-point or dual-point power

Larger kits can come with two supply circuits. Rheem's installation instructions for its H4HK kits say kits of 10 kW or less come set up for one supply circuit and kits above 10 kW come set up for two circuits, with an accessory kit to convert to one. Goodman's sheet shows both ways for its 15 kW kit at 208 V: circuit 1 at MCA 48 A and MOP 50 A, circuit 2 at MCA 21.7 A and MOP 25 A, or a single feed at MCA 69.9 A and MOP 70 A. Read the sheet carefully: the blower is counted on circuit 1 only. When you see two feeds, there are two breakers, two disconnect decisions and two sets of wire. The nameplate lesson covers how MCA sets the wire and MOCP caps the breaker. Rheem adds a warning worth remembering: the breakers supplied in its kits do not protect the supply wiring, and the protection at the panel must follow the table and the data label.

Staging and controls

Goodman describes its kits as having control circuitry arranged to permit staging, and Rheem says its 15 and 20 kW kits can be staged with a two-stage thermostat: one wire for the first stage and one for the second, joined together when staging is not wanted. Goodman's sheet also lists sequencers for better temperature control. Whether the strips run at all is a setting, not a size. Outdoor-temperature lockouts, timers and the difference between auxiliary and emergency heat are in Emergency Heat vs. Auxiliary Heat and Lockout Temperatures. If a stage does not come on, the sequencer, element and limit chain is in Electric Furnace Not Heating.

Minimum airflow per kit

More kW needs more air across the elements, or the limits open. The maker tells you how much. Rheem's H4HK instructions give no CFM numbers but list a minimum blower speed for each kit and orientation: low speed for the 3 to 10 kW kits, and for the 15 and 20 kW kits a speed that rises in the downflow position (medium for 15 kW, high for 20 kW). Lennox's electric heat conversion instructions for its KC and KH rooftop units list actual minimums that climb with kW: for belt-drive KH units 1,050 to 1,200 CFM for 7.5 kW up to 2,050 to 2,250 CFM for 30 kW, depending on orientation. Those are light-commercial figures shown only to illustrate the trend. Trane's wall mount air handler data lists the lowest allowed blower speed for each kit and a separate caution about airflow per ton. Do not set CFM below the maker's minimum.

Why one kit does not fit every air handler

The kit has to be one the air handler is listed for. Rheem's instructions say its 15 and 20 kW kits are not approved for A-cabinet air handlers, and Goodman's air handlers list their own kit series. The Goodman sheet also shows that the 20 kW kit is not offered for the air handler in the example above.

Field card: Heat Pump Backup Heat Strips

Gap in Btu/h, divide by 3,412, correct for voltage, then read the maker's table.

  • 1 kW of resistance heat is 3,412 Btu/h (EIA).
  • Size the kit to heat loss minus heat pump output at design temperature, then go up one size.
  • 208 V output is about 75 percent of the 240 V rating: (208 ÷ 240)² = 0.751.
  • MCA sets the wire, MOCP/MOP caps the breaker; both come from the maker's table.
  • Kits over 10 kW are often dual-circuit (Rheem); check single-point options in the table.
  • Staging, lockouts and minimum blower speed or CFM are the maker's settings. Do not go below the airflow minimum.
