Chiller Tube Cleaning and Eddy Current Testing: Brushing, Fouling and Plugged Tubes

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Water-cooled chillers move heat through hundreds of small tubes, and every layer of scale or slime on the water side of those tubes gets in the way. This lesson covers how tubes foul, how fouling shows in the log, how they are cleaned each year, how eddy current testing finds damage the eye cannot see, and what plugging a tube does to the ones left.

Check the maker

Check the maker. Intervals, torque values and plugging limits differ by model. This page is general education built from maker literature and trade sources read in 2026. Your chiller's own manual and your site procedures come first.

Why tubes foul

Goodway, a tube-cleaning equipment maker, says chiller efficiency is affected more by the cleanliness of the heat transfer surfaces than by any other single factor. It lists the contaminants as ranging from mud, sand and algae to hard mineral scale. Facilities Management Insights describes the same mix, mud, algae, sludge and scale, on the water side of the tubes, and notes that the rate depends on whether the loop is open or closed and on water quality. A condenser on a cooling tower is an open system, which is why it fouls faster than a closed loop. Trane and Carrier both warn in their manuals that untreated or badly treated water can cause scaling, erosion, corrosion, algae or slime, so water treatment is the first line of defense and cleaning is the second. Trane also calls for strainers on the entering water to keep debris from plugging tubes.

How fouling shows up

A fouled condenser cannot hand heat to the water as easily, so the refrigerant has to sit hotter than before. The York YK operation manual says trouble from fouled tubes is usually shown by a steady rise in head pressure over time, and that a rising condenser approach on the operating log can point to dirty tubes. It gives a check: the gap between leaving condenser water and saturated condensing temperature should not exceed the new-unit value by more than 4 F. For the full method, read Chiller Approach Temperatures: Reading the Trend and Reading a Chiller Log.

On the energy cost, we only repeat what sources say. Goodway's tube-cleaning article says fouling can cut heat transfer efficiency by 15 percent or more, and by another 10 to 20 percent when permanent scale or iron deposits are present. That is a heat transfer figure, not a kilowatt figure. We did not find a maker or federal source that gives energy per degree of approach, so this page does not give one.

Annual cleaning: which method fits

Facilities Management Insights says most chiller manufacturers recommend cleaning condenser tubes annually, and Carrier's AquaEdge manual, as quoted by Goodway, says the shell-and-tube heat exchanger tubes must be cleaned yearly. York says the condenser tubes should be cleaned annually or sooner if conditions warrant. Goodway's overview lays out the choices:

  • Rod and brush. Cheap and simple but slow. Debris can dry in the tube before the final flush.
  • Rotary cleaner. A flexible shaft with a brush and a water flush. Goodway calls it the most effective method for newer chillers with internally enhanced tubes, and it can remove hard scale.
  • Tube cleaning gun. Fast, but suited to light deposits such as mud and algae. Both water boxes must be open.
  • Chemical (acid). Can soften scale or clean to bare metal but takes the chiller out of service for at least 24 hours and involves hazardous chemicals. York says acid cleaning should be done by an expert and that tubes should always be brushed before acid cleaning. Facilities Management Insights likewise says chemical cleaning should be followed by mechanical cleaning.

York also says not to use a steel bristle brush on its tubes. Match the brush to the tube material and the maker's manual.

Water boxes, gaskets and safety

Cleaning means opening the water boxes. Treat the machine as a pressure vessel and an electrical load. Carrier's manual says to lock open and tag the electrical circuits while servicing, not to loosen water box cover bolts until the water box is completely drained, and to confirm the refrigerant side reads 0 psig before breaking any refrigerant connection. Trane's manual says to recover refrigerant to relieve pressure before opening the system and to use a new gasket with each water box cover. Trane adds that a box swapped end for end must go back right side up so the baffle arrangement stays correct. Tighten cover bolts in the sequence and to the torque in your manual. A weeping gasket afterward is a rework, so York notes that a drop in tube performance can come from water bypassing in the water box. See Lockout/Tagout and Verify Dead for the electrical steps.

Eddy current testing

Brushing tells you the inside is clean. It does not tell you what the wall looks like. Eddy current testing passes a probe through each tube and watches how its electromagnetic field is disturbed. Trane's tube testing page says it can detect, locate and record internal and external corrosion, deposits, wear or cracking, and notes that tube condition is not checked during standard maintenance. Conco's testing page describes the method as a way to determine percent wall loss in non-ferromagnetic heat exchanger tubing. A contractor, Projectile Tube Cleaning, lists flaws such as wall loss, surface-breaking defects, corrosion, wear and cracking. Ask your tester which flaw types their report covers.

Intervals vary by source. York's maintenance table says to perform eddy current testing and inspect tubes every 2 to 5 years. Facilities Management Insights says most manufacturers recommend eddy current inspection every three to five years, and Projectile Tube Cleaning gives 3 to 5 years, with more frequent testing if damage is actively occurring. Trane's manual recommends eddy current inspection of the condenser and evaporator tubes. Use your maker's number.

Plugging failed tubes

A tube that is thinned through or cracked can leak water into refrigerant or the reverse, so testers mark bad tubes and a technician plugs both ends. A plugged tube carries no flow and moves no heat. Makers set limits on how many tubes may be plugged before capacity or efficiency suffers; we did not find a published percentage in the maker literature we could open, so this page states none. Look it up in your chiller's manual or ask the maker.

Worked example

A condenser has 400 tubes. Eddy current testing finds 12 bad tubes and they are plugged.

  1. Percent plugged = plugged / total × 100 = 12 / 400 × 100 = 3.0 percent.
  2. Tubes left in service = 400 − 12 = 388.
  3. At the same gpm, flow per tube scales with the inverse of the tube count (same tube size, flow split evenly). Velocity ratio = 400 / 388 = 1.0309.
  4. Increase in velocity = 1.0309 − 1 = 0.0309, or about 3.1 percent.

Our own arithmetic. It assumes even flow and ignores fouling, so it is a way to see the trend, not a prediction. Higher velocity pushes a little more heat per tube and a little more pressure drop, so log the condenser water pressure drop too.

Approach trend: after cleaning, the condenser approach (saturated condensing temperature minus leaving condenser water) reads 2 F. Over the season it rises to 6 F.

  1. Change = 6 − 2 = 4 F.
  2. Ratio = 6 / 2 = 3, so the approach is three times its clean value.

Our own arithmetic. 4 F is exactly the margin York's manual allows above the new-unit value, so this chiller would be at that limit. We do not convert the 4 F to kilowatts because we have no sourced figure for it.

Recording results

Write down what you find so the next crew can compare. Record the date, which water box, the cleaning method, how dirty the tubes were before and after, the water pressure drop before and after, approach before and after, and for eddy current the tube locations, indications and which tubes were plugged, and the running total of plugged tubes. Keep the tester's report with the chiller file. For daily habits see Chiller Plant Daily Rounds.

Field card: Chiller Tube Cleaning and Eddy Current Testing

Clean the inside, test the wall, log both.

  • Tubes foul from mud, algae, sludge and scale; water treatment and strainers slow it.
  • Rising head pressure and a growing condenser approach point to dirty tubes.
  • Makers say clean condenser tubes yearly; use their method and brush type.
  • Lock out and tag, drain the water box, confirm 0 psig, use a new gasket.
  • Eddy current finds wall loss and cracks; intervals in sources run 2 to 5 years.
  • Percent plugged = plugged / total. Velocity rise = total / remaining.
  • Record approach, pressure drop and plugged count at every cleaning.
