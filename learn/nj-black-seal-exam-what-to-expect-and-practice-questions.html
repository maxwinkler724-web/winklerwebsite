NJ Black Seal Exam: What to Expect and Practice Questions

Part of the NJ Black Seal and Boilers Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Searching for NJ Black Seal exam questions usually turns up vague lists. This page gives you 20 original practice questions on low-pressure steam and hot water boiler operation, each with the answer, a short explanation and the source behind it.

These are not real exam questions

Read this first. Every question below was written by us for study. They are original study questions, not copies or leaks of any real test, and we do not know what is on the real one. The exam content is set by the New Jersey Department of Labor bureau that licenses boiler operators (the Bureau of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Compliance). Confirm anything about the real exam with that bureau.

What to expect on exam day

For the exam format, scheduling, retake waits and fees, use our NJ Black Seal Study Plan. For which license applies to which plant, and the experience path to the exam, see NJ Black Seal License Paths and Plant Size. We do not repeat those details here because they change, and the bureau is the final word. What this page adds is practice at the kind of reasoning the work calls for: where the water level should be, what each safety does, and what to do when something is wrong.

The picture to keep in your head

Most of the water-side questions below come back to this picture. The gauge glass shows the real level, the water column connects it to the boiler with no shutoff valves in the way, and the low-water cutoff is the control that stops the burner when the level falls. The Hartford loop and the safety valve are backups; they never replace a working cutoff.

Worked example: reasoning through a low-water cutoff test

Here is one test walked through step by step, using the probe-type cutoff test printed in the Weil-McLain SGO Series 4 steam boiler manual (pages 7 and 40). The numbers are a made-up example to show the thinking, and your boiler's manual and a trained person's procedure always govern.

  1. Know the target. The normal water line is halfway up the glass. Say the visible glass is 8 inches long. The line is then 4 inches above the bottom of the glass.
  2. Know the margin. New Jersey's checklist wants the glass positioned so its lowest visible point is at least 1 inch above the lowest permissible water level the maker recommends. So when the water is at the very bottom of the glass, there is still at least 1 inch of safe water left in the boiler.
  3. Power off for 5 minutes, then drain. Drain until the water is at the bottom of the glass. That is a 4 inch drop from normal (8 / 2 = 4).
  4. Power on and call for heat. The manual says the cutoff's red lamp should light. Wait 5 minutes. The boiler must not fire.
  5. Refill to the proper line. Then wait another 5 minutes. The boiler should fire again.
  6. Total the waiting time. 5 + 5 + 5 = 15 minutes of waiting, not counting the draining and refilling.
  7. Decide. If the burner fired with the water at the bottom of the glass, the test failed. Shut the boiler down and keep it off until the control is repaired or replaced. Never jumper it out to keep heat on.

Notice the reasoning: the test copies a real low-water event, the pass condition is "burner stays off while water is still visible", and the failure response is to stop, not to work around the control. The low-water cutoff lesson explains why a slow drain is the more honest test.

20 practice questions

Try each one before you open the answer. The questions cover water level and LWCO testing, safety valves, gauge glass care, pressure controls, the Hartford loop, purge and flame failure, blowdown and water treatment, and start-up and shutdown. Each source line names our own lesson that states the fact and the outside document behind it. Where we say a point comes from the lesson text only, we did not re-check the outside document for that point.

Question 1. A steam boiler's manual gives no special instruction. Where is the normal water line usually held in the gauge glass?

  1. At the very top of the glass
  2. About halfway up the glass
  3. Just above the bottom of the glass
  4. It does not matter if the burner shuts off
Show answer

Answer: B. Residential steam boiler manuals call for a water line about halfway up the glass. Running high invites wet steam and carryover; running low leaves little margin above the cutoff point.

Source: Steam Boiler Short-Cycling Causes; Weil-McLain SGO Series 4 steam boiler manual. Weil-McLain SGO manual: normal waterline is halfway up the gauge glass. Your own boiler's manual governs.

Question 2. What is the job of a low-water cutoff (LWCO)?

  1. Stop the burner before the boiler runs dry
  2. Hold steam pressure at its setting
  3. Open the safety valve at high pressure
  4. Keep boiler water warm for domestic hot water
Show answer

Answer: A. The LWCO watches the water level and shuts off the burner if it falls too low. Pressure is handled by the pressure control and safety valve, and warm-water holding is an aquastat low-limit function.

Source: Low-Water Cutoff Testing and Maintenance. Both lessons describe the LWCO as the device that stops the burner on low water.

Question 3. During a slow-drain test of a low-water cutoff on a steam boiler, when should the burner shut off?

  1. After the glass is completely empty
  2. Only after the pressure control opens
  3. Before the water leaves the bottom of the gauge glass
  4. Only when the feed pump stops
Show answer

Answer: C. The burner should shut off while water is still visible in the glass. If water disappears from the glass and the burner is still firing, the control has failed and the boiler stays off until it is repaired or replaced.

Source: Low-Water Cutoff Testing and Maintenance; Minnesota DLI: Boiler Safety. Minnesota DLI: during slow-drain tests the burner should shut off before the water drops out of sight in the glass.

Question 4. New Jersey's low-pressure pre-inspection checklist says an automatically fired boiler's low-water fuel cutoff must shut off fuel when the water drops to what point?

  1. The top of the gauge glass
  2. Halfway down the glass
  3. One inch above the burner
  4. The lowest visible part of the gauge glass
Show answer

Answer: D. The checklist ties the cutoff action to the lowest visible part of the glass. The same checklist bars shutoff valves between a steam boiler and its water column, so the sensing connection cannot be closed off by mistake.

Source: Low-Water Cutoff Testing and Maintenance; NJ DOL Low-Pressure Boiler Pre-Inspection Checklist. NJ DOL Low-Pressure Boiler Pre-Inspection Checklist (HG-603, HG-604, HG-606).

Question 5. Which rule applies to a boiler's safety valve piping?

  1. A shutoff valve is allowed if it is locked open
  2. No shutoff of any kind between the boiler and the safety valve
  3. A shutoff valve is allowed on the discharge pipe for maintenance
  4. The valve may be capped if it leaks
Show answer

Answer: B. No shutoff of any description may sit between the boiler and the safety valve, and none on the discharge pipe either. Capping a valve or tightening down a weeping one defeats the last line of defense.

Source: Boiler Safety Valves and Overpressure Protection; NJ DOL Low-Pressure Boiler Pre-Inspection Checklist. NJ DOL Low-Pressure Boiler Pre-Inspection Checklist (HG-701.5, HG-701.6).

Question 6. A safety valve on a low-pressure boiler is simmering (slightly leaking). What is the correct response?

  1. Tighten the adjusting screw to stop the leak
  2. Cap the discharge pipe
  3. Have it corrected promptly by a qualified person
  4. Ignore it until it pops
Show answer

Answer: C. A simmering valve may be poorly seated or corroded inside and can bind, so it should be corrected immediately by a qualified, authorized shop. Never adjust, gag or plug a safety valve.

Source: Boiler Safety Valves and Overpressure Protection; EMC Insurance: Boiler Operation and Maintenance. EMC Insurance tech sheet: slight leaking or simmering can indicate improper seating or corrosion and should be immediately corrected.

Question 7. A state boiler guide describes a manual test of a low-pressure boiler's safety valve. About how much steam pressure is present, and how often should it be done at minimum?

  1. About 75 percent of set pressure; at least quarterly
  2. Full set pressure; once every five years
  3. Zero pressure; weekly
  4. Twice the set pressure; monthly
Show answer

Answer: A. The guide lifts the lever with steam at about three-quarters of the valve's set pressure and says low-pressure boiler valves should be tested at least quarterly. NJ DOL's logbook guideline is stricter (monthly lift), so know that sources differ and the plant procedure and manufacturer govern.

Source: Boiler Safety Valves and Overpressure Protection; Minnesota DLI: Boiler Safety. Minnesota DLI Boiler Safety guide (75 percent of set pressure, at least quarterly per NBIC).

Question 8. When bringing a cold steam boiler on line, which practice is correct?

  1. Keep the vent open until steam appears and open the main stop valve slowly
  2. Close the vent first so pressure builds quickly
  3. Open the main stop valve fully at once
  4. Add cold water to the hot boiler to keep the level up
Show answer

Answer: A. Air has to leave before steam fills the space, and opening the main stop slowly lets the header warm and condensate drain instead of slamming through pipe, a classic cause of water hammer. Cold water into a hot boiler causes thermal shock.

Source: Boiler Start-up, Shutdown and Layup Basics. The lesson cites insurer start-up guidance; this point is taken from the lesson text.

Question 9. In the usual gauge-glass blowdown sequence on a steam boiler, which step comes first?

  1. Close the lower gauge cock
  2. Open the petcock with the lower cock open
  3. Close the petcock
  4. Remove the glass
Show answer

Answer: A. Close the lower cock, open the petcock to blow out sediment, crack the lower cock to flush, close the petcock, then open the lower cock fully. The water should return to its level right away; a slow return suggests plugged connections.

Source: Low-Water Cutoff Testing and Maintenance; Weil-McLain SGO Series 4 steam boiler manual. Weil-McLain SGO manual p. 38, 'To clean glass'. Follow your own manual and a trained person's procedure.

Question 10. About what operating pressure does a residential steam heating boiler typically run?

  1. 10 to 15 psi
  2. 5 to 10 psi
  3. About 1/2 to 2 psi
  4. Over 15 psi
Show answer

Answer: C. Residential steam is a very low-pressure system, commonly 1/2 to 2 psi. Higher pressure wastes fuel, causes banging and makes one-pipe air vents work against more resistance.

Source: Steam Boiler Pressure Controls and Gauges; PHCP Pros: Understanding Steam Boiler Pressure Controls. PHCP Pros: keep a residential steam boiler between about 1/2 and 2 psi and verify with a reliable gauge.

Question 11. A subtractive pressure control has its main (cut-out) setting at 2 psi and its differential at 1.5 psi. At what pressure does the burner restart?

  1. 3.5 psi
  2. 2 psi
  3. 1.5 psi
  4. 0.5 psi
Show answer

Answer: D. Subtractive means the differential is subtracted from the main setting: 2 - 1.5 = 0.5 psi. On an additive control you set the cut-in and the differential is added to find the cut-out, so identify the type from the label first.

Source: Steam Boiler Pressure Controls and Gauges; PHCP Pros: Understanding Steam Boiler Pressure Controls. PHCP Pros worked example (MAIN 2 psi, DIFF 1 1/2 psi, restart at 1/2 psi).

Question 12. What can happen if the pigtail (siphon) feeding a pressure control plugs with scale?

  1. The gauge reads high but the burner still shuts off correctly
  2. The control may not sense pressure and cannot stop the burner
  3. The water level rises
  4. The safety valve is isolated
Show answer

Answer: B. The pigtail carries pressure to the control. A plugged pigtail can leave the control blind to rising pressure, so it needs regular inspection and the other protections, the high limit and safety valve, become the only backups.

Source: Steam Boiler Pressure Controls and Gauges; PHCP Pros: Understanding Steam Boiler Pressure Controls. PHCP Pros: pigtails clog easily and a plugged pigtail can let the boiler run unchecked.

Question 13. What is the main purpose of the Hartford loop (equalizer) piping on a steam boiler?

  1. To raise system pressure
  2. To let steam pressure push water out of the boiler
  3. To balance pressure so boiler water is not pushed out into the wet return
  4. To vent air from the main
Show answer

Answer: C. The equalizer carries boiler pressure to the return side, so pressure is balanced and water stays in the boiler. This replaced the sediment-prone check valve used earlier.

Source: Hartford Loop and Near-Boiler Piping; HeatingHelp: The Hartford Loop. HeatingHelp (Dan Holohan): the equalizer balances boiler pressure so water stays in the boiler.

Question 14. A wet return on a Hartford-loop boiler springs a leak. Why does the loop help?

  1. It shuts off the fuel
  2. Water can only drain down to the loop connection, which sits above the crown sheet
  3. It refills the boiler automatically
  4. It replaces the low-water cutoff
Show answer

Answer: B. The loop limits how far the water can fall, buying time for the operator to notice. It is a backup only and never a substitute for a working low-water cutoff.

Source: Hartford Loop and Near-Boiler Piping; HeatingHelp: The Hartford Loop. HeatingHelp: the loop limits drainage to the connection height above the crown sheet and is a backup to, not a replacement for, the cutoff.

Question 15. Why is purging the fireside before lighting a cold boiler so important?

  1. It dries the refractory
  2. It clears unburned fuel and gases so ignition does not cause an explosion
  3. It raises the water temperature
  4. It tests the safety valve
Show answer

Answer: B. A state guide calls skipping the purge the most common cause of furnace explosions, and an insurer sheet says shortening or bypassing the manufacturer's purge time greatly increases explosion risk.

Source: Boiler Flame Safeguards, Purge and Lockout; Minnesota DLI: Boiler Safety; Fireye: Introduction to Flame Safeguards; EMC Insurance: Boiler Operation and Maintenance. Minnesota DLI (purge with dampers open) and Fireye (purge moves air changes through the space before ignition).

Question 16. On a burner with a separate pilot, the pilot flame is not proven during the pilot trial for ignition. What does the flame safeguard do?

  1. Opens the main valve anyway
  2. Keeps retrying forever without limit
  3. Closes the fuel valves and locks out for manual reset
  4. Skips to post-purge and runs normally
Show answer

Answer: C. If flame is not proven in the timed trial, fuel valves close and the control locks out. Find the cause (dirty scanner, ignition, fuel supply) and avoid repeated resets that hide the problem.

Source: Boiler Flame Safeguards, Purge and Lockout; Fireye: Introduction to Flame Safeguards. Fireye: a pilot flame failure causes lockout; typical pilot trial is 5 to 10 seconds, main trial 10 to 15 seconds (examples only).

Question 17. What does bottom (mud) blowdown remove, and how is it usually done?

  1. Dissolved solids at the surface, continuously
  2. Settled sludge, in short bursts
  3. Oxygen, with a deaerator
  4. Scale from the gauge glass, daily
Show answer

Answer: B. Bottom blowdown removes suspended solids that have settled into sludge, using short intermittent bursts. Surface blowdown skims dissolved solids and is often continuous or automatic.

Source: Why Boilers Blow Down; Boiler Water Treatment and Blowdown Basics; U.S. DOE Steam Tip Sheet 9; Spirax Sarco: Bottom Blowdown. DOE Steam Tip Sheet 9 and Spirax Sarco: short, sharp blasts remove settled sludge. Blowdown water flashes to steam, so only trained people follow the written procedure.

Question 18. Federal energy guidance says typical boiler blowdown is about what share of feedwater flow, and what happens with too little blowdown?

  1. 4 to 8 percent; deposits and carryover risk rise
  2. 40 to 80 percent; the safety valve lifts
  3. 0 percent; none is ever needed
  4. 25 percent; the burner overfires
Show answer

Answer: A. DOE gives about 4 to 8 percent, up to 10 percent with high-solids makeup. Too little lets solids build up into deposits, foaming and carryover; too much wastes heat, water and chemicals. Your plant's treatment spec sets the real limit.

Source: Why Boilers Blow Down; U.S. DOE Steam Tip Sheet 9. DOE Steam Tip Sheet 9 (typical rates are not targets).

Question 19. On a closed hot water heating system, how is an expansion tank's air precharge normally set?

  1. Equal to the system fill pressure, set at room temperature with the tank isolated or the system drained
  2. Twice the fill pressure
  3. Zero, so the tank is full of water
  4. To the relief valve setting
Show answer

Answer: A. Precharge equal to fill pressure leaves the tank room to absorb expansion. If precharge is lower, the tank starts partly full of water and has less room left.

Source: Expansion Tanks and Fill Pressure; Amtrol Extrol EX tank instructions. Amtrol Extrol manual: adjust precharge to equal system fill pressure, at ambient temperature only.

Question 20. When shutting down a steam boiler for the season, which practice is correct?

  1. Drain the boiler while it is still hot to save time
  2. Let it cool before draining, and drain only as the procedure says
  3. Add cold water to the hot boiler to speed cooling
  4. Leave a steel boiler empty with no warning sign
Show answer

Answer: B. Draining a hot boiler stresses the metal. Reduce load, stop the burner, keep the level right while it cools, and drain only afterward. An empty steel boiler should carry a clear sign that it is empty and not to be fired.

Source: Boiler Start-up, Shutdown and Layup Basics. The lesson cites insurer and state guidance; this point is taken from the lesson text.
