Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A gas furnace does not simply light a flame when the thermostat asks. It runs a careful checklist, one step at a time, and stops the moment something looks wrong. Knowing the usual order helps you see where a problem lives.

Why the order matters

Many furnaces are controlled by a circuit board that watches switches and sensors and moves through steps in a set order. If a step does not happen, the board usually stops and waits, retries, or locks out. Technician training often says: find the last step that worked, and the trouble is close to the next one. This page describes a typical modern furnace with an induced-draft fan and an electronic igniter. Older and simpler designs differ, and every maker has its own version.

A typical sequence, in general terms

  • Call for heat. The thermostat closes a circuit and the control board sees a low-voltage signal.
  • Safety check. The board confirms that its limit and rollout switches are in their normal, closed state. If one is open, it will not start.
  • Inducer starts. The combustion (inducer) fan runs to pull fresh air through the burner area and push exhaust out the vent.
  • Pressure switch proves airflow. A pressure switch closes to tell the board the fan is moving air. No proof, no ignition.
  • Pre-purge and igniter warm-up. Many boards wait a short time while the igniter warms or a spark circuit prepares. Exact times belong to the manufacturer.
  • Gas valve opens. The board energizes the valve and gas flows to the burners and the igniter lights it.
  • Flame is proven. A flame sensor tells the board that burning is real. If it does not, the board closes the valve and may try again, and after repeated failures it locks out.
  • Blower delay, then blower on. After the heat exchanger warms up, the indoor blower starts so it does not push cold air at people. A delay is common.
  • Run. The furnace heats until the thermostat is satisfied. A high-limit switch can interrupt the burn if air flow is poor and the exchanger overheats.
  • Shutdown. The thermostat opens, the gas valve closes, the flame goes out, the inducer may run briefly to clear exhaust, and the blower keeps running for a time to move the remaining heat.

One maker's example, with numbers

To make the order concrete, here is how one furnace control is described in its installation sheet: the White-Rodgers 50M56U-843 integrated furnace control. These numbers are one maker's example, not a rule. Other makers and other models use different warm-up times, delays and retry counts.

  1. The thermostat calls for heat. The inducer starts and the control checks the pressure switch (the sheet gives no time for this).
  2. Pre-purge: 30 seconds.
  3. Igniter warm-up: 17 seconds (up to 19 seconds on later tries, adding 1 second per retry).
  4. Ignition activation: 3 seconds, then a 4-second trial for ignition. Flame must be detected within those 4 seconds.
  5. Flame is proven. The sheet lists a flame establishing time of up to 0.8 second and a flame failure response time of 2.0 seconds.
  6. Blower on-delay: 30 seconds after flame is proven.
  7. When the call ends: inducer post-purge of 25 seconds, and a blower off-delay of 100 or 150 seconds depending on a switch setting.
  8. If ignition fails, the gas valves close, the control waits through a 60-second inter-purge, and it retries with 1 more second of warm-up. After two retries, a third failure locks the control out. If flame is lost after 10 or more seconds, the control recycles, up to 3 recycles. The lockout clears by interrupting the call for 1 to 20 seconds or by removing 24 volts for 1 second, and it resets itself after 60 minutes.

Notice how closely the real list follows the general order above. Learn the order of events first, and treat the seconds as that one control's settings.

Where common problems show up

  • Nothing happens at the call. Think power, door switch, thermostat signal, or an open limit.
  • Inducer runs, nothing else. Think pressure switch, blocked vent or intake, or a drain trap problem on a condensing unit.
  • Igniter glows or sparks, but no flame. Think gas supply, valve, or a manual shutoff left closed.
  • Flame lights, then goes out in a few seconds. Think a dirty or weak flame sensor, poor burner flame, or grounding.
  • Burner runs, then shuts off early. Think restricted airflow, a dirty filter or a limit switch doing its job.

Many boards tell you the step

Most modern boards have a small light or a display that flashes a code for the last fault. The wiring diagram and code chart in the furnace door or manual say what each code means. Reading that first often saves a lot of time, and it is a habit a good tech builds early.

Safe habits

Gas, electricity, and exhaust are all in the same box. Shut off power and use lockout/tagout before opening anything you plan to touch, and know where the gas shutoff is. Safety switches are there to protect people. Never jumper, tape, or bypass a limit, rollout or pressure switch to make a furnace run, because a tripped switch is telling you something real. If you smell gas, leave, and call the gas utility from a safe place.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: the installation and service instructions for the specific unit in front of you always govern. This lesson explains concepts. It does not give setpoints, wiring steps or test values for your equipment. The timings shown above are one maker's example only, and timings, setpoints and test values vary by manufacturer, model and local rules.

Field card: Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation

Know the order, find the broken step.

  • A typical order is call, safety check, inducer, pressure proof, ignition, gas, flame proof, blower, run, shutdown.
  • The board stops at the first step that does not prove, so find the last good step.
  • Flame lights then drops out often points at flame sensing, not gas.
  • Read the board's light or code before touching anything.
  • Never bypass limit, rollout or pressure switches.
  • Seconds differ by maker. One example control uses 30 s pre-purge, 17 s warm-up, 4 s to prove flame and a 30 s blower delay; yours may not.
