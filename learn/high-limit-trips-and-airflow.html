High-Limit Trips and Airflow

Part of the Gas Heat Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A furnace that heats for a minute or two, then shuts the burner and keeps blowing, is often telling you it overheated. The high-limit switch did its job. Your job is to find out why the furnace got too hot.

What the limit does

Limit switches are normally closed and open when the temperature gets too high. They may be self-resetting, manual reset, or a fuse link. When a limit opens, the board shuts off the burner. HVAC School notes that a limit circuit that stays open too long can trigger a lockout (a three-minute example) and one manufacturer guide shows a limit-circuit lockout that resets itself after some hours. The sequence is explained in Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation.

Airflow is the usual suspect

A training-site article states that airflow restrictions are almost always the cause of high limit trips. Less air through the heat exchanger means it cannot shed heat, so the plenum temperature climbs. Typical causes:

  • a dirty filter
  • closed or blocked supply registers, or blocked returns
  • duct blockage or undersized ducts
  • a weak or failing blower motor, or a dirty blower wheel
  • a dirty indoor coil on a system with A/C, since it sits in the same airstream

For the general method see Airflow and Static Pressure Basics and Ductwork Basics and Common Problems.

Numbers that tell the story

HVAC School suggests checking temperature rise (supply minus return air temperature) and external static pressure against the furnace nameplate. If either is out of the range printed there, look at airflow. Your nameplate gives the allowed rise range; do not rely on a number from a web page. A quick test mentioned by HVAC School is to run the furnace briefly without the filter, and see whether the limit stays closed. That points at the filter or at a restricted return, but put the filter back and fix the cause. HVAC School also notes that high gas pressure can overheat the furnace, so verify the manifold pressure against the data plate.

Other things to check

  • Blower speed tap or ECM setting: an incorrect heat speed lowers airflow. See ECM Blower Motors.
  • Soot or dirt on the switch: one article says heavy soot can falsely trip it. Soot also signals a combustion problem to investigate.
  • The switch itself: only after airflow, gas pressure and combustion are cleared.

Safety

Shut off power before opening the panels. Never bypass or jumper a limit, or install a higher-rated one. It protects the heat exchanger, and a cracked exchanger can put carbon monoxide in the air supply. See Combustion and CO Safety Basics.

Field card: High-Limit Trips and Airflow

Find out why it got hot.

  • Limits open on high temperature; the burner shuts off and the blower keeps running.
  • Airflow restrictions are the most common cause: filter, registers, ducts, blower.
  • Compare temperature rise and static pressure to the nameplate.
  • Check gas pressure and soot too; combustion can overheat the unit.
  • Never bypass a limit or swap in a higher-rated one.
