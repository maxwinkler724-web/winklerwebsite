Hot-Water No-Heat Diagnosis: Circulator, Zone Valve, Air and Pressure

Last updated: 2026-10-16

The boiler is hot, the house is cold. That one complaint has at least five common causes, and each leaves different clues on the gauge and the pipes. This lesson walks through them in the same way the Boiler Diagnosis Simulator does, so you can practice the thinking there.

Start with three readings

Before touching anything, read system pressure, supply temperature and return temperature. These three numbers split the five causes quickly. Compare cold pressure to the fill setting, and look at the supply-to-return difference. A boiler that is very hot with a very large supply-to-return difference is making heat that is not being carried away. A boiler with almost no pressure suggests a fill or leak problem. A rising pressure that reaches the relief valve points to the expansion tank.

1. Seized or failed circulator

If the burner fires, the boiler gets hot and noisy, and the radiators stay cold, suspect that water is not moving. A circulator can hum with full voltage at its terminals while the rotor is stuck. Technician advice from a controls article: do not trust voltage at the panel alone; measure across the relay contacts, then measure the circulator's running amps against its data plate, and confirm water is actually moving by checking the temperature difference between supply and return. A running motor does not prove circulation. Power off and isolate before spinning a shaft or replacing the pump, and purge air afterward.

2. Air lock in a zone

If one floor heats and another gurgles and stays cold, think air. Caleffi's publication on air elimination says a large pocket at a high point can block flow entirely if the circulator cannot push water over it, and that air also causes noise, poor heat transfer and wear on pump bushings. Purge one branch at a time until the discharge shows no visible bubbles for at least 30 seconds, then all circuits together. If air keeps coming back, look for the cause: tank placement, low fill pressure, a leaking fitting. Caleffi says the expansion tank belongs on the circulator inlet side, since on the discharge side pressure at some points can drop below atmospheric and pull air in.

3. Waterlogged or failed expansion tank

Heated water expands. The tank's air cushion absorbs it. An older plain steel tank can lose its air and become waterlogged; a bladder tank can fail too. Symptoms published by sources include rising pressure that reaches the relief valve, a dripping relief valve and, per an Amtrol FAQ, rapid pump cycling in other applications. Amtrol says the hydronic precharge should equal the system fill pressure. Check precharge only with the tank isolated and water-side pressure relieved, per the manufacturer. If the tank has no air or water comes from the air valve, replace the tank and set the precharge before it goes on. A relief valve that drips is a warning, not a nuisance; replacing the valve without finding the cause hides a problem, and a clogged valve may leave the boiler with no protection.

4. Fill valve not holding pressure

Gauge near zero, pipes ticking and gurgling, and a hot boiler: check the fill valve. Compare the inlet (house) pressure with the outlet (system) pressure. If the house has pressure and the system does not, the valve may be clogged. Caleffi says to keep at least 5 psi static pressure at the top of the system, and a cold fill is usually set a bit higher. The simulator uses 12 psi only as an example number; we have no maker source for it, so use the fill pressure in your boiler's manual. Do not feed cold water into a hot, nearly dry cast-iron boiler; that can crack sections. Let it cool and refill slowly.

5. Zone valve motor

If one zone calls and you hear a click but nothing opens, measure 24 volts at the valve motor while the thermostat calls. A forum technician thread describes the logic: the motor opens the valve, and an end switch then tells the boiler to fire. If 24 volts is present and the valve body moves freely by hand, the power head is the suspect. Pressing the end switch by hand to see if the boiler fires separates a switch problem from a motor or valve problem. Power off and follow the maker's steps for removing the head.

Close the loop

After the repair, verify: pressure steady, supply and return difference reasonable, every zone calling and heating. Log what you found. For a deeper look at zone hardware see Zone Valves vs. Zone Circulators, and try the Boiler Diagnosis Simulator.

Field card: Hot-Water No-Heat Diagnosis: Circulator, Zone Valve, Air and Pressure

Read pressure and temperatures first. Then pick one cause at a time.

  • Hot boiler, huge supply-return split: water is not moving (circulator, air lock).
  • Pressure climbing to the relief valve: expansion tank.
  • Pressure near zero: fill valve or leak.
  • One zone dead with a click: 24 V at the motor, then the motor or end switch.
  • Purge branch by branch; tank on the pump inlet side per Caleffi.
