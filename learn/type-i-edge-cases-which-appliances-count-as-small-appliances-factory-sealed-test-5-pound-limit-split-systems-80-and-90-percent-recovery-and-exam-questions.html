Type I Edge Cases: Which Appliances Count as Small Appliances

Last updated: 2026-11-04

Type I is the entry level of the federal refrigerant technician types, and it covers small appliances only. Exam writers love the edge cases: a 4 pound unit that does not qualify, a 6 pound unit that sounds small, a window unit that does. This lesson shows the test that settles all of them.

The definition, in plain words

The federal definition says a small appliance is one that is fully manufactured, charged and hermetically sealed in a factory, with 5 pounds or less of refrigerant. Read it as three factory words plus one number. If a unit fails any of the factory words, the number never matters. The federal list of examples includes household and commercial refrigerators and freezers, room air conditioners (window, portable and packaged terminal units), dehumidifiers, under-the-counter ice makers, vending machines and drinking water coolers.

Why the factory-sealed test matters more than size

Size is easy to misread. A split system can hold 3 pounds and still fail, because its line set is connected and charged in the field. A small walk-in condensing unit charged on site fails for the same reason. The point of the category is that a sealed, factory-built circuit has few joints and was never opened by an installer. Once a circuit is assembled or charged outside a factory, it is treated like the larger equipment classes, which fall under Type II or Type III work. Our own plain-language rule: ask how it was built and charged first, then ask how much it holds.

Common examples

  • Household refrigerators and freezers. Factory-sealed, small charge. Type I.
  • Window and portable room air conditioners. Sealed in the factory, and the federal examples list them. Packaged terminal units (the through-wall kind) also appear on the list, but only when the nameplate charge is 5 pounds or less.
  • Dehumidifiers, drinking water coolers, vending machines, under-counter ice makers. All named in the examples. Type I if sealed and 5 pounds or less.
  • Outside the category: a ductless or central split system, anything charged or completed in the field, and any appliance with more than 5 pounds, even if it is a sealed, self-contained unit.

What changes for recovery and access

For small appliances the recovery requirement is a percentage of the refrigerant in the appliance, not a vacuum depth. Per the federal text, when using recovery equipment made on or after November 15, 1993, you recover 90 percent if the compressor is functioning and 80 percent if it is not. With older recovery equipment the requirement is 80 percent. The text also allows evacuating the appliance to 4 inches of mercury vacuum instead. Larger appliances use evacuation levels that depend on pressure class and charge, covered in the lessons linked below.

The recovery section we read does not mention access fittings or piercing valves for small appliances. Many sealed units have no service port, so the technician has to create access. That practice has its own lesson, linked below, and the practical advice there is to use a brazed fitting rather than leave a piercing valve behind.

Worked example (our own, with assumptions)

Recovery for two of them. The refrigerator compressor runs, so the 90 percent amount applies: 0.30 lb x 0.90 = 0.27 lb to recover, leaving 0.03 lb behind. The window unit compressor is dead, so the 80 percent amount applies: 1.8 lb x 0.80 = 1.44 lb to recover, leaving 0.36 lb. Check: 0.27 + 0.03 = 0.30, and 1.44 + 0.36 = 1.80.

Exam-style questions

  1. A unit holds 4 pounds and was charged by the installer after the lines were connected. Type I? No. It was not charged in a factory.
  2. A sealed self-contained unit holds 6 pounds. Type I? No. Over 5 pounds.
  3. A household freezer has a running compressor and you use recovery equipment made after 1993. What percent? 90 percent.
  4. The same freezer has a seized compressor. What percent? 80 percent.
  5. True or false: a dehumidifier can be a small appliance. True, if factory sealed with 5 pounds or less.

Field card: Type I edge cases

Factory first, pounds second.

  • Small appliance: made, charged and sealed in a factory, 5 pounds or less.
  • Field charged or field assembled: not small, whatever the charge.
  • Split systems are outside Type I.
  • Over 5 pounds: outside Type I even when sealed.
  • Recovery: 90 percent running compressor, 80 percent if not (equipment made after 11/15/1993).
