RTU Gas Heat Section Basics

Part of the Gas Heat Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Gas heat in a rooftop unit combines fuel, fire and a flue. Everything in this section exists to light safely, stop on trouble and vent the products of combustion outside. Safety devices are never optional.

What is in the section

A gas heat compartment typically holds a gas valve, burners, an igniter, a flame sensor, a heat exchanger, a draft inducer fan and several safety switches. The sequence is much like a furnace: see Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation and Flame Safeguard and Ignition Basics. Always use the unit's own wiring diagram and sequence.

Safety switches in plain words

  • Limit switch. Watches temperature near the heat exchanger and shuts the burner off if things get too hot. One article gives a trip range of roughly 170 to 200 F for typical furnace limits as an example, and the unit's own rating governs. Some limits reset on their own after cooling.
  • Flame rollout switch. Mounted near the burners, it opens if flame rolls out of the combustion area. It usually needs a manual reset. A trip can point to a blocked vent, weak draft, dirty burners, high gas pressure, or a cracked heat exchanger. Resetting without finding the cause is the mistake to avoid.
  • Pressure switch. Proves the draft inducer is running and the vent path is open before ignition is allowed.
Never bypass, jumper or tape over any of these. A trip is a clue.

Gas piping and pressure

A manufacturer's manual says to check all gas connections with a commercial leak-detection solution and never with an open flame. It also warns about exceeding 0.5 psig during piping pressure tests with the unit connected, because that can damage the gas valve, and says to disconnect the unit's gas piping for higher-pressure tests. The manual lists inlet and manifold pressure ranges for its models, and notes that propane units need a conversion kit. Treat those numbers as examples from one unit. Your unit's data plate and manual set the values. Never re-drill an orifice, and never use gas piping as an electrical ground.

What an inspection looks at

A published RTU gas heat inspection template groups checks like this:

  • Setup: confirm the right unit, lockout/tagout, and PPE before opening the compartment.
  • Ignition parts: igniter condition and resistance against the maker's spec, secure wiring, clean and dry compartment.
  • Flame sensor and burners: clean sensor, correct position in the flame path, burners free of rust, blockage or lint.
  • Heat exchanger and gas train: look for cracks, corrosion, holes or soot trails, and for leaks at the valve and fittings.
  • Flue and venting: clear path, no nests, no corrosion, secure termination, and no sign of flue gas staining or backdraft.
Heat exchanger and combustion topics are covered in Heat Exchanger Basics and Combustion and CO Safety Basics.

Flue and air intake terminals

The manufacturer's manual stresses keeping flue and air intake terminals unobstructed in every season and sealing openings around vent and intake pipes, because of carbon monoxide risk. It also lists clearances from building openings and meters as unit-specific rules. Follow your unit's instructions and local code.

Safe habits

  • Lock out electrical power and shut the gas supply before working on the section, unless a manual procedure requires otherwise.
  • If you smell gas, do not operate electrical switches. Leave and call the gas supplier, as the manufacturer's manual says.
  • A cracked heat exchanger, a repeated rollout trip, or any CO alarm means shut the section down and escalate. Do not keep resetting.
  • Do not do anything beyond your training or license. Gas work follows local rules.

Field card: RTU Gas Heat Section Basics

Gas, fire, flue. Respect every safety device.

  • Limit, rollout and pressure switches are safety devices. Never bypass them.
  • A rollout trip needs a cause found, not just a reset.
  • Leak-check with solution, never with a flame.
  • Inspect igniter, sensor, burners, heat exchanger and flue.
  • Keep flue and intake terminals clear. Smell gas: leave and call.
