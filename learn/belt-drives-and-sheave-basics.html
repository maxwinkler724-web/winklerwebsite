Belt Drives and Sheave Basics

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Many larger rooftop units drive the supply blower with a motor, a belt and two pulleys. Belts are cheap and sheaves are simple, which is why mistakes with them are common.

The parts

A belt drive has a motor with a drive sheave (pulley), a belt or belts, and a driven sheave on the blower shaft. The speed of the blower depends on the ratio between the two sheave diameters. One HVAC School example: a 6 inch drive turning at 1,000 RPM and a 12 inch driven pulley gives 500 RPM. Moving to 600 RPM needs a 1.2 times ratio, so the drive diameter grows to about 7.2 inches. These are example numbers only.

Adjustable sheaves change speed, not tension

An adjustable sheave has two halves that move closer together or farther apart, which changes the effective diameter where the belt rides. Closer halves raise the belt and speed the blower up. HVAC School warns that this adjustment is for fan speed only and is not how you tension a belt. Belt tension is set with the motor mount adjustment. The same article advises against casual changes to sheave position, because they can upset air balance and fresh air intake. After any change, retension and check alignment.

Amps and overload

Blower speed changes can change motor load a lot. Measure blower motor amperage before and after a change and compare it to the motor nameplate. Never make a change that overloads the motor. Check airflow restrictions, such as dirty filters, closed dampers, or dirty coils, before blaming the drive, and take readings with panels closed so airflow is realistic. See Airflow and Static Pressure Basics.

Tension: not too tight, not too loose

The idea quoted in a belt article is that ideal tension is the lowest tension that does not slip at peak load. Too loose causes slip, noise and wear. Too tight shortens belt and bearing life. Two methods appear in trade sources:

  • Check deflection with a belt tension tool. A Browning V-belt manual gives the required deflection as the belt span times 1/64 inch (a 32 inch span means 1/2 inch), with the force taken from the maker's table and applied at the center of the span on one belt. Another trade source writes the same idea as 1/64 inch per inch of center distance.
  • Use the manufacturer's value if the unit gives one. A belt maker or unit label may list the right force.
Amps are not a tension gauge. They help you spot over-tensioning, but do not prove tension is right. New belts stretch. The Browning manual says to recheck tension after a jog start or 1 to 3 minutes of running, then at 8 hours, 24 hours and 100 hours, and periodically after that. It also says not to install new belts in worn sheave grooves.

Alignment

Misaligned sheaves wear belts fast. Check alignment with a straightedge, string or alignment tool, and use the belt groove rather than the outer face, because outer faces can vary in thickness. One source suggests staying within about 1/16 inch per 12 inches of center distance. Recheck after installing belts, since tension can shift alignment.

Belt and sheave condition

  • Replace belts as a complete matched set from one maker. Mismatched belts load unevenly.
  • Squealing under load usually points to low tension. Belt dust in the guard points to slipping. Do not use belt dressing to hide it.
  • Worn grooves let belts slip, and tightening a belt on a worn sheave only moves the problem. Replace worn sheaves rather than fitting new belts into them; ask the belt or sheave maker for their wear-measurement method (a sheave gauge is the usual tool). We found no published wear limit in the maker manual we read, so we do not give one.
  • Check bearing lubrication while you are there.

Safety

Lock out and tag out the disconnect before touching a belt, sheave or guard. A blower can start on a call from the controls. Put the belt guard back before restoring power, and keep hands, hair and loose clothing away from rotating parts. Write the sheave size, center distance, RPM and belt tension on the blower housing for the next tech, as HVAC School suggests.

Field card: Belt Drives and Sheave Basics

Sheave sets speed. Motor position sets tension.

  • Sheave changes speed. Motor mount sets tension.
  • Ideal tension is the lowest that does not slip.
  • Align using belt grooves; check again after tensioning.
  • Replace belts as a matched set and replace worn sheaves.
  • Lock out power and replace the guard every time.
