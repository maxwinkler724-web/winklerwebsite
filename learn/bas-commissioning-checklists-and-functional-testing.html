BAS Commissioning: Checklists and Functional Testing

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A new BAS can look perfect on the screen and still be wired wrong. Commissioning is the structured way to prove that every point, sequence and alarm does what the design says.

Two layers of checking

Commissioning guidance usually separates checks into two layers. Point-to-point verification proves each input and output is connected, named and scaled correctly. Functional performance testing proves the equipment and sequences work together as designed. A university commissioning specification adds a stage in between: a controls checkout by the BAS contractor before functional testing begins. For the basics of the system, see Building Automation Basics.

Point-to-point verification

A published template lays out the steps in a clear order.

1. Setup. The approved points list, sequence of operations and control drawings are on site, and test instruments are calibrated. 2. Mapping. Each point's name, type, controller address, description and units match the submittal and the graphics. 3. Analog inputs. Compare the BAS reading to a measured or simulated field condition within tolerance, confirm scaling, and force an out-of-range or disconnected condition to see the alarm. 4. Analog outputs. Confirm the commanded value matches, the device moves in the right direction and range, the fail position is right, and releasing an override returns to automatic. 5. Binary inputs. Confirm the status changes when the field contact operates and that alarms and interlocks respond. 6. Binary outputs. Confirm the device energizes and de-energizes, feedback matches the command, and the device goes to a safe state on loss of power or signal where safe to test. 7. Final review. Record every deficiency and its fix, review with the team and sign off.

The same template says it does not replace functional performance testing, code-driven testing for fire, smoke control and emergency shutdown, or manufacturer start-up procedures.

Calibration

A university specification asks for a three-point check of each analog point, low, normal and high, against a certified reference, using test equipment more accurate than the sensor. Not every owner asks for this, but it shows the idea: one point can hide a sensor that is right in the middle and wrong at the ends.

Sequences and tuning

After the points are proven, testers check that programming matches the written sequence in all normal modes, tune loops for fast but stable response, and test alarms, safeties, failure and restart modes. One university specification asks for a long fully automatic run with points trended, and for owner-witnessed demonstrations of loop response and trends. Those durations and sample intervals are that owner's numbers and differ from job to job.

Functional performance tests

These confirm the equipment and systems operate per design intent and the owner's project requirements, including normal, alternate and emergency modes. The Northwestern specification has contractors execute the tests with the commissioning authority witnessing, requires seasonal tests to be run in the right season, and requires the controls contractor to deliver annotated trend logs and alarm histories before claiming readiness. Those logs cover stability, recovery from setbacks, seasonal modes and power loss and restoration.

An acceptance procedure for condenser water reset shows a typical functional test: override the reset variable up and down through the BAS, then return to automatic, and watch whether the plant responds as the sequence says. See Cooling Tower Fan VFDs, Staging and Condenser Water Reset.

Deficiencies and retests

Commissioning keeps an issues log. The contractor fixes items, notifies the authority and retests. Contract terms about who pays for retests vary by owner. Final acceptance comes after documentation, training and any required seasonal checks.

Documentation to expect

Points lists, sequences, diagrams, as-built drawings, test reports, graphics and database backups, and operator and maintenance manuals. If you inherit a building, ask where they are.

Habits for the tech

  • Read the sequence before you test, not after.
  • Record the starting state of any point you override, and release it.
  • Test safeties only in ways the procedure allows. Never jumper or force a life-safety device.
  • Write down what you saw, not what you expected.

Field card: BAS Commissioning: Checklists and Functional Testing

Prove the point, then prove the sequence, then prove it again in the right season.

  • Point-to-point proves wiring, scaling, units and direction.
  • Functional tests prove sequences, alarms and failure modes.
  • Annotated trends are evidence, not decoration.
  • Release overrides and restore the starting state.
  • Fire and life-safety testing follows its own code-driven procedures.
