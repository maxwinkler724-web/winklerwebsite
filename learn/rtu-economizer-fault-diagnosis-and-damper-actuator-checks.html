RTU Economizer Fault Diagnosis and Damper Actuator Checks

An economizer that fails quietly costs money all year. The unit still cools, so nobody complains. This lesson is about finding the faults, using temperature and position checks you can do from the roof.

Why they fail so often

A Western Cooling Efficiency Center case study cites research saying 64% of economizers suffer from faults and failures. It lists common ones: a damper that fails to move (motor, broken link or jammed blade), a changeover setpoint set wrong, too much outdoor air, and a failed or disconnected temperature sensor. A damper stuck open adds heating and cooling load; one stuck closed misses free cooling. If you need a refresher on what the parts do, see Economizers Basics.

Check the damper mechanically

Pacific Northwest National Laboratory's retuning material says broken, loose or disconnected linkage and damaged seals can keep the damper from fully opening or closing. A simple test it describes: when the outdoor-air damper is commanded wide open, mixed-air temperature should land within about 4 to 5 degrees F of outdoor-air temperature, allowing for fan heat. If not, inspect the linkage and seals. With the unit locked out, move the damper by hand and see that it travels freely and fully. Lockout/tagout, hot surfaces, sharp blade edges and pinch points matter here.

Check the actuator and its signal

A failed actuator or disconnected wire leaves the damper unresponsive. A checkout procedure in PNNL training material says to confirm the outside-air damper moves to full open and to minimum without binding, and that under economizing the actuator modulates to hold the cooling setpoint. Compare the commanded position to the actual position, and check the supply voltage and control signal at the actuator before replacing it. Some actuators have a spring return, so a wiring fault can make them fall to the closed position.

Check the sensors

Failed or poorly calibrated outdoor, return or mixed-air sensors make bad decisions. PNNL suggests testing that, at minimum position, mixed-air temperature is within about 4 to 5 degrees F of return-air temperature, and recommends an averaging sensor for mixed air because mixing is rarely complete. For analog sensors, measure resistance or signal and compare to the manufacturer's table at a known temperature; switches and snap disks get a continuity check at their trip point. Record as-found and final values.

Check the settings and sequence

  • Changeover setting: if the dial does not match the climate, the unit may never economize. One Honeywell-based checkout lists several letter settings for dry and humid climates.
  • Minimum position: too much outdoor air wastes energy, too little breaks ventilation rules. PNNL says minimum position is typically 20% or less, but your drawings and local code govern.
  • Freeze protection and unoccupied operation: dampers should return to minimum in cold weather and stay closed when the building is unoccupied.
  • Simultaneous heating and cooling: PNNL notes the cooling should not run hard while the economizer is open; compare valve or stage and damper position over time.

Use the trend log

If the building has a BAS, trend the damper command, damper feedback, outdoor air, return air and mixed air. Dampers that are open at strange times, or temperatures that do not follow the command, tell you where to look. See BAS alarms and trend logs.

Field card: RTU Economizer Fault Diagnosis and Damper Actuator Checks

Command, position, mixed-air temperature: do they agree?

  • Damper stuck open wastes heat and cooling; stuck closed loses free cooling.
  • Test: damper commanded open should drive mixed air near outdoor air.
  • Test: at minimum, mixed air should be near return air.
  • Check linkage, actuator voltage and signal, then sensors, then settings.
  • Lock out before reaching into the damper section.
