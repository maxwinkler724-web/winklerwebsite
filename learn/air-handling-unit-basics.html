Air Handling Unit Basics

An air handling unit, or AHU, is a big box that cleans, heats, cools and moves air for a building or a part of one. Once you know the sections, any AHU becomes easier to read.

What an AHU is

An AHU is usually a metal casing with several sections in a row. Air comes in, passes through each section, and leaves into ductwork. Unlike a rooftop unit, an AHU often has no built-in refrigeration. Its coils are typically fed by a central plant, with chilled water for cooling and hot water or steam for heating.

The sections, in airflow order

  • Mixing box and dampers. Return air from the building is blended with outside air. Dampers set the blend, and they can also open for free cooling. See Economizers Basics.
  • Filters. Usually first in line to protect everything after them. A coarse filter often comes before a finer one. Technicians watch the pressure drop across a filter to judge when it is loaded.
  • Heating coil. Hot water, steam, electric or gas. A preheat coil may protect other parts from cold outside air.
  • Cooling coil. Chilled water or refrigerant. It cools the air and removes moisture, which drains away through a drain pan and trap.
  • Supply fan. Moves the air. It may be driven by a variable-speed drive. Large systems may add a return fan too.
  • Other options. Humidifiers, heat recovery devices, sound attenuators and flexible duct connections that keep vibration out of the ducts.

Constant volume or variable volume

A constant-volume unit moves the same amount of air all the time and changes the air temperature to match the load. A variable-volume unit changes the airflow and keeps the supply temperature steady. The second type feeds terminal boxes in the zones. See VAV Boxes Basics.

Reading an AHU as you walk up to it

  • Is the filter section dirty or bypassed? Are filters seated flat and sealed?
  • Are the fan belts, bearings and sheaves quiet and in good shape? Belt-drive units need the right tension and alignment, set per the maker.
  • Are the coil fins clean, and is the drain pan draining without standing water?
  • Are dampers moving freely, with linkages intact?
  • Do supply, return and mixed-air temperatures make sense together?

Common problems at a general level

Dirty filters and coils cut airflow. A stuck damper can send the wrong amount of outside air. A plugged condensate drain can flood the pan. A fan that spins backward after motor work moves much less air. A frozen coil often points to low airflow or a control problem. Always find the cause before resetting anything.

Safety

AHUs have fans that can start by themselves, high-voltage motors, hot or cold coils and sometimes stored energy in dampers. Lock out and tag out before opening a panel or touching a rotating part, and never bypass a safety device to keep something running. Many units have remote start, so verify the fan is locked out, not just switched off.

Field card: Air Handling Unit Basics

Follow the air through the box.

  • Typical order: mixing box and dampers, filters, heating coil, cooling coil, fan.
  • Coils usually come from a central plant (chilled water, hot water or steam).
  • Filters, belts, coils and drain pans are the routine wear points.
  • Constant volume changes temperature; variable volume changes airflow.
  • Lock out the fan, and check remote start, before opening a door.
