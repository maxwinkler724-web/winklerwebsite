RTU Cooling Sequence of Operation and the Safety Chain

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the Commercial Cooling Guide

When a rooftop unit blows warm air, the compressor is not always the problem. Often the compressor was never allowed to start, and the reason sits in a short chain of safety switches.

Safety first

Reading a sequence is paper work, but testing it means live voltage on a roof. Lock out and verify dead before touching wiring or panels (Lockout/Tagout and Verify Dead for HVAC), and never jumper a safety to “see if it runs.” A jumpered safety removes the protection that keeps a compressor from destroying itself.

The generic sequence

This is a general outline, not any one unit’s instructions. Thermostat terminals are explained in Thermostat Terminal Letters.

  1. Call for cooling. With the thermostat in Cool and the room warmer than setpoint, the Y circuit closes. The indoor blower is normally called on through the G circuit. How and when the blower starts differs by unit, so the unit’s own wiring diagram decides.
  2. Compressor contactor. The contactor coil is wired through the safety devices. One manufacturer’s published sequence for an electromechanical, no-economizer unit says the stage-one contactor pulls in only if the low-pressure control, the high-pressure control and the discharge-line thermostat are all closed.
  3. Compressor and outdoor fan. On that same model, when the contactor closes, the compressor and the outdoor fan motor start together. Other models may stage or cycle the fans differently.
  4. Second stage. If stage one cannot hold the temperature, a second call closes and a second contactor pulls in for the second compressor, again only through its own safety chain.

The safety chain

Think of the three devices as switches wired in series with the contactor coil. If any one is open, the coil gets no power and the contactor stays out. Each compressor stage has its own set.

  • Low-pressure control opens when suction pressure falls too low, which can mean low charge, poor airflow or a cold-weather condition (general practice).
  • High-pressure control opens when head pressure climbs, for example from a dirty condenser coil or a failed condenser fan (general practice).
  • Discharge-line thermostat opens when the discharge line is too hot.

More on what these devices protect is in RTU Refrigerant Circuit and Compressor Protection.

“No cooling, fan runs”

The indoor blower can run happily with a safety open, so the room gets air that is not cooled. A call for cooling is present, but the contactor is not pulled in. The question to ask is why the chain is open, not whether the compressor is bad. Read the circuit with the unit’s diagram: is the Y call arriving at the board, is there control voltage at the contactor coil, and which device in series has no voltage across to the next one? An open device is a symptom. A pressure switch that trips repeatedly is telling you to find the airflow, charge or coil problem behind it. For the pieces that fail downstream of the contactor, see Testing Capacitors, Contactors and Relays with a Meter.

Timers: check the unit’s own IOM

Many units add an anti-short-cycle delay and other timers, and some control boards lock out after repeated trips. The manufacturer page used for this lesson does not state any delay, and delay lengths vary, so this lesson gives none. Look them up in the installation, operation and maintenance manual (IOM) for the exact model. Startup steps are in RTU Startup and Checkout Basics, and the big picture is in Rooftop Unit Basics.

Under review

Low-ambient behavior and outdoor fan staging are not covered by the sources used here. Treat them as unit-specific until you have read that unit’s manual.

Field card: RTU Cooling Sequence of Operation and the Safety Chain

A cooling call does nothing if the chain is open. Find out why before blaming the compressor.

  • Cool mode and a warm room close Y; the indoor blower follows through G, in the way the unit’s diagram shows.
  • One maker’s example: the contactor pulls in only if low-pressure, high-pressure and discharge-line thermostat are all closed.
  • Fan running with no cooling can be an open safety, not a failed compressor.
  • Check Y arriving, then control voltage at the coil, then which series device is open. Do this with proper PPE and lockout rules.
  • Never jumper a safety. A repeat trip means find the cause (airflow, charge, coil, fan).
  • Delay timers are unit-specific: read that unit’s IOM.
