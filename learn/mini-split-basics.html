Mini-Split Basics

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A mini-split is a heat pump with no big duct system. A small outdoor unit connects to one or more wall, floor or ceiling units inside the rooms they serve.

The basic idea

A mini-split moves heat instead of making it. In cooling it pulls heat out of the room and sends it outside. In heating the flow reverses and it pulls heat from outdoor air into the room. If you know the idea behind a regular heat pump, this is the same cycle in a compact package. See Heat Pump Basics first if that is new.

Main parts

  • Outdoor unit: the compressor, the outdoor coil, a fan and the reversing valve. Many modern units use an inverter-driven compressor that can run slowly or quickly as needed.
  • Indoor unit (head): an indoor coil, a blower, a drain pan and the controls the room sees. Wall-mounted heads are the most common, but floor, ceiling cassette and small ducted types exist.
  • Line set: two copper refrigerant lines, one larger and one smaller, plus a power and communication cable and a condensate drain. Many mini-splits connect the lines with flared fittings, which can mean little or no brazing on the job.
  • Controls: a remote or app, and a control board in each unit that talks to the other one.

Why people choose them

They suit rooms with no ducts, additions, older houses and spaces that need their own temperature. Because there are no ducts, there are no duct losses. Inverter units adjust their speed instead of cycling hard on and off, which can feel steadier. A multi-zone outdoor unit can feed several heads, though each head and the outdoor unit must be a compatible match. Larger multi-room systems with many indoor units are covered in VRF and VRV Basics.

Planning an installation, in general terms

  • Size from a real load calculation, not from a rule of thumb. In general practice, an oversized head in a small room can short-cycle and feel clammy.
  • Place the indoor head where air can move through the room, away from direct blasts onto seats or beds, with clear space around it as the maker specifies.
  • Keep the line set short and neat, within the maker's length and height limits.
  • Evacuate and leak-check the lines the way the manufacturer says before opening the valves. Moisture and air inside the system cause trouble later (general practice, so follow the maker's evacuation steps).
  • Drain the condensate with proper slope and a safe termination. A mini-split makes water in cooling, and a head that leaks onto a wall is a common callback.
  • Mount the outdoor unit level, clear of snow, leaves and walls, with room for airflow and service.

Newer refrigerants

Many current units use A2L refrigerants, which are mildly flammable. Charge amount, room size and installation steps then matter more. See A2L and A3 Refrigerants. The rule that overrides everything on this page: the installation and service instructions for the specific unit in front of you, and the rules where you work, always govern. This lesson gives no charge amounts, line lengths or wiring details because they vary by model.
