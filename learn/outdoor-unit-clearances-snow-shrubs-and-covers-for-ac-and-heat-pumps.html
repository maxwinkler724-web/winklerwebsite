Outdoor Unit Clearances: Snow, Shrubs and Covers for AC and Heat Pumps

Last updated: 2026-10-16

People ask how much clearance an AC or heat pump needs, and the honest answer is "whatever the manual for that unit says." This lesson shows what several makers print, how to check a planned spot against a table, and what to do about snow, shrubs and winter covers.

The short answer

Most residential outdoor units need open space on every side for airflow and a clear zone above for the fan discharge. Carrier's 25HNB6/25HNB9 heat pump instructions (edition 03/15) call for 24 in. at the service end, 6 in. on one side, 12 in. on all remaining sides, and 48 in. above. Lennox gives a similar pattern for its ML18XC2 air conditioner (9/2023) and its older HP26 heat pump: 30 in. at the service panel, 36 in. on one side, 12 in. on one more, 6 in. on the last, and 48 in. on top. Goodman's installation manual (file labeled IOG-4009L, June 2021) asks for a top that is unobstructed, and if something is overhead, at least 60 in. between the top of the unit and that obstruction. Trane's installer's guide for the 2TWZ9C heat pump (02/09) says at least 12 in. from any wall or surrounding shrubbery. These numbers differ by model and by year, so they are examples, not a universal rule.

Mitsubishi Electric's installation manual for the MSZ-FD09NA and FD12NA wall-mounted systems (doc. JG79A015H01) is much tighter: it says to keep basically 4 in. or more open in front and on both sides. A small ductless unit and a big split-system condenser are not the same animal.

Worked example: checking a planned spot

Say you plan a Carrier 25HNB6 heat pump at the side of a house. Measure each side and compare to the manual's rule: 24 in. at the service end, 6 in. on one side, 12 in. on the rest, 48 in. above.

  1. Service end. Control-box end faces a lawn with 30 in. free. 30 is at least 24. Pass.
  2. The 6 in. side. The house wall is 8 in. away. 8 is at least 6. Pass. Only one side gets the smaller number.
  3. First 12 in. side. A fence is 14 in. away. 14 is at least 12. Pass.
  4. Second 12 in. side. A shrub's outer branches are 10 in. away. 10 is less than 12, a shortfall of 12 - 10 = 2 in. Fail.
  5. Above. The roof overhang is 52 in. up. 52 is at least 48. Pass.

Result: three passes and one fail. The fix is to trim the shrub back at least 2 in., or better, much more. Lennox's homeowner page recommends keeping plants at least two feet (24 in.) from the unit on all sides, which is a good habit even when the installation manual's minimum is lower. Shrubs keep growing, so leave room beyond the minimum.

Snow check, with an invented example depth of 14 in. of typical snow cover. Carrier says to elevate the unit to clear the estimated snowfall level; Lennox says the base should be elevated above the depth of average snows. With a 3 in. pad plus a 12 in. stand, the base sits 3 + 12 = 15 in. up. 15 is more than 14, so it clears with 1 in. to spare, which is thin. Goodman prints minimum heights by temperature: 2 1/2 in. at +15 F and above, 8 in. from -5 F to +14 F, and 12 in. below -5 F. Those are minimums for drainage and airflow, not snow depth, so use whichever number is larger.

Snow, stands and wall brackets

Lennox's heat pump instructions (508844-01) say not to place the unit where snow drifts, to raise the base above the depth of average snows, and to use a snow stand where freezing temperatures last. Carrier adds that roof or eave runoff should not fall on the unit. Trane says to elevate 3 to 12 in. depending on local weather. Mitsubishi's manual says to pick a spot not affected by snowfall or blowing snow and, in heavy-snow areas, to add a canopy, a pedestal and/or baffle boards, and to secure the unit legs when it sits high. We did not find any maker's published height rule for wall brackets, so ask your supplier and the bracket maker for the load rating, and follow the manual for the unit.

Keeping ice and snow off: let it drain, do not chip it

Lennox says to install the unit high enough to allow defrost water to drain and ice not to build up, and that a support frame must not block the drain holes in the base. Carrier says not to let water or ice build up in the base pan. Goodman notes that the base has cutouts under the outdoor coil for drainage. Lennox's frozen heat pump page says not to chip or break ice, because tools can damage the coils, fins and fan blades, and never to pour hot water, because the sudden temperature change can crack parts. It does say to clear snow away after storms without piling it against the sides, and to brush light snow off the top with a broom. See Heat Pump Iced Up, Defrost Cycle and Auxiliary Heat, and for how a blocked coil raises pressures, Dirty Condenser and Head Pressure.

Shrubs, fences and wind

Trane counts shrubbery in its 12 in. figure. Lennox says to trim shrubbery away and check for debris around the unit. Goodman says not to build a fence near the unit, or a deck or patio over it, without first consulting a dealer or servicer. A fence can also help: the U.S. Department of Energy says high winds can cause defrost problems, and a bush or fence upwind of the coil can block them. Lennox's HP26 instructions give a specific idea for roofs: a wind barrier at least as tall and wide as the unit, 24 in. from the sides toward the prevailing wind.

Dryer vents and other exhaust

Lennox's heat pump instructions say that exhaust from dryers, water heaters and furnaces should be directed away from the outdoor unit, and they list a spot near a dryer vent as one to avoid because lint can collect on the coil. We did not find a distance from any maker, so we give none. Lint on a coil acts like any other dirt; see the dirty condenser lesson above.

Should you cover it in winter?

Lennox's published position: covering is generally not recommended, especially with plastic or vinyl, modern units are made to live outdoors, and a heat pump that runs year-round should never be covered. Lennox lists moisture trapped under a cover (rust and corrosion), rodents sheltering inside, and a possible warranty problem. If falling ice or debris is the worry, Lennox's outdoor unit page suggests fixing a board such as plywood on top, or building a shade or awning, as long as airflow above the unit stays open. We did not read a position from the other makers, so check yours.

What we did not find

We found no maker or code figure on the page for gas meter or gas vent distances, so nothing here covers that. Ask your gas utility and local code office.

Field card: Outdoor Unit Clearances: Snow, Shrubs and Covers for AC and Heat Pumps

The unit's own manual sets the numbers.

  • Examples: Carrier 24 service, 6 one side, 12 others, 48 above; Lennox 30, 36, 12, 6, 48 above.
  • Check each side: measured must be at least the manual number.
  • Base above snow depth; Goodman minimum heights 2 1/2, 8 or 12 in. by temperature.
  • Let the unit drain; never chip ice or pour hot water on it.
  • Trim shrubs; Lennox suggests two feet clear.
  • Dryer exhaust away from the coil.
  • Do not cover a heat pump that runs in winter (Lennox).
