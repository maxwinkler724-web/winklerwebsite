Retrofitting R-404A Refrigeration to Lower-GWP Blends: Glide, TXV, Oil, Charging and Labeling

Last updated: 2026-10-29

R-404A has a 100-year global warming potential near 3,900. R-448A and R-449A are HFC/HFO blends at roughly 1,400. Swapping one for the other in a walk-in or a rack is a real job with real checks, not a "just add gas" drop-in. This lesson covers why people do it, what changes in the system, and when to leave it alone.

Check the maker's sheet and the rules

Check the maker's sheet and the rules. This is general education. The equipment maker, the refrigerant maker and EPA rules decide what is allowed on your system. Follow them over anything here.

Why retrofit: SNAP, the AIM Act and GWP

Two federal programs shape this. EPA's SNAP program reviews substitutes for ozone-depleting refrigerants and also lists acceptable substitutes by end-use. Its regulations page shows R-448A and R-449A listed as acceptable in multiple refrigeration end-uses, including retrofits, through a 2017 notice. A 2015 SNAP fact sheet shows R-404A changed to unacceptable for retrofits of supermarket systems, remote condensing units and stand-alone units as of July 20, 2016, and for new equipment on later dates by type. The AIM Act program, covered in our AIM Act lesson, caps HFC production and sets GWP limits for new equipment. EPA says its technology transitions rule was in a proposed-changes stage as of late 2025, so check the current text before you quote a limit.

Those rules are about new equipment and about what you may install. They do not by themselves force you to convert an existing R-404A system. The usual reasons to convert are cost and supply of R-404A, a customer's emissions goals, and possible energy savings. An EPA GreenChill webinar on retrofits expects roughly 5 to 10 percent energy gain on R-404A retrofits, but that is a projection, not a promise for your box.

GWP values from EPA

EPA's technology transitions GWP table lists R-404A at 3,922, R-448A at 1,386 and R-449A at 1,396. An older EPA commercial refrigeration document lists R-448A at 1,387 from the same 2007 assessment basis, so you may see a one-point difference between EPA pages. We use the current table.

How the blends differ from R-404A

  • Glide. R-404A behaves almost like a single fluid. A NIST study of horizontal boiling lists glide at its test conditions of 0.50 K for R-404A, 5.74 K for R-448A and 5.39 K for R-449A. That is about 0.9, 10.3 and 9.7 degrees F (our own conversion). The same note lists composition: R-448A contains R-32, R-125, R-134a, R-1234ze and R-1234yf, and R-449A contains R-32, R-125, R-134a and R-1234yf. Our glide lesson explains the bubble and dew columns.
  • Discharge temperature. The EPA GreenChill webinar compatibility summary lists a higher discharge temperature for R-404A retrofits. Check compressor discharge temperature after the change against the compressor maker's limit. We do not give a number because the sources we opened did not give a reliable one for these blends.
  • Capacity. The webinar compares compressor ratings by method. On a dew-point basis, R-404A shows 26,000 and the HFO blend about 24,500 (units not stated). On a midpoint basis, 26,100 versus about 25,400. Our own ratio: 24,500/26,000 is 94 percent, and 25,400/26,100 is 97 percent. The presenters concluded that compressor size increases were not needed. The lesson: a rating changes with the reference point, so compare like with like.
  • Mass flow. The sources we opened gave no mass-flow figures for R-404A versus these blends. A valve sized for one fluid is not automatically right for another, so use the valve maker's selection data for the new refrigerant.

What to check, in order

  1. Survey first. Record compressors, oil type, valves, controller refrigerant setting, line sets and leak history. The GreenChill guidelines say to confirm with the equipment and refrigerant makers before any retrofit, and that no refrigerant is a drop-in.
  2. Leak check and repair. The EPA retrofit guideline recommends a vacuum to 500 microns held to prove tightness, and replacing elastomer seals on opened joints. Do not convert a leaking system and hope the new charge stays in.
  3. Oil. R-404A is already a POE refrigerant; the EPA guideline lists one maker's data as not compatible with mineral oil. So the oil question is usually "confirm it is POE and dry," not "change to POE." Still follow the compressor maker, and see our compressor oils lesson.
  4. Filter drier. The EPA guideline says to replace the filter drier with one compatible with the new refrigerant and oil, and to change suction filters. The webinar does the same at the retrofit step. Do it after recovery, before you evacuate.
  5. Expansion valves. The webinar says replace valves as the survey determines and add adjustment kits where offered, then set the valves the next day. Re-measure superheat on every evaporator, per our TXV lesson.
  6. Controller settings. The webinar lists updating the controller to the new refrigerant's pressure-temperature data. See our rack lesson for the setup.
  7. Label the system. The EPA guideline says to label the refrigerant type, lubricant type and charge, and to complete a system data sheet.

Charging and reading the gauges

For blends, charge liquid only from the cylinder. The EPA guideline says to remove liquid from the cylinder "to ensure the correct composition," and to vaporize it before it reaches the compressor, for example with a metering device. The webinar says superheat is referenced to the dew point, subcooling is referenced to the bubble point, and pressure settings use the midpoint. Do not mix R-448A and R-449A, even though they perform alike.

How much to put in? The EPA guideline gives starting charges for other retrofit blends of about 85 to 95 percent of the old charge by weight (for R-22 retrofits), then trimming to performance. It does not give a figure for R-404A to R-448A or R-449A. We use 90 percent below as an assumption only; use the refrigerant maker's number for your system.

Worked example (our own example)

A walk-in freezer holds a 12 lb charge of R-404A. We convert with 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg (our own conversion constant) and 1,000 kg per tonne.

  1. Old charge: 12 lb x 0.45359237 = 5.443 kg.
  2. Old CO2e: 5.443 kg x 3,922 = 21,348 kg, or 21.35 tonnes CO2e (EPA GWP 3,922).
  3. Starting new charge (assumption, 90 percent): 12 x 0.90 = 10.8 lb = 4.899 kg.
  4. R-448A: 4.899 x 1,386 = 6,790 kg, or 6.79 tonnes CO2e.
  5. R-449A: 4.899 x 1,396 = 6,839 kg, or 6.84 tonnes CO2e.
  6. Difference: 21.35 - 6.79 = 14.56 tonnes for R-448A; 21.35 - 6.84 = 14.51 tonnes for R-449A.

These are the CO2e of the charge sitting in the box. The final charge will differ after you trim by superheat, subcooling and box temperature. A rack with a few hundred pounds scales the same way. Note also that a leak rate, not the charge alone, sets real emissions.

When not to retrofit

  • Old compressors or seals. The EPA guideline quotes a compressor maker's guidance that compressors made before 1973 should not be retrofitted with new refrigerants and oil, and warns about seal compatibility on older systems.
  • No maker support. If the equipment or refrigerant maker will not support the change for your model, stop. This is our own advice.
  • Chronic leaks or end of life. A system that cannot hold charge, or is near replacement, may be better replaced. This is our own judgment.
  • Capacity is already marginal. The EPA guideline warns that a lower-capacity fluid raises case temperatures. If the box barely holds temperature now, a slight capacity drop may push it over.
  • Not a listed use. Use only a refrigerant EPA SNAP lists for your end-use, and follow its conditions.

Field card: Retrofitting R-404A to Lower-GWP Blends

Survey, repair, dry, drier, valves, label, liquid charge.

  • EPA GWP: R-404A 3,922; R-448A 1,386; R-449A 1,396.
  • Glide at NIST test conditions: R-404A 0.5 K; R-448A 5.7 K; R-449A 5.4 K.
  • Superheat from dew point; subcooling from bubble point; pressure controls at midpoint.
  • Charge liquid only. Never mix R-448A and R-449A.
  • Replace the filter drier; confirm POE and dryness; set valves and re-check superheat.
  • Label refrigerant, oil and charge; update the controller.
  • Skip it for old compressors, leaking or end-of-life systems, or no maker support.
