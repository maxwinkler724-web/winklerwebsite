Reusing an Existing AC Line Set: Sizing, Flushing and Testing

Last updated: 2026-10-16

"Can I reuse my old AC line set?" is one of the most common questions when an outdoor unit is replaced. The honest answer from the makers is "sometimes, if it passes several checks, and some of them would rather you replaced it." This lesson walks through the checks in the order a tech would run them.

Check the manual for the new unit

Check the manual for the new unit. Each maker sets its own rules, and they change with the refrigerant and the model. The documents below show the pattern. The installation manual for the exact unit you are installing governs.

What the makers say, side by side

The wording ranges from "recommended to replace" to "allowed with conditions." Here is what we read:

  • Goodman/Amana. A 2015 Goodman bulletin (C-GN-SP-986) says an existing R-22 line set may go on a new R-410A air conditioner if the line sizes are correct for the new unit and the installer makes a reasonable effort to remove the leftover mineral oil. It does not endorse any flushing product and says Goodman has not tested them. It applies to air conditioners, not heat pumps.
  • York/Johnson Controls (Unitary Products). A 2013 installation manual says changing out the line set when replacing an R-22 unit with an R-410A unit is "highly recommended." If you reuse it, inspect for kinks, restrictions, corrosion and oil-trapping low spots, flush it with a commercial kit, and add a suction-line filter drier that comes off after 50 hours.
  • Lennox. The ML15KSPV (R-454B) manual says a line set or coil that held mineral oil must be flushed, and that skipping it voids the warranty. It also says not to flush and reuse components when the system holds contaminants, to replace the line set and indoor coil after a burnout, and that existing parts must be an AHRI match for the unit.
  • (-)P17AY R-454B series manual (document 92-111083-01-01). It says the existing line set must be correctly sized for the new unit and cleaned or replaced, and it recommends replacing it if possible. When reusing R-22 or R-410A lines, it says to blow them out with nitrogen to remove as much mineral oil as possible and to drain low spots. It also says to test the oil for acid; a positive result makes a suction-line filter drier mandatory.
  • Trane and Carrier. The manuals we read say little about flushing. Trane's R-454B condensing unit manual (5TTR5) says existing lines must be brazed, not soldered. Carrier's 25HBC5/25HCE4 heat pump manual says "Do not bury lines longer than 36 in."

Check 1: size and length against the table

Look up the new unit's line size table first. If the existing vapor line is larger than the table allows, the manuals warn about oil return. The (-)P17AY manual says "do not oversize vapor line," and the York manual says a larger-than-specified line "could result in oil return problems." See Line Set Sizing for Long Runs for why. Then compare the actual length and the lift to the maker's limits. Trane's 5TTR5 manual, for example, caps total line length at 150 feet and vertical change at 50 feet.

Check 2: is the copper itself still good?

Pressure rating is rarely the problem. The Copper Development Association publishes calculated working pressures for ACR copper tube. A secondary table that reproduces ASTM B280 figures lists annealed 3/8 inch coil at about 836 psi and 7/8 inch at about 495 psi, rated at up to 150°F. Nitrogen test pressures in the manuals we read run from 150 to 500 psig, so healthy tube has room to spare. The real questions are physical: kinks, flattened bends, corrosion, crushed spots, wet or damaged insulation, and old joints. Trane says existing lines must be brazed, not soldered, so a line set with soldered joints is a flag. Carrier's heat pump manual says not to bury lines longer than 36 inches, which is a reason to ask where yours runs. A line set inside a wall or under a slab that you cannot inspect or test is the strongest case for replacement.

Check 3: oil, flushing and burnout history

R-22 systems used mineral oil. R-410A and R-454B units use POE oil, and the mineral oil left in a reused line can foul the new metering device and coat the indoor coil. Lennox adds that residual mineral oil can insulate the tube surface and hurt heat transfer. See Refrigerant Oils.

Flushing options differ. Nu-Calgon's Rx11-flush sheet describes an HFC-based solvent that dissolves mineral oil, applied in a short burst per line (20 to 30 seconds per 50 feet up to 1/2 inch tube, 60 to 90 seconds up to 7/8 inch), then pushed out with nitrogen, then followed by a vacuum to boil off what remains. The sheet does not discuss POE compatibility, so check the equipment maker. Goodman, as noted, does not endorse any product. The (-)P17AY manual calls for nitrogen only. Use what the new unit's manual allows.

If the old compressor burned out, the maker usually wants new lines. Lennox recommends replacing the line set and indoor coil after a burnout. Goodman only says to clean the remaining components by generally accepted practice. York requires a 100 percent activated alumina suction-line drier in a burnout case. When in doubt, replace.

Check 4: metering device and filter drier

These are not part of the line set, but they get missed. Goodman requires a piston or TXV compatible with the new unit and bans capillary tubes. The (-)P17AY manual says the biflow drier shipped with the unit must go in the liquid line, and that the drier only holds moisture if the system is properly evacuated. Carrier says not to use drier with a working pressure under 600 psig and not to put a suction drier in the liquid line. Replace the drier whenever the system is opened.

Check 5: test, evacuate and braze correctly

An old line set that passes inspection still has to pass a pressure test. Manuals differ on pressure: Trane suggests 150 psig as a preliminary check, Lennox gives 150 to 160 psig, and the (-)P17AY manual allows up to 500 psig with at least 15 minutes of hold. Follow your manual, and never use oxygen or shop air. See Nitrogen Pressure Testing and Leak Detection.

Evacuation targets also differ: 500 microns (Carrier, Lennox, the (-)P17AY manual) or 350 microns with a rise limit (Trane). See Evacuation and Deep Vacuum Basics. For R-454B or R-32 equipment, Lennox says to recover all refrigerant before brazing and flow low-pressure nitrogen during the work; the full A2L routine is in A2L Installation and Brazing Practices.

Worked example: size, length and charge

A homeowner has a 3-ton R-22 system with an existing 60 ft line set (5/8 inch vapor, 3/8 inch liquid, 20 ft of lift). You install a Trane 5TTR5036A (R-454B). Trane's manual, Table 5.1, lists 3/4 inch vapor with 5/16 inch liquid as the rated sizes for the 5018A to 5036A units, and 5/8 inch vapor with 3/8 inch liquid as alternates.

  1. Vapor line: 5/8 inch is the alternate vapor size, listed as a pair with 3/8 inch liquid. Pass.
  2. Liquid line: 3/8 inch completes that alternate pair. Pass, but it holds more refrigerant per foot than 5/16 inch.
  3. Total length: 60 ft against the 150 ft maximum. Pass.
  4. Vertical change: 20 ft against the 50 ft maximum. Pass.
  5. Charge: the unit is charged for 10 ft of connecting line. Trane's weigh-in method counts the length minus 10 ft, and uses 0.47 oz per foot for 3/8 inch liquid line. So 60 - 10 = 50 ft, and 50 x 0.47 = 23.5 oz to add.
  6. Compare: with 5/16 inch liquid line (0.30 oz per foot), the same length would need 50 x 0.30 = 15 oz. The larger liquid line costs 8.5 oz more.

Rates differ by maker. Carrier's 25HBC5/25HCE4 and the (-)P17AY manual both use 0.6 oz per foot for 3/8 inch liquid line with a 15 ft factory length. Using the same 60 ft, that would be 45 x 0.6 = 27 oz. Never carry a rate from one manual to another unit. For the full weigh-in method, see Charging by Weight and Line Set Length Adjustments.

When replacement is the right call

Replace when a maker says so (York "highly recommended," the (-)P17AY manual "recommended," Lennox after a burnout), when the sizes are not on the table, when the line is buried or inaccessible, when it has kinks or corrosion, or when you cannot flush and test it to the maker's standard. For an owner, see My AC Uses R-22: Repair or Replace? Insulation is part of the job too; see Line Set Insulation Thickness.

Field card: Reusing an Existing Line Set

Size, length, oil, tightness, then charge.

  • Match vapor and liquid sizes to the new unit's table; oversized vapor lines hurt oil return.
  • Check length and lift against the maker's maximums.
  • Inspect for kinks, corrosion, soldered joints and hidden or buried runs.
  • Remove mineral oil the way the new manual allows; burnout usually means replace.
  • Check the metering device; install the new drier; test with nitrogen; evacuate to the manual's level.
  • Charge adjustment = (oz/ft x length) minus the factory allowance, or (length minus factory length) x oz/ft.
  • Rates are per maker. Trane 3/8 in: 0.47 oz/ft (10 ft base); others we read: 0.6 oz/ft (15 ft base).
