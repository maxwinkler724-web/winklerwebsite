How to Become an HVAC Technician: Paths and First Year

There is no single door into HVAC. Most people arrive through school, an apprenticeship or an entry-level job, and all of them end up learning the same trade on the job.

The main paths

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) lists the typical entry-level education for heating, air conditioning and refrigeration mechanics and installers as a postsecondary nondegree award, with long-term on-the-job training. as of 2026-10-16. Check the source for current details.

  • School. BLS says postsecondary programs generally run about 6 months to 2 years and lead to a certificate or associate's degree. The EPA 608 exam is typically included in these programs.
  • Apprenticeship. BLS describes apprenticeships as usually lasting several years, with paid on-the-job training plus yearly technical instruction. New Jersey's Department of Labor adds that registered apprenticeships are "earn-and-learn" and that each year normally includes about 2,000 hours of on-the-job training and a recommended minimum of 144 hours of related instruction. See where to look in New Jersey.
  • On-the-job start. BLS notes that employers sometimes consider candidates with only a high school diploma, and that newly hired technicians begin with basic tasks and take on harder work over time.
  • Military. Some programs help transitioning service members move into the trades. One Air Force article described a program that combined an 18-week HVAC course with a five-year union apprenticeship, with eligibility rules set by the program. Programs change, so ask your transition office.

What EPA 608 is

Under Section 608 of the Clean Air Act, EPA requires technicians who maintain, service, repair or dispose of equipment that could release regulated refrigerants to be certified. Tests must come from an EPA-approved certifying organization. The types are Type I (small appliances), Type II (high-pressure and very high-pressure appliances), Type III (low-pressure appliances) and Universal (all of them). EPA says Section 608 credentials do not expire. as of 2026-10-16. More in EPA 608 Basics.

What year one usually looks like

No source gives one schedule, so treat this as the pattern BLS describes: basic tasks first, harder work later. In practice that means carrying and staging equipment, helping with installs, learning to use a meter and a gauge set under supervision, and keeping records. BLS lists installing electrical components and wiring, inspecting and testing systems, replacing parts and keeping work records among the duties of the job. Good first-year study topics are electrical basics, the refrigeration cycle and lockout/tagout.

BLS outlook facts

as of 2026-10-16, BLS reported a median pay of $61,010 per year ($29.33 per hour) for May 2025, 440,900 jobs in 2025, and projected growth of 11 percent from 2025 to 2035, described as much faster than average. It also reported about 40,600 openings per year on average over the decade. These are national figures, not a promise for any one employer or town. BLS also notes the work is often in cramped spaces and all weather, with one of the highest injury and illness rates of all occupations, and hours that can include evenings, weekends and on-call time. Check the source for current details.

Licensing is separate

BLS says some states and localities require licenses and that requirements vary. In New Jersey, see NJ Trade Licenses: Who Needs Which and what the NJ HVACR contractor license covers. As a preview, the master license rule asks for five years of qualifying experience (or a degree plus one to three years), proof of age 21 and a two-part written exam; the lesson gives the details and sources.

Field card: How to Become an HVAC Technician: Paths and First Year

There is no single door into HVAC.

  • Paths in: school (about 6 months to 2 years per BLS), registered apprenticeship, entry-level job, or a military transition program.
  • EPA 608 is a federal refrigerant-handling certification. Types I, II, III and Universal. It does not expire.
  • Year one is mostly basic tasks, helping, and learning safety, electrical and refrigeration fundamentals.
  • BLS (as of 2026-10-16): median pay $61,010 (May 2025); 11% projected growth 2025-35. National figures only.
  • Licensing rules differ by state and locality. Look up yours.
