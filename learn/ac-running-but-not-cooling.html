Air Conditioner Running but Not Cooling: What to Check

Last updated: 2026-10-16

The air conditioner is on, you can hear it, but the house is still warm. Before you worry about a big repair, here is a calm checklist.

The short answer

When the air conditioner runs but the house stays warm, the cause is often simple: a thermostat setting, a dirty filter, blocked vents, or an outdoor unit that is not running. Ice on the pipes or indoor unit means the system should be switched off. Carrier advises this to protect the compressor. Low refrigerant needs a certified technician, and you should never try to fix that yourself.

What is going on inside

An air conditioner moves heat from inside your home to the outdoors. An indoor coil absorbs heat from the air, and an outdoor coil releases it. Anything that stops air from moving across either coil, or stops the outdoor part from running, reduces cooling. Typical causes include:

  • A clogged air filter restricting airflow.
  • A dirty outdoor coil that cannot shed heat. Dirt, debris, and plants can block it.
  • Closed or blocked supply and return vents.
  • A frozen indoor coil, often from restricted airflow or a refrigerant problem.
  • Low refrigerant, which is a leak somewhere rather than something that "uses up."
  • An outdoor unit that is not running at all.

The Department of Energy notes that a clean filter can lower the energy use of an air conditioner by roughly 5 to 15 percent, so this is the first place to look.

A note on timing

Pay attention to when the problem shows up. If it never cools, suspect a part that is not running, such as the outdoor unit. If it cools for a while and then stops, note whether ice has appeared. A short note about the pattern helps a technician much more than "it is broken." Also remember that very hot days ask more of any system, and an undersized or dusty system shows its limits first on those days.

Things you can safely check

  • Thermostat: set to Cool, and set lower than the room temperature. If the fan is set to On, warm air may blow between cooling cycles, so try Auto. Check that a schedule is not overriding you.
  • Filter: replace it if it is dirty.
  • Breaker and disconnect: look at the electrical panel for a tripped breaker, and check the outdoor disconnect box is closed. Reset a breaker once at most. If it trips again, stop.
  • Outdoor unit: is the fan spinning? Is the unit buried in leaves or grass clippings? Clear loose debris by hand with the power off at the disconnect.
  • Vents: open them and move furniture away from returns.
  • Ice: look at the copper lines and the indoor unit. If you see ice, turn the system off at the thermostat and let it thaw. Running it can damage the compressor.

Never open panels, touch gas piping or valves, handle refrigerant, or work on wiring. Those jobs belong to a licensed professional.

When to call a pro

Safety comes first. If you smell gas (a rotten-egg odor), hear a hiss, or a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, do not look for the cause. Leave the building with everyone, stay out, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not use switches or lighters on the way out.
  • The checks above do not restore cooling.
  • There is ice on the lines or indoor unit, or water around the indoor unit.
  • The outdoor unit will not start, or its fan is not spinning even though the thermostat calls for cooling.
  • The system runs nonstop without reaching the set temperature, or short cycles.
  • You hear unusual noises or smell burning.
  • You suspect low refrigerant. Only certified technicians should handle refrigerant.

Field card: AC Running but Not Cooling

Start with the easy things before you pay for a visit.

  • Check thermostat mode, setpoint, and fan setting (use Auto).
  • Replace a dirty filter and open all vents.
  • Confirm the breaker is on and the outdoor fan is spinning.
  • Ice means switch off and call. Do not keep running it.
  • Never touch refrigerant or open electrical panels.
