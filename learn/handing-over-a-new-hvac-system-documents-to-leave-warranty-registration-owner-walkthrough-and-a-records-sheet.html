Handing Over a New HVAC System: Documents to Leave, Warranty Registration, Owner Walkthrough and a Records Sheet

Last updated: 2026-11-03

The install is done and the system runs. The last job is the handoff: leaving the owner the papers, the numbers and the know-how to keep the system healthy. A good handoff takes about twenty minutes and saves many phone calls later. This lesson covers what to leave, what to show, and a one-page records sheet.

Why the handoff matters

An owner who gets a system with no paperwork cannot tell a future technician what was installed, cannot prove when it started, and may not know the warranty has a registration step. The Federal Trade Commission's consumer page on warranties says the details of coverage should be in writing, that "sometimes you'll have to return a product registration card first to qualify for warranty service," and that the receipt proves the date of purchase. So the handoff is partly about comfort and partly about keeping the owner's rights intact. This page is general education, not legal advice. Read the actual warranty text that came with the equipment.

The documents to leave

Put everything in one folder or large envelope and write the owner's name and the install date on it. Include:

  • Model and serial numbers for every major piece: outdoor unit, indoor unit or furnace, coil, thermostat. Copy them from the nameplates and photograph each nameplate.
  • Refrigerant type and charge. Write the refrigerant name from the nameplate and the factory charge, plus any amount added or removed during install. A future technician needs this before opening the system.
  • Startup readings taken on the day: supply voltage, running amps, temperature change across the coil, static pressure, and for gas heat the gas pressure and combustion results. Our startup readings lesson explains how to take them, so we only list them here.
  • Filter size and location. The nominal size printed on the filter edge, which way the airflow arrow points, and where the filter slot is. See filter size, direction and fit.
  • Manuals and warranty paperwork from the maker: owner's manual, thermostat manual, and the written warranty. Leave the installation contractor's own workmanship terms and contact details with them.
  • A copy of the signed contract or invoice and any permit or inspection sign-off paperwork the owner received.

Warranty registration windows

Many makers require the owner to register the equipment within a set period after installation, and the period differs from one maker to the next. We will not give a number of days here. Instead, show the owner the registration section of the paperwork, say who will do the registration (installer or owner), write the date it was done on the records sheet, and keep a copy of the confirmation. If the paperwork is unclear, tell the owner to contact the maker or ask the installer in writing. The FTC's business guide to federal warranty law notes that a registration card is treated differently in full and limited warranties, which is one more reason to read the written terms rather than assume.

The owner walkthrough

Walk the owner to each item and let them do it once while you watch. Doing beats watching.

Thermostat

Show how to change the setpoint, switch between heat, cool and off, and set the fan. Explain what the fan setting does; our lesson on fan auto versus on covers the tradeoffs. Tell the owner the schedule you left, and how to hold or override it.

Filter change

Have the owner open the filter slot, remove the filter, and put a new one in with the arrow pointing the right way. The U.S. Department of Energy says to clean or replace the air conditioning filter every month or two during the cooling season, and more often with constant running, dusty conditions or pets. Write the size on the sheet and on the filter slot cover with a marker.

Condensate drain and safety switches

Point out where the condensate line ends, and where the drain pan and any float or overflow switch sit. The Department of Energy warns that clogged drain channels keep a unit from reducing humidity and can stain walls or carpet. Explain, in plain words, what happens when a safety switch trips (the system may stop on its own) and what the owner should do: check the filter and drain, then call for service rather than bypass anything. Our lesson on secondary drain pans and float switches goes deeper. Also show the service switch and breaker locations, and the gas shutoff if there is one.

Outdoor unit clearances

Walk outside. The Department of Energy recommends trimming foliage back at least 2 feet from the outdoor condenser for airflow. Show the owner what to keep clear, and see outdoor unit clearances for snow, shrubs and covers. Tell them to follow the clearances in the installation instructions, which may be larger.

First-season check-in

Offer a check-in after the first full season of heating or cooling, or sooner if the owner notices anything odd. Ask what they have noticed: noise, rooms that are too warm or cold, water near the indoor unit, the thermostat acting strangely. Look at the filter, the drain and the outdoor coil, and compare a few readings to the startup numbers on the sheet. Note the date and any changes. Filter schedule calculation

Assumptions (ours): the filter is checked every 2 months during 8 months of heavy use and every 3 months during the other 4 months, and the stretches run without a break.

  1. Heavy-use season: 8 months ÷ 2 months per check = 4 checks.
  2. Light-use season: 4 months ÷ 3 months per check = 1.33 checks.
  3. Total: 4 + 1.33 = 5.33 checks per year.
  4. Check: 8 + 4 = 12 months, so the whole year is covered.

You cannot do a third of a check, so mark 6 dates on the sheet and keep the extra as a cushion. This is a checking schedule, not a changing schedule: change the filter whenever it looks loaded, and note that the Department of Energy's guidance for cooling season is every month or two.

A simple records sheet

Print the sheet, fill it out together with the owner, and sign it. Leave one copy in the folder and keep one copy. After that, the owner adds a line each time the filter is changed, a service visit happens or something unusual occurs. A future technician will thank them.

Field card: Handing Over a New HVAC System

Numbers, papers, practice, then a date for the next visit.

  • Leave model and serial numbers, refrigerant type and charge, startup readings, filter size and manuals.
  • Many makers require registration within a set period; check the paperwork and record the date.
  • Have the owner change the filter, find the drain and switches, and clear the outdoor unit.
  • Keep foliage trimmed back at least 2 feet from the outdoor unit, or more if the instructions say so.
  • Our sample schedule: 4 + 1.33 = 5.33 filter checks a year, so mark 6.
  • Book a first-season check-in and a next service date.
