Boiler Controls and Safety Devices: What to Write Down

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A test only counts if someone can read it later. Here is what a good record of boiler safety checks contains, and why the actual trip value belongs next to the set point.

Why set point and actual trip are different things

A set point is the number a control is adjusted to, such as the temperature at which a high limit should open. The actual trip (or test point) is where it really opened when someone checked it. A good record has both, because a limit set to one number that really opens at another is a finding. Michigan guidance summarized in our research notes describes the record as a dated, signed service report listing each device tested with its maker, model, set point and actual test point. New Jersey's advisory is shorter: operability and set points must be written down somewhere. Writing the actual value as well is simply the stronger habit.

The devices to list

State report forms that cite CSD-1 group devices like this. Operating controls: pressure or temperature control, forced circulation. Waterside safeties: low-water fuel cutoff, high steam pressure limit, high water temperature limit. Fuel and combustion safeties: main and pilot safety shutoff valves, combustion air switch, high and low gas pressure switches, oil pressure and temperature switches, purge air flow, flame safeguard and detector, low-fire start switch. Relief: safety or relief valve, with maker, model, size and capacity from the nameplate. The forms also ask for the boiler's maker, model, year, National Board and serial numbers, ratings and input, and for dated signatures.

One maker’s example schedule

To see how a routine looks, here is a summary of one model's example only: the A.O. Smith supplement for its HW 420, 520 and 670 gas hot water boilers. It is not a general rule and other boilers differ. That supplement lists daily readings of thermometers and gauges and a look at the flames; weekly shutdown checks on the pilot and main gas valves with stated response times and a flow switch check; monthly flue and draft hood inspection and, on power-vent units, a pressure switch check; semiannual heat exchanger and transformer voltage checks; and annual high-limit, operating-control and redundant gas valve leak tests. It asks for gauge readings, flame-failure shutdown times and transformer voltages to be logged. Your boiler's own manual is the authority.

Add a line for any part replaced (part number) and any adjustment made. The state forms in our research have no pass/fail column, so that column is a Trade Study Bench suggestion. Under the table, note which maker manual and which CSD-1 edition the test followed.

What insurers add

One insurer risk guide says to lift the relief valve lever to confirm it lifts and reseats, or have it bench tested, and to check the flame failure scanner often, preferably weekly. It points back to the maker and CSD-1 for the rest. In Michigan, summarized from a municipal risk guide, missing evidence of the test can lead an inspector to issue a shorter certificate and a violation notice. That is Michigan law; New Jersey differs. See also the NJ owner's duties in NJ Boiler Inspections, Certificates, Logbooks and Posting.

Where routine checks fit

Gas boiler task lists from the National Board, the City of Milwaukee sample manual and Travelers/BoilerRe place low-water cutoff checks daily or monthly, flame-failure timing weekly, and interlock and valve tests monthly to annually. Those lists conflict in places, for example on safety valve testing frequency, so write “per your insurer and jurisdiction.” Details are in Boiler Operator Rounds, Logs and Test Schedules and Low-Water Cutoff Testing and Maintenance.

Field card: boiler test record

Device, set point, actual trip, date, initials. Blank cells are not evidence.

  • List each device by name, maker and model.
  • Write the set point and the actual trip or test value; they can differ.
  • Date, sign or initial every line; note parts replaced and adjustments.
  • Record which maker manual and CSD-1 edition the test followed.
  • The maker's schedule is one model's example, not a rule for every boiler.
  • Qualified, licensed person only for tests that trip a safety; never defeat a safety.
