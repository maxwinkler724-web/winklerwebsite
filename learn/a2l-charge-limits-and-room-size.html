A2L Charge Limits and Room Size

With a mildly flammable refrigerant, one question comes up again and again: how much is in the system, and how big is the space it could leak into?

The core idea

A2L refrigerants are hard to ignite, but they are not non-flammable. If refrigerant leaks into a small closed space, it can reach a concentration that matters. If the same amount leaks into a big space, it spreads out and thins quickly. So safety depends on two things together: the amount of refrigerant that could escape, and the size and openness of the room it escapes into. Less charge or more room volume means lower concentration.

Why a small room is different

Think of a spoonful of food coloring. In a bathtub of water it is barely visible. In a small cup it is strong. A tiny closet holding an indoor unit or a small bedroom served by one head is the cup. This is why equipment standards and codes look at the charge in a system against the space served, and why some products include leak sensors and a mitigation response. See A2L Leak Detection Sensors and A2L Control Boards and Mitigation.

Things that change the picture

  • Total charge: the nameplate and manual state it, and field-added charge for long line sets increases it.
  • Room volume: the floor area and the ceiling height both count.
  • Where the equipment sits: a head high on a wall releases differently from a unit at floor level, since A2L refrigerants are heavier than air as a vapor.
  • Airflow and openings: doors, air movement and mitigation features can change how fast refrigerant disperses. Manufacturers say which of these they allow.
  • Connected spaces: in multi-zone or multi-head systems, the charge may be compared with the smallest room served, not the whole building.

Dispersal area and dispersal height

One city building-department bulletin on A2L permits shows how this is checked in practice: the allowed system charge is read from charts that compare charge against a "dispersal area" and a "dispersal height," for R-32 and R-454B, with or without air circulation. Dispersal height is measured to the lowest opening of the supply duct, return duct or equipment, since that is where leaked refrigerant would enter the space. The numbers sit in those charts, not in the bulletin text, and they differ by refrigerant and product.

Ways to make a space work

The same bulletin lists mitigation options that include air circulation, combining connected spaces, rerouting piping, safety shut-off valves, mechanical exhaust, and choosing equipment that fits the space. Which of these are allowed depends on the product and the code your area has adopted.

What to do on a job

  • Find the charge and the room-size or installation requirements in the manufacturer's installation instructions for that model.
  • Check the rules adopted where you work. Codes and product standards are updated and not every area adopts them on the same date.
  • If the space is too small for the charge, the instructions will say what options exist, such as a different model or added mitigation. Do not guess or reduce the room to a number that makes it pass.
  • Tell the customer or supervisor when a layout needs a different plan.

What this lesson leaves out on purpose

It gives no tables, formulas or limit values, and there is no single charge number that fits every A2L system. The numbers are in your installation manual and the current code text, and they differ by product. The rule that overrides everything on this page: the installation and service instructions for the specific unit in front of you, and the rules where you work, always govern. For the properties of the refrigerants themselves, see A2L and A3 Refrigerants.

Field card: A2L Charge Limits and Room Size

More refrigerant plus a smaller room means higher concentration if it leaks.

  • Charge and room size are considered together.
  • Field-added charge counts toward the total.
  • Dispersal area and height matter, and the numbers are in your installation manual.
  • Check manufacturer instructions and the local code for real limits.
  • Do not adjust the numbers to make a room pass.
  • This lesson has no limit values on purpose.
