No Heat First Checks: Homeowner and Tech Checklist

The heat goes out on the coldest night. A few safe checks can solve it, but some signs mean stop and call right away.

Safety box. If you smell gas or a CO alarm sounds, do not investigate. Get everyone outside to fresh air and call emergency services from outside. CDC and CPSC pages describe headache, dizziness, nausea and confusion as CO symptoms and say to get outside if you suspect poisoning. Never bypass a limit, switch or safety to get heat back, and never heat a home with a gas oven. See Carbon Monoxide Alarms and What to Do.

Homeowner checks that are reasonable

  • Thermostat. Mode on Heat, setpoint above room temperature, display lit. If blank or odd, try fresh batteries (Thermostat Blank or Wrong Temperature).
  • Power. Check that the furnace switch (it looks like a light switch) is on and the breaker has not tripped. Reset a breaker once at most. A breaker that trips again means stop.
  • Filter. A clogged filter chokes airflow and can make a furnace shut off on a safety. DOE guidance, written for cooling season, says to clean or replace filters every one to two months and more often in dusty homes or with pets, and the same habit makes sense in winter. See How Often to Change Your Air Filter.
  • Vents and registers open and unblocked.
  • Condensate and outdoor vents. High-efficiency furnaces drain condensate and use plastic vent pipes outside. If water is leaking (Water Leaking from AC or Furnace), or snow, ice or debris may cover an outdoor vent or intake, look from a safe spot. Do not poke into vents or flues.
  • Gas supply. You can see whether the shutoff to the furnace is open, but if you smell gas, that is the safety box above.

Leave to a professional

Opening the burner compartment, touching gas valves, flue parts, heat exchanger, or control wiring, resetting limits, or any smell of gas, soot, scorching or exhaust. CDC advises annual service of heating systems by a qualified technician and notes blocked chimneys can let CO build up.

Tech checklist, in order

  1. Call for heat. Is W reaching the board? Thermostat, wiring, and low-voltage power (terminals).
  2. Power. Disconnect, breaker, board power, transformer.
  3. Safeties. Limit, rollout, pressure switch, door switch, and why any is open. Find the cause, never jumper.
  4. Ignition. Inducer, pressure switch proof, igniter, gas valve, flame sense. Board LED codes help: Furnace No Heat: From LED Code to Part. For the normal order see Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation.

If it starts then shuts off, see Furnace Short Cycling. Check CO and combustion safety before leaving, and lock out when working on live parts. For rooftop gas heat, see RTU Gas Heat Lockouts and Rooftop Safety.

Field card: No Heat First Checks: Homeowner and Tech Checklist

Simple checks first, safety always. Gas smell or CO alarm: leave and call.

  • Thermostat: Heat mode, setpoint above room temperature, batteries and display okay.
  • Furnace switch on, breaker not tripped (reset once at most).
  • Filter clean, registers open, outdoor vents clear (look, do not poke).
  • Gas smell or CO alarm: go outside, then call from outside. Do not investigate.
  • Techs: call for heat, power, safeties, then ignition, in that order.
  • Never bypass a limit or safety. Find out why it opened.
