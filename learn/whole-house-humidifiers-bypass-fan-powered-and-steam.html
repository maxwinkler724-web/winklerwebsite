Whole-House Humidifiers: Bypass, Fan-Powered and Steam

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Winter air is dry, and a furnace makes it drier. A whole-house humidifier puts water back into the air that your ducts carry. There are three common kinds: bypass, fan-powered and steam. They differ in what moves the air and what turns water into vapor. Setting any of them too high is how you get wet windows.

The three types

Carrier's humidifier-types page describes the split this way. A bypass unit has no fan of its own. The furnace blower pulls air across a water-fed pad or panel, and the moisture evaporates into that air. A fan-powered unit has a built-in fan that pushes air across the panel, so it does not depend only on the furnace blower. A steam unit uses electricity to heat water into steam and sends the vapor into the ductwork, so it can run apart from the furnace heating cycle. Carrier lists up to 12 or 18 gallons per day for its bypass models, up to 18 for fan-powered, and up to 34 for steam.

Two things follow. Evaporative units (bypass and fan-powered) get their water into the air by evaporation, which needs warm air moving across a wet pad. Steam units make vapor themselves, so they need no heat from the furnace air but draw much more electrical power.

Controls and interlocks

A humidistat (or a digital control with an outdoor sensor) calls for humidity. The makers differ on what else has to be running. Honeywell's HE360 instructions say flatly that the furnace blower must be running to activate the humidifier. Aprilaire's owner's manual says that with its blower-activation feature off, the humidifier runs only while the HVAC fan is running or the system is producing heat. With the feature on, and the system idle for an hour, the control can start the fan to sample the air, then run the humidifier for up to two hours or until it reaches the setpoint. That mode needs hot water plumbed to the unit. Aprilaire's application guide adds that a manual humidistat needs a relay (its model 4851 turns on the blower with a humidity call), and that for its steam model any third-party control must be a dry contact, with no 24 volts applied to the humidistat terminals.

The lesson: read the maker's wiring diagram for your exact model. The control path is a low-voltage circuit like the ones in Relays, Transformers and 24V Control and Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation, and a wrong connection can leave the unit dead or running at the wrong times. We did not confirm a universal wiring rule, so this page does not give one.

Water panels, drains and upkeep

Evaporative humidifiers leave minerals behind. Aprilaire's manual says minerals not trapped by the replaceable water panel are flushed down the drain on drain-type models, that the drain line needs a steady downward slope and must not be flattened or blocked, and that the saddle valve on the water line is only for fully open or fully closed, never for regulating flow. It says to change the panel at least once a year, and its digital control has a light that flashes when a change is due. Honeywell's HE360 instructions say to clean the unit every one to three months depending on water quality, with power and water shut off first: flush the drain hose, remove mineral from the tray and nozzle, and fit the new pad with its black mark facing up. After service, water should be running out of the drain hose.

A blocked drain or overflowing tray is a cause of water around the furnace. Our condensate drain lesson covers the cooling side; a humidifier is one of the other sources that lesson tells you to rule out. On bypass units, Aprilaire says to close the built-in bypass damper before maintenance.

Why the setting has to follow the weather

The glass in a window is colder than the room. When room air touches a surface below its dew point, water condenses; the physics is in Psychrometrics for Technicians. The colder it is outside, the colder the inner glass and the outer walls get, so the same room humidity that is fine in October fogs windows in January. The EPA's mold guidance says warm, humid air meeting a cold surface may condense, and that mold is often found on walls in cold corners where that happens. It also says indoor relative humidity should stay below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent. The Mayo Clinic gives the same 30 to 50 percent range for the home and notes that condensation can support bacteria, dust mites and mold. For the wider air quality picture, see Indoor Air Quality and Ventilation Basics.

Both makers print the answer as a chart. Aprilaire's table for manual mode is: 40 F outside, 45 percent; 30 F, 40; 20 F, 35; 10 F, 30; 0 F, 25; minus 10 F, 20; minus 20 F, 15. Honeywell's HE360 chart on a numbered dial runs the same way, from 15 at minus 20 F up to 35 at 20 F. Aprilaire says to anticipate a drop in outdoor temperature and lower the setting ahead of it. If windows still fog or frost, its guidance is to lower the dial one step.

Worked example: set the humidistat for a 10 F night

  1. The forecast low is 10 F. Aprilaire's manual-mode table lists 30 percent RH at 10 F.
  2. With a digital control in automatic mode, the dial table gives 30 percent at 10 F for dial 5. Dial 4 would give 25 percent, dial 6 would give 35 percent. So pick dial 5.
  3. If the temperature falls to 0 F, the same dial 5 gives 25 percent on that table, so the control follows the cold on its own. A manual humidistat does not, so you would lower it from 30 to 25 yourself.
  4. Check windows after a cold night. Fogging means drop one step.

Capacity check. Aprilaire's sizing chart (from AHRI) lists the daily water a 2,000 square foot house needs: 8.5 gallons if tight, 17 if average, 25.5 if loose. Its Model 700 is rated 18 gallons per day, but only when the blower runs 100 percent of the time, plenum air is 120 F, and the water is cold. For an average house: 18 - 17 = 1 gallon per day of margin, about 17 / 24 = 0.7 gallon per hour. In a loose house, 25.5 - 18 = 7.5 gallons per day short, and the unit cannot keep up even at full output. Real run times are shorter than the rating assumes, so treat the margin as thin.

Field card: Whole-House Humidifiers

Fog on the glass means turn it down.

  • Bypass: no fan, uses furnace blower, air flows supply to return through the unit.
  • Fan-powered: own fan, less dependent on the blower.
  • Steam: electric steam into the duct, runs independent of furnace heat.
  • Check each maker for what must be running (blower or heat call).
  • Panel or pad: change yearly or per the maker; keep the drain sloped and clear.
  • Aprilaire manual mode: 10 F outside, 30 percent; 0 F, 25 percent.
  • EPA: keep indoor RH below 60 percent, ideally 30 to 50 percent.
