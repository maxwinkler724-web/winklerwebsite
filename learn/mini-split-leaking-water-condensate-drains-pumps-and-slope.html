Mini Split Leaking Water: Condensate Drains, Pumps and Slope

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A wall-mounted mini split pulls water out of your air all summer, and the indoor unit has to get that water out of the house. When you see drips, a wet wall or a puddle, the drain path has failed somewhere. This lesson follows the water from the coil to the outside so you can tell what an owner can check and what is a technician's job.

Where the water goes

Cool air holds less moisture, so water collects on the cold indoor coil and drips into a drain pan inside the unit. A drain hose carries it from the pan, usually out through the same hole in the wall as the refrigerant lines, to the outdoors. Several makers show the same plan. Fujitsu's compact wall unit manual says to put the drain hose at the bottom of the bundled piping, and LG's manual for its LSN240HSV says the same, adding that the hose should be on the lowest side so a higher position cannot make the pan overflow. Daikin tells the installer to keep the drain piping as short as possible.

Slope: what the makers say

Every wall-unit manual we opened asks for a downhill drain, but most give no slope number. Daikin's installation manual for its FVXM-B series says to install the drain hose with a downward slope and that traps are not permitted. Mitsubishi's manual for the MSZ-GS18NA says the hose should point downward and must not be crowded or bent, and it draws wrong layouts that include a raised hose, a ditch and water collecting in the line. LG's manual labels waving, ditch and accumulated drain water as poor layouts. Mitsubishi and the Daikin manuals also say not to leave the hose tip in water, with Mitsubishi giving a gap of at least 50 mm (about 2 in).

Two numbers do exist. Fujitsu's manual says to cut the wall hole so the outside end is 5 to 10 mm (3/16 to 13/32 in) lower than the inside end. And Hisense's installation manual for its external drain pump calls for a down-slope of 1/50 to 1/100 on drain piping and warns that any rising section can make water flow back and leak. That pump is for ceiling-type units, so treat 1/50 to 1/100 as a good reference for a long field-made run, and follow your own unit's manual first.

Worked example: how much drop is 1/100?

Suppose a field-extended drain runs 20 feet horizontally from the wall to a drain point.

  1. Convert the run to inches: 20 ft × 12 in/ft = 240 in.
  2. At 1/100, the drop is run divided by 100: 240 ÷ 100 = 2.4 in.
  3. At the steeper 1/50: 240 ÷ 50 = 4.8 in.

So the far end must sit at least 2.4 in lower than the wall end, and 4.8 in gives more margin. If the drain point is only 1.5 in lower, the run is too flat. Now a pump check. Hisense lists 700 mm as the maximum lift for its pump: 700 ÷ 25.4 = 27.6 in, about 2 ft 4 in. The Aspen Silent+ Mini Aqua tech sheet lists a 23 ft maximum recommended head. If the discharge must rise 9 ft: 23 − 9 = 14 ft of margin. The same sheet shows flow falling from 2.9 gallons per hour at zero head to 1.6 at maximum head, so a high lift means a slower pump.

Condensate pumps and their safety switches

When the drain cannot fall to the outside, a small pump lifts the water. The Aspen sheet lists a safety switch rated 3.0 A, normally closed, plus thermal protection. Sauermann's catalog lists its Si-20 and Si-30 with a maximum discharge head of 33 ft at 1 gph and a normally closed safety switch rated 8 A resistive at 250 V; the pump turns on at a 5/8 in water level and the safety level is 3/4 in. Little Giant's VCMX manual gives a pump-on level of about 1.6 in and an overflow switch at about 2.2 in, and it says to mount the pump level with the inlet below the coil drain, to use a backup or alarm wherever a failed pump could cause damage, and to service it at least once a year.

The safety switch exists to stop the equipment before water spills. Hisense's pump manual has its float switch wire plug into the indoor unit's circuit board and says to check the switch action after installation. A unit that has stopped next to a full pump tank is often the switch doing its job. Never jumper it. Wiring a switch into a particular board or thermostat circuit is a technician's task. For more on pumps, see the linked pump lesson below.

Common causes of a leaking mini split

  • Clogged pan or hose. The pan and hose are the usual choke points. Slime and debris build up in a drain over time; the drain basics lesson linked below covers why.
  • Kinked, uphill or waving hose. Mitsubishi says the hose must not be bent, and every maker above flags dips and rises.
  • Hose tip in water or pulled loose. Daikin and Mitsubishi both say to keep the end out of water, and Daikin says a loose hose connection on left-side piping can leak.
  • Unit not level. Mitsubishi and Daikin both have the installer mount the wall plate level, and LG's manual says to keep the unit level, so a unit that was hung out of level is worth a look.
  • Dirty filter or coil, and a frozen coil. ServiceMag, a trade publication, lists a dirty filter, a dirty coil, a failing blower and low refrigerant as causes of a frozen coil, and says that a plugged drain turns melted ice into an overflowing pan. Low refrigerant means a leak, which is not an owner repair.
  • Failed pump. A dead pump or a tripped safety switch leaves water standing.

Water outside in winter

If the leak is at the outdoor unit in heating mode, it may be normal. LG's support page says that during defrost, frost melts and water drips to the bottom, and it may look like smoke; steam and leaks can be frequent in humid weather, and it "does not mean a malfunction." Cold can also cause real drain trouble. Fujitsu's manual warns that below 32 F, drain water can freeze after cooling operation and block an outdoor hose, causing leaks at the indoor unit, and Mitsubishi says not to use a drain socket in cold regions because the drain may freeze and stop the fan.

Cleaning, and owner versus technician

Washing the filter is the owner habit that helps most; see Mini-Split Cleaning and Maintenance Habits. Owners can safely look for a visibly kinked hose, a hose end sitting in water, or an outdoor end blocked by leaves, after switching the unit off. Opening the unit, taking the coil down, pouring test water into the pan (Daikin's installer test uses about 1 L) and any pump wiring belong to a technician. So does any sign of a frozen coil that returns, since the possible causes include low charge. Start with Mini-Split Troubleshooting Checks.

Field card: Mini Split Leaking Water: Condensate Drains, Pumps and Slope

Water only moves downhill. Find the dip.

  • Pan to hose to outside: the hose falls the whole way, no traps, no kinks.
  • Keep the hose end open and out of water (Mitsubishi: 50 mm gap).
  • Slope reference: 1/100 is 2.4 in of drop over 20 ft; 1/50 is 4.8 in.
  • Pump safety switch stopping the unit is a warning. Do not bypass it.
  • Outdoor drips in heating can be defrost water (LG).
  • Owner: filter, visible hose, outdoor end. Technician: coil, pan, pump wiring, charge.
