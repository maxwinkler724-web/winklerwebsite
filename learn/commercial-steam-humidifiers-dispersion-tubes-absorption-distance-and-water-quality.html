Commercial Steam Humidifiers: Dispersion Tubes and Absorption Distance

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Winter air is dry, and a big air handler can pull a lot of it through a building. A commercial steam humidifier adds water vapor back, but steam that does not mix into the airstream turns into water inside the duct. This lesson covers the common types, where the steam goes, and the controls and water rules that keep the system dry and working.

Why a building humidifies

A U.S. Department of Energy technology alert on humidifiers (prepared with Oak Ridge National Laboratory) lists the reasons: product quality in manufacturing, limiting the spread of disease-causing bacteria in hospitals, limiting static electricity in clean rooms and computer rooms, and office comfort. DriSteem's design guide adds preserving materials and pharmaceutical processes. Health care adds a rule: an ACHR News article on amines notes that ASHRAE 170 sets a minimum humidity of 20 or 30 percent RH in many care spaces. For the house version of this topic, see Whole-House Humidifiers; here we look at the larger equipment.

Four ways to make steam

  • Electrode. Current passes through the water itself and heats it. Condair's installation page says the install requirements for electrode and resistive units are the same. Electrode cylinders depend on the water's conductivity, and Condair's EL manual picks the cylinder by conductivity (standard range 125 to 1,250 µS/cm).
  • Resistive. A heating element boils the water. Same duct and drain rules.
  • Gas-fired. A burner heats a tank and heat exchanger. Condair's gas-fired manual lists capacities from 50 to 600 lb/h, an exhaust vent run of up to 100 feet equivalent, and a requirement for combustion air. The ACHR article lists low energy cost but high maintenance per unit and hard flue routing.
  • Steam-to-steam. Boiler steam runs through a coil in a tank of ordinary water, and the tank makes separate, clean steam. Armstrong's CS-10 sheet describes it as a device that uses existing boiler steam to produce clean steam from untreated water.

Direct boiler steam and boiler chemicals

Some buildings inject boiler steam straight into the air. The catch is that central boilers usually add neutralizing amines to protect the condensate piping. The ACHR article says amines can irritate eyes, skin and the upper airway, and it rates direct injection as low first cost but with amines reaching the ventilation air. Armstrong's sheet says the same chemicals can ride along in the steam. The DOE alert also names additives in direct steam as an indoor air quality concern. Steam-to-steam and electric or gas units avoid the issue. Whether direct steam is allowed in a given building (a hospital, a lab) is a design and code question; ask the engineer.

Dispersion tubes and panels

Steam leaves the humidifier as pure vapor, but it cannot be dumped into the duct from one open pipe. A dispersion tube is a pipe with small holes (orifices) across the duct width. Armstrong's data sheet sizes tube length by duct width and requires two to four tubes, or a multi-tube panel, as the output goes up. A multi-tube panel (Armstrong's sheet calls its version a HumidiPack) puts the steam across the whole duct face so it mixes in a short distance. Condair's manuals show a steam hose that is short (4 m maximum for its EL unit), sloped at least 15 percent, and a condensate line with a trap that has to be filled with water before start-up.

Absorption distance, and why wet ducts grow mold

The absorption (non-wetting) distance is how far downstream the steam travels before it is fully absorbed and no longer visible as a fog. Condair's manual calls it BN. It gets longer when the air is cold, fast, already humid, or when the target humidity is high. Condair's UK page gives about 1 m at 2.5 m/s, 20 C and 50 percent RH for standard lances, and a Condair manual example of 1.4 m for a 30 to 70 percent RH rise. DriSteem's distributor Deppmann explains how to shorten it: put the tubes where the air is warmest (after a heating coil is preferred, a cold stream is a poor spot), expect laminar flow downstream of coils, discharge against or across the airflow, and know that turning vanes near the tubes must not get wet.

Anything in that distance can get wet: coils, filters, fans, elbows, duct liner. The EPA says humidity above 60 percent is likely to cause condensation that can lead to mold, that furnace humidifiers must be cleaned regularly, and that ducts where humidifiers are installed should be checked for water leaks. It also says to dry wet areas within 48 hours. Wet liner is a mold problem and a rust problem. See Mold on the AC Coil or in Ducts and Psychrometrics for Technicians for how saturation works.

Safety controls

Condair's EL manual shows two items in the external safety chain: an airflow (air proving) switch and a high-limit humidistat. It calls the safety humidistat highly recommended and says that limiting the supply air humidity in the normal control is not a substitute for it. Condair's gas-fired manual says to put the airflow switch in the same duct as the distributor so it proves air is moving, and to put the high-limit sensor downstream of the distributor, at least five times the absorption distance (or at least 3 m, 10 ft, if that distance is unknown). It suggests 85 percent RH for the setting. Condair's UK page uses the same five-times rule for sensors and 2.5 times for sub-micron filters. If the fan stops and the humidifier keeps steaming, the duct floods; the proving switch is what stops that. Ask the maker's manual how to prove that the humidifier drops out when airflow stops, and check it on service visits.

Water quality

Water supply drives maintenance. Condair's gas-fired manual allows potable water within stated limits (hardness 0 to 350 ppm, conductivity up to 1,500 µS/cm), or treated water such as RO or DI (hardness under 17 ppm, conductivity up to 100 µS/cm), and bans additives. Condair's EL manual, by contrast, says to use only untreated drinking water and to contact the supplier before using softened water, and does not mention RO or DI. Different units, different rules: read the manual for the exact model. Armstrong says its steam-to-steam tank can run on untreated water, solids build up on the exchanger, and softened or purified water reduces cleaning. The DOE alert says ultrasonic humidifiers need DI or RO water, which raises other operating costs. DriSteem's guide says ignoring water quality is a common cause of long-term maintenance trouble.

Cylinders, drains and service

Condair offers exchangeable and cleanable cylinders; scale and conductivity shorten cylinder life. Hot drain water is the other hazard: Condair lists 80 to 90 C (about 176 to 194 F) from the EL unit, under 60 C with its optional drain-water cooling (tempering) kit, and hoses and drain pipe rated for the temperature. Condair UK also says not to mount the unit directly above the drain, because rising steam can corrode the cabinet. Routine checks: cylinder condition and error codes, drain and trap, steam hose slope, tubes and orifices free of scale, wet spots at the first coil, filter or elbow downstream, and both safety devices.

Worked example: how many pounds of steam per hour?

Outdoor air at 5,000 cfm, 0 F and 75 percent RH, must be heated and humidified to 35 percent RH at 72 F. Constants (labeled): 4.5 is 60 minutes times about 0.075 lb/ft³ for standard air; 7,000 grains equals 1 lb. Our own steps:

  1. Outdoor moisture. The Engineering Toolbox psychrometric table (29.92 in. Hg) lists 5.58 grains per pound of dry air at saturation at 0 F. It lists saturation values only, so our own scaling is 0.75 × 5.58 = 4.19, which we round to 4 grains/lb.
  2. Supply moisture. The same table's 70 F row lists 110.28 grains/lb. Our own scaling: 0.35 × 110.28 = 38.6. The table has no 72 F row; 72 F air holds a little more, so we use a round 40 grains/lb.
  3. Difference: 40 − 4 = 36 grains/lb.
  4. Steam: lb/h = cfm × 4.5 × delta-grains ÷ 7,000 = 5,000 × 4.5 × 36 ÷ 7,000 = 810,000 ÷ 7,000 = 115.7 lb/h.
  5. Compare with a 60 lb/h humidifier: 60 ÷ 115.7 = 52 percent of the load. It is short by about 56 lb/h.
  6. Two 60 lb/h units give 120 lb/h, about 4 lb/h (3.7 percent) of spare. That is thin, and cold outdoor air is denser than standard air, so the real load is a bit higher.

The answer is a design day, 100 percent outdoor air. A mixed-air system with return air has a smaller load. A rated size from Condair or Armstrong still has to be checked against the supplier's sizing software for the real duct.

Field card: Commercial Steam Humidifiers

Steam in the airstream, not on the duct.

  • Types: electrode, resistive, gas-fired, steam-to-steam. Direct boiler steam may carry amines.
  • Absorption distance grows with cold, fast, humid air and higher targets.
  • Put tubes in warm, straight duct; keep coils, vanes, filters and liner past the distance.
  • High-limit sensor about 5x absorption distance downstream; airflow switch in the same duct.
  • Lb/h = cfm x 4.5 x delta-grains / 7,000. Example: 5,000 cfm, 36 grains = 115.7 lb/h.
  • Match water to the manual: untreated, softened, RO or DI differ by unit.
  • Drain water can be 80 to 90 C; check tempering and drain rating.
