Mini Split Wall Hole: Size, Slope, Sleeves and Line Set Covers

Last updated: 2026-10-16

The hole through the wall is the one part of a ductless install you cannot easily redo. Drill it too small and the bundle will not fit. Tilt it the wrong way and the indoor unit leaks. Leave the gap open and you invite drafts, rain and mice. This lesson covers what several makers ask for, with numbers.

Check the manual for your unit

The manual for your exact model wins. The sizes below come from manuals for specific units and differ from maker to maker. This page is general education, not an installation procedure. Local code and the building owner's rules also apply.

Hole size: what the makers say

There is no universal size. The manuals we opened ask for different hole saws:

  • Daikin's FVXM-B manual: a 65 mm hole (about 2-9/16 in).
  • Fujitsu's compact wall unit manual: 65 mm (2-9/16 in).
  • LG's LSN240HSV manual: a 70 mm (2.76 in) core drill.
  • Mitsubishi's MSZ-GS18NA and MSZ-GS15NA manuals: a 3 in (75 mm) hole.

GREE's public article on mini-split drains did not give a hole size, and the GREE tool-list document we opened did not either, so check your GREE manual. Bigger units, or a larger bundle with a long drain, can need more room, which is another reason to read the manual before you pick the saw. Daikin also says the hole position depends on whether the piping leaves the unit at the back, side or bottom.

Slope: the hole must fall toward the outside

Water in the drain hose runs downhill, and the hole is the one place the hose cannot be adjusted afterward. The makers give the fall in different ways:

  • Mitsubishi: the outdoor side should be 5 to 7 mm (6/32 to 9/32 in) lower than the indoor side.
  • Fujitsu: the outside end is 5 to 10 mm (3/16 to 13/32 in) lower than the inside end, and for left or right piping the hole is cut slightly lower so drain water flows freely.
  • GREE: its drain article suggests drilling the hole at a 5 to 10 degree downward angle to the outdoors.
  • Daikin: slope the hole downward toward the outside, with no amount stated in the manual we read.
  • LG: slant the hole slightly toward the outdoor side, with no amount stated.

Notice the two styles. Mitsubishi and Fujitsu state a drop between the two ends, a few millimeters. GREE states an angle. The worked example below shows these are not the same amount, so do not mix one maker's habit with another maker's unit. For the drain hose itself, see the condensate drain lesson.

Where the drain sits in the bundle

Fujitsu says to install the piping so the drain hose is at the bottom, taped to the underside of the pipes. LG says the drain hose goes on the lowest side of the bundle. Mitsubishi says to keep the hose below the refrigerant piping, uncrowded and not bent, and not to pull it while taping. Put together, the drain rides low so water always has a way out, even if the lines above it hold a little condensation. LG and Mitsubishi also warn against any rise, wave or dip in the hose.

Sleeves, caps and sealing

Mitsubishi's manuals have you insert a wall hole sleeve, cut off the extra length, and then seal the gap with putty and finish with a wall hole cover. The manual says the sleeve keeps the connecting wire from touching metal in the wall and protects the wiring from rodents. Fujitsu says to cut the wall pipe to the wall thickness and use a wall cap, and warns that without the pipe the connecting cable can touch metal and cause electric discharge. Daikin says to insert a wall embedded pipe and a wall cover, and requires them where the wall has a metal frame or metal board. It also asks for sealing around the pipes with field-supplied sealing material to prevent water leakage. LG says to seal small openings around the pipes with gum-type sealer.

For metal lath or tin-plate walls, Mitsubishi calls for a chemically treated wooden piece at least 20 mm (25/32 in) thick between the wall and the piping, or wrapping the piping in insulating vinyl tape.

Air, water and pests

Maker putty keeps the gap closed. GREE adds that sealing with putty or foam insulation blocks drafts and pests, and that a sleeve protects the line from sharp edges. The U.S. Department of Energy funded Building America Solution Center has a guide for pipe and wire holes in exterior walls. It says to stuff steel wool or metal screening into the gaps against pests, cover it with canned foam or caulk from the inside, then use water-resistant caulk for small gaps and spray foam for large ones from the outside. If you use maker putty, the manual's putty comes first, and the guide's layers are a reasonable add-on where the manual is silent.

Line set covers and UV

Outdoors, a snap-together line set cover hides the lines and shields the foam from sun. RectorSeal's Slimduct, for example, is made of weather-resistant PVC that the maker calls UV-stabilized and fire-resistant. It comes in three sizes (77, 100 and 140), with duct sections 78 in long, and its wall inlet fitting is where the line set passes through the exterior wall. The maker's literature says to choose the size by the dimensions of the line sets, drain hose and wiring. See the insulation lesson for what sunlight does to bare foam.

Fire-rated walls

This is a code question, not a mini-split question. The International Code Council's firestopping summary says that pipes passing through a fire-resistance-rated wall must be protected by a tested through-penetration firestop system, installed exactly as shown in its listing. Ordinary putty and foam from the mini-split box are not shown as that system. The Mitsubishi manual we read gives no fire-rated guidance. Ask the local building department before you penetrate a rated wall, such as one between dwelling units.

Worked example 1: slope across a 6 in wall

Take a wall that is 6 in thick, which is 6 x 25.4 = 152 mm.

  1. Mitsubishi: the outside is 5 to 7 mm lower. That is 5 / 152 = 0.033 and 7 / 152 = 0.046 as a ratio. Per foot, 0.033 x 12 = 0.39 in and 0.046 x 12 = 0.55 in. The angle is about 1.9 degrees to 2.6 degrees (the angle whose tangent is 0.033 or 0.046).
  2. Fujitsu: 5 to 10 mm lower, which is 0.20 to 0.39 in.
  3. GREE angle of 5 to 10 degrees: drop = 152 mm x tan(angle). tan 5 degrees is 0.0875, so 152 x 0.0875 = 13.3 mm (0.52 in). tan 10 degrees is 0.1763, so 152 x 0.1763 = 26.8 mm (1.06 in).
  4. For comparison, the 1/4 in per foot drain-pipe rule GREE quotes would give only 6 / 12 x 0.25 = 0.125 in (3.2 mm) over this wall, but that rule is for drain pipe, not for the wall hole.

So GREE's angle gives about 13 to 27 mm of drop, while Mitsubishi's spec gives 5 to 7 mm. They are different instructions, so follow the manual in the box.

Worked example 2: cover length and fittings count

Assumed route (made up for the example): wall inlet, 10 ft horizontal along the wall, one 90 degree turn, then 6 ft down to the condenser.

  1. Total run: 10 + 6 = 16 ft = 16 x 12 = 192 in.
  2. Pieces: 192 / 78 = 2.46, so you need at least 3 sections of 78 in (234 in total).
  3. Cuts: leg 1 is 120 in, which is one full 78 in section plus 42 in cut from a second one. Leg 2 is 72 in, so it takes a third section, since only 36 in is left from the second (78 - 42 = 36).
  4. Fittings: 1 wall inlet, 1 elbow, 1 coupler (joining the two pieces of leg 1), 1 end fitting at the condenser. That is 4 fittings.

Real fittings take up some length, and the maker's fitting dimensions can change the cut pieces, so use this as a shopping count and then measure on the wall. Strap kits for holding the line set are also sold.

Keep the connections reachable

None of the Mitsubishi, LG or Fujitsu pages we read set a specific rule for flare nut access, though Daikin's manual says you open the front panel and take off the front grille to reach the connections. Make the flares and check torque using the flare lesson, and leave the connections where the maker puts them, not buried in the wall.

Field card: Mini Split Wall Hole: Size, Slope, Sleeves and Line Set Covers

Size, tilt, bundle, seal.

  • Hole size: 65 to 75 mm depending on maker. Read the manual first.
  • The outside end is lower. Mitsubishi 5 to 7 mm, Fujitsu 5 to 10 mm, GREE 5 to 10 degrees.
  • Drain hose lowest in the bundle, no rise or dip.
  • Sleeve and cap or cover, then putty or maker sealer. Metal walls need the maker's extra steps.
  • Rated wall: tested firestop system, ask the building department.
  • Cover: total run / 78 in, round up, then count fittings.
