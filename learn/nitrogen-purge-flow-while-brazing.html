Nitrogen Purge Flow While Brazing

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Heat copper with air inside and the inside of the pipe grows black scale. Flowing nitrogen is cheap insurance. The tricky part is how much, and the honest answer is a range, not a rule.

Why bother

HVAC School explains that heating copper with air inside forms oxide scale, and that loose scale can plug small passages such as expansion valve screens and oil return openings. Its advice, passed along from training sources, is to purge first and then reduce to a low flow. For A2L systems, AHRI also says to use nitrogen before and during all brazing.

Two settings, not one

  • Purge: a high flow to push air out of the line first. HVAC School lists regulator-maker purge ranges that run from the 20s into the 50s of cubic feet per hour, depending on the tool.
  • Brazing: a low flow while the joint is hot and while it cools. HVAC School suggests about 2 to 5 SCFH, using 3 SCFH as an example, and lists regulator-maker brazing ranges of about 3 to 6 SCFH.
  • A welding supplier's regulator page suggests starting around 2 to 3 CFH.

Put these together and you get roughly a few SCFH while brazing, with a faster purge first. Nobody published a standard that fixes one number, so these are suggestions, and the regulator maker's instructions and your procedure decide.

Pressure is not flow

The feed pressure on the flow tool is not the pressure you want inside the copper. HVAC School says the pipe needs an open exit and very low backpressure, a pressure reading does not tell you flow, and a flow tool is not a shutoff-proof safeguard. Never seal the outlet while gas flows. The welding supplier also notes that too much flow can raise pressure in the tube, can reduce filler penetration and can cool the joint.

A simple routine

  • Fit a regulator made for nitrogen, never a cylinder valve alone, and never any other gas.
  • Purge first at the higher setting with an open exit.
  • Drop to the low brazing flow before you heat, and keep it until the joint has cooled.
  • Release hose pressure and close the cylinder before changing hookups.
  • Keep the vent path clear, away from flame, and away from you.

Know the limits of the numbers

Fittings, boots and plugs have their own limits, and one product boot is described as not to be pressurized above 20 PSIG. Read the tool's instructions. If the numbers on this page and the equipment maker's instruction disagree, the maker wins.

Field card: Nitrogen Purge Flow While Brazing

Purge fast, braze low, never pressurize the line.

  • Nitrogen prevents scale that can plug small passages.
  • Purge first, then drop to a low brazing flow.
  • Sources suggest roughly 2 to 6 SCFH while brazing. No standard sets one number.
  • Pressure on the regulator is not flow, and the line must have an open exit.
  • Follow regulator, tool and equipment maker instructions.
