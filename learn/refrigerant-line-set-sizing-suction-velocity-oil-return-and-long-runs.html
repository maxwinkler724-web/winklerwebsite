Line Set Sizing for Long Runs: Velocity, Oil Return and Limits

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A long line set pulls two ways. A bigger vapor line loses less pressure, but a line that is too big moves gas too slowly to carry oil home to the compressor. This lesson shows how that trade-off works, what several makers publish for long runs, and how to check a planned job against one maker's tables.

Check the maker's document for your model

Check the maker's document for your model. Limits differ by brand, series and compressor type. The numbers below show the pattern; the document for your exact unit governs.

Why line size matters: pressure drop versus oil return

Suction gas rubs against the tube wall as it travels. That friction is pressure drop. A trade article on suction line sizing (hvacrschool.com) says too much drop raises the compression ratio and the compressor temperature without making the evaporator any colder, and it puts the common targets at under 3 psi for R-22 and under 5 psi for R-410A, lower being better.

The opposite problem is oil. A little oil leaves the compressor with the discharge gas, and it has to come back. Oil and refrigerant mix as liquids but poorly as vapors, so in the suction line the oil depends on moving gas to carry it. An oversized line slows the gas and lets oil creep or pool. An undersized line wastes capacity. For the oil side of the story see Refrigeration Superheat Checks and Oil Return and Refrigerant Oils.

Suction riser velocity

A vertical riser is the hard spot, because the gas has to lift the oil against gravity. Published minimum velocities for oil return differ by source, which is a good reason to use the maker's chart and not a number from memory:

  • The hvacrschool.com article, citing a York/Johnson Controls guide: 1,000 feet per minute (fpm) in vertical lines and 800 fpm in horizontal lines.
  • ACHR News (Ice Breaker: Suction Lines): 1,500 fpm in vertical sections and 700 fpm in horizontal sections.
  • Lennox's commercial split piping guideline (form 485116): about 800 fpm in vertical suction risers and about 600 fpm in horizontal runs.

Every source puts the riser number above the horizontal one. Lennox's guideline is for commercial splits, so treat it as an example of the same idea. For systems with wide capacity swings, the ACHR article describes two parallel risers, with the smaller one sized to carry minimum load.

What the makers publish for long runs

Here is a sample of limits for residential splits. Always confirm against the model you are installing.

  • Goodman TP-107M (R-410A, 18,000 to 60,000 Btuh). It covers line sets longer than 80 ft. Maximum equivalent length is 250 ft for single-stage scroll or reciprocating compressors and 150 ft for single-stage rotary and two-stage units. Maximum actual length is 200 ft for the first group and 150 ft for the others. Outdoor unit above: no lift limit for a single-stage air conditioner beyond the length cap, 80 ft for a single-stage heat pump (listed exceptions at 200 ft), 25 ft for two-stage. Outdoor unit below: 80 ft.
  • Carrier and Bryant Residential Piping and Long Line Guideline (LLGP-7XA, 1- and 2-stage Puron). At equal level: 200 ft actual and 250 ft equivalent. Outdoor above indoor: up to 200 ft vertical. Outdoor below indoor: up to 80 ft vertical. It prints no vapor line table; vapor sizes and capacity loss come from each model's product data.
  • Trane 4TTR6 installer's guide (18-AC98D1-7E). Maximum total line length 150 ft including lift, maximum lift 50 ft, for models 4TTR6024N through 4TTR6060N. Vapor line is 3/4 in on the 2 and 3 ton models, 7/8 in on the 4 ton and 1-1/8 in on the 5 ton. For other lengths the guide points to Trane's piping application guide.
  • Lennox SL22KLV (R-454B heat pump). About 180 ft equivalent, 150 ft actual, 60 ft liquid lift and 60 ft vapor riser.

The spread is wide: Goodman two-stage units top out at 25 ft of rise with the outdoor unit above, while Carrier allows 200 ft. A generic rule of thumb is dangerous.

When to downsize the vapor line

Downsizing means going below the size the maker lists for a standard install. The makers treat it as a table lookup, not a judgment call. Goodman gives capacity multipliers by equivalent length for each suction size, and says to pick the size from the equivalent length and check the recalculated capacity. Some sizes are barred outright, such as 7/8 in on 2-ton two-stage heat pumps. That article gives an example of the cost: 3/4 in on a 5 ton loses about 1 percent under 50 ft equivalent, while 5/8 in on a 3.5 ton is "off the chart." If the chart does not list the size for your capacity and length, do not use it.

Traps

Trap rules depend on where the outdoor unit sits. Goodman requires an oil trap at the indoor unit with the outdoor unit above only when the elevation difference exceeds 80 ft. With the outdoor unit below the indoor unit, Goodman and Carrier both call for an inverted trap in the suction line at the indoor unit, with its top above the top of the indoor coil, so liquid cannot drain down toward the compressor. Carrier says vapor line traps are not required with the outdoor unit above. Lennox's commercial guideline calls for one trap at the base of a 5 to 60 ft riser. The ACHR article advises shallow traps with short horizontal legs so oil does not pool.

Crankcase heater, hard start and charge

A long line set holds more refrigerant, which raises the chance of refrigerant migrating into the sump during an off cycle. Goodman's TP-107M calls for a crankcase heater of at least 40 W on any compressor that does not ship with one, plus a hard start assist and an indoor TXV, in every arrangement it covers. Carrier's product data also requires a crankcase heater, a start capacitor and relay for single-phase scroll compressors, and a hard shut-off TXV or liquid line solenoid in long line use. For how heaters work, how long to power them, and how to test one, see Crankcase Heaters.

Charge is a separate step. Goodman says all residential R-410A outdoor units ship with enough for 15 ft of line set and gives 0.60 oz per foot for 3/8 in liquid alone, rising to 0.78 oz per foot with a 1-1/8 in suction line. The full method is in Charging by Weight and Line Set Length Adjustments. Goodman then has you confirm subcooling at the indoor unit, targeting 7 to 9 degrees F.

Worked example: 80 ft run with a 25 ft rise

You plan a 3 ton (36,000 Btuh) single-stage R-410A air conditioner with a scroll compressor. The line set is 80 ft long. The outdoor unit sits 25 ft above the indoor coil, so the suction line has a 25 ft riser. The run has six long-radius 90 degree elbows. Check it against Goodman TP-107M. This job sits right at the edge of that document, which is written for line sets longer than 80 ft, so treat the check as a conservative one.

  1. Limits. The equivalent length cap for single-stage scroll is 250 ft and the actual length cap is 200 ft. The 80 ft run is under both. For an air conditioner with the outdoor unit above, lift is not restricted beyond the equivalent length cap, so 25 ft passes.
  2. Equivalent length with 3/4 in suction. Table 5-1 gives 1.5 ft per long-radius 90 degree elbow at 3/4 in. Six elbows: 6 x 1.5 = 9 ft. Equivalent length = 80 + 9 = 89 ft. The next column up in Table 5-3 is 100 ft, so use that column.
  3. Capacity multiplier. In the 36,000 Btuh rows at 100 ft, 3/4 in suction shows 0.99 (about 1 percent loss), 5/8 in shows 0.95 (about 5 percent loss) and 7/8 in shows 1.00.
  4. Choice. 3/4 in holds the loss near 1 percent. 7/8 in would lose almost nothing, and footnote 3 does not bar it here because that footnote is about two-stage heat pumps. A bigger line slows riser gas, so confirm against the unit's product data. Do not drop to 5/8 in.
  5. Accessories. The Section 3 table for an air conditioner calls for a 40 W crankcase heater, a hard start assist and an indoor TXV. It does not call for a liquid line solenoid, an inverted trap or an oil trap here, because the oil trap threshold is an 80 ft elevation difference and this rise is 25 ft. Carrier's guideline would add a hard shut-off TXV, since it requires one beyond 50 ft total or 20 ft of vertical separation.
  6. Added charge. Goodman lists 0.67 oz per foot for a 3/8 in liquid and 3/4 in suction line set. The unit ships for 15 ft, so (80 - 15) x 0.67 = 65 x 0.67 = 43.55 oz, about 43.6 oz to add. Liquid alone at 0.60 would be 65 x 0.60 = 39 oz, so the suction size matters. Then confirm 7 to 9 degrees F of subcooling at the indoor unit.

Trane's limits (150 ft total, 50 ft lift) are also met, but each maker's own table decides sizes and accessories for its own equipment.

Field card: Line Set Sizing for Long Runs

Big enough for pressure drop, small enough for oil return.

  • Too small wastes capacity; too big slows gas and strands oil. Risers need the most velocity.
  • Published oil-return minimums differ by source, so use the maker's chart.
  • Limits vary a lot: Trane 150 ft and 50 ft lift; Goodman two-stage 25 ft rise with outdoor above.
  • Use equivalent length (add fitting losses) to pick the vapor size from the maker's table.
  • Outdoor below indoor: inverted suction trap at the indoor unit. Long runs: crankcase heater, hard start, TXV.
  • Add charge by weight for the extra feet, then confirm subcooling.
