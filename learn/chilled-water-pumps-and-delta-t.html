Chilled Water Pumps and Delta-T

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the Commercial Cooling Guide

Delta-T is just the gap between the water leaving the chiller and the water coming back. That one number tells you a lot about how well a chilled water plant is doing.

What delta-T is

Delta-T is the chilled water return temperature minus the supply temperature. The water absorbs heat at the coils, so it should come back warmer than it left. A common textbook design has water leaving the chiller at 7 °C (about 44 °F) and returning at 12 °C (about 54 °F), a 5 degree C spread. That 44 °F leaving temperature is also the standard AHRI rating condition: one manufacturer’s rating page lists 44 °F leaving chilled water at 2.4 gpm per ton, which works out to roughly a 10 °F spread, with 85 °F entering condenser water at 3.0 gpm per ton. A rating condition is a yardstick for comparing machines, not a target for your plant. Your plant's design values will differ, so look them up for your system.

Flow, load and delta-T are linked

The heat a water loop carries depends on how much water flows and how much its temperature changes. For the same load, less flow means a bigger delta-T, and more flow means a smaller one. That is why pumps and delta-T go together: a pump moves the water, and the coils decide how much heat each gallon picks up.

What the pumps do

Chilled water pumps circulate water through the chiller and out to the coils. In older plants the pumps run at constant speed and control valves at the coils adjust flow. In newer plants variable-speed drives slow the pumps when the load drops, which saves electricity. Primary-secondary piping is one common way to keep the chiller's flow steady while the building's flow changes.

Low delta-T syndrome

When the real delta-T is smaller than the design delta-T, a plant has low delta-T syndrome. The water is coming back too cold because it is not picking up as much heat as it could. One published explanation lists these typical causes:

  • Oversized or poorly sized coils and valves that transfer heat badly.
  • Scaled or fouled heat-transfer surfaces.
  • Poor balancing, which sends more flow than a coil needs.
  • Excess flow in general.

Why it matters

With low delta-T the plant moves a lot of water for little cooling. Chillers cannot reach their full usable capacity, so extra chillers may be started to meet a load that fewer could handle. That costs energy and wear. Pumps also burn power moving water that is not doing much work.

Things a tech can check

  • Compare supply and return temperatures to the design values and to last week's log.
  • Look at coil valves that hunt or are stuck open or oversized, since a valve that passes more water than the coil needs sends back water that did little work.
  • Check coil cleanliness, air-side and water-side, and strainers.
  • Look for bypass or three-way valves sending water around the coil.
  • Check that balancing has not been undone by changes. See Hot-Water Balancing Basics for the same idea on heating loops.

Safety

Chilled water systems may be under pressure and pumps have rotating shafts and high-voltage motors. Lock out and tag out before opening a panel or touching a rotating part, and never bypass a safety device to keep something running. Do not adjust a pump or valve in a way the plant's operating procedure does not allow.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: the manufacturer's instructions for the equipment in front of you and the rules where you work always govern. This lesson explains concepts. It gives no setpoints, and your site's numbers are the ones that count.

Field card: Chilled Water Pumps and Delta-T

Delta-T tells you how hard each gallon is working.

  • Delta-T = return temperature minus supply temperature.
  • Lower flow gives a bigger delta-T for the same load; more flow gives a smaller one.
  • Low delta-T wastes pump energy and can force extra chillers to run.
  • Common causes: bad coil sizing, fouling, poor balancing, excess flow, leaking valves.
  • Record supply and return daily so changes stand out.
