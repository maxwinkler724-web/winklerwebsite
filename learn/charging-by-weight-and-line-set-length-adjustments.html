An outdoor unit ships with refrigerant in it, enough for a stated length of line set. Your job on a new install is to work out how far the real line set is from that stated length, then add or remove the difference by weight. This lesson goes deeper on that one step. For the bigger picture of charging methods, start with Charging Methods Overview.

What the factory charge actually covers

The nameplate charge is not "the" charge for your job. It is the charge the maker figured for a matched indoor unit plus a stated length of line. The stated length differs by brand, so read the installation manual for the exact model every time.

  • Carrier and Bryant (Puron air conditioners). The 34SCA5 manual says units are factory charged for 15 ft of 3/8 inch liquid line, and it calls the factory charge for that line set 9 oz. Bryant's BA14 manual uses the same wording.
  • Goodman. Its long line set bulletin TP-107M says residential R-410A outdoor units are factory charged for 15 ft of line set.
  • Trane. The 4TTR2 and 4TTR4 installer's guide says the units are charged for fifteen feet of connecting line. For longer lines or a different line size it sends you to the charging charts in the Service Facts, and it gives no ounces-per-foot number in the guide I read.
  • Mitsubishi Electric. The MSZ-FS installation manual says the outdoor unit is charged for pipe length up to 25 ft, and extra refrigerant is needed beyond that.
  • Daikin. One Daikin R32 split manual (RXF20-42F) says extra refrigerant is needed only when the liquid piping is longer than 10 m.

So "15 feet" is common on ducted residential units, but it is not universal. Mini-splits often allow a longer run before you add anything.

Ounces per foot depends on the liquid line size

Liquid refrigerant is dense, so the liquid line holds most of the charge you add or take out. A thinner line holds less per foot. Carrier's 34SCA5 table gives R-410A charge per foot of liquid line:

Liquid line ODoz per ft
3/8 in0.60
5/16 in0.40
1/4 in0.27

Goodman's TP-107M uses 0.60 oz per linear foot for a 3/8 inch liquid line alone, and lists slightly higher values (0.63 to 0.78) when the suction line size is counted for very long runs. Those apply to line sets longer than 80 ft, so do not use them for a normal install. Mitsubishi's manual gives its rate as 1.08 oz for each 5 ft beyond 25 ft. Divide it out and that is about 0.22 oz per foot (my arithmetic, not the manual's).

Notice how different the numbers are. A rate from one maker's manual does not transfer to another unit. Never use a number you remember. Use the table that came with that model.

The adjustment formula

Carrier prints this formula: (oz per foot x total line length) minus the factory charge for the line set equals the charge adjustment. For the 34SCA5 that factory allowance is 9 oz. A positive answer means add, a negative answer means remove.

Worked example

You install a Carrier 34SCA5 with a 3/8 inch liquid line, 40 ft long. The manual's rate is 0.60 oz per foot.

  1. Line set charge: 0.60 oz/ft x 40 ft = 24 oz.
  2. Subtract the factory allowance: 24 oz - 9 oz = 15 oz.
  3. Result: add 15 oz of refrigerant on top of what the unit shipped with.

Check it a second way: the unit already covers 15 ft, so 40 - 15 = 25 ft extra, and 25 ft x 0.60 = 15 oz. Same answer. That shortcut only works when the line is 3/8 inch.

When the line set is short

The formula can go negative. Carrier's own example uses 15 ft of 1/4 inch liquid line: 0.27 x 15 = 4.05 oz, minus 9 oz gives -4.95 oz. The manual says the net result is to remove 4.95 oz from the system. Bryant's BA14 manual shows the same example. This surprises people because 15 ft matches the factory length. The catch is that the factory allowance assumes 3/8 inch line, and a 1/4 inch line holds less. Recover the extra into a recovery cylinder; do not vent it. See Refrigerant Recovery, Step by Step. A second Carrier example, 45 ft of 5/16 inch line, works out to (0.40 x 45) - 9 = 9 oz to add.

Before any of this, the line set should be leak tested and evacuated. See Evacuation and Deep Vacuum Basics and Mini-Split Installation Basics. Daikin's R32 manual lists leak test and vacuum drying as the step before charging.

Scale accuracy

A weigh-in is only as good as the scale. One Yellow Jacket 220 lb charging scale lists accuracy of plus or minus 0.05 percent of reading plus 10 g, and a resolution of 0.17 oz. For a 15 oz addition (about 425 g), that works out to 0.0005 x 425 = 0.2 g plus 10 g, roughly 10 g or 0.36 oz of possible error. That is small. Bigger errors come from habits: a hose that is not zeroed correctly, a cylinder that touches the wall, a bumped scale, or reading the wrong direction. Zero the scale with hoses attached, take the weight change from the cylinder, and keep it still and level.

Liquid or vapor from the cylinder

Blends can separate if you draw only the vapor, because the lighter components leave first. An ACHR News excerpt from a National Refrigerants reference guide says zeotropic blends must be removed from the cylinder as a liquid, and that you should not push liquid into the suction line to slug the compressor. Mitsubishi's FS installation manual says to charge slowly with liquid refrigerant, and warns that otherwise the refrigerant composition in the system may change and hurt performance. Practical read for an apprentice: follow the cylinder label and the unit manual, and meter liquid in slowly rather than opening a valve wide. Chemours lists a glide of about 1.1 K for Opteon XL41 (R-454B) and says it can be topped off after leaks, which describes how closely its components behave, not how to draw it from the cylinder. Follow the cylinder label.

Verify after you weigh in

Weighing gets you close. It does not prove the system is right, because line length estimates, a different indoor coil, or a leftover restriction can still throw it off. Once the system runs and settles, check against the maker's target. Goodman's long line bulletin requires a TXV and says to charge to 7 to 9 degrees F subcooling at the indoor unit, adding if below 7 and removing if above 9. A Carrier 34SCA5 table lists required subcooling by unit size for TXV systems. For the method, see Superheat and Subcooling Basics and Metering Devices: TXV and Piston. If the readings disagree with your math, check airflow first and read Undercharge vs. Overcharge: A Symptom Table before adding or removing anything else.

Field card: Charging by Weight and Line Set Length Adjustments

Find the factory length, measure the real line, then weigh the difference.

  • Read the manual: the factory charge covers a stated length (15 ft in Carrier, Bryant, Goodman and Trane; 25 ft in the Mitsubishi FS manual).
  • Rate depends on liquid line size. Carrier 34SCA5: 3/8 in 0.60, 5/16 in 0.40, 1/4 in 0.27 oz/ft.
  • Adjustment = (oz/ft x total length) - factory allowance. Negative means remove.
  • Example: 40 ft of 3/8 in = 24 - 9 = add 15 oz.
  • Evacuate first. Zero the scale with hoses on. Meter liquid in slowly.
  • Verify by subcooling or superheat to the maker's target, after airflow is confirmed.
