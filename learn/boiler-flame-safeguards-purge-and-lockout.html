Boiler Flame Safeguards, Purge and Lockout

A flame safeguard has one job: never let fuel flow unless a flame is proven. Understanding its sequence turns most burner problems from mysteries into questions about which step stopped.

The sequence in plain steps

Manufacturer literature describes a pattern that most automatic burners follow. First the control checks that its safety circuits are in their proper standby state. A call for heat starts the blower. An airflow switch must prove air is moving before anything else advances. Then a timed pre-purge moves air through the combustion space to clear any unburned fuel. A pilot or ignition source is then proven during a short trial, and finally the main flame is proven in its own trial. When the call ends, a post-purge may run after the fuel valves close.

Trial for ignition

The trial for ignition is a short timed window to prove flame. One manufacturer document says the pilot window typically lasts roughly five to ten seconds and the main window roughly ten to fifteen, depending on the setting and applicable code. Treat those as examples: your control’s nameplate and manual set the real values. If flame is not proven in time, fuel valves close and the control locks out.

Flame detectors

The control watches flame with a sensor, usually a flame rod, an ultraviolet scanner or an infrared scanner, matched to the amplifier in the control. A dirty or misaimed scanner can cause nuisance lockouts. Insurance guidance suggests cleaning scanners as needed. Cleaning is not the same as adjusting a safety device, and operators should not tamper with settings they were not trained and authorized to change.

What lockout means

A lockout is a safety shutdown that needs a manual reset. The literature lists causes such as an interlock not proven in time, flame not proven during a trial, or flame seen when none should exist (a false flame). Losing flame or a non-recycling input during operation can cause an immediate lockout. The right response is to check the cause, reset once, and escalate if it repeats. Repeated resetting hides the problem and can pile up unburned fuel.

Why purge is sacred

A state guide says that purging the fireside before lighting a cold boiler or relighting after a flame loss is essential, and that skipping it is the most common cause of furnace explosions. An insurer sheet adds that the purge time is set by the equipment maker and that shortening or bypassing it greatly increases explosion risk. Another guide describes a three-to-five-minute purge for high-pressure steam boilers and shutting off fuel and re-purging if ignition does not occur within five seconds; that is an example from one guide, not a universal rule.

Interlocks and the series chain

Limits, the low-water cutoff, airflow proof, fuel-pressure switches and similar devices sit in a chain that must be complete before fuel can flow. When the chain opens, the burner stops. This is why a low-water trip and a flame lockout feel alike from the outside and why panel indicator lights matter. A trade article warns that lights burn out and alarm contacts can fail, so use panel test buttons regularly instead of assuming normal.

Safe habits

  • If you smell gas or a CO alarm sounds, leave first and call from outside. See combustion-and-co-safety-basics.
  • Never jumper a limit, interlock or scanner to get a boiler to run.
  • Test flame supervision, shutoff valve tightness and air-fuel interlocks at the interval the manufacturer and local rules require; one insurer guide mentions at least yearly for gas and oil units, which is an example.
  • Log lockouts with the time, the code shown and what you found.
Under review. Lockout reset policy and test frequencies at your plant are site-specific. They are not stated here as rules.

Field card: Boiler Flame Safeguards, Purge and Lockout

Air proven, space purged, flame proven in time, or the fuel stops.

  • Sequence: safety check, airflow proof, pre-purge, pilot trial, main trial, run, post-purge.
  • Trial windows are short and timed by the control; values here are examples only.
  • Lockout needs manual reset; find the cause instead of resetting repeatedly.
  • Never shorten or bypass the purge or jumper an interlock.
  • Gas smell or CO alarm: leave first, then call.
