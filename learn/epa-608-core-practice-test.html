EPA 608 Core Practice Test (Free, Explained)

Last updated: 2026-10-16 · 25 questions

The Core section covers ozone depletion, refrigerant regulations, recovery, safe handling and general refrigeration basics that apply to every type of equipment.

The real exam is a multiple-choice test given by an EPA-approved certifying organization. Question counts, passing scores and open- or closed-book rules differ by organization, so confirm them with the organization you plan to test with and on the EPA Section 608 page (checked 2026-10-16).

How to use this test. Read each question, choose your answer, then open “Show answer” to see why and where it comes from. These are original practice questions, not real exam questions. Every explanation links to the source it is based on.

1. Which part of the atmosphere contains the ozone layer?

  1. Troposphere
  2. Stratosphere
  3. Mesosphere
  4. Ionosphere
Show answer

Answer: B. Stratosphere

Ozone sits in the stratosphere and filters UV-B.

Source: EPA basic ozone layer science

2. What makes CFCs harmful to ozone?

  1. Hydrogen
  2. Chlorine
  3. Fluorine
  4. Carbon
Show answer

Answer: B. Chlorine

Chlorine atoms freed by UV act as a catalyst that destroys many ozone molecules.

Source: EPA basic ozone layer science

3. Which has ODP of zero?

  1. R-12
  2. R-22
  3. R-134a
  4. R-11
Show answer

Answer: C. R-134a

HFCs have no chlorine. They still have GWP.

Source: EPA ozone-depleting substances page

4. Removing refrigerant from a system into an external container is called:

  1. Recycling
  2. Reclaiming
  3. Recovering
  4. Charging
Show answer

Answer: C. Recovering

Recovery moves refrigerant out of a system into an external container, without processing it.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

5. Processing refrigerant to new-product purity at a certified facility is:

  1. Recovery
  2. Recycling
  3. Reclaim
  4. Evacuation
Show answer

Answer: C. Reclaim

Reclaim is verified by lab analysis.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

6. Recovery cylinders must never be filled beyond what percent of capacity?

  1. 60
  2. 70
  3. 80
  4. 90
Show answer

Answer: C. 80

Liquid expands with heat; overfilling risks rupture.

Background source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

7. A recovery cylinder is colored:

  1. All yellow
  2. Gray body, yellow top
  3. Green
  4. Blue with white top
Show answer

Answer: B. Gray body, yellow top

Gray body with a yellow top is the standard color code identifying a recovery cylinder.

Background source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

8. A technician finds a disposable cylinder nearly empty. What is the rule?

  1. Refill it from recovery
  2. Never refill it
  3. Refill if inspected
  4. Refill only with R-22
Show answer

Answer: B. Never refill it

One-way cylinders are not to be refilled.

Source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

9. Which gas is appropriate for pressure-testing a system?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Compressed air
  3. Dry nitrogen with regulator
  4. Acetylene
Show answer

Answer: C. Dry nitrogen with regulator

Oxygen + oil can explode; air carries moisture and oxygen.

Source: Aircon Spares, Nitrogen pressure testing

10. Refrigerant vapor is heavier than air. The main hazard in a small basement mechanical room is:

  1. Fire only
  2. Asphyxiation
  3. Noise
  4. Corrosion of ducts
Show answer

Answer: B. Asphyxiation

Vapor pools low and displaces oxygen.

Source: R-134a Safety Data Sheet (National Refrigerants)

11. Refrigerant touching an open flame can produce:

  1. Hydrogen
  2. Phosgene and acids
  3. Nothing harmful
  4. Only water
Show answer

Answer: B. Phosgene and acids

Burning refrigerant decomposes it into toxic phosgene and corrosive acids such as hydrochloric and hydrofluoric acid.

Background source: R-134a Safety Data Sheet (National Refrigerants)

12. Technician certification must be renewed:

  1. Every year
  2. Every 3 years
  3. Every 5 years
  4. Never
Show answer

Answer: D. Never

Certification does not expire.

Source: EPA Section 608 Technician Certification

13. A refrigerant with safety class A2L is:

  1. Highly toxic, non-flammable
  2. Lower toxicity, mildly flammable
  3. Lower toxicity, highly flammable
  4. Higher toxicity, non-flammable
Show answer

Answer: B. Lower toxicity, mildly flammable

A = lower toxicity; 2L = lower flammability.

Source: ASHRAE 34 safety classes summary (hvactoolkit.org)

14. Propane (R-290) safety class is most likely:

  1. A1
  2. A2L
  3. A3
  4. B1
Show answer

Answer: C. A3

Hydrocarbons are highly flammable.

Source: ASHRAE 34 safety classes summary (hvactoolkit.org)

15. Which of these is an A2L refrigerant?

  1. R-410A
  2. R-32
  3. R-134a
  4. R-404A
Show answer

Answer: B. R-32

R-32 (also R-454B, R-1234yf).

Source: ASHRAE 34 safety classes summary (hvactoolkit.org)

16. Is there a separate "Type IV" certification for A2L refrigerants?

  1. Yes, since 2026
  2. No, A2L knowledge is part of the existing exam
  3. Only for chillers
  4. Only in some states
Show answer

Answer: B. No, A2L knowledge is part of the existing exam

The regulation (40 CFR 82.161) defines only Type I, II, III and Universal. A2L knowledge is part of the existing exam.

Source: 40 CFR 82.161(a)

17. R-410A cylinders are color-coded:

  1. Light green
  2. Orange
  3. Rose
  4. Light blue
Show answer

Answer: C. Rose

R-410A cylinders are rose-colored, a standard identifier for that refrigerant.

Source: AHRI Guideline N-2017

18. Why are zeotropic blends charged as liquid?

  1. Faster
  2. To avoid changing the blend composition
  3. To save oil
  4. To avoid sight glass bubbles
Show answer

Answer: B. To avoid changing the blend composition

Vapor charging fractionates the blend.

Source: National Refrigerants Blends Tutorial

19. Best way to confirm a deep evacuation is complete?

  1. Compound gauge reading
  2. Micron gauge and a hold test
  3. Listening to pump
  4. Time elapsed
Show answer

Answer: B. Micron gauge and a hold test

A micron gauge reads the true vacuum level, and a hold test confirms the system stays tight.

Source: Appion, Successful Evacuation

20. A rise in vacuum pressure after the pump is isolated indicates:

  1. Success
  2. A leak or moisture still in the system
  3. Faulty gauge only
  4. Low oil
Show answer

Answer: B. A leak or moisture still in the system

Vacuum rising after isolation means a leak or moisture boiling off inside the system.

Source: Appion, Successful Evacuation

21. Non-condensables in a system cause:

  1. Lower head pressure
  2. Higher head pressure
  3. No change
  4. Lower suction
Show answer

Answer: B. Higher head pressure

Non-condensable gases such as air occupy condenser space and raise head pressure.

Source: HVAC School, Non-non-condensables

22. When you replace a compressor, you should also replace:

  1. Contactor
  2. Filter-drier
  3. Thermostat
  4. Fan
Show answer

Answer: B. Filter-drier

After a compressor burnout or replacement, the filter-drier should be replaced to remove acid and moisture.

Background source: ACHR News, Working With POE Oils

23. A technician sells recovered refrigerant to a different owner without processing. Allowed?

  1. Yes
  2. Only if reclaimed to purity spec
  3. Yes if labeled
  4. Yes if same color cylinder
Show answer

Answer: B. Only if reclaimed to purity spec

Used refrigerant sold to a different owner must be reclaimed by a certified reclaimer (40 CFR 82.154(d)); returning it to the same owner's equipment is allowed.

Source: 40 CFR 82.154

24. Mixing refrigerants in one recovery cylinder typically means:

  1. It's fine
  2. It likely must go to a reclaimer and may be unusable
  3. Add more oil
  4. Heat it
Show answer

Answer: B. It likely must go to a reclaimer and may be unusable

Mixed refrigerants cannot be reliably separated in the field, so the cylinder usually goes to a reclaimer.

Background source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

25. Which tool measures mass of refrigerant added or recovered most reliably?

  1. Manifold gauges
  2. Scale
  3. Thermometer
  4. Sight glass
Show answer

Answer: B. Scale

A scale weighs the cylinder, giving the most accurate measure of refrigerant added or recovered.

Background source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety
