Condensing Boiler Annual Maintenance: What a Proper Service Includes

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A condensing boiler "serviced" in ten minutes with a wipe of the flame sensor has not been serviced. The makers' manuals list a longer job: clean the combustion side the approved way, replace what is worn, prove the condensate path, and measure the flame. This lesson pulls those items together and shows where the makers differ.

Why the manuals matter more than a checklist

Every maker publishes its own procedure, and they are not interchangeable. If a manual below is silent on something, that does not make the step optional; it means we did not find it on the pages we read. Your boiler's own installation and service manual always wins.

Weil-McLain's service bulletin SB-1203 adds a first-year inspection for condensing boilers: look at the heat exchanger flueways, burner, igniter and cover plate seal. Buildup in the flueways can point to contaminated combustion air or gas. If problems show up it suggests considering a 6-month service schedule. If the flueway is clean and parts are in very good condition, annual start-ups continue and the next internal inspection can wait two years, never more than three.

1. Combustion chamber and heat exchanger

  • Lochinvar Knight. Nylon brush only; the manual says not to use a metal brush. Steps: shut down and let the boiler cool, remove the access cover, burner and gas/air arm, vacuum the heating surfaces without solvents, brush the dry surfaces, vacuum again, wipe with a cloth dampened with warm water, rinse debris out at low pressure, let it dry completely, then flush the boiler. The manual notes the boiler contains ceramic fiber to be handled as the service manual says.
  • Weil-McLain AquaBalance Series 2. The burner and exchanger must be clean and free of scale, with no chemical products or wire brushes.
  • Buderus SSB. The cleaning instructions say the heat exchanger interior should be cleaned annually, using only the Axi-Therm Clean F9A decalcifier named in the document (10 to 20 minutes at most, then rinse). They say not to use any other chemical cleaner, including cleaners for aluminum exchangers.
  • HTP Elite FT. The manual recommends cleaning the heat exchanger at least once a year to prevent lime scale buildup.
  • Triangle Tube Prestige. Inspect the jacket interior and clean and vacuum if necessary; clean the heat exchanger and flueways if combustion or performance is poor.

A brush, a vacuum and a rinse is the common core, but one maker forbids chemicals and another names a single product. The wrong cleaner on the wrong metal is exactly what those warnings are about.

2. Burner, refractory and gaskets

Lochinvar says to watch the flame through the viewing window at high and low fire. If it looks wrong, cool the boiler and clean the burner with a vacuum or compressed air (not indoors), and check that the gasket is in good condition and correctly positioned on reinstall. Triangle Tube says to clean the burner head with compressed air only. Viessmann's Vitodens 100-W instructions check the burner gauze, electrodes, insulation ring and burner gasket for damage and call for a new burner gasket, correctly positioned. None of the pages we read gives a fixed replacement interval, so replace what is damaged and use the maker's part.

3. Ignition and flame electrodes

Lochinvar removes both electrodes, cleans deposits with sandpaper, replaces them if they will not clean up, and checks the ignition ground wire for continuity. Weil-McLain requires the electrode to be free of scale and properly positioned. Triangle Tube says to sand off white oxide, then clean and reposition. Viessmann says to check the electrode gaps and, if they are not as specified or the electrodes are damaged, replace the electrodes and gaskets. We found no gap number on the pages we read, so take it from your manual. For the microamp side see Flame Sensor Cleaning and Microamps. Lochinvar's figure is a flame signal of at least 10 microamps at high fire; lower may mean a fouled or damaged electrode.

4. Condensate trap and neutralizer

Lochinvar: remove the clean-out cap at the bottom of the trap, drain the debris, refill with fresh water. The trap must stay full whenever the boiler runs or flue gas can escape through the drain line. Navien: remove the wire clip under the cabinet, pull the trap cup, clean it, and seat the o-ring on reassembly. Triangle Tube: clean the condensate drain assembly and fill it with fresh water. Weil-McLain tells owners to confirm monthly that the trap holds water.

Lochinvar says condensate pH is typically 3 to 5 and a neutralizing filter is required where local code calls for one. A Navien manual says to periodically check the media level in the neutralizer and test pH at its outlet. HTP says condensate should be neutralized. We found no media replacement interval, so check level and outlet pH and replace when the media is spent.

5. Vent and air intake

Weil-McLain: visually check vent and air piping for blockage, leakage or deterioration. Triangle Tube: vent and air inlet piping must be in good condition, sealed tight and properly supported. Lochinvar's start-up list checks every vent and air connection and seam for a gastight seal and the pipe for corrosion, damage or sagging. Navien says to clean the intake air screens inside the cabinet, which keep debris out of the blower, burner and heat exchanger. See vent categories.

6. Water side

  • Pressure. Lochinvar and HTP give 12 psi as a minimum cold fill or gauge reading; Weil-McLain AquaBalance says about 18 psi or the system design pressure. Use your manual and the building height.
  • Expansion tank. Weil-McLain says a gauge reading above 24 psig with a 30 psig relief valve may point to an expansion tank problem. Lochinvar says to rule out a waterlogged or undersized tank before blaming a weeping relief valve. Viessmann has a step to check the expansion vessel charge. See Expansion Tanks and Fill Pressure.
  • Relief valve. Lochinvar: lift the lever at least once a year, discharge piped to a safe place.
  • Water quality. Weil-McLain AquaBalance: pH 7.0 to 8.5, hardness under 7 grains, chloride under 50 ppm. HTP Elite FT: pH 6.5 to 8.5, hardness under 7 grains, chloride under 100 ppm, total dissolved solids under 500 ppm. Lochinvar: consult a water treatment company above 7 grains, never fill with chlorine over 200 ppm, and replace freeze protection fluid periodically because its inhibitors degrade. Compare with the right book; see boiler water tests.

7. The combustion test

Lochinvar: enter Service Mode, remove the flue temperature sensor, put the probe in that port, and measure at full fire. Reinstall the sensor afterward; the manual warns that skipping this can spill flue gas into the room. Its table for natural gas lists CO2 8.0 to 10 percent and O2 3.0 to 6.5 percent, and says CO should be under 150 ppm for a properly installed unit. Out of range sends you to the service manual's troubleshooting section, not to a screw first. Those are Lochinvar's numbers; other makers print other numbers, and we could not read every table, so use your own boiler's. For what the readings mean, see Combustion Analyzer Readings.

Worked example: readings against the manual

At full fire on natural gas a Lochinvar Knight reads CO2 9.2 percent, O2 4.6 percent, CO 38 ppm as measured. These numbers are hypothetical.

  1. CO2 (range 8.0 to 10.0): 9.2 − 8.0 = 1.2 above the floor; 10.0 − 9.2 = 0.8 below the ceiling. In range.
  2. O2 (range 3.0 to 6.5): 4.6 − 3.0 = 1.6; 6.5 − 4.6 = 1.9. In range.
  3. CO as measured (under 150): 150 − 38 = 112 ppm of margin; 38 ÷ 150 = 0.25, a quarter of the limit.
  4. CO air-free, using the formula in our analyzer lesson, CO × 20.9 ÷ (20.9 − O2): 20.9 − 4.6 = 16.3; 20.9 ÷ 16.3 = 1.28; 38 × 1.28 = 48.7 ppm. The page we read does not say whether the 150 ppm is air-free or as measured, so compare both: 38 and 48.7 are each under 150.

All three pass. A failing case: CO2 7.4, O2 7.2, CO 160. That is 8.0 − 7.4 = 0.6 below the CO2 floor, 7.2 − 6.5 = 0.7 above the O2 ceiling, and 160 − 150 = 10 over the CO limit. Check venting, intake, gas pressure and burner condition before adjusting anything.

Before you leave

Run a full cycle, confirm ignition, flame signal, leaks and a full trap, and record the readings so next year's visit can compare. For commercial plants see the PM schedule; for why returns must run cool see Boiler Return Temperature and Condensing Boilers.

Field card: Condensing Boiler Annual Maintenance: What a Proper Service Includes

Clean it the maker's way, replace what is worn, prove the trap, then measure.

  • Heat exchanger: nylon brush, vacuum, rinse; follow your maker on chemicals.
  • Burner and electrode gaskets: replace if damaged, seat correctly.
  • Electrodes: clean, check gap and ground wiring against your manual.
  • Trap: clean, refill, keep full. Neutralizer: check media and outlet pH.
  • Vent and intake: sealed, supported, clear; clean intake screens.
  • Water: pressure, expansion tank, relief valve lever, pH and hardness per your maker.
  • Combustion: full fire, compare CO2, O2, CO to the manual, restore the sensor.
