Condenser Water Treatment: Scale, Corrosion and Biological Growth

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the Commercial Cooling Guide

Open tower water is constantly evaporating, and what stays behind causes three linked problems: scale, corrosion and biological growth. A water-treatment vendor designs the program. Operators still need to understand why the numbers on the log matter.

Why the water changes

In an open recirculating tower, only pure water evaporates. The dissolved minerals stay in the loop and become more concentrated. A U.S. Department of Energy presentation describes this concentration as the root of scale, corrosion and biological problems, and defines cycles of concentration as the ratio of dissolved solids in the blowdown to those in the makeup water. Blowdown is the water drained off to hold that ratio in range. The same presentation gives one example: moving from 3 to 5 cycles cut system water use by 16 percent. That is an example, and the right level depends on the makeup water and the chemistry.

Scale

Hardness minerals, mainly calcium and magnesium, combine with carbonate, phosphate or sulfate and drop out on warm surfaces once the water is saturated. High alkalinity and high pH raise the chance. A vendor explainer adds that scale insulates, so the chiller has to work harder for the same cooling. Scale control uses inhibitors, pH or acid control and a cap on cycles.

Corrosion

Low pH, low alkalinity, high dissolved solids and chlorides, and dissolved oxygen all push corrosion. It often starts under deposits where you cannot see it. The DOE presentation notes that softening the makeup water reduces scale but can increase corrosion, and that mixed metals call for galvanic corrosion monitoring. Corrosion control uses film-forming inhibitors and holding pH in the program's band.

Biological growth

Warm water, sunlight, debris as food and stagnant corners favor microbes. Biofilm insulates like scale, shelters corrosion and can harbor Legionella. The DOE presentation lists controls such as consistent oxidizing biocide residuals supplemented with other biocides and dispersants, checking for algae and biofilm, covering open decks, eliminating dead legs and stagnant areas, and regular microbiological testing. Building water management programs are often built on ASHRAE Standard 188, and the presentation says the building owner is responsible for compliance. ASHRAE 188 is a published consensus standard, and its 2021 edition was reworded so codes can adopt it. Under review: which states or cities require it. For the safety side, see Cooling Tower Water Management and Legionella Awareness.

They feed each other

This is the key idea. Scale shelters microbes, microbes make deposits and corrosion, corrosion makes rough surfaces that collect scale. A program that fixes only one will eventually fail. That is why a plant uses a combination of chemical feed, blowdown, filtration and good housekeeping.

Conductivity and blowdown

Conductivity is a quick proxy for dissolved solids. In automated systems, a controller opens the blowdown valve when conductivity rises above a setpoint, and the DOE presentation says rising conductivity signals greater corrosion and scale potential. Vendor material lists common conductivity setpoints, but those vary by program and water, so use your treatment company's numbers. A conductivity probe still needs cleaning and calibration. Automation does not replace routine testing.

What routine monitoring generally includes

A vendor monitoring schedule describes weekly checks of conductivity and cycles, pH, biocide residual and inhibitor level, with monthly and quarterly items such as hardness, alkalinity, corrosion coupon readings and microbial counts. Its schedule and target values are one vendor's view and not a standard, so follow your site's water management plan. A corrosion coupon is a pre-weighed metal strip left in the loop for weeks, then weighed again to estimate metal loss.

What techs can do

  • Report fouled fill, algae, slime or odor, which are early flags.
  • Note when blowdown stops working. A stuck valve or a dirty probe changes everything downstream.
  • Watch approach temperature. A rising approach can come from scale on the chiller condenser tubes. See Chiller Approach Temperatures: Reading the Trend.
  • Do not add chemicals or change feed rates unless it is your job and you follow the safety data sheets and the treatment company's instructions.

Chemical handling needs eye protection, gloves and training. Treat the chemicals as hazardous.

Field card: Condenser Water Treatment: Scale, Corrosion and Biological Growth

Evaporation concentrates the water, and concentration causes trouble.

  • Cycles of concentration and blowdown hold minerals in range.
  • Scale, corrosion and biological growth reinforce each other.
  • Conductivity is a proxy for dissolved solids and often controls blowdown.
  • Biocide and water management plans protect against Legionella; the owner is responsible.
  • Setpoints and chemicals belong to the treatment program, not to guesswork.
