Thermostat Terminal Letters: R, C, W, Y, G Explained

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Each letter on a thermostat base is a request to the equipment. Know the request, and the pile of wires makes sense. Wire color tells you almost nothing.

Read the letter, not the color

Thermostat makers say there is no standard color code. Identify a wire by the terminal it is on. Thermostat wiring is low voltage, but guides still tell you to switch off power to the heating and cooling system first, and to photograph the wiring and label each wire before removing it. If a terminal has two names, such as W and O/B, label it with both. Make a note of any wire not landed on a terminal rather than tagging it.

Power: R, Rc, Rh and C

  • R is the 24 volt power from the transformer. If there is only one power wire, it goes on R.
  • Rc and Rh are the cooling and heating power sides. Two separate power wires usually mean two transformers. A small jumper between them joins the two for a single-transformer system, and it comes out first if you are landing a separate Rc wire.
  • C is the common, the return to the transformer, which powers the thermostat. It plays the role a neutral plays on a 120 volt circuit. A smart thermostat generally wants one. See Thermostat Wiring and Smart Thermostats.

Calls: W, Y and G

  • W calls for heat. W2 is a second stage, and on a heat pump it is usually auxiliary heat.
  • Y calls for cooling by energizing the contactor in the outdoor unit. Y2 is a second cooling stage.
  • G runs the indoor blower. It is the fan, not a ground.

Heat pump and extras

  • O/B controls the reversing valve. Some brands energize it in cooling (O), others in heating (B). Which one applies depends on the equipment. See Reversing Valve Diagnosis and O vs B.
  • E is emergency heat, a manually selected mode. Auxiliary heat (often X or AUX) comes on automatically. See Emergency Heat vs Auxiliary Heat and Lockouts.
  • L is a fault-indicator terminal on some thermostats, labeled L/A by one maker, as a heat pump fault input.
  • S, S1, S2 can carry an outdoor sensor, ACC an accessory, and K a wire-saver on some models.

Conventional vs heat pump, in one picture

A conventional cooling and furnace system typically uses R (or Rh and Rc), G, Y, W and C. A heat pump typically adds O or B for the reversing valve. Multi-speed fan wires and line-voltage systems do not suit ordinary retail thermostats. Thick black or red wires suggest line voltage, so stop and check.

Why you must still check the wiring diagram

Some equipment uses nonstandard terminal layouts, and communicating systems use different markings entirely (see Communicating Controls Basics). The wiring diagram and manual for your equipment settle every disagreement, including this page. The 24 volt side is explained in Relays, Transformers and 24V Control.

Field card: Thermostat Terminal Letters: R, C, W, Y, G Explained

The letter is the truth. The color is a guess. Photograph, label, then remove.

  • R = 24 V power; Rc and Rh = cooling and heating power sides; a jumper joins them on one transformer.
  • C = common return; it powers a smart thermostat.
  • W = heat (W2 second stage), Y = cooling (Y2 second stage), G = indoor fan.
  • O/B = heat pump reversing valve; which one applies depends on the brand.
  • E = emergency heat by hand; AUX/X = automatic backup heat; L = fault indicator on some models.
  • Power off, photograph, label every wire by terminal. Check the equipment's own wiring diagram.
