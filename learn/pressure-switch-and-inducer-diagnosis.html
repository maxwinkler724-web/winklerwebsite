Pressure Switch and Inducer Diagnosis

Before a modern furnace lights, it has to prove that exhaust gases can leave the building. The inducer fan creates the draft and the pressure switch proves it. When the switch will not close, the furnace waits, retries, and eventually locks out.

The job of each part

The pressure switch is normally open. When the inducer pulls the heat exchanger into slight negative pressure, the switch closes. The control board checks that the switch is open before the inducer starts, then requires it to close within an allotted time, then wants it to stay closed through the run. Its place in the order of events is shown in Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation.

Why it will not close

The sources agree that the switch itself is rarely the first culprit. Causes they list:

  • Inducer problems: weak motor, wrong voltage, seized or noisy bearings, a dirty wheel.
  • Venting blockage: debris, nests, or a blocked termination.
  • Tubing problems: brittle or cracked hose, a hose that is pinched, clogged, or disconnected, or water trapped in the hose or switch.
  • Condensate trouble on 90 percent furnaces: a clogged trap or drain that backs up and starves the draft, sometimes clearing when the furnace is off.
  • Wind or an unusual venting condition.
  • Wiring and the switch: loose connections or a failed diaphragm. A flapping noise can mean a busted diaphragm.

A good order of checks

  1. Slow down and read the code. Some boards separate "switch did not close" from "switch did not open," and those point in opposite directions. (HVAC School cites one maker's codes for each.)
  2. Check voltage at the furnace against the data tag.
  3. Check the inducer amp draw against its rating, and listen and feel for rising amps, heat or noise.
  4. Inspect and clear the tubing and the port at the heat exchanger. Remove the hose and drain any water.
  5. Measure the draft with a manometer in inches of water column and compare it to the switch's closing pressure, which is a negative value printed on the switch or in the manual.
  6. Check the vent and, on condensing units, the trap, drain and any condensate pump.
  7. Only then consider the board wiring or the switch itself.

HVAC School warns against replacing the switch without finding the root cause and against stopping once a reset makes the furnace run. Both lead to callbacks.

A switch that is closed too early

If the switch is closed before the inducer starts, the board should fault. That means a jumper, a short, or a switch stuck closed. Never jumper a pressure switch to make a furnace run. It exists so that exhaust is not released into the home when a vent is blocked, and a blocked vent may mean carbon monoxide. See Combustion and CO Safety Basics.

Safety

Power off and lock out before disconnecting wires. Shut off gas and power before replacing a switch. Run a complete heating cycle afterward, and confirm no new exhaust smell or draft issues.

Field card: Pressure Switch and Inducer Diagnosis

Prove the draft before you blame the switch.

  • The switch is normally open and closes when the inducer makes draft.
  • Check inducer voltage and amps, tubing, vent and condensate path first.
  • Measure draft in inches W.C. against the switch's closing value.
  • Closed before the inducer starts means a jumper, short, or failed switch.
  • Never jump a pressure switch; find the cause of the blockage.
