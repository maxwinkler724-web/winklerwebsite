Commercial Cooling Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Large buildings are cooled by plants, not by a single box on the wall. A chiller makes cold water, a cooling tower or air-cooled condenser rejects the heat, pumps and controls move and manage it, and rooftop units serve smaller zones. This guide collects the lessons for technicians who are moving from residential work into commercial and plant equipment.

Begin with how a chiller plant fits together and how air-cooled and water-cooled machines differ. Then study the daily work: what to record on rounds, how to read a chiller log, what approach temperature tells you, and how startup and shutdown sequences protect the machine. The tower and condenser-water lessons cover fans, fill, drift, basin checks, staging and water treatment.

Cooling towers also carry a water-safety responsibility. Legionella bacteria can grow in poorly managed systems, and New Jersey now has a law that requires water management programs for buildings with cooling towers. Read the water-management lessons before you work on a tower. As always, manufacturer instructions and your site procedures take priority over anything general on this site.
