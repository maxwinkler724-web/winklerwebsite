Preventive Maintenance Schedule for a Commercial Gas Boiler, Daily to Annual

A boiler room runs on small, repeated checks. Here is how published sources spread those checks across the day, week, month, half-year and year, so you can see the pattern and build your own sheet.

How to read the intervals below

Every interval here is a labeled example from a named source, not a rule. Three public documents agree on most of the pattern: the National Board's suggested maintenance log program, a sample maintenance plan manual from the City of Milwaukee boiler inspection office, and an insurer's control and safety device guideline (Travelers/BoilerRe). The US Department of Energy's federal O&M guide covers the efficiency side. Your boiler's manual, your insurer and your jurisdiction decide what you actually must do. Source strength for the schedule as a whole: settled, because several independent documents line up. The timing of one item, the safety valve test, is where they disagree, covered below.

Before any hands-on task, apply lockout/tagout and verify dead, and remember that a gas train and a combustion chamber are never a place to improvise. See Lockout/Tagout and Verify Dead for HVAC.

Daily

  • Drain the water gauge glass and watch the level return.
  • Blow down the low-water cutoff and confirm it works, then check the burner flame and look around for leaks. The National Board and Milwaukee examples place these on each shift for attended high-pressure steam.
  • Record stack temperature, water test results and deaerator condition in the log (DOE guide example).

Weekly

  • Record the pilot and main flame signal readings, so you can see weak sensors drifting before they cause lockouts.
  • Check flame-failure cutoff timing and confirm the fuel shutoff valves close.
  • Check the water treatment tank level.

Monthly

  • Test the fan and combustion air interlocks, the low-fire start interlock, and the high and low gas pressure interlocks.
  • Leak-check the main gas shutoff valves.
  • Review water treatment results and grease bearings that call for it.
  • Test the low-water cutoff on hot water boilers.
  • Lift the safety valve by hand, where your sources and manufacturer allow it.

Semiannual and annual

The National Board and Milwaukee examples put these at roughly six months: a slow-drain test of the steam low-water cutoff, gauge recalibration, inspection of the burner, flame-failure parts and interlock wiring, and a check of the combustion control. At the annual outage they list fireside and waterside cleaning, reconditioning or replacing the low-water cutoff, checking the gas drip leg and strainer, a pilot turndown test, new gaskets, leak testing of fuel valves, a test of all controls and safeties, a combustion test and tune, and the inspection by a commissioned inspector.

The DOE guide separately says to run a combustion analysis and tune at least yearly, and twice a year for heavy use. For steam traps it gives example cycles by pressure class: about every three months for process, every six months for high and medium pressure, and twice per heating season for building heat, with the whole program checked at least yearly. See Steam Trap Testing Methods.

The conflict: when to test the safety valve

The sources do not match. The Travelers/BoilerRe guideline lists a safety valve test every three months on low-pressure steam, and every six months on unattended high-pressure steam. The National Board and Milwaukee examples instead describe a monthly hand lift with a full test to ASME procedures at the semiannual step. These can both be sensible in their own setting, but they are not the same schedule. The right answer is per your insurer and jurisdiction. Ask them, write the answer on your sheet, and never test a valve in a way the manufacturer or inspector forbids. Background is in Boiler Safety Valves and Overpressure Protection.

Other example intervals from the insurer guideline

  • Low-pressure hot water: manual-reset low-water cutoff test monthly; relief valve and high-limit test yearly.
  • Hot water supply and storage heaters: test the temperature and pressure relief valve about every two months and replace it about every three years.
  • Low-pressure steam: low-water cutoff test daily, overhaul yearly.

What is not settled

The maker manual we reviewed for a common commercial boiler line names the same topics (cutoffs, flame safeguard, gauges, tubes, burner) but gave no numbers in the parts we could read, and tells owners to keep a daily, weekly, monthly and yearly log. State inspection cycles and posting rules differ by place; for New Jersey owners start with NJ Boiler Inspections, Certificates, Logbooks and Posting. Nothing here is a claim of compliance with any code or standard.

Building your own sheet

Use one column per interval, plus a blank column headed "manual interval" so the maker's number overrides ours. Add boxes for initials, date and the reading. Add a condition-trigger line too, such as a drifting flame signal or stack temperature creeping up, because a trend can call for service sooner than the calendar does. Related habits are in Boiler Operator Rounds, Logs and Test Schedules.

Field card: Gas Boiler PM Schedule

Small checks, often, written down. The log shows the trend.

  • Daily: gauge glass, low-water cutoff blowdown, flame, leaks, stack temperature, water results.
  • Weekly: flame signal readings, flame-failure timing, fuel shutoff valves, treatment tank.
  • Monthly: air and gas pressure interlocks, main gas valve leak check, hot water cutoff test.
  • Six months and yearly: cutoff slow-drain or overhaul, gauges, combustion test and tune, full safeties test, inspector.
  • Safety valve timing: per your insurer and jurisdiction. Sources differ.
