A2L Service Habits: Ignition, Ventilation and Tools

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the A2L Refrigerants Guide

A2L refrigerants are hard to ignite, which is why they are allowed in new equipment. Hard is not impossible. A handful of habits keeps the margin on your side.

Survey the job first

AHRI's A2L best-practices sheet starts with the basics: look over the jobsite for ignition sources and flammable vapors, and make sure the area is well ventilated before you start. Trade guides add concrete examples. HVAC School cites a university study in which hot wires, matches, lighters and candles ignited A2L leaks while lit cigarettes, hot plates, static sparks and a hair dryer did not. That is one study, so treat it as a reason to be careful with anything glowing or flaming, not as a list of safe items.

Moving air matters

ServiceMag notes that leaked A2L vapor is heavier than air and tends to collect low, and the R-32 safety data sheet from Daikin says the same for R-32 (relative vapor density about 1.86, air = 1), so leaks pool low, so sweep a detector near the floor of closets, air handler bottoms, basements and crawlspaces, and move air before hot work. Open attics and mechanical rooms usually clear more easily, but the check is still cheap. Confirm the equipment is approved for the space it sits in by reading the maker's literature.

Tools made for the job

  • AHRI says to use recovery tools rated for A2L refrigerants and to recover into a DOT-approved cylinder without exceeding its fill weight.
  • HVAC School also says vacuum pumps and recovery gear should be rated for flammable refrigerants.
  • Look at the label on the machine. An older tool is not automatically out, but the maker decides, not habit.
  • Use a proper capacitor discharge tool, not a screwdriver, and make sure the equipment is grounded, as HVAC School advises.
  • Use a leak detector suited to the refrigerant, and never a flame.

Cylinders and labels

Cylinder colors and valve threads have changed for newer refrigerants. Sources describe left-hand valve threads and resettable relief devices on A2L cylinders, but color descriptions differ between articles, so read the label and safety data sheet every time and treat an unmarked cylinder as unknown.

Never mix, never vent

AHRI's sheet is blunt: do not vent, and do not mix refrigerants. HVAC School adds that A2L refrigerants should not be used to retrofit equipment built for non-flammable refrigerants. Safety features in A2L equipment, such as the detection system, are part of why the charge is acceptable.

Brazing and nitrogen

AHRI says to use nitrogen before and during all brazing. More on flow in Nitrogen Purge Flow While Brazing. Recover the full charge before you open any joint, and confirm with a leak check that no refrigerant remains.

Order of authority

The rule that overrides this page: the instructions for the exact unit in front of you, your employer's procedures and the codes where you work always govern. This lesson explains concepts only.

Field card: A2L Service Habits: Ignition, Ventilation and Tools

Control ignition, move air, use rated tools, never mix.

  • Survey for ignition sources and flammable vapors before you start.
  • Vapor can settle low: check low spots and move air.
  • Use recovery and vacuum gear the maker rates for A2L.
  • Read the cylinder label every time; colors alone are not enough.
  • No venting, no mixing, no flame to find leaks.
