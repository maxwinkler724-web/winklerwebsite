Rooftop Unit Basics

A rooftop unit, or RTU, is a complete heating and cooling system in one box that sits on the roof. It is one of the most common pieces of equipment a new commercial tech will meet.

What an RTU is

An RTU packages the compressor, the cooling coil, the outdoor coil and fans, the indoor blower, filters and a heat source (often gas, sometimes electric or a heat pump) into a single cabinet. Putting it on the roof saves floor space inside and keeps most of the noise and service mess outdoors. Air travels to and from the building through ducts that connect to the unit at the roof curb.

The main parts

  • Compressor and refrigerant circuit. The compressor moves refrigerant. The evaporator pulls heat out of the building air, and the condenser sheds that heat outdoors.
  • Fans. A supply blower moves air through the building. Condenser fans move outdoor air across the outdoor coil.
  • Filters. They protect the coil and blower. Published training material suggests checking or changing them on a schedule somewhere between monthly and every few months, depending on how hard the unit runs and how dirty the area is.
  • Heat section. Many units include gas or electric heat for cold weather. Gas heat adds its own safety concerns, covered in our gas heat lessons.
  • Controls. A control board or thermostat input decides when to run the fan, cooling stages and heat. Many units also have an economizer, covered in Economizers Basics.

How the air moves

Return air from the building is pulled back to the unit. It may mix with some outdoor air for ventilation, passes through the filters and the coil, and is pushed back out as supply air. Knowing this path matters because most airflow problems are really filter, coil, belt or damper problems, not refrigerant problems.

Routine checks, in general terms

  • Look the unit over for loose panels, damaged insulation, oil stains and debris.
  • Check and replace filters.
  • Inspect electrical connections for heat damage or looseness, with power safely locked out first.
  • Check belts, motor mounts and blower wheel condition on belt-drive units.
  • Keep the condensate drain clear.
  • Keep condenser coils clean and fins straight.
  • Record what you find. A service log shows trends that a single visit cannot.

Roof and electrical safety

Roof work adds fall hazards on top of the usual equipment hazards. Know the edge protection rules for your site. Before opening a panel, lock out and tag out the disconnect and verify the circuit is dead with a meter. Stored energy in capacitors and rotating fans can still hurt you. Never defeat a safety switch or door interlock to keep a unit running.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: the manufacturer's instructions for the exact unit, and the rules at your site, always govern. This lesson explains concepts only and gives no setpoints or wiring steps.

Field card: Rooftop Unit Basics

The whole system in one box, on the roof.

  • An RTU packages compressor, coils, fans, filters, heat and controls together.
  • Most complaints trace to airflow: filters, coils, belts, dampers.
  • Check filters and coils first on any service call.
  • Lock out and tag out before opening panels, and respect roof edges.
  • Log what you find so trends show up.
