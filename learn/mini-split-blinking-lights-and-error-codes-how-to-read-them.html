Mini-Split Blinking Lights and Error Codes: How to Read Them

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A blinking light on a mini-split is the unit telling you what it found. The trick is that the same blink count can mean different things on different brands, and even on different models from one brand. This lesson shows where units report faults, how three makers describe it in their own manuals, and which findings are yours to handle and which belong to a technician.

Models differ: read your own manual

Models differ. Every example below comes from one named manual for one model family. Do not carry a code from one brand to another, or assume your model matches. Find the model name on the indoor unit label and use that maker's table. Fujitsu General's own support page for older systems says it plainly: the same LED count or code can mean a different fault on another model.

Three places a unit reports a fault

  • Indoor unit lamps. A lamp on the indoor unit blinks a counted pattern. Count the blinks between pauses, and note which lamp it is. Fujitsu General says to count and record each flash sequence, including lamp color and timing where shown.
  • Remote control self-diagnosis. Many models can show a stored or live code through a button sequence on the remote or a wired controller. The sequence differs by maker, as the examples below show.
  • Outdoor board LEDs. Some faults are only shown on the outdoor control board. The Mitsubishi MSZ-FH indoor service manual says an outdoor fault can take up to 1 minute to show on the indoor lamp, and for a lamp that stays lit it points you to the outdoor board LED. That is technician territory, since it means opening the outdoor unit.

Mitsubishi Electric: two different ways to ask

Two Mitsubishi service manuals show how much the method varies. The Mr. Slim PKA series indoor service manual (OCH758) describes self-diagnosis on a wireless controller (PAR-SL97A-E) by pressing the CHECK button twice. On a wired PAR-40MAA controller it goes through the menu: Service, Check, Diagnosis, Self check. It also says that after two start beeps, if nothing else beeps and the operation lamp stays off, there are no error records. Its self-diagnosis action table lists remote controller transmission error (E3) and signal receiving error (E5), and says codes starting with F, U or E (other than E0 to E7) are outdoor codes covered in the outdoor manual. For any other code, use the table for your exact model.

The MSZ-FH wall unit (service manual OBH683) works differently. It has a "failure mode recall" function: hold the remote's OPERATION SELECT and TEMP buttons, press RESET, release RESET first, keep holding about 3 more seconds until all the display indicators appear, then point the remote at the indoor unit and press STOP/OPERATE. A short beep confirms it. Then the left OPERATION INDICATOR lamp reports the stored fault by blink count. Same maker, different procedure.

Fujitsu: two lamps, counted together

Fujitsu General's FAQ says to count the blinks of the operation and timer lamps and give that pattern, with the model name, to service. It also warns that lamps blink without a fault in two cases: alternating blinks when power was cut during operation, and simultaneous blinks during a test run. The ASU9RL2 service manual describes the lamps as OPERATION (green), TIMER (orange) and ECONOMY (green), with rapid flashes at 0.1 s on and 0.1 s off and normal flashes at 0.5 s on and 0.5 s off. In its error table, 1 operation flash with 1 timer flash is a serial communication error, and 4 with 1 is a room temperature sensor error. That table is for that model family only.

Daikin: codes on the remote display

Daikin's split service manuals (the FTXP20-35M and the Sensira FTXF series) describe showing the code on the remote: hold a remote button for about 5 seconds until an icon in the display blinks, then press the button repeatedly until a continuous beep sounds and the code appears. The manuals show the button by symbol, so use the diagram in your own manual. Two entries from the FTXP20-35M manual: U4-00 is an indoor/outdoor unit communication problem, and A5-00 is high pressure peak cut or freeze protection, which applies in cooling, stops the unit, and resets automatically when the indoor heat exchanger temperature returns to range. The same manual lists U0-00 as shortage of refrigerant.

Worked example: decoding a blink count step by step

This is a hypothetical call, using the MSZ-FH (OBH683) table so every step has a source. A customer says the left lamp blinks and the unit will not run.

  1. Watch two full cycles. The manual says to check at least 2 consecutive blinking cycles. The lamp blinks a number of times, then goes dark for a 2.5 second pause, then repeats.
  2. Count between pauses. Say you count 3 blinks, twice in a row.
  3. Decide which table applies. The manual has two tables with different meanings. If you got the pattern by entering recall mode (stored faults), use the recall table. If the unit is blinking by itself on a live fault, use the troubleshooting check table.
  4. Look up the count. In recall mode, 3 blinks is serial signal (communication). In the live check table, 3 blinks is the indoor fan motor. Same count, two answers.
  5. Act on the finding. A communication fault points at wiring, connectors or boards between the units. A fan motor fault points at the indoor blower. Neither is an owner repair.

The manual says the two tables' patterns differ from each other, which is exactly why step 3 comes before step 4.

What an owner can do

  • Note the pattern and the model. Write down the lamp, the count and the model name. Fujitsu General asks for exactly this when you request service.
  • Stop and remove power if something is wrong. The Fujitsu ASBA24JFC operating manual says that for a malfunction such as a burning smell, stop operation, turn off the breaker or unplug the unit, and consult authorized service. It also says the unit's own power switch does not fully disconnect power.
  • Check the basics. The same manual's symptom table asks owners to check first whether the plug is out, there was a power failure, or a breaker or fuse tripped. Your own manual may describe a restart procedure; follow its wording instead of a generic one.
  • Airflow and ice. Daikin's A5 entry is a freeze protection stop in cooling, a reminder that restricted airflow is worth ruling out. Clean filters on the schedule in your manual, see Mini-Split Cleaning and Maintenance Habits, and keep the outdoor unit clear.

What needs a technician

  • Communication faults such as Daikin U4-00 or the Fujitsu serial communication pattern. They involve inter-unit wiring and control boards.
  • Sensor faults, such as the room temperature or coil thermistor patterns in the tables above.
  • Refrigerant-related faults, such as Daikin U0-00 shortage of refrigerant. Refrigerant work requires EPA 608 certification.
  • Anything shown only on the outdoor board or needing covers removed. Boards and capacitors can hold a charge.

For the technician's order of checks, see Mini-Split Troubleshooting Checks; for how the system works, Mini-Split Basics.

Field card: Mini-Split Blinking Lights and Error Codes

Count first, then find the right table.

  • Faults show on indoor lamps, through remote or controller self-diagnosis, or on outdoor board LEDs.
  • Record lamp, count, timing and model name before anything else.
  • Watch at least two full blink cycles.
  • The same count can mean different faults on different models, or in different modes of the same unit.
  • Use the maker's table for the exact model.
  • Owners: stop, remove power if a malfunction is serious, check basics, call service.
  • Communication, sensor and refrigerant faults are technician work.
