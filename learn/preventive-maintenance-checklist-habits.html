Preventive Maintenance Checklist Habits

Good plants rarely fail without warning. The warning usually sits in a log, if someone writes the numbers down and then looks at them.

Three kinds of maintenance

Corrective maintenance happens after something breaks, and is often the most expensive because a failed part can hurt others and downtime costs money. Preventive maintenance is scheduled inspection and service so small problems are fixed before they grow. Predictive or condition-based maintenance uses measurements and history to decide when work is needed. Real plants use all three.

A rounds log in practice

A rounds log is a sheet or app where an operator records readings at each visit: temperatures, pressures, levels, amps, runtime hours and notes like noise or leaks. A log is only useful if the readings are taken the same way, at the same spots, with the same gauges. It is the record of what normal looks like for that plant.

What to write down

  • Date, time, your name and the unit.
  • Pressures and temperatures: supply and return water, steam pressure, discharge and suction.
  • Motor amps and any change in sound or vibration.
  • Water levels, make-up water meter, and blowdown or treatment readings.
  • Anything odd, even if it seems small: a drip, a smell, a warm bearing.

Watching trends

One reading says little. A series of readings tells a story. Steady drift in the same direction, such as supply temperature creeping up, a pump drawing more amps each week or make-up water use rising, is a sign something is changing. Compare with the same time yesterday, last week and last season. Sudden jumps matter too. See Chilled Water Pumps and Delta-T and Boiler Water Treatment and Blowdown Basics for numbers worth trending.

Checklist habits that stick

  • Keep checklists short enough to finish every time.
  • Use the same route and order so nothing is skipped.
  • Write the actual number, not just a check mark.
  • Note the unit's own normal range once and keep it on the sheet.
  • Report and tag a problem when you find it, and write down what was done.
  • Review old logs on a schedule, not only after a failure.

Routine items that pay off

Filters, belts, coils, strainers, drain lines, steam traps, and gauge and sensor checks are classic items. Which ones apply, and how often, comes from the equipment maker and your site's maintenance plan.

Safety

A walk-around is not permission to open or reset things casually. Lock out and tag out before opening a panel or touching a rotating part, and never bypass a safety device to keep something running. If a reading is outside the limits, report it by the plant's procedure rather than adjusting controls to make the log look right.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: the manufacturer's instructions for the equipment in front of you and the rules where you work always govern. This lesson explains concepts. It gives no setpoints, and your site's numbers are the ones that count.

Field card: Preventive Maintenance Checklist Habits

Write it down, then look at it.

  • Corrective fixes a failure; preventive schedules service; predictive uses data.
  • Take readings the same way every time and record real numbers.
  • Trends and drift catch problems earlier than single readings.
  • Keep the checklist short, in a fixed order, with normal ranges listed.
  • Report abnormal readings; never adjust to make a log look good.
