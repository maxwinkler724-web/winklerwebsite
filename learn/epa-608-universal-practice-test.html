EPA 608 Universal Practice Test: All 4 Sections

Last updated: 2026-10-16 · 40 questions

Universal certification means passing all four sections: Core, Type I, Type II and Type III.

The real exam is a multiple-choice test given by an EPA-approved certifying organization. Question counts, passing scores and open- or closed-book rules differ by organization, so confirm them with the organization you plan to test with and on the EPA Section 608 page (checked 2026-10-16).

How to use this test. Read each question, choose your answer, then open “Show answer” to see why and where it comes from. These are original practice questions, not real exam questions. Every explanation links to the source it is based on.

This page samples ten questions from each section. For more, take the full section tests below.

1. Which part of the atmosphere contains the ozone layer?

  1. Troposphere
  2. Stratosphere
  3. Mesosphere
  4. Ionosphere
Show answer

Answer: B. Stratosphere

Ozone sits in the stratosphere and filters UV-B.

Source: EPA basic ozone layer science

2. What makes CFCs harmful to ozone?

  1. Hydrogen
  2. Chlorine
  3. Fluorine
  4. Carbon
Show answer

Answer: B. Chlorine

Chlorine atoms freed by UV act as a catalyst that destroys many ozone molecules.

Source: EPA basic ozone layer science

3. Which has ODP of zero?

  1. R-12
  2. R-22
  3. R-134a
  4. R-11
Show answer

Answer: C. R-134a

HFCs have no chlorine. They still have GWP.

Source: EPA ozone-depleting substances page

4. Removing refrigerant from a system into an external container is called:

  1. Recycling
  2. Reclaiming
  3. Recovering
  4. Charging
Show answer

Answer: C. Recovering

Recovery moves refrigerant out of a system into an external container, without processing it.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

5. Processing refrigerant to new-product purity at a certified facility is:

  1. Recovery
  2. Recycling
  3. Reclaim
  4. Evacuation
Show answer

Answer: C. Reclaim

Reclaim is verified by lab analysis.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

6. Recovery cylinders must never be filled beyond what percent of capacity?

  1. 60
  2. 70
  3. 80
  4. 90
Show answer

Answer: C. 80

Liquid expands with heat; overfilling risks rupture.

Background source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

7. A recovery cylinder is colored:

  1. All yellow
  2. Gray body, yellow top
  3. Green
  4. Blue with white top
Show answer

Answer: B. Gray body, yellow top

Gray body with a yellow top is the standard color code identifying a recovery cylinder.

Background source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

8. A technician finds a disposable cylinder nearly empty. What is the rule?

  1. Refill it from recovery
  2. Never refill it
  3. Refill if inspected
  4. Refill only with R-22
Show answer

Answer: B. Never refill it

One-way cylinders are not to be refilled.

Source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

9. Which gas is appropriate for pressure-testing a system?

  1. Oxygen
  2. Compressed air
  3. Dry nitrogen with regulator
  4. Acetylene
Show answer

Answer: C. Dry nitrogen with regulator

Oxygen + oil can explode; air carries moisture and oxygen.

Source: Aircon Spares, Nitrogen pressure testing

10. Refrigerant vapor is heavier than air. The main hazard in a small basement mechanical room is:

  1. Fire only
  2. Asphyxiation
  3. Noise
  4. Corrosion of ducts
Show answer

Answer: B. Asphyxiation

Vapor pools low and displaces oxygen.

Source: R-134a Safety Data Sheet (National Refrigerants)

11. Type I covers appliances with how much refrigerant or less?

  1. 2 lb
  2. 5 lb
  3. 10 lb
  4. 50 lb
Show answer

Answer: B. 5 lb

Factory-sealed, self-contained.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

12. Which is a Type I appliance?

  1. Rooftop unit
  2. Window AC
  3. Centrifugal chiller
  4. Split system
Show answer

Answer: B. Window AC

A window air conditioner is a self-contained, factory-sealed unit, so it falls under Type I.

Background source: EPA608practicetest.net Type 1 study guide (secondary)

13. Recovery requirement with the compressor operating (equipment made after Nov 1993):

  1. 80%
  2. 90%
  3. 99%
  4. 50%
Show answer

Answer: B. 90%

90% if the compressor is operating; 80% if it is not.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(b)

14. When the appliance's compressor does not run, recovery target is:

  1. 90%
  2. 80%
  3. 60%
  4. 100%
Show answer

Answer: B. 80%

When the compressor is not running, recovery must reach 80 percent of the charge.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(b)

15. A passive/system-dependent recovery device depends on:

  1. Its own compressor
  2. The appliance's compressor
  3. Water pressure
  4. A vacuum pump only
Show answer

Answer: B. The appliance's compressor

A passive recovery device relies on the appliance's own compressor to move refrigerant.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

16. After using a piercing valve on a system to be put back in service, you should:

  1. Leave it
  2. Install a permanent access valve and leak check
  3. Add more refrigerant
  4. Wrap with tape
Show answer

Answer: B. Install a permanent access valve and leak check

A piercing valve is temporary, so it must be replaced with a permanent access valve, then leak checked.

Background source: ACHR News, Temporary Benefits of Saddle Valves

17. Before scrapping a refrigerator you find the refrigerant has not been recovered. You:

  1. Cut the line
  2. Recover it first and document
  3. Let it drain on the dock
  4. Weld it
Show answer

Answer: B. Recover it first and document

Refrigerant must be recovered before scrapping, and the recovery should be documented.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.155

18. A window AC with a dead compressor: heating the compressor helps because:

  1. It lowers pressure
  2. It drives refrigerant out of the oil
  3. It cleans the system
  4. It starts the pump
Show answer

Answer: B. It drives refrigerant out of the oil

Warmth boils off refrigerant dissolved in oil.

Background source: EPA608practicetest.net Type 1 study guide (secondary)

19. A self-contained recovery machine:

  1. Uses appliance's compressor
  2. Has its own compressor
  3. Uses nitrogen
  4. Needs a vacuum pump
Show answer

Answer: B. Has its own compressor

A self-contained recovery machine has its own compressor, so it does not depend on the appliance.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

20. Dehumidifier with 1.5 lb of R-410A is:

  1. Type I
  2. Type II
  3. Type III
  4. Exempt
Show answer

Answer: A. Type I

Small appliances holding 5 pounds or less of refrigerant are Type I, so a 1.5-pound dehumidifier qualifies.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

21. Which refrigerant is high-pressure?

  1. R-11
  2. R-123
  3. R-22
  4. R-113
Show answer

Answer: C. R-22

R-22 is a high-pressure refrigerant, while R-11, R-113 and R-123 are low-pressure.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (pressure definitions)

22. Fastest way to recover from a large system is usually:

  1. Vapor only
  2. Liquid recovery (push-pull on large systems)
  3. Wait
  4. Nitrogen purge
Show answer

Answer: B. Liquid recovery (push-pull on large systems)

Liquid-phase recovery moves refrigerant much faster than vapor recovery on large systems.

Background source: Contracting Business, Effective Refrigerant Recovery Techniques

23. R-407C should be charged as:

  1. Vapor
  2. Liquid
  3. Either
  4. Neither
Show answer

Answer: B. Liquid

R-407C is a zeotropic blend, so it must be charged as liquid to keep its composition.

Source: Arkema Forane R407C spec sheet

24. Oil used with HFC systems is typically:

  1. Mineral
  2. POE
  3. Water
  4. Alkylbenzene only
Show answer

Answer: B. POE

Check nameplate.

Background source: Arkema Forane R407C spec sheet (POE required with R-407C)

25. A burned-out compressor will likely cause:

  1. Acidic oil
  2. Lower pressure only
  3. Nothing
  4. Quiet operation
Show answer

Answer: A. Acidic oil

Test oil, replace driers, flush if required.

Background source: ACHR News, Performing a clean-up after a burnout

26. You must pressure-test with:

  1. CO2 only
  2. Dry nitrogen via regulator
  3. Air
  4. Oxygen
Show answer

Answer: B. Dry nitrogen via regulator

Dry nitrogen through a regulator is the safe, standard medium for pressure testing.

Source: AirConSpares, Nitrogen pressure testing HVAC systems

27. Before opening a section of a split system:

  1. Recover to the required level
  2. Vent
  3. Start the compressor
  4. Cap the line only
Show answer

Answer: A. Recover to the required level

Refrigerant must be recovered to the required level before opening any section of the system.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(a)

28. A rack system leak check method NOT appropriate:

  1. Electronic detector
  2. Soap bubbles
  3. Open flame leak check near the rack
  4. Ultrasonic
Show answer

Answer: C. Open flame leak check near the rack

Open flame leak checks are unsafe near refrigerants, which can decompose into toxic gases.

Source: Dwyer-Omega, Halogen leak detection (halide torch/phosgene)

29. Rule on leak repair:

  1. Never required
  2. Required for covered appliances over thresholds, with verification tests
  3. Only for CFCs
  4. Only for residential
Show answer

Answer: B. Required for covered appliances over thresholds, with verification tests

Thresholds are 20% commercial refrigeration, 30% industrial process, 10% comfort cooling (40 CFR 82.157 for CFC/HCFC appliances of 50 lb or more). HFC appliances of 15 lb or more follow the AIM Act rule, 40 CFR Part 84, since Jan 1, 2026; confirm details on EPA and eCFR.

Source: 40 CFR 82.157(a),(c),(d),(e) and 40 CFR 84.106

30. Evacuation prior to charge removes:

  1. Oil
  2. Moisture and air
  3. Refrigerant
  4. Dirt
Show answer

Answer: B. Moisture and air

Evacuation removes air and moisture, which would otherwise cause corrosion and non-condensable buildup.

Source: AirConSpares, How to evacuate an AC system

31. Which is a low-pressure refrigerant?

  1. R-22
  2. R-123
  3. R-134a
  4. R-410A
Show answer

Answer: B. R-123

R-123 is a low-pressure refrigerant, unlike R-22, R-134a and R-410A.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 low-pressure appliance definition (names R-11, R-123, R-113, R-245fa)

32. Why do low-pressure chillers need a purge unit?

  1. To add oil
  2. To remove air and non-condensables that leak in
  3. To raise pressure
  4. For leak testing
Show answer

Answer: B. To remove air and non-condensables that leak in

Low-pressure chillers operate below atmosphere, so air and moisture leak in and must be purged.

Source: ESCO 608 Type III manual p.18 (purge unit removes non-condensables)

33. Leaks in a low-pressure chiller typically:

  1. Push refrigerant out only
  2. Draw air and moisture in
  3. Do nothing
  4. Raise pressure
Show answer

Answer: B. Draw air and moisture in

Because operation is below atmospheric pressure.

Source: ESCO 608 Type III manual p.18 (leaks let air in)

34. The rupture disc relieves at about:

  1. 5 psig
  2. 15 psig
  3. 50 psig
  4. 150 psig
Show answer

Answer: B. 15 psig

Low-pressure chiller rupture discs are typically rated to relieve around 15 psig.

Source: Trane CDHF CenTraVac IOM (rupture disk breaks above 15 psig)

35. Recovery goal for low-pressure systems is:

  1. 0 psig
  2. A deep vacuum, 25 mm Hg absolute
  3. 10 in Hg
  4. 50% of charge
Show answer

Answer: B. A deep vacuum, 25 mm Hg absolute

Per 40 CFR 82.156 (low-pressure: 25 mm Hg absolute).

Source: 40 CFR 82.156 Table 1 / 82.158 Table 2 (low-pressure 25 mm Hg absolute)

36. Before opening a low-pressure chiller to service tubes:

  1. Evacuate to deep vacuum
  2. Bring to atmospheric pressure and remove refrigerant first
  3. Run without refrigerant
  4. Heat with torch
Show answer

Answer: B. Bring to atmospheric pressure and remove refrigerant first

The vessel must reach atmospheric pressure and have refrigerant removed before opening it.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(a)(1)(ii) (pressurize to no higher than 0 psig before opening)

37. When raising pressure for tube work, you should:

  1. Use compressed air
  2. Use controlled warm water or heating blankets, staying below the rupture disc rating (not nitrogen)
  3. Open a valve
  4. Use a torch
Show answer

Answer: B. Use controlled warm water or heating blankets, staying below the rupture disc rating (not nitrogen)

For low-pressure refrigerants, EPA rules bar pressurizing with methods such as nitrogen that need purging afterward. Use controlled heat (warm water or heating blankets) and stay under the rupture disc rating.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.156(a)(1)(ii); leak-test guide (heat preferred)

38. Water in the refrigerant side of a chiller can cause:

  1. Lower power
  2. Freezing and corrosion
  3. Cooler water
  4. Nothing
Show answer

Answer: B. Freezing and corrosion

Water in the refrigerant side causes freezing and corrosion, damaging tubes and components.

Background source: ESCO 608 Type III manual p.18 (trapped water can freeze during evacuation)

39. R-11 and R-123 chillers are typically:

  1. Reciprocating
  2. Centrifugal
  3. Rotary screw only
  4. Scroll
Show answer

Answer: B. Centrifugal

R-11 and R-123 are used in low-pressure centrifugal chillers, not reciprocating or scroll units.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.152 + ESCO p.18 (R-11/R-123 low-pressure, purge on centrifugal chillers)

40. Recovery from a low-pressure chiller should be done with:

  1. Any vapor pump
  2. Certified recovery equipment rated for low pressure
  3. A vacuum cleaner
  4. Nitrogen only
Show answer

Answer: B. Certified recovery equipment rated for low pressure

Low-pressure chillers need recovery equipment rated for low pressure to avoid damage or leaks.

Source: 40 CFR 82.158 (certified equipment; Table 2 low-pressure level)
