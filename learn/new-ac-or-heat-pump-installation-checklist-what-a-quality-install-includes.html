New AC or Heat Pump Installation Checklist: What a Quality Install Includes

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A new system is only as good as the install. This lesson turns the public summaries of the ACCA quality installation standard, the ENERGY STAR installation program and maker manuals into questions an owner can ask and records an owner can request.

Why ask at all

A 2016 Department of Energy report on residential installation quality says poor installs can waste a large share of a system's output, citing a working-group estimate of 20 to 40 percent. The ENERGY STAR program page puts rough savings on four of them: duct leakage 15 percent, sizing 5, refrigerant charge 4 and airflow 3. Those are program estimates, not promises, but they show where to look.

ANSI/ACCA 5 QI is the national standard behind most of this. ACCA describes it as covering design, equipment installation, distribution, and system documentation with owner education. You need the questions and the paperwork, not the standard itself.

1. Sizing and equipment selection

Ask: "Did you run a load calculation, and can I have a copy?" The QI standard calls for ACCA Manual J (or an equivalent the local authority approves) for the load, and Manual S limits for residential equipment selection. DOE's guide says loads should come from a calculation, not rules of thumb, which is how so many oversized systems got installed. Request: the Manual J summary form and the Manual S selection. See Heat Load Calculation: Intro to Manual J, and AC Short Cycling for what oversizing does.

2. A matched system on paper

Ask: "What is the AHRI reference number for the outdoor unit, indoor coil and air handler or furnace I am buying?" Trane's air handler guide says to install only AHRI approved, matched indoor and outdoor systems, and Goodman's manual says the system must be maker approved and AHRI matched. Carrier's manual ties the factory charge to an approved indoor unit and says to check the AHRI directory for whether a piston or an expansion valve is required. Request: the AHRI directory printout and the model and serial numbers of every piece. Efficiency terms are in What SEER2, HSPF2, AFUE and EER2 Mean.

3. Airflow measured, not assumed

Ask: "What was the measured airflow and static pressure, and what was it supposed to be?" ACCA 5 QI requires measured airflow at the design fan speed to be within 15 percent of design, inside the maker's allowed range, with external static pressure inside the maker's range too. It says airflow must be verified before charging. Ducts have to be designed for that airflow; see Duct Sizing Basics and Airflow and Static Pressure Basics. Request: the numbers and the method used; methods are compared in Measuring Airflow (CFM).

4. Duct leakage tested and sealed

Ask: "Were the ducts tested for leakage after sealing?" The QI standard's limits are 10 percent of total airflow for new construction with ducts inside the thermal envelope, 6 percent if any part is outside, and for existing homes 20 percent or a 50 percent improvement. The test is at 25 Pascals. It names sealing materials listed to UL 181A or 181B. DOE warns against cloth-backed duct tape, which it says can dry out and fail within months. Request: the leakage test result. See Sealing Duct Leaks.

5. Brazing with nitrogen, then a pressure test

Ask: "Do you flow nitrogen through the lines while brazing, and do you pressure test before the vacuum?" The QI standard's informative appendix recommends nitrogen purging while brazing, followed by a nitrogen pressure test. Makers vary: Goodman's manual says to sweep the line with nitrogen or inert gas while brazing, with no flow rate given, while the Carrier manual I read says to wrap service valves in a wet cloth and uses dry nitrogen only to break a vacuum. Both say never to use air or oxygen for leak testing. See Nitrogen Purge Flow While Brazing, Brazing Copper Refrigerant Lines and Nitrogen Pressure Testing.

6. Evacuation to the maker's number

Ask: "What micron level did you reach and hold, using what gauge?" The target is the maker's, and makers differ. In the manuals I read, Carrier says 500 microns by deep vacuum, Trane's air handler guide says 350, and Goodman says 250 or less, then a 10 minute hold that stays at or under 1000. So there is no single national number to quote. Request: the final reading, and the maker's number it was compared against. See Evacuation and Deep Vacuum Basics.

7. Charge by the maker's method

Ask: "How was the charge set and verified, and what was the line set length?" Factory charge usually covers a stated length (15 feet of 3/8 inch liquid line in both the Carrier and Goodman manuals I read), and extra line is added by weight using the maker's figure, 0.6 oz per foot in both. Then the charge is verified, subcooling for expansion valve systems and superheat for fixed orifice, against the maker's target. ACCA 5 QI allows plus or minus 3°F on subcooling and plus or minus 5°F on superheat against the maker's value. Request: the line length, ounces added and final readings. See Charging by Weight, Charging Methods Overview and Superheat and Subcooling Basics.

8. Condensate drain with a trap and a backup

Ask: "Where does the drain end, is it trapped, and what happens if it clogs?" Trane's air handler guide says the primary drain must be trapped outside the unit, drain lines must fall at least 1/4 inch per foot, the unit must be level within 1/8 inch, and a drain pan is required under the whole unit when it sits in or above a finished ceiling or in an unconditioned space. It also says to pour water into the pan to confirm drainage. Request: to watch that test. See Secondary Drain Pans and Float Switches and Water Leaking From AC or Furnace.

9. Electrical from the nameplate

Ask: "Which breaker size and wire did you use, and where is the disconnect?" Both Carrier and Trane point to the unit rating plate for the circuit protection value instead of printing one number for all models; Trane's tables show minimum circuit ampacity and maximum overcurrent protection by model and heater. Goodman says copper conductors only and HACR-type breakers or time-delay fuses, and Carrier says the disconnect must be within sight of and reachable from the unit. In New Jersey a licensed electrician normally handles new circuits; see the permit lesson. Request: a photo of the nameplate and the breaker. See Condenser Disconnects, Fuses and Breakers.

10. Pad, clearances and thermostat

Carrier asks for a solid pad level within about 2 degrees, with 24 inches at the service end, 48 inches above and 6 to 12 inches on the other sides. Goodman's residential clearances are different, 10 to 20 inches by side. Clearances are maker-specific, so ask which manual the installer followed. DOE lists thermostat placement and settings among the common problems; see Where to Put a Thermostat and Thermostat Wiring.

11. Paperwork and owner education

ACCA 5 QI expects an installation file for the owner and a record of model and serial numbers kept by the contractor, plus owner education. Request the permit and inspection sign-off, load calculation, AHRI match, airflow, leakage, charge and vacuum readings, warranty registration and maker manuals. The ENERGY STAR program page says to ask for completed commissioning and verification forms. See Equipment Warranties and Registration, and What to Ask an HVAC Contractor Before Hiring.

Worked example: airflow per ton from a measured CFM

These numbers are made up for illustration, with the method taken from the QI standard. A 3 ton system (3 x 12,000 = 36,000 Btu/h) has a Manual D design airflow of 1,200 CFM. The installer reports 1,050 CFM at the design fan speed.

  1. Airflow per ton, measured: 1,050 ÷ 3 = 350 CFM per ton.
  2. Airflow per ton, design: 1,200 ÷ 3 = 400 CFM per ton.
  3. Allowed band, 15 percent of design: 1,200 x 0.15 = 180 CFM. Low limit 1,200 − 180 = 1,020 CFM. High limit 1,200 + 180 = 1,380 CFM.
  4. Compare: 1,050 is above 1,020, so it passes the 15 percent test, but only just. It is 150 CFM below design, which is 150 ÷ 1,200 = 12.5 percent low.

What to do with that: ask whether the number is inside the maker's allowed CFM range too, and what the static pressure was. For reference, Trane's TEM6 guide lists 350 CFM per ton as its factory default cooling setting, and ACCA lists 350 to 450 CFM per ton only as an informative footnote, not a requirement. The maker's table governs.

Field card: New AC or Heat Pump Install Checklist

Ask for the number, the target and the paper.

  • Load calculation and equipment selection on file.
  • AHRI match printed for every piece.
  • Airflow measured: within 15 percent of design (ACCA 5 QI), inside maker range.
  • Duct leakage tested; limits 6 to 10 percent new, 20 percent existing.
  • Nitrogen when brazing, then a pressure test.
  • Vacuum to the maker's micron target; makers differ (250, 350, 500 seen).
  • Charge by weight for line length, verified by the maker's method.
  • Trapped drain, pan where needed, water test.
  • Breaker and wire from nameplate; disconnect in sight.
  • Permit, warranty registration and a file of readings.
