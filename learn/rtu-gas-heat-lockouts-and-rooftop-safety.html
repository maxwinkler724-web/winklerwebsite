RTU Gas Heat Lockouts and Rooftop Safety

A rooftop that will not heat is half a service call. The other half is getting on the roof and working there without getting hurt.

What a lockout is

A publicly posted Lennox rooftop instruction sheet describes a typical pattern: the unit uses spark ignition with no pilot, makes up to two more ignition tries if the burner does not light, then locks out. It describes a reset by turning the thermostat off and back to heat for a safety shutdown, or by cycling main power on units with electronic temperature controls. It does not state how long the lockout lasts. Other brands differ, so read the unit's own sequence and fault table.

The switches in the heat section

The same sheet lists flame rollout switches, a primary limit and an auxiliary limit, and a combustion air inducer proving switch that checks the inducer before the gas controller can run. They exist to stop heat when flame, temperature or venting is wrong. A trip is a symptom. Find out why airflow was low, why a vent was blocked or why flame rolled, rather than resetting and walking away. Never jumper a switch.

Diagnosing in order

  • Read the control board code first; it usually says which part of the chain failed.
  • Check basics: power, thermostat call, filters, dirty blower wheel or restricted return, gas supply.
  • Look at the flue passages and inducer for obstruction or debris.
  • Check the flame sensor and igniter last of the easy list, following the maker's procedure.

Gas safety on the roof

  • If you smell gas, leave and call from a safe place.
  • If overheating occurs or the gas does not shut off, the Lennox sheet says to close the manual gas valve before turning off electrical power.
  • Turn off power at the disconnect before service, and note some units have more than one supply.
  • Keep combustibles and flammable vapors away from the unit, and label wires before removing them.

Getting on and around the roof

OSHA's general industry fall-protection rule says an open side or edge four feet or more above a lower level needs a guardrail, a safety net or a personal fall protection system, with specific provisions for low-slope roofs. Employers decide the system on site, so ask what yours requires before you climb. Also watch sharp sheet-metal edges, wet or icy surfaces, skylights and unmarked roof openings, and carry tools in a way that leaves hands free for the ladder.

Field card: RTU Gas Heat Lockouts and Rooftop Safety

Reset once, find the cause, and respect the roof edge.

  • Typical sequence: a few ignition tries, then lockout. Check your unit's own sequence.
  • Rollout, limit and inducer switches protect you. Never bypass them.
  • Read the board code, then check airflow, venting, gas and flame.
  • Smell gas: leave. Overheating: close the gas valve first.
  • Know the fall-protection rules and your employer's plan before you go up.
