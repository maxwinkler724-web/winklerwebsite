BACnet, Modbus and LonWorks in Plain English

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Controllers need a shared language to talk to each other. In buildings you will most often hear three names: BACnet, Modbus and LonWorks. You do not need to program them to benefit from knowing what they are.

What a protocol is

A protocol is the agreed set of rules for how devices send and read messages. Two devices can be wired together perfectly and still not understand each other if they use different protocols. That is why jobs often involve a gateway, a box that translates between one language and another.

BACnet

BACnet was developed under ASHRAE for building systems such as HVAC, lighting, access control and fire detection. Sources describe it as an open standard, so devices from different makers can work together. It commonly runs over an RS-485 serial network called MS/TP or over Ethernet and IP. One comparison article points out that BACnet data is self-describing, so a point comes with a name and type, which makes readings easier to interpret. In day-to-day HVAC, BACnet is what you will most often see on newer controllers, VAV boxes, chillers and building front ends.

Modbus

Modbus was published by Modicon in 1979 for programmable logic controllers. It is open, simple and widely supported, and uses a master and follower arrangement over serial (RS-485) or over TCP/IP. A comparison article notes that Modbus values are just numbered registers that you can only interpret with the device's documentation, and an Electrical Contractor article adds that it does not address higher-level functions like scheduling, alarming and trending. Techs meet Modbus on energy meters, VFDs, boilers, chillers and other equipment with a communication port, where it is often used to read data into the BAS.

LonWorks

LonWorks was created by Echelon and is governed by LonMark International. It was designed for control networks of many small devices talking peer to peer, using a Neuron chip, over twisted pair and other media. It was common in older buildings. One comparison article says its ecosystem is shrinking and mainly serves legacy installations, so expect to find it on existing systems rather than new ones.

Which do I need to know?

  • If you will touch building controls, learn the BACnet basics first: device addresses, network numbers and the MS/TP wiring rules in the product manual.
  • If you are reading data from equipment, expect Modbus and a register list from the manufacturer.
  • If you see LonWorks, treat it as a legacy system: find the original documentation and a person who knows the network.
  • If something does not connect, ask whether it is wiring, addressing, a setting such as baud rate, or a real protocol mismatch needing a gateway.

Cautions

Network settings are shared by many devices. A duplicate address or changed baud rate on one device can take down others on the same trunk, so do not change them without a plan and permission. Wiring details, termination and distance limits belong to the product manuals and the network standard. Many dated articles on this topic, including the 2008 magazine piece cited below, describe the market of their time, so check current manufacturer information.

Field card: BACnet, Modbus and LonWorks in Plain English

Same wires do not mean same language.

  • BACnet: open standard from ASHRAE, common on newer building controls.
  • Modbus: simple, open, register-based, common on equipment and meters.
  • LonWorks: peer-to-peer network from Echelon, mostly legacy now.
  • A gateway translates between protocols.
  • Duplicate addresses and changed settings can crash a network. Ask first.
