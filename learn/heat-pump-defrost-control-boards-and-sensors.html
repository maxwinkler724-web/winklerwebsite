Heat Pump Defrost Control Boards and Sensors

Last updated: 2026-10-16

The defrost cycle is covered in another lesson. This one is about the small board and the sensors that decide when to start it, how to end it, and what it flashes at you when something is off.

Three jobs: start, run, end

A defrost control board has to decide when frost has built up enough to hurt performance, reverse the system to melt it, and stop at the right moment. Some boards use a timer plus a temperature check. Others use demand logic that compares temperatures to detect lost performance. Either way the board also keeps the compressor from short-cycling and manages the reversing valve and outdoor fan during the swap.

Thermostat or sensor

Older and simpler designs use a defrost thermostat: a switch that is simply open or closed. One HVAC School example describes a unit's switch that closes near 30 degrees F and opens near 65 degrees F. Because it closes below freezing, an ice bath is not a good way to test it.

Newer boards use a sensor, usually a thermistor whose resistance changes with temperature. You test it by letting it settle to a known temperature, measuring resistance, and comparing to the manufacturer's chart. The same source stresses that a thermistor cannot be tested by jumpering it out the way a thermostat can.

Two sensors: coil and outdoor air

Demand-style boards often use two readings, an outdoor ambient sensor and an outdoor coil sensor, and look at the difference. One manufacturer instruction sheet says its control uses both, requires both, and judges frost by comparing them. A Trane checkout document gives example resistances for its ambient sensor (about 10,300 ohms near 75 degrees F and about 31,900 near 32 degrees F) and says an open, shorted or out-of-range reading points to a bad sensor, while loose or bent pins on the board can cause faults too. Those numbers belong to those products; always use the chart for the part in front of you.

Starting and ending

Examples from published instructions show the idea, not universal rules:

  • One Trane control only starts demand defrost when outdoor temperature is below 49 degrees F, coil temperature is below 35 degrees F and the temperature difference is increasing.
  • The same document says defrost ends on coil temperature (default 47 degrees F in that example), and that defrost ending in under 10 seconds, or ending only on time, suggests a fault such as a sensor problem, wind or a charge issue.
  • An Emerson control lets an installer choose a termination temperature with switches, and has a maximum defrost time of 14 minutes and a short-cycle lockout of 5 minutes at 60 Hz.

Test pins and LED codes

Most boards have a way to force defrost: briefly shorting marked test pins. Use only the pins and method in that board's instructions, and do not leave a test jumper in place; one Trane document warns that a persistent short causes a fault indication. Many boards also flash a status LED. One Emerson sheet uses a flash count for defrost mode, coil sensor fault and ambient sensor fault, and the Trane document uses faster flashing for faults such as low or high temperature difference or repeated time-terminated defrosts. Codes differ by maker, so read the board label or manual.

Habits that save time

  • Before replacing a board, check sensor resistance and wiring.
  • Look at the coil: ice only on part of it can be a charge, airflow or distributor issue, not the board.
  • Check the reversing valve and outdoor fan response when you force defrost.
  • Remember that the board often also drives the reversing valve command, so it ties into reversing valve diagnosis.
  • De-energize before touching sensors, connectors or the board.

Field card: Heat Pump Defrost Control Boards and Sensors

The board listens to sensors. Prove the sensors before blaming the board.

  • Thermostats are switches; sensors are thermistors read with an ohmmeter and a chart.
  • Demand boards compare coil and ambient readings.
  • Defrost that ends instantly or only on time is a clue to sensors, airflow or charge.
  • Force defrost only with the maker's test method and remove the jumper after.
  • LED codes are manufacturer specific. Read the label.
