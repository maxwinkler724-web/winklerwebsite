Chiller Plant Fundamentals

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the Commercial Cooling Guide

A chilled-water plant cools large buildings by moving heat with water. This lesson walks through the equipment, the two water loops, two terms people find confusing (delta-T and approach), and what operators write down on rounds.

The big idea

A chiller does not make cold. It moves heat from one place to another. Warm water comes back from the building, the chiller removes heat from it, and the cooled water goes back out to the building. The removed heat has to go somewhere, so a second water loop carries it to a cooling tower that releases it to the outdoors.

The main equipment

  • Chiller: a machine that uses a refrigerant cycle to move heat. Its main parts are a compressor, an evaporator, a condenser and an expansion device.
  • Evaporator: the heat exchanger where the refrigerant boils and absorbs heat from the building's water. The water gets colder here.
  • Condenser: the heat exchanger where the refrigerant gives up that heat, plus the heat of compression, to a second stream of water and turns back to liquid.
  • Cooling tower: an outdoor device that cools the warm condenser water. A small part of the water evaporates, and evaporation carries heat away.
  • Pumps: one set moves chilled water around the building, and another set moves condenser water between chiller and tower.

Two water loops

Chilled-water loop

Cold water leaves the evaporator, travels to air handlers and coils around the building, picks up heat from the air, and returns warmer. The temperature of the water leaving the chiller is often called the supply temperature, and the water coming back is the return temperature.

Condenser-water loop

Water leaves the condenser warm, goes to the cooling tower, is cooled by air and evaporation, and returns to the condenser. Cooler tower water makes the chiller's job easier, which is why weather affects plant performance.

The loops never mix. They exchange heat through the chiller's heat exchangers only.

Delta-T in plain words

Delta-T (written with the Greek letter delta) just means a temperature difference. For chilled water it is the return temperature minus the supply temperature. It tells you how much heat each unit of water is carrying. As an example for orientation only, many plants are designed for a spread somewhere around ten to sixteen degrees Fahrenheit. Your plant's own design data is what counts.

A delta-T that is much smaller than designed means water is moving fast without picking up much heat. That wastes pumping energy and can force extra equipment to run. It is a common finding in older buildings.

Approach in plain words

Approach is how closely one temperature gets to another in a heat exchanger. Two are commonly watched:

  • Evaporator approach: how far the leaving chilled-water temperature sits from the refrigerant's temperature in the evaporator.
  • Condenser approach: how far the refrigerant's temperature in the condenser sits above the leaving condenser-water temperature.

A small approach usually means clean tubes and good heat transfer. As scale, fouling or air and other gases build up, the approach grows. That is why operators trend approach over time, and a slow rise can be an early warning that cleaning or service is needed.

What operators log on rounds

Rounds are scheduled walk-throughs where operators look, listen and record readings in the same way each time. Typical entries include:

  • Date, time, outdoor conditions, which equipment is running, and load
  • Chilled-water supply and return temperatures, and condenser-water in and out temperatures
  • Refrigerant pressures and temperatures, plus calculated approaches
  • Motor current or percent load, oil level and pressure where applicable
  • Pump pressures, seal condition, noise and vibration
  • Cooling tower basin level, fan operation, water treatment readings
  • Machine room conditions, such as temperature, ventilation and refrigerant monitor status
  • Alarms, leaks and anything that seems different from yesterday

The log is only useful when the readings are honest and recorded in the same units. If a gauge looks wrong, note that rather than copying the last number.

Safety around the plant

Machine rooms combine moving equipment, electrical hazards, pressure and refrigerant. Stay out of guards and panels unless you are qualified and authorized, follow lockout and tagout rules, and know where exits and alarms are. If a refrigerant alarm sounds, leave and report it. Cooling towers also need water treatment and cleaning programs to control scale and microbial growth, which are handled by trained staff.

Takeaways

Heat moves from building water to refrigerant to condenser water to the outdoors. Delta-T tells you how hard the water is working, approach tells you how well the heat exchangers are performing, and a careful log turns small changes into early warnings.

Field card: Chiller Plant Fundamentals

A chiller moves heat from the building water loop to the condenser loop and out the tower.

  • Two water loops: chilled water and condenser water.
  • Delta-T is the supply-to-return temperature difference on a loop.
  • Approach is how close a leaving temperature gets to the refrigerant saturation temperature.
  • Log the same readings the same way every round so trends stand out.
  • Report changes in the trend, not just alarms.
