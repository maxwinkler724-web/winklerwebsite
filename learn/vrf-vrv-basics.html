VRF and VRV Basics

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Variable refrigerant flow (VRF) systems, sometimes sold under the VRV name, use one outdoor unit to serve many indoor units, each in its own zone. Piping refrigerant through a building changes the rules.

The basic idea

A VRF outdoor unit uses variable-speed compressors and fans. It speeds up or slows down to match what all the indoor units are asking for. Each indoor unit is a direct-expansion coil in the room, so heat moves by refrigerant itself rather than through long ducts or water pipes. Fresh air usually comes from a separate ventilation unit.

Branch controllers and heat recovery

Simple heat pump VRF systems heat or cool as a group. Heat recovery systems add a branch controller or branch selector box between the outdoor unit and the indoor units, and come in two-pipe and three-pipe piping arrangements depending on the maker. The box directs the right form of refrigerant, hot gas for heating or cooler refrigerant for cooling, to each zone. That lets one zone cool while another heats, and moves heat from where it is not wanted to where it is.

Why charge and leaks matter here

VRF systems tend to have long refrigerant lines, many joints and a large total charge, all feeding rooms where people sit. If a leak happens in a small room, refrigerant can build up and displace air. Safety standards such as ASHRAE 34 and 15 look at refrigerant type, total charge, and room volume to set limits on how much can be put into a space. Designers must check those numbers for the project, and some systems add leak detection and shutoff valves, depending on the maker and the code. As A2L refrigerants spread, these rules matter even more. Our lessons on A2L leak detection sensors cover related ideas.

What this means for a tech

  • Piping size, length and charge are specified by the maker. Do not improvise.
  • Charge is often set by calculation and by manufacturer procedures, not by a simple gauge reading.
  • Leak search matters more because there are so many joints, so leak checks and good brazing practice are part of the job.
  • Never bypass leak detectors, shutoff valves or safeties.
  • Use the manufacturer's software or tools for commissioning and fault codes.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: the manufacturer's installation and service manuals, the refrigerant's safety classification, and the local code govern. This lesson gives concepts only.

Field card: VRF and VRV Basics

One outdoor unit, many zones, a lot of refrigerant to respect.

  • VRF uses variable-speed compressors to serve many indoor units.
  • Branch controllers route refrigerant so zones can heat and cool at once.
  • Long lines and many joints raise leak risk.
  • Room volume and total charge decide how much refrigerant is allowed.
  • Follow the maker's charge and commissioning steps, and never defeat safeties.
