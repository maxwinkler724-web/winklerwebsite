Apprentice HVAC Tool List: What to Buy First and What to Borrow

New techs often spend too much too soon. Buy the basics you will use every day, ask what your school or employer expects, and borrow the specialty gear until you know you need it.

Ask before you buy

Every school and shop is different, so the first step is a question, not a purchase: what do you require, what do you supply, and what will I be expected to own? One community college tool list says its tools can be bought as a kit on the first day of class or individually, that students who already own some items need not buy them again, and that it recommends buying quality tools. It does not say which items the school provides, which shows why you should ask. as of 2026-10-16. Check the source for current details.

Start with these categories

That same college list is a useful picture of what a student program expects. Its categories, in my words:

  • Hand tools: screwdrivers, nut drivers, hex keys, adjustable wrenches, ratcheting wrenches, a socket set and pliers.
  • Cutting tools: tubing cutters, snips, diagonal cutters, a hacksaw, and a wire stripper and crimper.
  • Sheet-metal tools: a crimper, a folding tool, a seamer and layout tools such as a scratch awl.
  • Measuring and layout: a tape measure, a level and an inspection mirror.
  • Refrigeration service tools: a gauge manifold set with hoses, service wrenches, and flare and swage tools.
  • Meters: a multimeter and a digital thermometer.
  • Light: a flashlight. You will use it daily.

Items on that list are what one school asks for, not a universal rule. Your program may differ.

Safety gear comes first

That college tool list did not include safety equipment, so do not assume the tool list covers it. OSHA's PPE page names gloves, safety glasses and shoes, earplugs or muffs, hard hats, respirators and coveralls as examples of personal protective equipment. It says that when other controls are not enough, employers must provide PPE and train workers in its use, but it does not say there who pays for it, and links to a separate page on payment. So ask your employer directly what they provide. Eye and hand protection are cheap, so do not borrow them from a stranger. See Refrigerant Safety, Combustion and CO Safety Basics and Lockout/Tagout and Verify Dead.

Meters and gauges: buy carefully

A meter is a safety tool as well as a diagnostic one. Read Multimeter Safety and CAT Ratings before choosing one, and check the rating printed near the jacks. A gauge set is what you use to read the refrigerant side, so learn superheat and subcooling with it under supervision. Only certified technicians should handle refrigerant; see EPA 608 Basics.

What to borrow or wait on

No source cited here sorts tools into buy-now and borrow-first, so this is a suggestion to confirm with your mentor. Specialty and expensive items, such as recovery machines, vacuum pumps and leak detectors, are usually shared or supplied by a shop until you are doing that work yourself. Ask before you buy them. See Evacuation and Deep Vacuum Basics and Recovery, Recycle and Reclaim. Keep receipts and label your tools, since shared job sites mix them up.

Questions for your employer or school

  • Is there a required tool list or kit, and is it optional?
  • What tools, meters and safety gear are supplied?
  • Are there any tool allowances or payment plans?
  • Who maintains and calibrates shared equipment?

New techs often spend too much too soon.

  • Ask your school or employer for the tool list and what they supply before spending anything.
