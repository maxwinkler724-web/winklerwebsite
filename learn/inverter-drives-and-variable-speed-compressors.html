Inverter Drives and Variable-Speed Compressors

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A single-speed compressor sits across the line and is on or off. An inverter-driven compressor has a small power electronics system between the wall and the windings. That changes what you can measure, what can hurt you, and what a symptom means.

The big idea

An inverter drive takes incoming AC, converts it to DC, smooths and stores it, and then switches it back into a controlled output. The output frequency determines compressor speed, so the equipment can match its output to the load instead of cycling. Technician literature describes this as a purpose-built, smarter cousin of the variable frequency drive you may know from larger motors, built into the equipment. Studies of variable-capacity heat pumps show they can run at low speed for long stretches and be quite efficient while doing so.

Why you cannot just check the contactor

On older equipment, a call for cooling closed a contactor and the compressor ran. On many inverter systems there may be no contactor you can test the old way, or the outdoor board decides when and how fast to run based on messages from the indoor unit and its own sensors. A call at the thermostat does not mean the compressor is allowed to start. The outdoor board can hold off for protection reasons, for startup sequences, or because it is waiting on something you have not looked at yet.

Likewise, an amp reading on a compressor lead is a different animal when the waveform is not a plain line-frequency sine wave. Ordinary meters can read inaccurately on drive output (one meter maker reports many true-RMS meters reading 20 to 30 percent higher than the drive's own voltage reading on a drive output, a voltage figure rather than an amp figure), and what is normal depends on the product. Use the manufacturer's measurement guidance.

Stored energy and power safety

The drive's capacitors hold charge after power is removed. Technician-level material on inverter boards stresses waiting for those capacitors to discharge and confirming that with a proper meter before touching anything. How long to wait, which test points to use and what voltage counts as safe are manufacturer-specified and are not something to guess.

  • Follow the manufacturer's discharge and verification procedure every time.
  • Use a meter that is rated for the voltages involved, and verify it on a known source.
  • Remember that OSHA's lockout/tagout standard expects stored or residual energy to be relieved or made safe and isolation to be verified before work starts.
  • Keep compressor leads and board test points from touching the cabinet or each other during any powered test.
  • Some manufacturer diagnostics require power on with parts disconnected. Treat those as live tests and follow the manual's cautions exactly.

Indoor and outdoor units talking to each other

Many inverter systems are matched sets, where the indoor control tells the outdoor unit what capacity is needed and the outdoor board reports back its status and faults. Communication errors, mismatched components or incorrect settings can look like a compressor problem. More on that in Communicating Controls Basics.

Typical symptoms and how to approach them

  • Unit does not start, no obvious reason. Check the board's fault indicators first, confirm incoming power quality and communication status, and read the sequence for startup holds.
  • Starts, then shuts down or repeats a fault. Overcurrent, overheating, low or high pressure protection and communications faults all produce this pattern. Use the fault table and note when in the cycle it happens.
  • Runs but capacity is poor. Check for airflow, charge and sensor issues the way you always have, and consider that the unit may be limiting speed by design for protection or by a configuration.
  • Noise or vibration at certain speeds. Variable-speed units can show issues at specific frequencies. Piping, mounting and installation can matter.
  • Board failure suspected. Rule out incoming power problems and wiring first. The manufacturer's board check procedure and expected values apply. Be aware that a board can fail partially, and balanced readings do not always clear it.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: the installation and service instructions for the specific unit in front of you always govern. This lesson explains concepts. It does not give setpoints, thresholds, sensor positions or wiring steps, and those vary by manufacturer and by model.

Humidity and runtime

Because inverter equipment runs longer at lower output, it affects how the house feels. Some systems have humidity-focused modes that slow the airflow to remove more moisture. Customers may call about a unit that "never shuts off." Often that is just how it works, but verify with the manufacturer's expected behavior and rule out a real problem.

Field card: Inverter Drives and Variable-Speed Compressors

An inverter compressor is a motor with a computer in front of it. Diagnose the computer's view first.

  • Inverters convert, store and rebuild power. The output frequency sets speed.
  • A call at the thermostat does not guarantee a start. The board decides.
  • Capacitors hold energy after power is off. Discharge and verify per the manufacturer.
  • Read the board's fault codes and communication status before condemning parts.
  • Test values and procedures vary by maker. Use the unit's service manual.
