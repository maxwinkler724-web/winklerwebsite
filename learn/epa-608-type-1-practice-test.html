EPA 608 Type I Practice Test (Free, Explained)

Last updated: 2026-10-16 · 18 questions

Type I covers small appliances such as household refrigerators, window air conditioners and similar factory-sealed equipment.

The real exam is a multiple-choice test given by an EPA-approved certifying organization. Question counts, passing scores and open- or closed-book rules differ by organization, so confirm them with the organization you plan to test with and on the EPA Section 608 page (checked 2026-10-16).

How to use this test. Read each question, choose your answer, then open “Show answer” to see why and where it comes from. These are original practice questions, not real exam questions. Every explanation links to the source it is based on.

1. Type I covers appliances with how much refrigerant or less?

  1. 2 lb
  2. 5 lb
  3. 10 lb
  4. 50 lb
Show answer

Answer: B. 5 lb

Factory-sealed, self-contained.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

2. Which is a Type I appliance?

  1. Rooftop unit
  2. Window AC
  3. Centrifugal chiller
  4. Split system
Show answer

Answer: B. Window AC

A window air conditioner is a self-contained, factory-sealed unit, so it falls under Type I.

Background source: EPA608practicetest.net Type 1 study guide (secondary)

3. Recovery requirement with the compressor operating (equipment made after Nov 1993):

  1. 80%
  2. 90%
  3. 99%
  4. 50%
Show answer

Answer: B. 90%

90% if the compressor is operating; 80% if it is not.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(b)

4. When the appliance's compressor does not run, recovery target is:

  1. 90%
  2. 80%
  3. 60%
  4. 100%
Show answer

Answer: B. 80%

When the compressor is not running, recovery must reach 80 percent of the charge.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(b)

5. A passive/system-dependent recovery device depends on:

  1. Its own compressor
  2. The appliance's compressor
  3. Water pressure
  4. A vacuum pump only
Show answer

Answer: B. The appliance's compressor

A passive recovery device relies on the appliance's own compressor to move refrigerant.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

6. After using a piercing valve on a system to be put back in service, you should:

  1. Leave it
  2. Install a permanent access valve and leak check
  3. Add more refrigerant
  4. Wrap with tape
Show answer

Answer: B. Install a permanent access valve and leak check

A piercing valve is temporary, so it must be replaced with a permanent access valve, then leak checked.

Background source: ACHR News, Temporary Benefits of Saddle Valves

7. Before scrapping a refrigerator you find the refrigerant has not been recovered. You:

  1. Cut the line
  2. Recover it first and document
  3. Let it drain on the dock
  4. Weld it
Show answer

Answer: B. Recover it first and document

Refrigerant must be recovered before scrapping, and the recovery should be documented.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.155

8. A window AC with a dead compressor: heating the compressor helps because:

  1. It lowers pressure
  2. It drives refrigerant out of the oil
  3. It cleans the system
  4. It starts the pump
Show answer

Answer: B. It drives refrigerant out of the oil

Warmth boils off refrigerant dissolved in oil.

Background source: EPA608practicetest.net Type 1 study guide (secondary)

9. A self-contained recovery machine:

  1. Uses appliance's compressor
  2. Has its own compressor
  3. Uses nitrogen
  4. Needs a vacuum pump
Show answer

Answer: B. Has its own compressor

A self-contained recovery machine has its own compressor, so it does not depend on the appliance.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

10. Dehumidifier with 1.5 lb of R-410A is:

  1. Type I
  2. Type II
  3. Type III
  4. Exempt
Show answer

Answer: A. Type I

Small appliances holding 5 pounds or less of refrigerant are Type I, so a 1.5-pound dehumidifier qualifies.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (definitions)

11. If the person scrapping a freezer is not certified:

  1. May recover with any tool
  2. The appliance should go to a certified recovery party
  3. Cut line
  4. Dispose ignoring rules
Show answer

Answer: B. The appliance should go to a certified recovery party

Refrigerant must be recovered before disposal. Final processors must recover it or hold a signed statement or contract showing it was recovered, kept 3 years (40 CFR 82.155).

Source: 40 CFR 82.155

12. Many modern household refrigerators use isobutane (R-600a). Its safety classification is:

  1. A1
  2. B1
  3. A3
  4. A2
Show answer

Answer: C. A3

Isobutane is highly flammable, so it carries the A3 classification.

Source: ASHRAE 34 safety classes summary (hvactoolkit.org)

13. Before brazing a line on a circuit that still holds refrigerant, the best practice is to:

  1. Keep refrigerant in and braze quickly
  2. Remove the refrigerant from the area first, because heat can break it down into toxic byproducts
  3. Heat the line to dry it
  4. Spray water on the joint
Show answer

Answer: B. Remove the refrigerant from the area first, because heat can break it down into toxic byproducts

Heat can decompose refrigerant into irritating and toxic byproducts, so the circuit must be recovered first.

Source: R-134a Safety Data Sheet (National Refrigerants)

14. Which refrigerant replaced R-12 in many household refrigerators?

  1. R-22
  2. R-404A
  3. R-1234yf
  4. R-134a
Show answer

Answer: D. R-134a

R-134a became the common replacement for R-12 in household refrigeration.

Background source: HVAC Laboratory, R-134a

15. A technician certified only for Type I appliances may NOT:

  1. Recover refrigerant from a window AC
  2. Service a household refrigerator
  3. Service a rooftop packaged unit
  4. Service a dehumidifier
Show answer

Answer: C. Service a rooftop packaged unit

Rooftop packaged units are Type II, outside a Type I-only certification.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.161(a)

16. Recovered refrigerant must be stored in:

  1. A DOT-approved cylinder intended for refrigerant service
  2. An unlabeled bottle
  3. A propane tank
  4. A milk jug
Show answer

Answer: A. A DOT-approved cylinder intended for refrigerant service

Only approved cylinders are designed for the pressures and transport conditions involved.

Background source: ACHR News, Refrigerant recovery compliance and safety

17. On a factory-sealed small appliance with a process tube, after charging, the tube should be:

  1. Left open to ambient air
  2. Pinched off and sealed
  3. Taped over
  4. Filled with oil
Show answer

Answer: B. Pinched off and sealed

A sealed process tube keeps the charge in and prevents leaks.

Background source: EPA608practicetest.net Type 1 study guide (secondary)

18. Servicing a system that uses a flammable refrigerant requires:

  1. Equipment rated for flammable refrigerants, with no ignition sources nearby
  2. A standard open-flame torch leak check
  3. A compressed-air purge
  4. Any vacuum pump
Show answer

Answer: A. Equipment rated for flammable refrigerants, with no ignition sources nearby

Flammable refrigerants call for rated equipment and control of ignition sources.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.158
