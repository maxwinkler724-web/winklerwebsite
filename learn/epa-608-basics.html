EPA 608 Certification Basics

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the EPA 608 Guide

If you work on equipment that holds refrigerant, federal law expects you to be trained in handling it properly. This lesson explains what EPA Section 608 certification is, how the exam is organized, and how to take it.

What Section 608 is

Section 608 is part of the U.S. Clean Air Act. It sets national rules for how refrigerants are handled during the servicing, repair and disposal of air conditioning and refrigeration equipment. The big ideas are simple: do not knowingly release refrigerant into the air, recover it when equipment is opened or retired, and make sure the people doing the work know how.

Technician certification is the part of the program that proves you have that knowledge. It is a certification of knowledge about refrigerant handling. It is not a general HVAC license, and it does not cover electrical, gas or building-code topics.

Who needs it

In general, technicians who maintain, service, repair or dispose of equipment that could release refrigerant into the atmosphere need to be certified. Buying many regulated refrigerants is also limited to certified technicians, with some exceptions. Exact coverage, including which jobs and which refrigerants are included, is set out in the regulations, so check the official rules or ask your employer's compliance contact if you are unsure whether a task applies to you.

The exam sections

The test is divided into a shared section and sections for different equipment families. You earn a certification type by passing the Core section plus the section for that equipment type.

SectionWhat it covers, in general
CoreThe shared foundation: why the rules exist, ozone and climate basics, refrigerant handling and recovery concepts, safety, shipping and containers, and the main regulatory ideas.
Type ISmall appliances that come factory-sealed with a small refrigerant charge, such as household refrigerators and window units.
Type IIEquipment that uses high-pressure refrigerants, such as many split systems, rooftop units and commercial refrigeration.
Type IIIEquipment that uses low-pressure refrigerants, such as certain large chillers.
UniversalNot a separate test. You earn it by passing Core plus Types I, II and III.

Many people sit for Universal because it opens the widest range of work. Others take only the type they need right now and add more later.

How and where you take it

EPA does not give the exam itself. Instead, EPA approves outside organizations to offer it. These certifying organizations include trade schools, associations and testing companies. EPA publishes a list of approved organizations, and the exam is given under supervision, which is called proctoring. Depending on the organization, that may mean a test center, a classroom, or a remote session with a live proctor. Costs, schedules and study materials vary, so compare a few approved options.

When you pass, the organization issues your credential. Keep a copy, because employers and parts suppliers often ask to see it. If you lose it, the organization that certified you is the place to start.

Does it expire?

No. EPA's certification page says Section 608 credentials do not expire, and the regulation sets no renewal period. Once you have earned a certification type, it stays valid. (The regulation does allow EPA to require recertification in the future by public notice, so keep an eye on EPA announcements.) That said, rules and refrigerants change, and good technicians keep learning. Staying current is a professional habit rather than a renewal requirement.

A note on the question bank

Reported by trade press, not confirmed on an EPA page. A trade publication (ACHR News, listed in the sources) reports that the exam question bank is being revised and that exams from the current bank can be given only through January 1, 2027. The EPA pages we could open do not state this date, so treat it as a report, not an official rule. Before you study or register, confirm with your certifying organization which version of the exam you will be given and which study materials match it.

Because this is a moving target, avoid buying study materials that are not clearly labeled for the exam version you will take. Official fact sheets from EPA and your testing organization are the safest starting point.

Study tips

  • Learn the reasons behind the rules, not just the rules. Understanding why venting is restricted makes the details easier to remember.
  • Work through the Core material first, because it supports every other section.
  • Pay attention to the vocabulary: recover, recycle and reclaim mean different things (see our lesson on that topic).
  • Take timed practice quizzes written in your own words or by reputable publishers, and review each miss.
  • Never rely on memorized leaked questions. They are unreliable, they may be out of date, and they do not make you safe on the job.

Field card: EPA 608 Certification Basics

Section 608 is the US rule set for handling refrigerant; you certify through an EPA-approved organization.

  • Four sections: Core, Type I, Type II, Type III. All four = Universal.
  • Exams are proctored and closed-book.
  • Certification does not expire.
  • Confirm exam dates and the current question bank with your certifying organization.
  • Study materials are not approved or reviewed by the EPA.
