HVAC Equipment Warranties: Registration and What Can Void Them

Last updated: 2026-10-16

The warranties we read for new furnaces, air conditioners and heat pumps work like two warranties in one: a short basic one, and a longer one you only get by registering on time. Then a list of exclusions decides whether a claim gets paid. This lesson reads six manufacturers' own warranty pages and documents so you know what to look for before you need it.

Read your own paperwork first

This is general information, not legal advice. Terms differ by brand, model, install date and state, and makers revise them. Everything below describes the documents we read in 2026. Your unit's warranty is the one that counts, so find it using your model number.

Base versus registered: the registration window

Several makers split coverage into a base tier and a longer registered tier, and the registered tier depends on a deadline counted from installation.

  • Carrier. Its warranty page says to register within 90 days of installation. If you miss it, a 5-year parts-only warranty applies. If you register in time, you choose between 10 years of parts only, or 5 years of parts plus 3 years of labor.
  • Trane. Its warranty page requires registration within 60 days of installation. Otherwise the Base Limited Warranty applies, which it says is 5 years of parts in most cases. Registered is typically 10 years.
  • Lennox. Its extended warranty form says to register within 60 days of installation (or closing on a new build). Basic coverage is 5 years of parts. Registering adds 5 more parts-only years, or as an alternative 2 more parts years plus 3 years of labor.
  • Goodman. The warranty summary we read for its SSX and SSZ air conditioners and heat pumps says to register online within 60 days of installation. Registered owner-occupied homes get up to 10 years on parts, and the compressor for the registered owner's lifetime. Not registered is up to 5 years.
  • Rheem. Its warranty document says to register within 90 days of installation or closing. Many models get 10 years of parts when registered and generally 5 when not.
  • Mitsubishi Electric. Its registration page says residential systems registered within 90 days of installation get a 12-year parts and compressor warranty.

Some places do not allow a registration requirement. Lennox lists California, Florida, Georgia (for units installed on or after January 1, 2026) and Quebec. Trane notes Florida sales on or after July 1, 2024. Goodman notes California and Quebec. Check your state before assuming you lost anything.

Parts versus labor

In the documents we read, that long coverage is mostly for parts only. Trane says neither of its limited warranties covers labor. Goodman says it supplies a replacement part but not labor, freight or refrigerant. Rheem's document says labor is not covered unless you have a separate labor agreement with your contractor. Two makers do offer a labor option: Carrier's 5-plus-3 choice, available only if the installing dealer takes part in its program, and Lennox's 2-plus-3 alternative. So a 10-year parts warranty can still leave a large repair bill for the person doing the work. See How Long Do HVAC Systems Last? Repair or Replace for how that weighs against a repair-or-replace decision.

Who must install it

Lennox's extended warranty requires installation per the maker's instructions by a licensed professional installer or licensed contractor. A Carrier-site furnace warranty document says the same, and adds local codes. Rheem asks for a "qualified, trained HVAC professional" and excludes internet purchases not installed that way. Goodman covers units ordered online only if a dealer who follows codes and licensing installs them. Mitsubishi's registration page says to use a Diamond Contractor (or a Ductless Pro) to qualify for the 12 years. Ask about licensing and proof before you hire; see What to Ask an HVAC Contractor Before Hiring.

What can void or limit a claim

"Void" is the word people search for, but the documents mostly use exclusions: specific causes of failure that the maker will not pay for. These show up repeatedly.

  • Improper installation. Goodman excludes faulty installation. Trane's owner blog lists improper installation and says a do-it-yourself or handyman approach will void the warranty.
  • Improper maintenance or no maintenance. Goodman excludes damage from improper use, maintenance, operation or servicing. Rheem requires you to maintain the equipment per its instructions. The Carrier-site furnace document requires proof of at least annual maintenance.
  • Wrong or used refrigerant. Goodman excludes unapproved refrigerant and used or recycled refrigerant. Lennox says refrigerant must match the nameplate. Rheem excludes refrigerant as a maintenance item.
  • Parts the maker did not supply. Goodman excludes parts not supplied or designated by the maker. Rheem excludes unauthorized components and systems that do not match its specifications. Carrier's page advises genuine Carrier parts.
  • Flood, lightning and electrical problems. Goodman excludes floods, fires, winds and lightning, plus interruptions or inadequate electrical service. Rheem excludes flood, lightning and acts of God. The Carrier-site document excludes voltage conditions, blown fuses and open breakers. None of the three names a power surge in those words, so ask how your maker treats one.
  • Moving the unit. Goodman ends coverage when the unit leaves its original location. Rheem and the Carrier-site document also require the original location.

Proof of maintenance

If a maker can ask for proof, you want records. Keep invoices from each service visit, with the date, the contractor and what was done. Lennox also says that within 30 days of service or maintenance (60 days for leak searches and repairs) you must use the same dealer unless Lennox consents. Filter changes and coil cleaning are listed as owner upkeep that is not covered by Goodman and by the Carrier-site document, so those are your job but they are not warranty claims. A tune-up record from a professional is the strongest paper trail; What an HVAC Tune-Up Should Include: A Homeowner Checklist shows what the visit should cover.

Selling the house or buying one

Transfer rules vary a lot. Trane says its registered warranty can be transferred within 90 days of the home sale for a one-time $99 fee, and if it is not, the buyer keeps what remains of the base warranty. Lennox's extended warranty is generally not transferable, though labor coverage passes to the new owner for the rest of its three years. Carrier's furnace document gives later owners reduced coverage. Rheem's 10-year coverages require the original purchaser. Goodman's summary covers the original owner and does not describe a transfer. Mitsubishi's registration page requires the original owner to live in the home. If you are buying, ask the seller for the registration confirmation and the install date.

Worked example: the deadline and the years left

Say a unit is installed on March 14, 2026. Use Carrier's 90-day window first.

  1. Days left in March after the 14th: 31 - 14 = 17.
  2. Add April (30): 17 + 30 = 47.
  3. Add May (31): 47 + 31 = 78.
  4. Days still needed: 90 - 78 = 12, so the deadline is June 12, 2026.

With a 60-day window like Trane's: 17 + 30 = 47 through April, then 60 - 47 = 13, so the deadline is May 13, 2026. The shorter window closes a month earlier.

Now the years. Parts coverage runs from installation: 5 years unregistered ends March 14, 2031, and 10 years registered ends March 14, 2036. On March 14, 2029, three years in, the registered owner has 10 - 3 = 7 years left and the unregistered owner has 5 - 3 = 2 years left. A missed deadline costs 10 - 5 = 5 years of parts coverage. This example is hypothetical and uses the Carrier and Trane figures above; your unit's terms may differ.

The federal rule on tie-in clauses

The FTC's guide to the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act describes the Act as the federal law governing warranties on consumer products. It says tie-in provisions, meaning terms that make you use a particular company's items or services to keep coverage, are generally prohibited. Its example is a vacuum warranty that demands genuine filter bags. Exceptions apply when the item or service is provided free under the warranty or the FTC grants a waiver. The same guide says a warrantor may deny coverage for damage caused by parts or service it did not provide. It also says a full warranty cannot require you to return a registration card, while its limited-warranty example does. In plain terms: a maker generally cannot insist you use its own brand of service as a condition of coverage, but it can refuse to pay for damage that bad work or wrong parts caused. How that applies to a real dispute is a legal question for a professional or your state consumer office.

Field card: HVAC Warranties

Register fast, keep records, use qualified installers.

  • Registration windows in the documents we read: 60 days (Trane, Lennox, Goodman) or 90 days (Carrier, Rheem, Mitsubishi).
  • Registering often doubles parts coverage from 5 to 10 years; labor is usually separate.
  • Typical exclusions: faulty installation, poor maintenance, wrong refrigerant, unapproved parts, flood or lightning, a moved unit.
  • Keep install paperwork, registration confirmation and every service invoice.
  • Transfer to a buyer varies; Trane allows it within 90 days of sale for a fee.
  • The FTC says tie-in clauses are generally prohibited, but damage from outside parts or service can be excluded.
