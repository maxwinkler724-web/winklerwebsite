Recover, Recycle and Reclaim Explained

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the EPA 608 Guide

Three similar-sounding words describe three different things. Knowing the difference is a core part of refrigerant handling and a common area for exam questions.

The three words

TermPlain-English meaningWho does it
RecoverTake refrigerant out of a system and put it into a proper container. Nothing is cleaned or tested.A certified technician using approved recovery equipment.
RecycleClean recovered refrigerant for reuse, for example by removing oil and some moisture with filters. It is not lab tested to new-product purity.Done on site or in a shop, and generally for reuse in the same owner's equipment.
ReclaimProcess refrigerant until it meets the purity standard of new refrigerant, confirmed by testing.An EPA-certified reclaimer, a company set up and approved for this work.

A helpful way to remember them: recover is collect, recycle is clean up, and reclaim is make like new and prove it.

Why recovery comes first

Before a technician opens a system that holds refrigerant, the refrigerant has to go somewhere safe. Recovery equipment pulls it out as vapor or liquid and stores it in a container made for the job. Recovery machines and containers have to be suitable for the refrigerant involved, and the rules say how thoroughly a system must be emptied depending on its size, type and age. Those details are in the regulations and in your training materials, so rely on those rather than on a summary like this one. As an example of how it works, the regulation text (40 CFR 82.158, read through Cornell's law library copy) sets different vacuum levels by appliance type: low-pressure appliances are pulled to 25 mm Hg absolute, and a high-pressure appliance holding under 200 lb of HCFC-22 is pulled to 0 in. Hg, while other high- and medium-pressure appliances depend on the appliance's charge size and on whether the recovery equipment was made before or after November 15, 1993 (4, 10 or 15 in. Hg). The technician's evacuation table is in 40 CFR 82.156(a), and the date refers to the recovery equipment, not the appliance. For small appliances, a technician using equipment made on or after that date recovers 90 percent of the refrigerant if the appliance's compressor works or 80 percent if it does not (82.156(b)). That copy may lag behind changes, so confirm on EPA's current eCFR page.

Why venting is prohibited

Older refrigerants damaged the ozone layer, which protects living things from harmful ultraviolet light. Newer refrigerants do not hurt ozone the same way, but many are strong greenhouse gases that trap heat in the atmosphere. In both cases, releasing them on purpose is harmful, and the law prohibits it in most circumstances. The regulation lists a few narrow exceptions, such as the tiny amounts that escape when a technician makes or breaks a connection in good faith. It is not a loophole for "just letting the rest out."

There is also a practical side. Refrigerant costs money, and reclaiming it keeps valuable material in use. Recovering rather than venting protects the environment and the budget.

Who may reuse refrigerant?

The general idea is a ladder of trust:

  • Recovered or recycled refrigerant may typically go back into the appliance it came from, or into other equipment owned by the same person or company (40 CFR 82.156). Selling used refrigerant, or moving it to a different owner, generally requires reclamation by a certified reclaimer first, with a few narrow exceptions listed in 40 CFR 82.154.
  • Reclaimed refrigerant that meets the purity standard can be sold and used in other equipment, because it has been tested.
  • Mixed or contaminated refrigerant usually cannot be reused on site and may need to go to a reclaimer or be destroyed.

The exact rules, including special cases, are written in the regulation. If you handle used refrigerant for resale, shipment or transfer, confirm the current requirements before you act.

Good habits

  • Label recovery containers clearly with what they hold.
  • Do not mix refrigerants. A cylinder of mixed refrigerant can become waste.
  • Weigh what you recover and keep records as your employer or the rules require.
  • Use only equipment that is in good working order and suited to the refrigerant, including flammable ones.
  • When in doubt, ask a supervisor or compliance contact. Questions are cheaper than violations.

Quick recap

Recover removes. Recycle cleans for the same owner. Reclaim restores to a tested standard at a certified facility. Venting is not an option.

Field card: Recover, Recycle and Reclaim

Three different words for three different jobs.

  • Recover: remove refrigerant into an approved container.
  • Recycle: clean it for reuse, typically back in the same owner's equipment.
  • Reclaim: process to new-product purity at a certified reclaimer.
  • Do not vent regulated refrigerant.
  • Check current EPA rules for what is allowed in your situation.
