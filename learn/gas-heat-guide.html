Gas and Oil Heat Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Gas and oil heat look simple from the thermostat: a call for heat, a flame, warm air. Behind it sit a start-up sequence, safety switches, a fuel supply sized for the load, and a vent that has to carry combustion products out of the building. This guide groups the free lessons on those pieces so you can follow one furnace from the gas meter to the flue.

Begin with the sequence of operation and the main safeties, then the diagnosis lessons that turn a blink code into a failed part. Next come the measurements that prove a furnace is set up correctly: clocking the meter to check input, reading a combustion analyzer, and understanding what the vent category on the nameplate allows. Gas pipe sizing is here too, because an undersized line shows up as a furnace problem.

Gas work has real hazards: fuel gas, carbon monoxide and fire. These lessons explain the ideas; they are not a substitute for the appliance manual, the fuel gas code adopted where you work, or the licensing rules for gas work in your state. Follow the manufacturer instructions and your local rules first.

Sequence and safeties

  • Gas Furnace Sequence of Operation: The typical order of events in a gas furnace, from the thermostat call for heat to shutdown, explained in general terms.
  • Two-Stage and Modulating Gas Furnaces: How Staging Works: Single-stage, two-stage and modulating gas furnaces compared: how low or high fire is picked, two pressure switches, manifold checks, and airflow math.
  • Flame Safeguard and Ignition Basics: How hot surface igniters, spark ignition, and flame sensing with rectification work in general terms, and why flame safeguards matter.
  • Pressure Switch and Inducer Diagnosis: How the inducer and pressure switch prove draft before a furnace may light, and an ordered way to find why the switch will not close without bypassing it.
  • Hot Surface Igniter Care and Testing: What a hot surface igniter does, why it breaks, how to test it with a meter, and how to handle and replace one without shortening its life.

Oil burners

  • Oil Burner Sequence: Cad Cell and Primary Control: How a home oil burner lights and proves flame: the cad cell, the primary control's timing, ohm readings, lockout and why repeated resets are risky.
  • Oil Burner Nozzles, Pump Pressure and Firing Rate: Read an oil nozzle's GPH, angle and pattern, see why pump pressure changes firing rate by a square root, and turn GPH into Btu/h with an example.

Diagnosis

  • No-Heat Furnace: From LED Code to Part: A calm, step-by-step way to diagnose a gas furnace that will not heat, starting at the control board's blink code and ending at the actual failed part.
  • RTU Gas Heat Section Basics: A safety-first tour of the gas heat section in a rooftop unit: the parts, the safety switches, what an inspection looks for, and what never to do.

Fuel, combustion and venting

  • Sizing Natural Gas Piping: The Longest Length Method: How gas pipe is sized by load in cfh and length: convert Btu/h to cfh, use the longest length method, and read a Schedule 40 table in a worked example.
  • Clocking a Gas Meter and Checking Furnace Input: How to clock a gas meter: time the test dial, convert to cubic feet per hour and BTU/h, compare to nameplate, and learn what causes over- or under-firing.
  • Combustion Analyzer Readings: O2, CO2, CO Air-Free and Excess Air: What each combustion analyzer number means: O2, calculated CO2, CO as measured vs air-free, excess air, stack temperature and efficiency, with a worked example.
  • Furnace and Boiler Vent Categories I to IV Explained: What vent categories I to IV mean: pressure, condensing flue gas, vent materials, why condensing furnaces cannot use a chimney, and orphaned water heaters.
  • Combustion and CO Safety Basics: Combustion air, venting, carbon monoxide, and why working CO alarms and careful testing matter around gas heat.
  • Carbon Monoxide Alarms and What to Do When One Sounds: Where CO comes from, where to put alarms, how long they last, and the calm, safe steps to take if one sounds or you smell gas.
