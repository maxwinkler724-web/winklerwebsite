Mini-Split Installation Basics: Line Set, Vacuum and Commissioning

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Most mini-split callbacks are born during installation: a bad flare, a lazy vacuum, a drain with no fall. The manuals agree on a lot, and where they differ, the manual for your unit wins.

Plan before you drill

Installation write-ups start with paperwork. Match the indoor and outdoor units, then check voltage, circuit sizing, line length and height limits, clearances and permit needs against the documentation for that exact model. One manufacturer indoor-unit manual sends you to the outdoor-unit manual for line length, height difference, bends and charge limits. It also asks for clear service space, a surface that can carry the weight and a place where condensate can drain. For R-32 equipment it adds a room-area check tied to the charge, and a worked example in that manual shows how a charge of a few pounds ties to a minimum floor area. See A2L Charge Limits and Room Size.

Mounting

Level the wall plate or ceiling hangers, support the unit properly and carry it by the hanger plates, not by the pipes or drain hose. For ceiling cassettes, one manual specifies anchored suspension bolts and vibration protection for long drops. Set the outdoor unit on a solid, level pad with airflow room, away from dryer vents and snow or leaf traps. A best-practices page from another maker adds that both units must be anchored to avoid vibration and noise.

Line set and flares

Manufacturers say to use clean copper made for refrigeration, the sizes the units call for, and to keep within the minimum and maximum lengths. One manual warns against joints and extensions in the factory-provided connection style. Cut square, ream burrs, and form a smooth 45 degree flare with no cracks. A guide on installation workflow says a clean flare is the main factor in a leak-free connection and that nuts should be tightened with a torque wrench to the maker's value, because over-tightening can crack a flare. That manual lists torque ranges by pipe size, and the ranges differ between models, so do not use a remembered number. Wrap each refrigerant pipe separately in insulation without gaps, and thicken the insulation in humid conditions when the manual says to. Protect exposed insulation from sunlight.

Nitrogen, leak test and vacuum

Outdoor units often ship with a nitrogen holding charge, and manuals tell you not to release it until you are ready to connect. If brazing is involved anywhere in the job, flow oxygen-free nitrogen during the work; see Nitrogen Purge Flow While Brazing. Then pressure test with nitrogen to the value the maker gives, check every connection with a detector suited to the refrigerant, and only then evacuate. One best-practices page describes pulling below 500 microns and holding to confirm there is no leak. An installation guide stresses using a calibrated micron gauge, isolating the pump and meeting the maker's decay criteria, because elapsed time alone proves nothing. More on method in Evacuation and Deep Vacuum Basics.

Condensate drain

A drain with no fall is a service call waiting to happen. The manual above wants the hose pushed fully on, clamped and wrapped, supported at intervals, never rising above the connection and never left to sag. It gives a minimum slope as an example and says to add an air vent if the slope falls short. Test it. The same manual calls for pouring about two liters of water into the pan, running cooling mode, and checking the pump, outlet and every joint for leaks, noting that its drain pump works only in cooling mode.

Electrical and commissioning checklist

  • Ground the units, size wiring and breakers per the nameplate, and follow code for disconnects.
  • Make refrigerant connections before electrical connections, and disconnect electrical before refrigerant pipes, per one manual.
  • Check terminal torque, remove shipping materials and open service valves before power-up.
  • Set addresses and options from the remote if the model needs them, then run each mode.
  • Expect a pause before the compressor starts after a stop or a power interruption. One maker's FAQ (Fujitsu General) says about 3 minutes; other models differ, so check the manual, including any warm-up rule for the crankcase heater.
  • Verify drainage, airflow, noise and vibration, and record model and serial numbers, readings and settings.
  • Show the owner the filter, the remote and who to call.

Weigh in any added charge on a scale and follow the maker's method. Only people with the right licenses and certifications should handle refrigerant and line-voltage work; see NJ Trade Licenses: Who Needs Which.

Order of authority

The rule that overrides this page: the manual for the exact unit in front of you, your employer's procedures and the codes where you work always govern. This lesson explains concepts only, and every number below is a labeled example from one source, not a setting to copy.

Field card: Mini-Split Installation Basics: Line Set, Vacuum and Commissioning

Plan, mount level, flare clean, test, deep vacuum, prove the drain.

  • Check line-length, height and charge limits in the manual before ordering.
  • Flare cleanly and torque to the maker's value; do not overtighten.
  • Pressure test, then evacuate with a micron gauge and a decay check.
  • Give condensate a continuous fall and test it with water.
  • Commission and document: readings, serials, settings and owner handoff.
