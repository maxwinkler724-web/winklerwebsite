Get Your AC Ready for Summer: A Spring Startup Checklist

Last updated: 2026-10-16

This is the short list a homeowner can do before turning on the AC for the first time this year, in the order that makes sense, plus how to tell a normal start from a call-worthy one.

Safety first. Power off before you touch anything behind a panel. Trane's owner's guide says to disconnect all electrical power to the indoor unit before removing access panels, and notes there may be more than one disconnect. Homeowner work on this page stays outside the cabinets: filter, the area around the outdoor unit, the thermostat and the drain opening. Refrigerant, wiring and electrical parts belong to a licensed tech.

This is the cooling twin of Get Your Heating System Ready Before the First Cold Snap. For what a paid visit should include, see What an HVAC Tune-Up Should Include.

Step 1: Book the tune-up early

ENERGY STAR says to check the cooling system in the spring, because contractors get busy once summer arrives. Its list of professional tasks includes inspecting the condensate drain, cleaning the coils, checking the refrigerant level and cleaning and adjusting the blower. It adds that airflow problems can cut efficiency by up to 15 percent.

Step 2: Filter

This is the one job every source gives to the homeowner. The Department of Energy says to clean or replace the filter every month or two during the cooling season, and more often if the system runs constantly, the home is dusty or you have pets that shed. ENERGY STAR says about once a month, and warns that a dirty filter can raise energy costs and damage equipment. Trane's owner's guide says monthly, and twice a month in seasons when the unit runs more. A Goodman use-and-care booklet says to turn the power off before pulling a filter and to replace a disposable one with the same size. Trane adds that the arrows should point the way the air flows.

The sources do not agree on one interval, so follow the instructions for your own equipment, and start the season with a clean filter. See How Often to Change Your Air Filter and MERV.

Step 3: The outdoor unit

Walk around the condenser (the box with the fan outside). Trane's guide says airflow over the coil is what keeps the unit efficient, and tells owners not to stack things against the unit, drape anything over it or plant shrubs right next to it. DOE says to keep debris away and to trim foliage back at least 2 feet. A Goodman booklet says grass clippings, leaves, dirt and lint can be drawn into the coil, lowering efficiency and possibly damaging the unit, and that a dryer vent should not discharge near it.

  • Remove leaves and trash from around and on top of the unit.
  • Cut back foliage to at least 2 feet (24 inches) from the cabinet.
  • Move stored items, bikes and covers off it.
  • Look at the fins. DOE says to check for dirt building up on the condenser coil. If they look clogged, ask your tech to clean the coil.

Step 4: Power, breaker and disconnect

Do not open the electrical box on the outdoor unit. Just confirm the supply is on. Trane's guide says that if the system will not run, check that the main switch is on and reset the breaker or replace a burned-out fuse. A Goodman booklet says to reset a tripped breaker by moving it fully OFF and then back ON. If it trips again, stop; repeated trips mean something is wrong and a tech must find it. See AC or Heat Pump Keeps Tripping the Breaker. If the outdoor unit does not start while the indoor fan runs, read Outdoor AC Unit Not Running but Indoor Fan Is.

One manufacturer note is worth knowing. A Goodman installation reference says that when the system is first powered, it should be left to stabilize for 10 minutes with a fixed orifice and 20 minutes with an expansion valve, and that some outdoor units or thermostats delay compressor operation, so wait 15 minutes before calling for service. I could not confirm a universal crankcase heater warm-up time from the four brands, so check your own manual and ask your tech.

Step 5: Condensate drain

Cooling removes water from the air, and it has to go somewhere. DOE says to occasionally pass a stiff wire through the unit's drain channels, since clogged channels can prevent humidity removal and cause moisture damage. ENERGY STAR notes that a plugged drain can cause water damage in the house. A Goodman booklet for one of its systems says the outside drain should be dripping while the emergency overflow drain above it should stay dry. If the pan or line is overflowing, see Water Leaking From AC or Furnace: Condensate Drain, and ask your tech to clear it.

Step 6: Thermostat

Put fresh batteries in if yours uses them, set the mode to COOL and the fan to AUTO. Trane's guide suggests 78°F for cooling and, when you are away, raising it to 85°F. A Goodman booklet says most people prefer 76 to 78 and that constant on-off changes are not helpful. If the humidity is high, Trane says use the fan only in AUTO. A pro should also check the thermostat's accuracy, per DOE.

Step 7: Run a test on a mild day

Do this before the first heat wave, on a day mild enough to be comfortable but warm enough that you want cooling. I did not find a manufacturer minimum outdoor temperature among the pages read, so do not force the issue on a cold day; check your owner's manual. Set the thermostat a few degrees below room temperature and then watch for:

  • The indoor blower and the outdoor fan both running (allow the delays above).
  • Air from the registers that feels cooler. A Goodman booklet says the cooling coil lowers air temperature about 15 to 20 degrees, which gives a rough idea of what to expect.
  • Water draining at the outside drain, not leaking indoors.
  • No new loud noises or burning smells. Trane says to contact a servicer about unusual noise. See Furnace, AC and Boiler Noises.

Worked example: a sample yard

A condenser sits on a pad beside the house. Its cabinet is 30 inches square. A boxwood shrub has its nearest foliage 14 inches from the cabinet, and a fence sits 20 inches away on another side.

  1. The DOE figure is 2 feet. Convert: 2 × 12 = 24 inches.
  2. Compare: 24 − 14 = 10 inches too close on the shrub side. Trim the foliage back at least 10 inches.
  3. The fence is a fixed object, not foliage, so DOE's trim rule does not cover it. The minimum clearances for walls and fences come from the unit's installation manual. A Goodman reference lists residential clearance numbers (10 to 20 inches) and 60 inches above the unit, but we could not confirm which side of the unit each number applies to. Ask your installer or read your own manual.

Filters, same yard. If the cooling season is five months (May through September), DOE's every-month-or-two interval means about 3 to 5 checks (5 ÷ 2 = 2.5, round up to 3; 5 ÷ 1 = 5). ENERGY STAR's monthly check means 5. Trane's twice a month in heavy-use seasons means 10. Use the interval in your manual.

What normal looks like, and when to call

Normal: the system cycles on and off, the air feels cool but not icy, and water drains. Trane says to call your dealer if the unit is inoperative or if you still have insufficient cooling after checking the filter, airflow and outdoor coil. A Goodman booklet says to contact a qualified servicer at once if you notice an abnormal condition. Call a pro for:

  • Ice on the indoor coil or lines (a Goodman booklet lists a dirty filter among the causes; see frozen coil causes).
  • A breaker that trips again after one reset.
  • Warm air with the outdoor unit running; see AC Running but Not Cooling.
  • Water pooling or dripping indoors.

For techs: what the visit adds

The homeowner list is airflow and drainage. The tech list adds measurement. DOE says a tech should check the refrigerant level, test for leaks, measure airflow across the evaporator and verify electrical controls, and ENERGY STAR adds measuring motor voltage and current. Charge is judged with Superheat and Subcooling Basics, and airflow with Airflow and Static Pressure Basics. A dirty coil shows up as high head pressure; see Dirty Condenser and Head Pressure, and Undercharge vs Overcharge Symptom Table for symptoms.

Field card: Get Your AC Ready for Summer: A Spring Startup Checklist

Clean filter, clear coil, working drain, then test.

  • Book the spring tune-up before summer rush.
  • Filter: check monthly or per your manual; arrows point with the airflow.
  • Outdoor unit: clear debris, foliage back at least 2 ft (24 in).
  • Breaker/disconnect on; one reset only, then call.
  • Drain: confirm it drains; clear stuck lines with a pro.
  • Thermostat: COOL, fan AUTO, fresh batteries.
  • Test on a mild day. Allow start delays. Noise, ice, water, repeat trips: call.
