A2L and A3 Refrigerants

Refrigerants carry a safety class made of a letter and a number. This lesson explains how to read it and why the flammable classes are becoming part of everyday HVAC work.

Reading a safety class

The industry rates refrigerants with a short code. The letter describes toxicity and the number describes flammability.

  • A means lower toxicity. B means higher toxicity.
  • 1 means the refrigerant did not propagate a flame in standard testing.
  • 2L means mildly flammable: it is hard to ignite and burns very slowly.
  • 2 means flammable.
  • 3 means highly flammable.

So A1 is the familiar "low toxicity, does not burn" group. A2L is low toxicity and mildly flammable. A3 is low toxicity and highly flammable. Remember that a code describes a refrigerant in general. The label on the actual equipment and the manufacturer's instructions tell you what applies on the job.

Why A2L is showing up

Many older refrigerants have a high global warming potential, and national policy is steering new equipment toward alternatives with a lower one. Many of the lower-impact options turn out to be mildly flammable. That is why A2L refrigerants are appearing in new air conditioners, heat pumps and chillers. The EPA's Significant New Alternatives Policy, called SNAP, reviews substitutes and sets conditions for how some of them may be used, including conditions for flammable ones. Those rules are updated over time, so read the current EPA pages. One maker's regulatory guide describes EPA's 2023 Technology Transitions rule as setting a GWP limit of 700 for new residential and light commercial air conditioners and heat pumps from 2025, which pushes new equipment toward A2Ls such as R-32 and R-454B. Installation rules for older R-410A equipment changed after that, so check EPA's current notice.

Why mildly flammable does not mean harmless

The "L" stands for low burning velocity. It is not a promise that nothing can go wrong. Under the wrong conditions, such as a leak in a closed room with an ignition source, an A2L can burn. Equipment built for these refrigerants includes design features to manage that risk, such as leak detection and controlled fan operation in some designs. Never bypass or disable those features.

What changes for the technician

  • Ignition sources. Think about sparks, open flames and hot surfaces near the work area before opening any part of a system.
  • Ventilation. Fresh airflow helps keep any leaked refrigerant from collecting. R-32 vapor is heavier than air per its safety data sheet, so leaks pool low.
  • Tools and equipment. Recovery machines, scales, gauges and vacuum pumps need to be rated for the refrigerant in use. Do not assume your old tools qualify.
  • Cylinders and transport. Flammable refrigerant cylinders have their own labeling and handling expectations.
  • Training. Manufacturer instructions for these products are more detailed. Read them before touching the equipment.

A3: highly flammable

A3 refrigerants are mostly hydrocarbons such as propane and isobutane. They have been used for years in small, sealed products like some refrigerators and freezers, where the amount of refrigerant is limited by design. The rules and cautions around them are strict because they ignite more easily than A2L. Work on such equipment only when you are trained for it and following the manufacturer's directions.

Certification and flammable refrigerants

Section 608 testing is meant to reflect the work technicians actually do. As flammable refrigerants move from rare to routine, training providers are adding them. EPA's certification page lists the four certification types and says credentials do not expire, but it does not single out A2L refrigerants. Check the EPA's certification page and your test provider for the current exam outline rather than relying on a study guide from a few years ago.

Bottom line

Treat any refrigerant as a hazard until you have read its label and the equipment manufacturer's instructions. For A2L and A3, add one more habit: think about ignition and ventilation before you start, not after something goes wrong.

Field card: A2L and A3 Refrigerants

Read the class code, then read the label and manual.

  • The letter is toxicity; the number is flammability.
  • A2L means mildly flammable; A3 means highly flammable.
  • Mildly flammable can still burn. Control ignition and ventilate.
  • Use tools and recovery gear rated for the refrigerant.
  • Training and manufacturer instructions matter more than ever.
