NJ Boiler Inspections, Certificates, Logbooks and Posting for Owners

An owner-focused overview of New Jersey boiler and pressure vessel inspection cycles, certificate posting, logbooks and reporting, with items to confirm with the Bureau.

Why this matters

In New Jersey, boilers, many pressure vessels and refrigeration plants are regulated by the state Department of Labor's Bureau of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Compliance. This lesson is a plain overview for owners and new techs. It rests on the Bureau's 2020 information guide, its program page and the Bureau's reprint of the law and rules. Fees and limits may have changed since the guide was written.

How often equipment is inspected

The Bureau guide says boilers and refrigeration plants are inspected every 12 months. Hot water heating boilers are on a different cycle: internal inspection every 24 months and external every 12 months. Extra inspections, about six months apart, can be required when conditions warrant or when abuse or an unsafe condition is found. Pressure vessels that are not boilers generally follow a 3 to 5 year cycle depending on use, age and condition; the exact interval for a given vessel is set by the inspection, so ask. We read these figures on the Bureau's own pages, but we did not read the rule text that sets the intervals.

Who inspects, and the first inspection

New or replacement equipment must get its first inspection from the Bureau. After that the owner can choose the State or an authorized insurance company inspector; the guide says insurers do roughly 70 to 80 percent of inspections. After installing new or replacement equipment, the owner has 30 days to notify the Bureau. Municipal permits are also needed, and a town may hold a certificate of occupancy until the inspection is done.

Which equipment counts

The guide's thresholds are worded in a few ways, and small dwellings are exempt, so check the exact test for your building with the Bureau. As a labeled example, boilers serving six or more families or 25 or more people are covered at 110 sq ft of heating surface, 210 kW or 40,000 Btu/hr; water heaters count at 200,000 Btu/hr, 200 F or 120 gallons under the same occupancy test.

Certificate and posting

After a satisfactory inspection the Bureau issues a certificate. It must be framed and displayed where the equipment is, or in the plant office, and be available on request. The jurisdiction number must be permanently attached to the equipment. Not displaying the certificate carries a penalty of $500 to $5,000 per day. The rules also require engineers, contractors, owners and operators to keep required certificates and licenses posted conspicuously.

Logbooks and rounds

The rules call for a boiler operator's log at every plant over 100 horsepower, which each operator reviews and signs at the end of the shift. For low pressure plants, the operator must check the plant at least twice in 24 hours, with at least seven hours between checks, and tours are logged. Small exempt low pressure plants still must keep a log on the premises of their operational inspections. We read these in the Bureau's reprint, so confirm against the official N.J.A.C. text. Rounds are covered in Boiler Operator Rounds, Logs and Test Schedules.

Incidents and testing after service

An "occurrence" is an event that reduces a boiler's pressure-containing capability or needs immediate repair. The owner must promptly send the Bureau chief a detailed report, and tell the Bureau immediately for an injury or explosion. Do not move or disturb the equipment without the chief's permission, except to save a life or limit damage. A Bureau advisory dated September 6, 2023 says boilers must be tested after maintenance or repair, with device operability and set points recorded in the log, maintenance record or invoice. Read the advisory itself for the code details.

Penalties

For using equipment in violation of the inspection law, the guide lists $500 to $10,000 for a first offense and $500 to $25,000 for later offenses.

Check before you rely on this

Call the Bureau at (609) 292-2921 or check the official N.J.A.C. text. This is not legal advice, and your insurer and local code also apply. Still open for us: the exact rule text on intervals and license posting, and a Bureau announcement on pressure vessel inspections that we did not read.

Field card: NJ Boiler Inspections and Logs

Yearly inspection, framed certificate, signed log. Confirm details with the Bureau.

  • Boilers and refrigeration plants: inspected every 12 months. Hot water heating boilers: internal every 24 months, external every 12 (per the Bureau).
  • New or replacement equipment: tell the Bureau within 30 days; the Bureau does the first inspection.
  • Certificate framed and displayed at the equipment or plant office; jurisdiction number stays on the equipment.
  • Plants over 100 hp keep an operator log, signed by each operator at end of shift; low pressure plants are checked at least twice per 24 hours.
  • After any service, test safety devices and record set points; report occurrences to the Bureau and do not disturb the equipment.
  • Verify with the Bureau, (609) 292-2921, and the official N.J.A.C. text.
