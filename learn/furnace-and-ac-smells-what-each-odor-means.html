Furnace and AC Smells: What Each Odor Means

Last updated: 2026-10-16

If your furnace smells like burning, the first question is how long it has been going on and what else you notice. A dusty smell on the first cold day is common. A rotten egg smell, an electrical smell or a carbon monoxide alarm is not. This page sorts the common odors, says how urgent each is, and adds a short tech note so you can follow what a technician tells you.

Start here: leave first, diagnose later

  • Rotten egg or sulfur smell. Natural gas has no smell of its own. Connecticut's gas pipeline regulator explains that gas companies add mercaptan, a harmless chemical that gives gas its odor. Its steps: leave the building if the smell is strong or you are unsure, then call your gas company. Do not flip light switches, use the doorbell, use a phone near the smell, or adjust the thermostat, and do not return until the gas company says it is safe. The federal pipeline safety agency (PHMSA) gives a similar list for a leak and says to call 911 from a safe location.
  • A carbon monoxide alarm. The Consumer Product Safety Commission says to get outside to fresh air, call 911 or the fire department, count heads, and stay out until responders say it is safe. Do not hunt for the source. See our carbon monoxide alarm lesson.
  • Smoke, or a burning electrical or plastic smell. Trane says to shut the system off and call for repair.

Carbon monoxide has no smell

This matters because many people wait for a smell that never comes. The CPSC calls carbon monoxide colorless and odorless, and the EPA calls it colorless, practically odorless and tasteless, with leaking chimneys and furnaces and poorly adjusted combustion equipment among its sources. So a vague exhaust or chemical odor near a fuel-burning appliance is worth taking seriously, but the absence of any smell proves nothing. Only a working CO alarm detects the gas. Our combustion and CO safety basics lesson explains why furnaces make it.

The odor table

The urgency labels below are our own suggested guide, built from the sources cited in each row. They are not a manufacturer or regulator rating. "Wait" means keep an eye on it and watch the clock. "Call soon" means arrange a visit within a few days. "Leave now" means get out of the house first and call from outside. When in doubt, call.

How to read the chart

Worked example: the first cold day

You switch the furnace on for the first time this fall and the house smells like burning toast. Here is how to work through it.

  1. Note the start time. Say the burners start at 6:00 pm.
  2. Rule out the red items. No rotten egg smell, no smoke, no alarm, no electrical or plastic odor. If any is present, stop here and leave or shut the system off as in the table.
  3. Check at 15 minutes. Trane says dust burn-off should fade within about 10 to 15 minutes, so 6:00 + 15 min = 6:15 pm. If it is fading, you are done.
  4. Allow the longer window. Lennox says to let the unit run an hour or two. 1 hour to 2 hours is 60 to 120 minutes, so the latest you should still smell it is 6:00 pm + 120 min = 8:00 pm. That is 4 to 8 times Trane's 15 minutes, which is why we call the normal range a spread, not a single number.
  5. Do the safe checks if it lingers. Past 6:15 pm, check that the filter is clean, since HVAC.com's guide also points to filters, and look near the vents for a fallen object. Do not open the furnace.
  6. Decide at 8:00 pm. Still burning after 120 minutes, or getting stronger, is outside both makers' windows. Shut the furnace off and call.

The smell also should be dust-like. A plastic, rubber, metallic or gunpowder smell, or one concentrated at a single vent, is not burn-off, according to the HVAC.com guide (a repair company, not a maker). Those times are examples from published guidance. Your furnace's own manual governs, and a new furnace can have its own break-in odor.

Field card: Furnace and AC Smells: What Each Odor Means

Smell something: green, amber or red?

  • Rotten egg or sulfur: leave, call the gas company from outside.
  • CO alarm or smoke: out, call 911, stay out.
  • CO itself has no smell.
  • Dusty smell on first heat: time it. 15 minutes (Trane), up to 2 hours (Lennox).
  • Hot plastic or electrical: shut off, call.
  • Oil smell away from the burner: call service; possible tank leak.
  • Musty or dirty sock: filter, then call.
  • Do not rely on smell for refrigerant leaks.
