DHW Priority on Boilers with Indirect Water Heaters: Priority Timeouts and Cold-House Complaints

Last updated: 2026-10-16

When an indirect water heater calls for heat, many systems pause the radiators or baseboard so the boiler can put everything into the tank. That is called domestic hot water priority. It makes showers recover fast, and in very cold weather it is also the reason someone says the house felt chilly during a long shower. This lesson explains how priority works, why timeouts exist, and how to estimate how long a house can coast.

Check the label and the manual first

Your boiler and control manuals rule over this page. Priority is set up differently on every control. Gas, oil and electrical work belongs to a licensed professional where you work.

How priority works

Two things can ask the boiler for heat: a room thermostat (space heating) and the tank sensor or aquastat on the indirect heater. With priority, the tank call wins. A boiler maker's indirect heater manual says the demand for space heating is interrupted until the hot water demand is satisfied, and that this provides the maximum delivery of hot water. A priority relay or boiler control does the interrupting. It can close off the zone valves or stop the heating circulators while the tank pump runs.

Why priority is used

The boiler's output has to be shared. The same maker's manual describes the alternative, non-priority, where output is split between space heating and water heating, so water heating can be reduced when both call. It recommends priority when boiler output is below 100,000 Btu/hr, when the output needed for hot water is more than 50 percent of the output needed for space heating, or when space heating can tolerate interruption during long draws. It also says the heating delay is usually unnoticed because the indirect heater recovers quickly. A national-lab guide says room temperature recovery after hot water priority could be significant, depending on draw demand, duration, outdoor temperature and thermostat cycles.

Priority timeouts and override

Priority has a failure mode. A different boiler maker's manual warns that a failed thermostat or circulator could delay space heating indefinitely when priority is used. That is why many controls limit it. One zone-priority relay described in that manual turns the priority feature off if the priority zone calls for more than 30 minutes, so all zones run independently; the manual says this is meant to prevent freeze-ups if the water heater has a problem. A boiler control maker's instructions describe another version: priority lasts 45 minutes of continuous indirect call, then the control overrides priority and energizes the circulator for space heating. It also releases priority sooner if the tank is satisfied or if the boiler reaches 170 F. Those numbers (30 and 45) belong to two products, not a standard. Your control may differ or have none.

The cold-house complaint

Typical report: "In January the house gets cold when we run the shower, the washer and the dishwasher back to back." Several things stack up. A boiler maker's manual says the indirect heater delivers its rated output only with a boiler that meets the table values, so a smaller boiler makes the call longer. The tank call also bypasses outdoor reset on at least one control we read, so the boiler fires to its high limit during the call; see Outdoor Reset and Weather Compensation. Whether the occupants notice depends on how fast the house loses heat and how much it stores. The example below puts numbers on that.

Worked example: how long can the house coast?

Every input here is an assumption chosen by us for teaching, not a measurement of any house. The Btu definition comes from the U.S. Energy Information Administration; the tank figures come from a boiler maker's manual table.

  • Assumption (Our own): design heat loss 40,000 Btu/hr with the house at 68 F and outdoors at 5 F.
  • Assumption (Our own): effective thermal mass of the house 12,000 Btu per F (about 2,000 sq ft at 6 Btu per sq ft per F).
  • Assumption (Our own): heat stored in the radiators and piping is ignored.
  • Assumption (Our own): boiler output 100,000 Btu/hr, and the emitters can pass all of it during recovery. Real baseboard often cannot, so recovery is slower.

Step 1, heat loss per degree. 40,000 Btu/hr divided by the 63 F difference (68 minus 5) is about 635 Btu/hr per F (Our own arithmetic).

Step 2, loss on a 10 F day. The difference is 68 minus 10 = 58 F. 635 times 58 is about 36,800 Btu/hr (Our own).

Step 3, coasting rate. With no heat input, the house cools by loss divided by thermal mass: 36,800 divided by 12,000 is about 3.1 F per hour (Our own).

Step 4, how long is the priority call? The DOE Energy Saver worksheet counts a shower as 20 gallons, so three showers are 60 gallons. The boiler maker's table lists a continuous draw of 170 gallons per hour for a 40-gallon-class indirect heater (rated with a 180 F boiler supply and 58 F cold water, and a boiler output of 120,000 Btu/hr). 60 divided by 170 is 0.35 hour, about 21 minutes.

Step 5, temperature drop. 3.1 F per hour times 0.35 hour is about 1.1 F (Our own). At a 45-minute limit: 3.1 times 0.75 is about 2.3 F.

Step 6, recovery. Restoring 1.1 F takes 1.1 times 12,000 = 13,200 Btu. Surplus output is 100,000 minus 36,800 = 63,200 Btu/hr. 13,200 divided by 63,200 is 0.21 hour, about 13 minutes at best (Our own).

Colder day. At 0 F the loss is 635 times 68 = about 43,200 Btu/hr, a drop rate of about 3.6 F per hour (Our own). A 21-minute call costs about 1.3 F.

Reading the result. In this matched case a single long draw is barely felt. Complaints come from mismatches: a leaky house with little mass, back-to-back draws, a boiler too small for the coil, or a call stuck on by a failed sensor.

Sizing the indirect and the boiler

The tank's coil only delivers its rating if the boiler can supply that much heat. A boiler maker's table lists a 40-gallon-class heater needing 120,000 Btu/hr of boiler output for its first-hour and continuous ratings, and the larger models more. Another maker's manual says an average residence with one shower or more needs its Model 40 or larger, and to size up for hot tubs or several showers at once. For the heating side, see Sizing a Replacement Hot Water Boiler. A national-lab boiler guide warns that oversized equipment can turn on and off repeatedly before the demand is met, so a big boiler bought for the tank is not free.

Temperatures matter too. One manual says the boiler's maximum supply setting should be at least 30 F above the tank setpoint; the same manual's troubleshooting section gives a different check, at least 10 F above the tank setpoint plus half the supply differential. Manuals disagree, so follow yours.

Combi boilers and priority

A combi has no storage tank, so a hot water draw takes heat straight from the burner and heating pauses for as long as water runs. A federal presentation on combined systems reports that priority is generally unnecessary for radiant floors, because mains pressure drives most of the flow, but considered necessary for hydronic air handlers to keep supply air above 100 F. It also says priority schemes can work well and go unnoticed in low-load homes. Because there is no tank, a long draw on a combi means the heating pause lasts as long as the draw does (our reading, not a measured figure). For causes of no heat or no hot water on a combi, see Combi Boiler: No Hot Water or No Heat.

What a tech checks

  • Read the priority setup. Which control does it, which zones or circulators it pauses, and whether the tank call is wired to the right terminals (one control maker says to check that the indirect end switch is on its indirect terminals).
  • Find the timeout. Is there one, how long is it, and is it adjustable? Compare to how long a real tank call lasts.
  • Time a full tank call. A call that runs very long points to low boiler supply, a weak tank pump, a clogged coil or a bad sensor. See Indirect Water Heaters on a Boiler.
  • Confirm the heating side recovers. If a zone never restarts after a call, see Hot Water, No Heat.
  • Compare boiler output to the tank table and check the check valve on the tank branch.

Field card: DHW priority on boilers

Tank call wins; the house coasts; the timeout is the safety net.

  • Priority pauses space heating so all boiler output goes to the indirect coil.
  • One manual recommends it below 100,000 Btu/hr boiler output or when hot water needs more than half the heating output.
  • Timeouts differ by product: 30 minutes in one relay manual, 45 minutes in one control. Read yours.
  • Our own example: about 3 F per hour coast rate, so a 21-minute call costs about 1 F (assumptions stated above).
  • Check wiring, timeout, call length, boiler output versus the tank table, and zone restart.
  • Combi boilers pause heating for as long as the tap runs.
