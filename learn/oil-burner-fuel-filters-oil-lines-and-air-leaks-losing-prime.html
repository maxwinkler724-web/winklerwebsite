Oil Burner Fuel Filters, Oil Lines and Air Leaks: Why a Burner Loses Prime

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A burner that cannot hold prime may have plenty of oil in the tank. The trouble can be the path between tank and pump, where air can sneak in. This lesson walks that path and the numbers the pump makers publish for it.

Who does this work. Bleeding a pump, changing a filter and replacing fuel line are jobs for a qualified oil burner technician working from the burner and pump instructions. Check New Jersey licensing with the state agencies.

The path from tank to nozzle

Oil leaves the tank through a shutoff valve. Many installations add a fire or safety valve, and a filter sits in the line before the burner. The burner's fuel unit (the pump) pulls oil through all of that, strains it again in its own strainer, raises the pressure and sends it to the nozzle. The model residential code chapter we read (Seattle's 2021 adoption of the International Residential Code, section M2204.2) requires a manual shutoff valve between the oil tank and the burner, with ready access. It also says oil pressure at the appliance inlet must not exceed 3 psi (M2204.3). Beckett's CleanCut manual cites NFPA 31 for its own 3 psi limit and for keeping the return line free of valves. We did not read NFPA 31 itself, so ask the local authority what applies.

Everything upstream of the pump is on the suction side. While the burner runs, the pump is pulling, so the line is under vacuum. A small hole there does not leak oil out. It lets air in.

One-pipe and two-pipe systems

A one-pipe system has a single supply line. The pump passes along only the oil the nozzle uses, and any extra stays inside the pump. A two-pipe system adds a return line to the tank. Suntec's data sheet says its pump ships set up for one-pipe use with no bypass plug installed; for two-pipe use, the return port plug comes out and the bypass plug goes in. Beckett warns that on a one-pipe system the bypass plug must not be installed, because the shaft seal will rupture. Beckett also says the two-pipe return line should end 3 to 4 inches above the supply inlet in the tank, otherwise air can enter and the burner can lose prime.

Why the difference matters for air: Suntec says bleeding in two-pipe operation is automatic, while a one-pipe unit must be bled by loosening the bleeder. That matches the Danfoss note in our ran-out-of-oil lesson.

Vacuum and lift limits from the makers

Lift is the vertical distance from the oil level (worst case, the tank bottom) up to the pump. Each foot of lift costs vacuum, and the line adds more as oil flows through tubing, fittings and filters. Beckett defines running vacuum as the total of all pressure drops from the tank to the pump inlet. The published limits:

  • Suntec A-series data sheet: 6 inches of mercury (in Hg) maximum for single pipe, 12 in Hg for two-pipe, with the second figure stated as the limit to prevent air separating from the oil.
  • Suntec J4/J6 installation sheet: 6 in Hg one-pipe, 13.5 in Hg two-pipe, maximum lift 8 ft one-pipe and 17 ft two-pipe.
  • Beckett CleanCut manual: 6 in Hg one-pipe, 12 in Hg two-pipe, one-pipe lift 8 ft; under 17 in Hg for the CleanCut XL.

Use the figure on your own pump's sheet. What high vacuum means, per these makers: you are past the pump's rated limit, which risks air coming out of solution in the oil, and the burner can then sputter, pulse or lock out. Since running vacuum is the sum of the drops, a clogged filter, kinked tubing, too much length or too much lift all push it up. A tech reads it with a vacuum gauge in a 1/4 inch inlet port (Suntec, Beckett); Beckett's tune-up list names a 0 to 30 in Hg gauge.

Worked example: how long can the line be?

Suppose a tank bottom sits 6 ft below the pump (H = 6 ft), the measured line from tank to pump is 46 ft in total, and the burner has a 0.75 GPH nozzle. All numbers here are made up for practice; the table values come from Suntec's J4/J6 installation sheet.

Part 1, one-pipe vacuum. Suntec gives a one-pipe length formula for 3/8 inch copper tubing, L = (6 − 0.75H) ÷ (0.0086 × Q), and for 1/2 inch tubing L = (6 − 0.75H) ÷ (0.00218 × Q), with L in feet, H in feet and Q in GPH. The 6 in the formula is the 6 in Hg limit. Rearranged (our algebra, not a Suntec chart), the vacuum at the pump inlet is 0.75 × H plus the friction term.

  1. Lift part: 0.75 × 6 = 4.5 in Hg.
  2. Friction, 3/8 inch tubing: 0.0086 × 0.75 × 46 = 0.297, about 0.3 in Hg.
  3. Total: 4.5 + 0.3 = 4.8 in Hg, under the 6 in Hg limit. For 1/2 inch tubing: 0.00218 × 0.75 × 46 = 0.075, so about 4.6 in Hg.
  4. Check against the formula: L = (6 − 4.5) ÷ (0.0086 × 0.75) = 1.5 ÷ 0.00645 = about 233 ft of 3/8 inch tubing. Suntec warns that elbows, valves and filters reduce the length, so real margin is smaller.

Part 2, two-pipe table. Suntec's two-pipe table for the J4 pump at 1725 rpm gives the maximum total line length L = H + R, calculated for 57 SSU oil. At H = 6 ft, 3/8 inch tubing allows 38 ft, and 1/2 inch tubing allows 100 ft. Our line is 46 ft in total. 46 is more than 38, so 3/8 inch tubing is too small for a two-pipe pump on this job, while 1/2 inch passes (46 is less than 100). The sheet marks 3/8 inch tubing "try to avoid." At 3450 rpm the same row falls to 18 ft for 3/8 inch, which shows why you read the row for your pump.

Filters: what the makers say

General Filters' page for its canister filters says the cartridge must be replaced annually. It also says the bowl must be replaced at the first sign of corrosion or another defect, and in any case by the Ultimate Replacement Date on the label. Beckett's tune-up checklist, for the burner, says to remove the filter element and install a new kit, make sure the canister is clean and tight, and check the tank if you find sludge or water. Beckett also says to remove the pump strainer cover, clean the strainer, and replace the cover gasket. A cartridge that clogs between annual visits points toward water or sludge in the tank, a subject for the tank lesson. The Gar-Ber page describes spin-on cartridges, some with a water block, and a vacuum restriction indicator that shows when a filter needs changing; it gave no interval or micron rating we could read, so use the maker's data for your model.

Every filter change opens the suction line, so the canister seal, O-ring and washer are air-sealing parts. InspectApedia notes the O-ring and fiber washer on center-bolt canisters.

Air leaks: fittings, flares and what they look like

Beckett's checklist says to check that oil lines are clean and not kinked, that all fittings are leak-free, and to use flare fittings, not compression style. Beckett's CleanCut manual and Suntec's J4/J6 sheet both say not to use Teflon tape or compression fittings and that lines must be airtight. Suntec adds that on a one-pipe unit the inlet line joints must be perfectly tight to maintain prime. A Suntec AN-series guide says air bubbles in the flame or fuel point to a leak in the suction line or at the pump, and tells you to retighten fittings and replace the cover gasket if the pump will not draw.

An air leak may show no oil on the floor. Per the Suntec guide, a leak can show up as air bubbles in the flame. A burner that loses its flame is shut off by the control, as covered in the primary control lesson; it can lock out. Do not keep pressing reset, since oil sprayed with no flame can ignite later with a bang (see the ran-out-of-oil lesson).

Bleeding the pump

For a one-pipe unit, Beckett and Suntec both say to open the bleed valve one turn counterclockwise and bleed until all air bubbles disappear, then retighten it securely. Beckett adds: keep bleeding for 15 seconds after the last air is seen. Beckett's tune-up checklist says no froth should be visible at the bleed port. On a two-pipe unit, bleeding is automatic but can be sped up by opening the bleed valve. Pressure setting follows; see the nozzle and pump pressure lesson.

Field card: Oil Supply Path and Prime

Air gets in on the suction side.

  • Path: tank, shutoff and safety valve, filter, tubing, pump strainer, pump, nozzle.
  • One-pipe: no bypass plug, bleed by hand. Two-pipe: bypass plug in, return line ends 3 to 4 inches above the inlet, self-bleeds.
  • Vacuum limits are per pump sheet: Suntec A-series 6 in Hg one-pipe, 12 in Hg two-pipe; J4/J6 6 and 13.5.
  • Lift max (J4/J6, CleanCut): 8 ft one-pipe. Two-pipe J4/J6: 17 ft.
  • Flare fittings, no compression, no Teflon tape.
  • Filter cartridge: replace annually (General Filters). Strainer and gasket at the tune-up (Beckett).
  • Example: lift 6 ft, 0.75 GPH, 46 ft of 3/8 inch: about 4.8 in Hg one-pipe. Two-pipe J4 1725 rpm table: 38 ft allowed for 3/8 inch, so 46 ft fails; 1/2 inch allows 100 ft.
