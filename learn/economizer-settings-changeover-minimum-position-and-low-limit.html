Economizer Settings: Changeover, Minimum Position and Low Limit

Last updated: 2026-10-16

An economizer can have perfect dampers and still waste money or leave a room stuffy because its settings are wrong. These are the four settings worth understanding.

Safety first

Setting a controller means a live unit on a roof with moving dampers and fans. Lock out before reaching into the damper section, and when a procedure needs power, follow the maker’s steps. Damper and actuator checks are in RTU Economizer Fault Diagnosis and Damper Actuator Checks; the basic idea is in Economizers Basics.

Changeover: when is outdoor air good enough?

Changeover is the point where the controller decides outdoor air is suitable for free cooling. Dry-bulb changeover looks only at outdoor temperature. Enthalpy changeover also counts moisture, since humid air carries more heat than its temperature suggests. A public-agency training document (PNNL) uses a Honeywell controller with letter settings A to D as an example of matching the setting to climate. In that example, the dry-climate setting keeps the economizer enabled below about 85°F dry bulb (or within a stated enthalpy range), and the humid-climate setting disables it above about 55°F dry bulb (or a lower enthalpy range). Those figures belong to that controller. Your unit’s manual gives its own settings.

The practical lesson: in general, a humid-climate unit left on a dry-climate setting can pull in muggy air and add moisture, and a dry-climate unit on a humid setting loses free cooling it could have had.

Differential control

Instead of a fixed limit, differential control compares outdoor air to return air. In the same document, the economizer is enabled only when outdoor air is cooler than return air. A differential enthalpy setup uses one sensor outdoors and one in the mixed or return air. It needs two working sensors, so a bad one skews the decision.

Minimum position

When outdoor air is not suitable, or the building is occupied, the outdoor damper should sit at a minimum position for ventilation, not closed. The PNNL example sets minimum on the controller’s potentiometer, then says to verify the actual outdoor-air percentage with charts or formulas. That verification is the point: a dial setting is only a guess until the air is measured. The document gives no default percentage, and neither does this lesson. The right amount is a design and code question (see Rooftop Unit Basics).

Mixed-air low limit

Mixed air is the blend of return and outdoor air before the coil. If too much cold outdoor air comes in, the supply gets cold. One maker (Trane, ReliaTel controls) describes a low limit: when mixed air falls below 45°F, the actuator closes to its minimum position, and normal operation resumes once mixed air rises above 48°F. Treat that as one maker’s example. A unit that keeps hopping between closed and open near the limit may have a sensor reading wrong.

Sensor flash codes

Controllers often report a failed sensor with flashes. On the same maker’s controls, 4 flashes point to the return-air sensor and 8 flashes to the mixed-air sensor. Another brand will use other codes. The maker’s order of troubleshooting is: check the voltage with the sensor connected, then the circuit resistance (wiring), then the sensor itself. Resistance charts live in the maker’s service manual.

Under review

Linkage problems and exact settings for your unit are outside these sources. Use the maker’s documents.

Field card: Economizer Settings: Changeover, Minimum Position and Low Limit

Right hardware, wrong setting, still wasted energy. Settings come from the unit’s manual.

  • Dry-bulb looks at outdoor temperature; enthalpy also counts moisture.
  • One agency’s example: dry-climate setting enables free cooling below about 85°F, humid-climate setting disables it above about 55°F dry bulb.
  • Differential control enables the economizer only if outdoor air is cooler than return air.
  • Set minimum position, then measure the actual outdoor-air percentage.
  • One maker’s example: below 45°F mixed air closes to minimum, back to normal above 48°F; 4 flashes return sensor, 8 flashes mixed-air sensor.
  • Sensor troubleshooting order: voltage, then wiring resistance, then sensor.
