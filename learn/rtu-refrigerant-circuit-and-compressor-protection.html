RTU Refrigerant Circuit and Compressor Protection

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the Commercial Cooling Guide

A compressor is the most expensive part of a rooftop unit, and it is fragile in specific ways: liquid, heat, lost oil and rapid restarts. RTUs include protections for each.

The circuit in one paragraph

An RTU has the same refrigeration cycle as any air conditioner: compressor, condenser coil, metering device and evaporator coil. If that is new, start with Refrigeration Cycle Basics, Metering Devices: TXV and Piston and Compressor Basics and Failure Modes. Most RTUs ship with a factory charge, which is why gauge hookups are kept to a minimum on small units.

High-pressure switch

A high-pressure switch opens the control circuit if pressure is too high for the refrigerant and unit. Trade sources list common causes such as a failed condenser fan motor, a dirty or blocked condenser coil, bent fins, or a blockage in the system. Some are manual reset, because a trip means something should be fixed. Repeatedly resetting without a diagnosis risks bigger failures. Some control modules count trips within an hour and then lock out.

Low-pressure switch

A low-pressure switch protects against low suction pressure, which can freeze the evaporator and let liquid refrigerant reach the compressor. It usually resets on its own when pressure recovers. Causes include low charge from a leak, a failed or slow indoor blower, a plugged coil or restricted airflow, or a failed component. One control module manual describes letting the compressor run for two minutes on a low-pressure opening before shutting down. A source also notes a low-pressure switch must not be adjusted without checking the refrigerant's pressure-temperature relationship. Where the switch is preset and not adjustable, leave it that way. See Superheat and Subcooling Basics.

Anti-short-cycle delay

Every start draws high inrush current and moves oil through the system. If a compressor restarts before pressures have equalized and the motor has cooled, damage follows. A time delay prevents quick restarts. A control module manual gives a 300-second anti-short-cycle delay and a random start delay of about 270 to 300 seconds after power loss, so several units do not start at once. A relay article says delays under about three minutes give little protection. These are examples; your unit's controls set the real timing.

Crankcase heaters and startup

When a compressor is off, refrigerant vapor can move to the coldest place in the system, often the compressor, and condense into the oil. At startup the sudden pressure drop can make that mixture foam and wash oil away from the bearings. Liquid entering the compression chamber can break parts. A crankcase heater keeps oil warmer than the rest of the system to stop this. HVAC School advises energizing the heater for at least 24 hours before first startup on a new installation or a system that has been off for a long time, and keeping it energized in every off cycle. One article warns heaters can be overpowered in very cold weather and that pump-down and accumulators are other protections. Some scroll compressors do not require a heater below a charge limit set by the compressor maker, so check the manual.

Rotation and phase

Three-phase scroll compressors must turn the right way. See RTU Startup and Checkout Basics. A phase monitor or protection module may be installed; do not remove it to get the unit to run.

Refrigerant safety

Follow EPA rules and the unit's label for refrigerant handling. Newer RTUs may use A2L refrigerants; one manufacturer's manual says to use only equipment rated for the refrigerant, to recover before repair, and never to pressurize a system with air or gases containing oxygen. Lock out power, and remember that oil like POE absorbs moisture readily. See Refrigerant Safety.

Field card: RTU Refrigerant Circuit and Compressor Protection

Protect the compressor from liquid, heat and short cycling.

  • High-pressure and low-pressure switches protect the compressor. Never jumper them.
  • Diagnose the reason for a trip. Repeated resets risk damage.
  • Anti-short-cycle delay gives pressures and oil time to settle.
  • Crankcase heater on before first start and during every off cycle.
  • Use the manufacturer's values for pressures, delays and charge limits.
