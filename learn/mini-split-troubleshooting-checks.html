Mini-Split Troubleshooting Checks

Mini-splits are small, quiet and talkative. They usually tell you what is wrong through a blinking light or a code, if you know where to look it up.

Start with the complaint and the safe basics

Ask what the customer sees: no cooling, no heat, noise, water, a blinking light. Then look before touching. Check that power is available and the breaker is on. Note the mode, setpoint and whether the remote has batteries. Many service calls end at a setting or a dirty filter. Before opening any panel, turn off and lock out the power, and remember the capacitors and boards in an outdoor unit can hold a charge. Refrigerant and high voltage are not for guessing.

Use the fault code, from the manufacturer

Most mini-splits report problems through a blinking indicator on the indoor unit, a display on the remote, or an LED on the outdoor board. The meaning of a code is different for every brand and often every model line, so use the manufacturer's fault-code table for the exact model. Do not carry codes in your head from one brand to another. The table usually points to a circuit: a sensor, a fan motor, communication between the units, the compressor drive or protection.

A general order of checks

  • Power and communication. Is the supply correct at the outdoor unit, and is the cable between indoor and outdoor intact and connected as the diagram shows? A communication fault often means a wiring or connector problem.
  • Airflow. Dirty filters, a clogged indoor coil or a blocked outdoor coil cause poor performance, freeze-ups and protective shutdowns.
  • Sensors. Temperature sensors that read wrongly make the board behave oddly. Check them with the maker's resistance chart rather than assuming.
  • Fans and motors. Does each fan spin freely and respond when called?
  • Drain. A plugged or poorly sloped drain makes water at the head. Some units have a float or water-level safety.
  • Refrigerant side. Only after the easy items. Measure the way the manufacturer specifies; many inverter units use charging methods that differ from a fixed-speed system, so see Superheat and Subcooling Basics for the general idea and the maker for the method. If refrigerant has to be added or recovered, follow recovery rules and the A2L precautions where they apply.

Habits that save time

  • Write down the code, the model and serial number before you change anything.
  • Change one thing at a time and re-test.
  • Take a photo of the wiring before disconnecting anything.
  • If a protection device trips, find out why. Never jumper out a safety or sensor to keep a unit running.

When to stop

If you reach a step that needs a specialty tool, a licence you do not hold, or a procedure you do not know, stop and get the right person. Compare with the general Troubleshooting Method lesson. The rule that overrides everything on this page: the installation and service instructions for the specific unit in front of you, and the rules where you work, always govern.

Field card: Mini-Split Troubleshooting Checks

Look up the code in the maker's table, then work simple to complex.

  • Lock out power before opening panels.
  • Use the manufacturer's fault-code table for the exact model.
  • Check power, wiring, airflow, sensors, fans and drain before refrigerant.
  • Change one thing at a time.
  • Never bypass a safety or sensor.
