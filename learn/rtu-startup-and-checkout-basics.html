RTU Startup and Checkout Basics

A rooftop unit that is started wrong can fail in its first week. This lesson covers the general shape of a startup and checkout. The manufacturer's startup form for the exact unit is the real checklist.

Start with the paperwork and the roof

Review the unit's installation and startup instructions, wiring diagram and any building automation requirements before you climb. Plan safe roof access, ladder use and lockout/tagout. HVAC School tips for RTU work also suggest bringing the right materials and confirming controller access if the job needs it. Many units have more than one disconnect, so find them all.

Look before you power up

A manufacturer's startup article lists checks that apply across many units:

  • Look for shipping damage and make sure the unit is level with clearance for airflow and service.
  • Check that access doors and handles work.
  • Remove all shipping braces and brackets.
  • Tighten fan set screws and spin fans by hand to see that they move freely.
  • Confirm the supply voltage matches the nameplate, connections are tight, any transformer tap is correct, and overcurrent protection matches the nameplate.
  • Install field-supplied sensors where the instructions say.
For belt drives, see Belt Drives and Sheave Basics.

Filters, coils, and airflow

Do not run the unit without return air filters in place. Check that coils are clean and that outdoor air inlets are not blocked. Airflow affects everything downstream, from heat exchanger temperature to compressor protection. See Airflow and Static Pressure Basics.

Rotation matters

On three-phase units, motors and scroll compressors can run backward if the phase wiring is wrong. One manufacturer's service manual describes the idea for a scroll: connect gauges, energize, and look for suction pressure to fall and discharge pressure to rise. If pressures do not move normally, power is shut off and two power leads are swapped, then the check is repeated. Backward running makes noise, gives no cooling, and over time can damage the compressor. The same manual says if the check fails, the evaporator fan may also be turning the wrong way. Always follow the steps in your unit's manual and lock out power before changing leads. A phase monitor, where fitted, may also need to be set up correctly.

Cooling checks, in general

Compare operating readings with the manufacturer's data. HVAC School RTU tips suggest limiting gauge hookups on small factory-charged units because each hookup loses some refrigerant, and using suction pressure and superheat as diagnostic data. Thermal imaging can help compare units in a set. Discharge line temperature can warn of compressor overheating. See RTU Refrigerant Circuit and Compressor Protection.

Heating checks, in general

Gas heat has its own checks: gas piping leak test with a leak-detection solution (never an open flame), proper manifold pressure, temperature rise, and clear flue and air intake terminals. See RTU Gas Heat Section Basics.

Controls, economizer and the finish

Run the controls through their modes. Inspect any economizer, energy recovery, or variable-speed devices, and know where good reference material is for them. See Economizers Basics. Finish by closing panels, noting readings on the startup form, and recording what you found.

Field card: RTU Startup and Checkout Basics

The startup form for that exact unit rules. This is the outline.

  • Use the manufacturer's startup form for that exact unit.
  • Lock out power, find every disconnect, plan the roof access.
  • Remove shipping braces, check fans spin, verify voltage against the nameplate.
  • Never run without filters. Check rotation on three-phase motors and scrolls.
  • Record readings so the next tech has a baseline.
