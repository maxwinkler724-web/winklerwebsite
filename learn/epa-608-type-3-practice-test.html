EPA 608 Type III Practice Test (Free, Explained)

Last updated: 2026-10-16 · 25 questions

Type III covers low-pressure appliances such as centrifugal chillers.

The real exam is a multiple-choice test given by an EPA-approved certifying organization. Question counts, passing scores and open- or closed-book rules differ by organization, so confirm them with the organization you plan to test with and on the EPA Section 608 page (checked 2026-10-16).

How to use this test. Read each question, choose your answer, then open “Show answer” to see why and where it comes from. These are original practice questions, not real exam questions. Every explanation links to the source it is based on.

1. Which is a low-pressure refrigerant?

  1. R-22
  2. R-123
  3. R-134a
  4. R-410A
Show answer

Answer: B. R-123

R-123 is a low-pressure refrigerant, unlike R-22, R-134a and R-410A.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 low-pressure appliance definition (names R-11, R-123, R-113, R-245fa)

2. Why do low-pressure chillers need a purge unit?

  1. To add oil
  2. To remove air and non-condensables that leak in
  3. To raise pressure
  4. For leak testing
Show answer

Answer: B. To remove air and non-condensables that leak in

Low-pressure chillers operate below atmosphere, so air and moisture leak in and must be purged.

Source: ESCO 608 Type III manual p.18 (purge unit removes non-condensables)

3. Leaks in a low-pressure chiller typically:

  1. Push refrigerant out only
  2. Draw air and moisture in
  3. Do nothing
  4. Raise pressure
Show answer

Answer: B. Draw air and moisture in

Because operation is below atmospheric pressure.

Source: ESCO 608 Type III manual p.18 (leaks let air in)

4. The rupture disc relieves at about:

  1. 5 psig
  2. 15 psig
  3. 50 psig
  4. 150 psig
Show answer

Answer: B. 15 psig

Low-pressure chiller rupture discs are typically rated to relieve around 15 psig.

Source: Trane CDHF CenTraVac IOM (rupture disk breaks above 15 psig)

5. Recovery goal for low-pressure systems is:

  1. 0 psig
  2. A deep vacuum, 25 mm Hg absolute
  3. 10 in Hg
  4. 50% of charge
Show answer

Answer: B. A deep vacuum, 25 mm Hg absolute

Per 40 CFR 82.156 (low-pressure: 25 mm Hg absolute).

Source: 40 CFR 82.156 Table 1 / 82.158 Table 2 (low-pressure 25 mm Hg absolute)

6. Before opening a low-pressure chiller to service tubes:

  1. Evacuate to deep vacuum
  2. Bring to atmospheric pressure and remove refrigerant first
  3. Run without refrigerant
  4. Heat with torch
Show answer

Answer: B. Bring to atmospheric pressure and remove refrigerant first

The vessel must reach atmospheric pressure and have refrigerant removed before opening it.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(a)(1)(ii) (pressurize to no higher than 0 psig before opening)

7. When raising pressure for tube work, you should:

  1. Use compressed air
  2. Use controlled warm water or heating blankets, staying below the rupture disc rating (not nitrogen)
  3. Open a valve
  4. Use a torch
Show answer

Answer: B. Use controlled warm water or heating blankets, staying below the rupture disc rating (not nitrogen)

For low-pressure refrigerants, EPA rules bar pressurizing with methods such as nitrogen that need purging afterward. Use controlled heat (warm water or heating blankets) and stay under the rupture disc rating.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.156(a)(1)(ii); leak-test guide (heat preferred)

8. Water in the refrigerant side of a chiller can cause:

  1. Lower power
  2. Freezing and corrosion
  3. Cooler water
  4. Nothing
Show answer

Answer: B. Freezing and corrosion

Water in the refrigerant side causes freezing and corrosion, damaging tubes and components.

Background source: ESCO 608 Type III manual p.18 (trapped water can freeze during evacuation)

9. R-11 and R-123 chillers are typically:

  1. Reciprocating
  2. Centrifugal
  3. Rotary screw only
  4. Scroll
Show answer

Answer: B. Centrifugal

R-11 and R-123 are used in low-pressure centrifugal chillers, not reciprocating or scroll units.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.152 + ESCO p.18 (R-11/R-123 low-pressure, purge on centrifugal chillers)

10. Recovery from a low-pressure chiller should be done with:

  1. Any vapor pump
  2. Certified recovery equipment rated for low pressure
  3. A vacuum cleaner
  4. Nitrogen only
Show answer

Answer: B. Certified recovery equipment rated for low pressure

Low-pressure chillers need recovery equipment rated for low pressure to avoid damage or leaks.

Source: 40 CFR 82.158 (certified equipment; Table 2 low-pressure level)

11. Which agency administers Section 608?

  1. OSHA
  2. EPA
  3. DOE
  4. NIST
Show answer

Answer: B. EPA

Section 608 of the Clean Air Act is administered by the EPA.

Source: EPA Section 608 technician certification types

12. An HCFC is:

  1. R-12
  2. R-22
  3. R-134a
  4. R-32
Show answer

Answer: B. R-22

R-22 has hydrogen, chlorine, fluorine, carbon.

Source: EPA Ozone-Depleting Substances (HCFC-22 listed as HCFC)

13. A CFC is:

  1. R-410A
  2. R-12
  3. R-1234yf
  4. R-290
Show answer

Answer: B. R-12

R-12 is a CFC, containing only chlorine, fluorine and carbon with no hydrogen.

Source: EPA Ozone-Depleting Substances (CFC-12 listed as CFC)

14. Which refrigerant is an HFO?

  1. R-22
  2. R-134a
  3. R-1234yf
  4. R-12
Show answer

Answer: C. R-1234yf

R-1234yf is a hydrofluoroolefin (HFO) with a double bond, giving it a very short atmospheric life.

Source: Wikipedia R-1234yf (hydrofluoroolefin, HFO)

15. GWP measures:

  1. Toxicity
  2. Heat-trapping ability relative to CO2
  3. Ozone harm
  4. Flammability
Show answer

Answer: B. Heat-trapping ability relative to CO2

GWP compares how much heat a gas traps over time relative to carbon dioxide, which has GWP 1.

Source: EPA Understanding Global Warming Potentials

16. Hose connections should have:

  1. Shutoff valves close to the end to limit releases
  2. No valves
  3. Only tape
  4. Brass caps only
Show answer

Answer: A. Shutoff valves close to the end to limit releases

Shutoff valves near hose ends limit refrigerant released when disconnecting hoses.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.158(d)(7) (low-loss fittings on all hoses)

17. The best place to read a cylinder's capacity:

  1. Hose
  2. Cylinder markings/tare weight
  3. Gauge
  4. Dryer
Show answer

Answer: B. Cylinder markings/tare weight

The stamped markings and tare weight on the cylinder show its capacity reliably.

Background source: HVACR School: recovery tank handling (tare weight and water capacity stamped on cylinder)

18. A recovery cylinder should be filled no more than about:

  1. 100% liquid full
  2. 80% liquid full by volume
  3. 50% liquid full
  4. Any amount
Show answer

Answer: B. 80% liquid full by volume

Industry guidance is to stop at about 80 percent liquid full at 77 F (not a quoted DOT rule), leaving room for liquid expansion; follow cylinder markings and your supplier.

Background source: HVACR School: recovery cylinder fill (80% liquid by volume, AHRI Guideline K)

19. A technician must have what to purchase R-410A in most cases?

  1. Nothing
  2. EPA 608 certification
  3. A CDL
  4. A fire permit
Show answer

Answer: B. EPA 608 certification

Sale of refrigerant, including HFCs since 2018, is restricted to certified technicians with limited exceptions (40 CFR 82.154(c)).

Source: 40 CFR 82.154(c)(1) (sale restricted to certified technicians)

20. Recycling equipment cleans refrigerant by:

  1. Distilling
  2. Filtering and oil separation
  3. Freezing
  4. Burning
Show answer

Answer: B. Filtering and oil separation

Recycling equipment filters contaminants and separates oil, but does not reach reclaim purity.

Source: EPA Section 608: recovering, recycling and reclaiming (recycling = oil separation + filter-driers)

21. A moisture indicator sight glass turning color shows:

  1. Flammable gas
  2. Moisture in the refrigerant
  3. Low oil
  4. Overcharge
Show answer

Answer: B. Moisture in the refrigerant

The moisture indicator changes color when moisture is present in the refrigerant.

Source: Sanhua DSG liquid-line drier/sight glass (moisture indicator color zones)

22. Acid in a system is found with:

  1. Acid test kit on oil sample
  2. Soap bubbles
  3. Thermometer
  4. Ear
Show answer

Answer: A. Acid test kit on oil sample

An acid test kit on an oil sample reveals acid contamination, often from compressor burnout.

Source: Qwik acids in refrigeration (check oil sample with test kit; burnout)

23. Goggles and gloves are needed because:

  1. Style
  2. Frostbite and eye injury risk
  3. Noise
  4. Heat
Show answer

Answer: B. Frostbite and eye injury risk

Refrigerant can cause frostbite on skin and serious eye injury, so PPE is essential.

Source: Open Exam Prep: PPE and refrigerant exposure (frostbite, eye injury)

24. Where should a technician NOT release refrigerant?

  1. Into a recovery cylinder
  2. Into the atmosphere
  3. Into a reclaimer's drum
  4. Into a recovery machine
Show answer

Answer: B. Into the atmosphere

Venting refrigerant to the atmosphere is prohibited under Section 608, so release is never allowed.

Source: 40 CFR 82.154(a)(1) (no intentional release)

25. A vacuum pump alone is NOT a substitute for:

  1. Evacuation
  2. Recovery
  3. Leak testing
  4. Drying
Show answer

Answer: B. Recovery

You recover first, then evacuate.

Background source: 40 CFR 82.156/82.158 (recovery to required evacuation levels with certified equipment)
