Ice Machine Cycle: Three Makers Compared

Every cube ice machine runs fill, freeze, harvest and bin check, but each maker uses different sensors, timers and alarms.

Same loop, different numbers

Ice Machine Basics: The Freeze and Harvest Cycle gives the general loop. This lesson sets three makers' manuals next to each other, so you can see how much the details differ. Every number belongs to the one model named and is an example, not a setting. Use the manual for the machine in front of you.

Example 1: a Hoshizaki crescent cuber service manual

After a 5-second delay the machine fills for one minute, repeating until the float switch closes. Harvest starts the timer when a thermistor reads about 48 degrees F. Harvest has a 2-minute minimum, and the table gives 3 to 5 minutes average with a 20-minute backup. Freeze holds at least 5 minutes after the thermistor reaches 36 degrees F, and the float switch ends it. Expected freeze is 20 to 40 minutes, and freeze timer options run 50 to 100 minutes. A pump-out drains the tank on the second harvest after start, then every 10 cycles by default. Beeps flag alarms: one for high evaporator temperature, two for a harvest over 20 minutes twice in a row, three for a freeze over the timer twice in a row.

Example 2: a Scotsman training sheet

This model fills the reservoir before the pump or compressor starts. Freeze ends when a water level sensor sees water falling, and fans go off just before harvest to build heat. Harvest time is learned from the previous cycle's release time. Bin full is two photo-electric eyes: if blocked 20 seconds or more, it shuts down after the next harvest and waits 4 minutes. Two maximum harvests in a row shut it down, and diagnostic lights, not beeps, point to the water or refrigeration side.

Example 3: a Manitowoc S model manual

This one purges water first, prechills the evaporator, then freezes. Harvest begins when water touches an ice thickness probe for about 10 seconds, after a 6-minute freeze lock. Maximum freeze is 60 minutes, and maximum harvest 3.5 minutes on a single evaporator. A bin switch or curtain held open 30 seconds shuts the machine for 3 minutes. Safety limits count repeats before stopping the unit. The manual says to clean and sanitize at least every six months and to use only its approved cleaner and sanitizer.

Testing parts: one example

Manuals tell you how to prove a part before you replace it. The Hoshizaki manual gives a thermistor table, with about 6.0 kilohms at 32 degrees F, and says a reading in ice water should land between 4.7 and 6.2 kilohms or the thermistor is replaced. For the bin control it has you clear ice from the control, confirm the power light is on, press the actuator and watch the light flash, then disconnect the two-pin connector and check continuity: closed when calling for ice, open when pressed. It also has you press the actuator during the first five minutes of a freeze to confirm the machine shuts off. If a control passes, the board is the next suspect, not the first. Another maker will use different parts and steps, which is the reason to open that maker's manual.

What they share

  • A fill or purge, a prechill, a freeze that ends on a sensor, a hot-gas harvest, and a bin control.
  • Timers that stop a stuck stage: a maximum freeze, a maximum harvest, and a count of repeats before shutdown.
  • Alarm language that differs: beeps on one, lights on another.

How to use this in the field

  • Find which stage is wrong, then find that maker's sensor for that stage.
  • Test the sensor and bin control by the manual's method before replacing a board. Thermistors, for example, are checked against a resistance table and ice-water reading.
  • Look at water first. The Hoshizaki manual points to a filter or softener for hard water, and to a clogged filter or too little flow as causes of no fill. See Ice Machine Water Quality, Scale and Sanitizing.
  • Supply voltage and ambient limits are on the spec sheet. The Hoshizaki manual lists voltage, ambient and water limits and auto-resetting alarms for low and high voltage.

Lock out before opening panels, never bypass the bin control or a high-temperature safety, and sanitize only with the maker's chemicals.
