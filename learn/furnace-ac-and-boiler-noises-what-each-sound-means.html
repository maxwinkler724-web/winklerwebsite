Furnace, AC and Boiler Noises: What Each Sound Means

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A furnace making a loud noise is a good reason to stop and listen. Some sounds are harmless and some mean you should shut the equipment off now. This page sorts the common ones, says how urgent each is, and gives a short tech note so you can follow what the technician tells you.

Start here: the shut-off-now list

These four situations skip the guessing. Act first, diagnose later.

  • A boom or bang each time the burners light. Trane lists banging among the warning signs of furnace trouble and says to shut the furnace off in the meantime while you call for repair. Delayed ignition means the burners do not light right away, so gas and air build up in the combustion chamber before they catch, and Goodman's GR9S80 manual gives it its own troubleshooting section. Turn the furnace off at its switch and have a technician look at it before it runs again.
  • A rotten egg smell, with or without a sound. Connecticut's gas pipeline regulator explains that gas companies add mercaptan to give natural gas its rotten egg smell. Its steps: if the smell is strong or you are unsure, leave the building, then call your gas company from a phone away from the odor. Avoid light switches, the doorbell, phones in the house and anything else that could spark, and do not adjust the thermostat. The federal pipeline safety agency adds that a hiss or roar can be a sign of escaping gas, and says to call 911 from a safe place.
  • A burning smell, or smoke. Trane says a burning-toast smell the first time you run the heat is usually dust burning off and should fade in 10 to 15 minutes. A burning electrical or plastic smell means shut the system off and call for repair. Carrier calls smoke or a burning smell an urgent safety warning on a furnace.
  • A carbon monoxide alarm. The Consumer Product Safety Commission says to get everyone outside to fresh air, call 911 or the fire department, count heads, and stay out until responders say it is safe. Do not hunt for the source. Our carbon monoxide alarm lesson and combustion and CO safety basics cover the rest.

The sound table

The urgency labels below are our own suggested guide, built from the makers' advice cited in each row; when in doubt, call. "Wait" means it is reasonable to keep an eye on it and book normal service. "Call soon" means arrange a visit within a few days and do not ignore it. "Shut off now" means turn that equipment off and call. Trane's general rule is worth keeping in mind: if a normally quiet unit suddenly gets loud, shut it off and call for repair.

Where each sound usually comes from

Worked example: a boom at light-off

This is what a technician works through, not a homeowner job. The order comes from the delayed ignition section of Goodman's installation and service manual for its GR9S80 family of 80 percent furnaces. The manual says delayed ignition is unlikely unless safety controls have been bypassed or altered, and that if it happens you check these in order:

  1. Gas pressure. Put a manometer on the gas valve inlet tap and read it with the burners firing and other gas appliances running. The manual's inlet range for natural gas is 4.5 to 10.0 inches of water column. Say the reading is 3.8: that is 4.5 - 3.8 = 0.7 inches below the minimum, so the supply or piping needs work, or a call to the gas utility. Then read manifold pressure at the valve outlet. Target is 3.5 inches, within plus or minus 0.3, so 3.5 - 0.3 = 3.2 up to 3.5 + 0.3 = 3.8. A reading of 3.9 is outside that band.
  2. Burner position. Burners should sit in their locating slots, level front to back and side to side.
  3. Carry-over. If the lighter tube or cross lighter is blocked, clean it. This is the crossover path that lets flame jump burner to burner.
  4. Orifices. Replace any main burner orifice that is deformed or not aligned with its burner.

The readings above are made-up examples. The limits come from that manual, and your furnace's own manual governs. The manual does not list ignition timing as a check in this section, so we do not teach it as one. For igniter care see hot surface igniter care.

Field card: Furnace, AC and Boiler Noises: What Each Sound Means

New loud noise: stop, listen, then pick wait, call soon or shut off.

  • Boom at burner light-off: shut off, call.
  • Rotten egg smell: leave, call the gas company from outside. Hiss plus gas smell: same.
  • Burning electrical or plastic smell, smoke: shut off, call.
  • CO alarm: outside, call 911, stay out.
  • Pop or tick in ducts after the blower stops: usually expansion.
  • Squeal or grind: bearings or belt; call soon.
  • Hum at the outdoor unit, no start: shut off at the disconnect.
  • Do not open panels, touch gas piping or handle refrigerant.
