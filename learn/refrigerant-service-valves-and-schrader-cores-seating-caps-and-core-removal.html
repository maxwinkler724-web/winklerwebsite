Refrigerant Service Valves and Schrader Cores: Seating, Caps and Core Removal

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Every outdoor unit has two service valves, and each one has a small access port with a Schrader core inside. They look like plain brass fittings, but a loose cap, a stem run the wrong way or an over-tightened core can leak charge for years. This lesson covers how the valves work, how makers say to open them, and why the little caps matter.

Check the unit's own manual

Check the unit's own manual. Torque values and steps differ by maker and model. This page was built from installation manuals and tool pages read in 2026 and is general education. If the label on a valve cap gives a torque, that label wins.

Two kinds of service valve

Stem (angle) type. A threaded stem moves a seat. Turned all the way in (clockwise) it is front seated: the line toward the outdoor unit is closed off, and the gauge port is open to the line set side. Turned all the way out it is back seated: the stem rests against its stop, the port to the unit is open, and the stem seal is taken out of play. Lennox installation manuals say to back the stem out counterclockwise until it stops, using a hex-head extension: 3/16 inch for liquid-line valves and 5/16 inch for vapor-line valves.

Ball type. A quarter-turn valve. Lennox says to remove the stem cap and rotate the stem 90 degrees counterclockwise to open and clockwise to close. Trane's installation manual for a 4TWR5 heat pump says the vapor valve takes a 1/4 turn counterclockwise to fully open, while its liquid valve is the hex-stem kind: insert a 3/16 inch hex wrench and back out until the stem just touches the rolled edge, about five turns. Trane's older installer's guide says its liquid valve is not a back-seating valve, and the 4TWR5 manual warns to use extreme caution when opening the liquid valve.

Open them last

A Lennox 13HPX manual cautions not to open the service valves until the line set and indoor coil have been leak tested and evacuated. After evacuation, the Lennox XC15 manual says to open the liquid and suction valves to release the charge, then put the stem caps back. See Evacuation and Deep Vacuum Basics for the vacuum steps and Leaks and Leak Repair Basics for finding a leak once charge is in.

The Schrader port and its core

The access port holds a spring-loaded core, the same kind of valve you see on a tire. A hose fitting has a pin that pushes the core open; pull the hose off and the spring closes it. The cap is the backup seal. That is why Lennox lists a "gauge port seal cap" torque of its own. The Lennox and Trane manuals also tell you to remove the cores before brazing so heat does not harm them, and to put them back after the valve body cools. Trane adds to replace the pressure tap core before attaching hoses for evacuation. For hose handling, see Hooking Up Manifold Gauges.

Why the cap is a seal

Yellow Jacket's RealTorque guide says Schrader valves are the second most common place leaks are found in HVAC and refrigeration systems, and that improper tightening is one likely cause. A cap with a good seal catches a weeping core. Lennox gives an exact number for caps and a fallback when you have no torque wrench:

  • Stem cap and service port cap: finger tighten, then 8 ft-lb (11 N·m).
  • No torque wrench: stem cap finger tight plus 1/12 turn; service port cap finger tight plus 1/6 turn.
  • Trane's manuals give no number, only finger tight plus 1/6 turn for the caps.

Trane and Lennox disagree on style, not on the idea: seat the cap, do not guess. Never leave a cap off.

Core removal tools

A core in the port is a restriction in the vacuum path. A core removal tool pulls the core under pressure, lets you pull vacuum or charge through an open port, and reinstalls it without releasing charge. Yellow Jacket's page says its tool "removes Schrader valve, eliminating obstruction for faster evacuation." Its kit page claims up to 10x faster evacuation than a 1/4 inch hose, but it gives no test conditions, system size or times, so treat that as marketing and not as a number to plan with. The tool's RealTorque version clicks when a core reaches 3 to 5 in-lb (0.34 to 0.56 N·m by the maker's own conversion). The Fieldpiece double valve core removal tool is listed as A2L compatible, with an 800 psi maximum and a sight glass, and a side port for a micron gauge. Yellow Jacket's page lists compatibility with A1, A2L and A3 refrigerants. For pump and gauge checks, see Vacuum Pump Care.

Steps from the Yellow Jacket guide: connect the tool with its ball valve open, engage the core and unscrew it, pull the rod back, close the ball valve, then run vacuum or charge through the tool. To reinstall, push the rod forward and turn clockwise until the tool clicks.

A2L note

None of the outdoor unit manuals we read mention A2L. An Emerson article says some A2L equipment has a panel covering the service ports, and tells technicians to use A2L-rated gauges and tools. For ignition sources and ventilation, see A2L Service Habits.

Worked example: a torque check

A Lennox manual says 8 ft-lb for a service port cap. The click-type core tool reads in-lb. Convert with 12 in-lb in each ft-lb.

  1. Cap: 8 ft-lb x 12 = 96 in-lb.
  2. Core, low end: 3 in-lb. 96 / 3 = 32 times the core torque.
  3. Core, high end: 5 in-lb. 96 / 5 = 19.2 times the core torque.
  4. Factory stem torque range from the same manual: 9 ft-lb x 12 = 108 in-lb, and 25 ft-lb x 12 = 300 in-lb.
  5. Fallback turns: 1/6 turn = 360 / 6 = 60 degrees; 1/12 turn = 360 / 12 = 30 degrees.

Takeaway: the cap takes about 19 to 32 times the torque the core does. Do not tighten a core like a cap, and do not tighten a cap like a core. Use the right tool for each.

Field card: Service Valves and Schrader Cores

Cap is the seal. Core is the valve. Different torque.

  • Angle valve: hex wrench out until it stops (back seated). Ball valve: quarter turn.
  • Do not open valves until the lines are leak tested and evacuated.
  • Lennox: caps 8 ft-lb = 96 in-lb. Core tool clicks at 3 to 5 in-lb.
  • No torque wrench: finger tight plus 1/6 turn (port cap), 1/12 turn (stem cap) per Lennox.
  • Remove cores before brazing; replace after the body cools.
  • Core removal tools open the vacuum path; maker speed claims are marketing, not a plan.
  • A label on the cap overrides this card.
