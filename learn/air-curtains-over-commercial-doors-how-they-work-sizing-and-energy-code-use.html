Air Curtains Over Commercial Doors: How They Work, Sizing and Energy Code Use

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Walk into a busy restaurant back door or a big-box store and you often feel a steady stream of air coming down from a long box over the opening. That box is an air curtain. This lesson explains what it does, how makers pick one for a door, how to run the arithmetic, and where energy codes and food codes mention it.

What an air curtain does

An air curtain is a fan unit mounted across the top of a doorway that blows a flat sheet of air down over the opening. The sheet is not a wall. It is a moving layer that slows the exchange of air between inside and outside and keeps flying insects, dust and fumes from drifting through. Berner, a maker, says a unit has to deliver its rated velocity at floor level to seal a doorway, and that strong wind gusts, negative pressure inside the building and heavy door traffic can call for more airflow. Because it cuts infiltration, the conditioned air you paid to heat or cool stays in the building longer. It does not make an open door as tight as a closed one.

Mounting height and width

Two rules come up in every maker's literature. First, width: the Mars Air Systems installation manual says the unit must be equal to or wider than the opening. Powered Aire lists models in one-foot steps from 3 to 12 ft wide. Second, height: every model has a maximum mounting height. Berner warns that mounting above the rating weakens the jet and lets energy, dust and insects through. Mars rates its models for two jobs. Its Standard series is listed for environmental separation up to 12 ft and flying insect control up to 10 ft. Its High Velocity series is listed to 14 ft and 12 ft, and its Extra Power series to 16 ft and 14 ft. Note that insect control has a lower height limit than temperature separation.

The Mars manual also says to place the discharge about 1 inch above the opening, and for each extra inch you raise the unit, move it 3/8 inch away from the wall. Read the manual for your model; this is only the idea.

Outlet velocity and AMCA 220

AMCA 220 is the laboratory test standard for air curtains. According to the preview of the standard on the ANSI store, it covers airflow rate, how evenly air leaves the outlet, power use and how far the jet reaches. It does not cover field installation. AMCA's air curtain page says units should carry the AMCA certified ratings seal, and lists Berner and Mars among certified makers.

Berner's page gives an example of 2.5 m/s at the floor, which is about 492 feet per minute (2.5 x 196.85). That is an example, not a code number. Other numbers appear in other documents. Berner's code page quotes the ASHRAE 90.1 vestibule alternative as a minimum of 400 fpm at 6 inches above the sill, across 100 percent of the opening. Maricopa County, Arizona, in its public guidance for food establishments, asks for 1,600 fpm in a 3-inch column at service entrances and 600 fpm in an 8-inch column at customer entrances. Different rules, different measuring points. Always check which one your jurisdiction uses.

Heated and unheated units

Unheated units just move air. Heated units add electric, hot water or steam coils, and Powered Aire also lists indirect and direct gas for larger industrial units. Powered Aire says some two-speed heated units can run low as a space heater on a thermostat and jump to high when the door switch trips. Mars says heated units must be mounted on the inside, protected side of the opening. Heated units have their own supplement and wiring in the Mars literature, so follow the maker's heated product instructions and use a licensed electrician or gas fitter where required. For other heat sources in a shop or warehouse, see Garage and Workshop Heaters and Infrared Tube Heaters for Warehouses and Garages.

Door switch controls

Most doorway units run only when the door is open. Mars describes a door limit switch that starts the unit when the door opens and stops it when the door closes, needing only light pressure. On units with a control panel, the selector has to be set to the on or automatic position first. Powered Aire lists a door switch for two-speed units, a variable speed controller for its low-profile model and a switch with a timer for its pass-through window model. Mars recommends a variable frequency drive on freezer and cooler doors to control floor velocity. A fan that runs all day on a door that opens twice an hour wastes power; a switch that sticks closed means no curtain at all.

Food service and insect control

Restaurants and kitchens use air curtains where a door is kept open for deliveries or ventilation. The model food code language, as shown in Wisconsin's adopted Food Code and Minnesota's rule, lists screens (16 mesh to the inch), "properly designed and installed air curtains to control flying insects," or other effective means for openings kept open. Local health departments set the details. Maricopa County's guidance requires NSF Standard 37 units, airflow running vertically downward and across the entire opening, and control by door-activated micro-switches. Powered Aire sells a model listed to NSF 37 for food service doors. Ask your health department before you buy. Air curtains also change room pressure, so see Commercial Kitchen Ventilation: Hoods and Make-Up Air Basics and Make-Up Air Units.

The energy code vestibule alternative

ASHRAE 90.1 section 5.4.3.4, as quoted in a Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) report, says building entrances that separate conditioned space from the outdoors need an enclosed vestibule. The doors must be self-closing, and the inner and outer doors must be at least 7 ft apart when closed. The report lists exceptions by climate zone and building size, such as revolving doors, small spaces and warm zones. That PNNL report does not mention air curtains. The air curtain option came from the 2015 IECC. ACHR News and an AMCA announcement carried by Berner say the 2015 IECC lets an air curtain be considered as an alternative to a vestibule if it is tested to ANSI/AMCA 220 and installed per the maker's instructions. Powered Aire says its ETA and CHA models are tested to that standard for this purpose. Your local adopted code edition and amendments control, so confirm with the code official.

Worked example: a 10 ft wide by 12 ft high doorway

One maker's published data is used here only to show the arithmetic. It is not a product recommendation.

This is an invented opening. We use Mars Air Systems figures from its installation manual and from a food service retailer's listing of its specs.

  1. Width rule. The unit must be at least as wide as the opening. The opening is 10 ft, which is 120 inches. A 120-inch unit covers it, so one unit across the width is enough. Mars gives no units-per-width ratio; if you ran two side by side, the manual allows no more than 6 inches between them.
  2. Height rule. The door is 12 ft high, so the unit sits at about 12 ft. The Standard series is rated for environmental separation up to 12 ft: no margin, and its insect control limit is 10 ft. The High Velocity series is rated for separation to 14 ft and insect control to 12 ft. Choose High Velocity.
  3. Model. The retailer lists the Mars HV2120-3UA for door heights of 12 to 14 ft, 120 inches wide, at 6,693 CFM.
  4. Airflow per foot. 6,693 CFM / 10 ft = 669 CFM per foot of width (rounded). For comparison, the 120-inch Standard unit (STD2120-3UA) is listed at 4,341 CFM, or 4,341 / 10 = 434 CFM per foot.
  5. Result. One 120-inch High Velocity unit, 6,693 total CFM, mounted inside at the top of the opening, with a door switch.

Check the actual rating sheet before ordering. Listings change, and a maker's selector may account for wind, traffic and pressure.

Field card: Air Curtains Over Commercial Doors

Wide enough, low enough, and on only when the door is open.

  • Unit width equal to or greater than the opening.
  • Stay under the model's rated mounting height; insect control height is lower than temperature separation height.
  • The jet must reach the floor at the rated velocity.
  • Heated units go on the inside, warm side.
  • Door limit switch runs the fan only when the door is open.
  • Food service: check local rules, often NSF 37 and door switch.
  • Energy code: 2015 IECC allows an AMCA 220 tested air curtain as a vestibule alternative; confirm your adopted code.
  • Example: 10 ft by 12 ft, one 120-inch High Velocity unit, 6,693 CFM, 669 CFM per foot.
