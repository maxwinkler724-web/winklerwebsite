Lockout/Tagout and Verify Dead for HVAC

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A switch turned off is a promise, not proof. Lockout/tagout is the habit that turns the promise into something you can trust with your hands.

Why it exists

OSHA's control-of-hazardous-energy standard covers servicing equipment where unexpected startup or a release of stored energy could injure someone. That is the general industry standard (29 CFR 1910.147); for construction work, OSHA's construction electrical rule on lockout and tagging of circuits is 29 CFR 1926.417. HVAC equipment has several energy sources: line-voltage electricity, a 24 volt control circuit that can start a motor through a contactor, gas, refrigerant and water under pressure, moving fans and belts, and heat. This lesson focuses on the electrical side. Your employer's written procedure and local rules govern the exact steps.

The sequence, in plain words

  1. Prepare. Know what energy the equipment has, what hazards come with it, and how to control it. Find every source, including a second feed or a remote control circuit. Tell affected people that work is starting.
  2. Shut down the equipment in the normal, orderly way.
  3. Isolate. Operate the energy isolating devices, such as the disconnect or breaker. OSHA says push buttons, selector switches and other control-circuit devices are not isolating devices, and its electrical rule says they cannot be the only means of de-energizing. A thermostat setting is not isolation.
  4. Lock and tag. Each isolating device gets your lock (holding it off) and a tag saying not to operate it. A tag alone is allowed only where a lock cannot be used, with extra protection.
  5. Release stored energy. Relieve or restrain anything that can still hurt you. For electrical equipment that includes capacitors, which can hold a charge after power is off. OSHA says to discharge them and to treat any capacitor you handle as energized. If energy can build back up, keep checking until the work is done.
  6. Verify. Confirm that the equipment is isolated and de-energized before you begin.

Verifying with a meter

Verification means testing, not trusting a label. OSHA's electrical rule has a qualified person test the circuit elements, including for induced voltage or backfeed. Fluke's guidance on absence-of-voltage testing describes the live-dead-live check from NFPA 70E: prove the meter on a known live source, test the circuit, then prove the meter again. Hidden feeds, mislabeled breakers and capacitors that recharge slowly are the reasons to test every time. See Multimeter Safety and CAT Ratings for the meter side.

Putting energy back

Before removing locks, check the work area, make sure tools and spare parts are out, guards and panels are back, and everyone is clear. Notify affected people before restoring power. In general the person who put a lock on removes it.

Cord-and-plug and live work

OSHA's standard does not apply to cord-and-plug equipment when unplugging controls the hazard and the plug stays under the control of the person working. Work on energized parts is a different matter: under OSHA's electrical rule only qualified persons may do it, with the right training, protective equipment and insulated tools. If you are not that person, de-energize. A student should never do live work without an instructor.

Common ways it goes wrong

  • Opening a disconnect on the wrong unit.
  • Trusting a thermostat call to be off.
  • Skipping the meter check because you are in a hurry or know the equipment.
  • Not thinking about a capacitor. See Testing Capacitors, Contactors and Relays with a Meter.

Field card: Lockout/Tagout and Verify Dead for HVAC

Off is not dead. Lock it, discharge it, prove it.

  • List every energy source before you start: line voltage, control voltage, stored charge, gas, pressure, moving parts.
  • Control switches, thermostats and push buttons are not isolation. Use the disconnect or breaker.
  • Your own lock and tag on each isolating device, per your site procedure.
  • Release stored energy; treat capacitors as charged until discharged by an approved method.
  • Verify: meter on a known source, test the circuit, meter on the known source again.
  • Energized work is for qualified persons only. If unsure, stop and ask.
