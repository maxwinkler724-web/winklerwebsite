Rooftop Unit Preventive Maintenance Checklist: What to Check and How Often

Most rooftop unit trouble starts with something small and dull: a dirty filter, a clogged drain, a loose belt. A short, repeated checklist catches it early.

One maker's manual, used as an example

The intervals below come from the maintenance section of one manufacturer's installation, operation and maintenance manual for a commercial rooftop line (Trane Precedent, document RT-SVX38D-EN). They are labeled examples, typical for that model family, not universal rules. Other makers differ, so use your own unit's manual and put its numbers on your sheet. A second Trane split-system manual we reviewed in part lists the same monthly cooling-season items. Source strength: settled for the items below as one maker's published guidance; not a claim about every unit.

Lock out and verify dead before reaching into any panel, and treat the roof itself as a hazard. See Lockout/Tagout and Verify Dead for HVAC and RTU Gas Heat Lockouts and Rooftop Safety.

Monthly, all year

  • Lock out power first.
  • Inspect the return air filters and clean or replace them. How the load changes with the building is covered in How Often to Change Your Air Filter.
  • Confirm the condensate overflow float moves freely.

Monthly in the cooling season

  • Check drain pans and condensate piping for standing water and blockage. See Water Leaking From AC or Furnace: Condensate Drain.
  • Inspect the evaporator and condenser coils and clean them if dirty.
  • Rotate the condenser fans by hand, and check bearings and mounting hardware.
  • Inspect the economizer hinges, pins, blades and linkage, and lubricate. See Economizers Basics.
  • Check the supply motor and fan shaft bearings, and inspect the belt. See Belt Drives and Sheave Basics.
  • Tighten electrical terminals and check the panels.
  • With the unit running, log ambient temperature, suction and discharge pressure, and superheat for each refrigerant circuit. Reading them is covered in Superheat and Subcooling Basics.

Monthly in the heating season

  • Filters again, and the supply fan motor bearings.
  • Look in the main and heat-section control boxes for loose parts and damaged insulation.
  • Clean the burner area and confirm the gas heat operates. See RTU Gas Heat Section Basics. Combustion and CO checks belong to a qualified person; see Combustion and CO Safety Basics.

Annual

  • Clean the evaporator and condenser coils at least once a year, and more often in dirty places. Follow the manual's limits for detergent, sprayer and temperature. The split-system manual we reviewed gave an example limit of a 150 F solution and 600 psi sprayer pressure, but your manual governs. Why dirty coils hurt: Dirty Condenser and Head Pressure.
  • Repaint corroded surfaces.

New belts

The manual says to recheck tension twice in the first two or three days after installing new belts, then inspect them periodically and replace any that look frayed.

What is only partly sourced

Some online maintenance lists add semiannual contactor and capacitor checks, economizer damper calibration, heat exchanger inspection with a CO test, and belt replacement on units over five years old. We found those only from a software vendor's blog without real citations, so treat them as ideas to compare with your manual, not as intervals. A related reminder is in Condenser Fan Motor and Capacitor Failures. Also note that no public text of the ASHRAE/ACCA maintenance standard was available to us, so nothing here is "per Standard 180".

Turn it into a sheet

Make columns for the month, initials, the readings and a blank "manual interval" cell. Add a condition line: rising discharge pressure, falling airflow or a belt that keeps slipping can justify earlier service than the calendar. For start-up readings to compare against, see RTU Startup and Checkout Basics.

Field card: RTU PM Checklist

Power off first, then filters, drains, coils, belts, bearings, economizer, and log the numbers.

  • Monthly all year: lockout, filters, condensate overflow float.
  • Cooling season, monthly: drain pans, coils, condenser fans, economizer linkage, belt and bearings, terminals, log pressures and superheat.
  • Heating season, monthly: filters, fan bearings, control boxes, burner area, confirm heat runs.
  • Yearly: coil cleaning within the maker's limits, repaint rust.
  • These are examples from one maker's manual. Use yours.
