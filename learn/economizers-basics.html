Economizers Basics

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the Commercial Cooling Guide

An economizer lets a cooling system use cool outdoor air instead of running the compressor. When it works, it saves energy. When it fails quietly, it wastes it for years.

The idea

On a mild or cool day, outdoor air can be cooler than the air in the building. An air-side economizer is a set of dampers, actuators, sensors and a controller that lets the unit bring in that outdoor air for free cooling, so the compressor runs less or not at all.

The parts

  • Outdoor air damper. Opens to let outdoor air in.
  • Return air damper. Closes down as the outdoor damper opens, so the air mix stays workable.
  • Relief or exhaust path. Air brought in has to leave somewhere, so a relief damper or fan lets it out.
  • Actuator. The motor that moves the dampers.
  • Sensors and controller. They decide whether outdoor air is suitable.

How it decides

Two common approaches show up in training material. A dry-bulb control compares outdoor air temperature to a limit. An enthalpy control also accounts for moisture in the air, because humid air carries extra heat even if the temperature looks cool. Your unit's manual says which approach it uses and how to set it.

Minimum outdoor air

Buildings need fresh air for the people in them. When outdoor conditions are not suitable for free cooling, the damper goes to a minimum ventilation position rather than fully closed. When the building is unoccupied, the controls may close it entirely. The right minimum position is a design and code matter, not a guess.

Common failures

  • Dampers stuck open or stuck closed, from seized linkage or a failed actuator.
  • Sensors that read wrong, or an outdoor sensor placed where it does not sense the true outdoor air.
  • Controllers with wrong settings or bad wiring.
  • A stuck-open damper in hot or cold weather lets unconditioned air in all day, and nobody notices until the bills or complaints arrive.

Checking one in general terms

Use the manufacturer's test or checkout procedure to drive the actuator through its range. Watch the dampers actually move, and confirm they seal when closed. Compare the sensor reading with a trusted thermometer. Keep hands and tools clear of dampers, and lock out power before touching linkage.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: setpoints and test steps come from the unit's manual and the local energy and ventilation rules. This lesson does not give them.

Field card: Economizers Basics

Free cooling, if the dampers and sensors can be trusted.
