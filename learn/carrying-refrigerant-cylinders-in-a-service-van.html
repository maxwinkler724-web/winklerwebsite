Carrying Refrigerant Cylinders in a Service Van

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the EPA 608 Guide

Every van with a few cylinders in the back is in the shipping business whether the tech thinks so or not. One federal rule makes small loads simple. It still has edges worth knowing.

The idea: materials of trade

Federal hazardous-materials rules have a section for tradespeople who carry small amounts of hazardous goods to do their jobs. In 49 CFR 173.6 the load is called materials of trade. Refrigerant cylinders, including flammable A2L cylinders that generally ship as Division 2.1 gas (confirm the class for your product on its label or safety data sheet), can fall under it when the load stays small. This lesson gives the general rule. It is not legal advice.

What the text says in general

  • All materials of trade on one vehicle together may not weigh more than 200 kg, which is 440 pounds gross.
  • A cylinder of Division 2.1 or 2.2 gas may not exceed 100 kg, or 220 pounds gross.
  • Packages must be secured against shifting and protected from damage, and cylinders need not have an outer box.
  • DOT specification cylinders must be marked and labeled as the regulations require.
  • The driver must be told hazardous material is aboard, and told about the requirements of the section.

The 440-pound figure is the total for everything counted as materials of trade on the vehicle, not just refrigerant. Propane, torch cylinders and other items can count too.

Practical habits

  • Secure cylinders upright with caps on, in a space that is not sealed off from air. Manufacturer guidance says a cylinder generally should ride with its relief valve in the vapor space, which usually means upright.
  • Arkema describes a special permit that allows certain of its cylinders to ride horizontally; if you rely on a permit, carry a copy and read its terms.
  • Do not leave cylinders rolling in a cargo area with tools and recovery gear.
  • Keep labels readable and keep the safety data sheet reachable.
  • Count the pounds: a few 25-pound disposable cylinders are far below the limit, but a full recovery job plus other gear adds up.

When it stops being simple

Arkema's guidance describes added requirements above 440 pounds combined, such as shipping papers and possibly driver training, and placards and a certified driver above 1,001 pounds. A shop van that carries a lot of refrigerant on a supply run is in a different situation from a service call.

Check the other layers

Cities, counties and states can be stricter, and your employer may have its own cylinder-in-vehicle policy or insurance rules. Check the current text of the regulation and your own rules before assuming the general rule fits your van.

Field card: Carrying Refrigerant Cylinders in a Service Van

Small loads are simple. Know your total and secure it.

  • 49 CFR 173.6 materials of trade is the general rule for small loads.
  • Limits described in the text: 440 lb total, 220 lb for a single Division 2.1/2.2 cylinder (gross).
  • Secure against shifting; keep labels and markings intact; tell the driver.
  • Upright is the usual default; special permits have their own terms.
  • State, local and employer rules may be stricter. Check them.
