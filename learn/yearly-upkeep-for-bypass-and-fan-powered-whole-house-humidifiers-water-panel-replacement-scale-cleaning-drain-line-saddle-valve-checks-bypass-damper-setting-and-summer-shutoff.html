Yearly Upkeep for a Whole-House Humidifier: Water Panel, Scale, Drain and Summer Shutoff

Last updated: 2026-11-04

A whole-house humidifier works all winter, then sits wet and warm for months if nobody shuts it down. The yearly routine is short: new water panel, a scale clean, a look at the water and drain lines, the right damper position, and the water turned off before cooling season. This lesson walks through that routine for bypass and fan-powered units.

Where this fits

We cover what the unit types are in Whole-House Humidifiers: Bypass, Fan-Powered and Steam and how much water they use in the per-day water use lesson. This page is only about keeping one running well. Steam units work differently and are not covered here. Always follow the manual for your exact model; the details below are common ground between several maker manuals, not a replacement for yours.

Safety first

One maker's user manual says to disconnect power and shut off the water supply before removing the cover. We agree, and we add one rule of our own: if you are not comfortable working near the furnace blower and its low-voltage wiring, stop and call a pro. Never put oil on any part of the humidifier, as the same manual says.

1. Replace the water panel

The water panel (also called an evaporator pad) is the wetted media that air moves across. Water runs through it, minerals stay in it, and the air picks up the moisture. Two maker manuals we read give the same advice: replace it at least once a year. One says to do it before each heating season, and sooner if it is heavily scaled or the water is high in sulfate. Another maker's manual says one of its larger models needs a new panel twice per heating season. Some units have a change-panel light. Where that light exists, the manual says to run the reset step after the swap, so read that page.

Our own tip: write the install date on the new panel frame with a marker, and use only the panel made for your model. Panels are not all the same size or shape.

2. Clean scale from the tray and distribution tray

Scale is the crusty mineral layer left when water evaporates. The EPA page on home humidifiers says minerals in tap water can build up as crusty deposits, and that scale can be a breeding ground for microorganisms. For central units it points you to the maker's cleaning directions and says not to let water stand for long periods.

  • How often. One manual says the humidifier needs cleaning at minimum at the start of every heating season, and more often with hard water. Another sets a cleaning interval of every 1 to 3 months depending on water quality.
  • What to use. A maker manual says to wash the distribution pan with mild detergent and warm water, and to soak scaled pads in ordinary household vinegar. If scale is heavy, replace the pad. The same manual notes that hard-water deposits are harder to clean than soft-water ones.
  • Rinse well. Our own rule: rinse everything with clean water until no cleaner smell remains, and let parts drain before reassembly.

3. Check the drain line, feed valve and saddle valve

  • Drain line. A maker manual says to check the drain line for blockages and to flush the drain hose with running tap water, bending it to loosen deposits, and to make sure the hole in the frame is open. Our own addition: the line should run downhill with no sags or kinks, ending at a drain.
  • Saddle valve. One manual tells owners to fully close it for summer. A saddle valve is the small tap clamped onto a water pipe. Our own advice: look for drips, green or white crust, or damp insulation around it. Leaks there are a plumbing repair, so call a pro.
  • Feed valve. The solenoid feed valve opens when the humidistat calls. One manual says setting the humidistat to OFF cuts power to it. If water keeps entering when the control is off, treat it as a service call.
  • Cabinet. Look for water stains below the unit or in the plenum. Our own rule: any water outside the humidifier is a stop-and-call sign.

4. Set the bypass damper

A bypass unit uses a small duct between the supply and return so some air flows through the panel. One maker manual says to mark the damper Closed position as Summer and the Open position as Winter. Two maker manuals say to close the bypass damper for summer shutdown. Closing it in summer keeps cooled, moist air from circulating through the humidifier. Fan-powered units are different, so check the manual.

5. Turn the water off for cooling season

One manual says to make sure the humidifier water supply is off during the cooling season, to close the saddle valve and then set the humidistat to OFF. Another says no control adjustment is needed beyond closing the damper and the saddle valve. Our own checklist at shutdown: panel dry or removed per the manual, tray clean, water off, damper closed, humidistat off. Standing water is what the EPA page warns about.

Worked example (our own): minerals left on a pad in one season

Assumptions, all ours, to show the arithmetic. Your numbers will differ.

  • Hardness: 10 grains per gallon. The Virginia Tech extension table places 7.0 to 10.5 grains per gallon in the hard class.
  • Water fed to the humidifier: 8 gallons per day. See the per-day lesson linked above for how to estimate yours.
  • Season: 120 days.
  • Half of the minerals in the feed water stay on the pad and tray; the other half leave through the drain. This split is only an assumption.
  • Conversion: 1 grain per gallon equals 17.1 mg/L (Montana State extension). We also use 3.785 liters per gallon and 453.6 grams per pound, standard conversions.
  1. Water over the season: 8 x 120 = 960 gallons.
  2. Liters: 960 x 3.785 = 3,634 L.
  3. Hardness in mg/L: 10 x 17.1 = 171 mg/L (as calcium carbonate).
  4. Minerals in the feed water: 3,634 x 171 = 621,400 mg, about 621 g, or roughly 1.4 pounds.
  5. Left behind at half: about 310 g, roughly 0.7 pound.

That is nearly three-quarters of a pound of crust in one season for these assumed numbers. With 3 grains per gallon water, the same math gives about 93 g retained. The lesson: hardness strongly changes how often to clean. Hardness as calcium carbonate is a standard way to report it, and real deposits also contain other minerals, so treat the result as an estimate. A softener or other treatment changes the picture; see the makeup water softener lesson before assuming softened water is right for a humidifier.

Keep the setpoint sensible

The EPA page says not to humidify above 50 percent indoor relative humidity, and to lower the setting if water condenses on windows or walls. See the humidistat lesson for how the control works.

When to call a pro

  • Any leak at the saddle valve, supply tubing or cabinet, or water in the ductwork.
  • Water that keeps running with the humidistat off.
  • A drain line that stays blocked after flushing.
  • No humidifier operation when the furnace blower runs, which points to control wiring.
  • Scale or corrosion that cleaning does not remove.

Field card: Yearly Upkeep for a Whole-House Humidifier: Water Panel, Scale, Drain and Summer Shutoff

New panel, clean tray, clear drain, damper set, water off in summer.

  • Before cleaning: disconnect power and shut off water.
  • Replace the water panel at least once a year; sooner if scaled.
  • Clean scale with detergent or vinegar per the manual; rinse well.
  • Flush the drain hose; keep a steady downhill slope.
  • Damper: open in winter, closed in summer (bypass units).
  • Cooling season: close the saddle valve, humidistat off.
  • Leaks or runaway water: call a pro.
