Outdoor Reset and Weather Compensation

A building needs far less heat on a 45 F day than on a 5 F day, yet many boilers send the same hot water to the radiators all winter. Outdoor reset fixes that mismatch.

The idea

An outdoor sensor tells the control how cold it is. As the outdoor temperature rises, the control lowers the target temperature of the water leaving the boiler. As it falls, the target rises. The relationship is plotted as a reset curve. One reference gives an example where supply water drops from about 180 F in design cold to about 140 F as it gets mild, while noting that every building needs its own curve.

Why it helps

  • Comfort. Lower-temperature water in mild weather flows longer at gentler output, so rooms swing less and overshoot less. Radiant floors, which respond slowly, benefit most.
  • Efficiency. Less heat is lost from piping and the boiler cycles less. Condensing boilers can reach much higher efficiency when return water is low enough. A vendor article claims roughly 1 percent fuel savings per 4 F reduction in water temperature, but treat that as a marketing figure to verify.
  • Code and programs. Many places require basic reset on new commercial work. Check the rules that apply to you.

Limits and cautions

A conventional (non-condensing) boiler must not be run with return water too cold. Published guidance suggests keeping return around 130 to 140 F, because colder water lets flue gases condense and attack the boiler and chimney. So with those boilers, reset needs a return-temperature protection scheme, a minimum boiler temperature, or both. Condensing boilers are designed for low water and are the natural partner for reset.

Large temperature spreads between supply and return can also stress the boiler. Many are designed for a modest temperature rise, so check the manual.

Setting up and troubleshooting

  • Start with the maker's default curve and adjust slowly, because the system responds over hours or days, not minutes.
  • If rooms are cold only in mild weather, the curve may be too low at the mild end. If they overheat in mild weather, it may be too high.
  • A bad or badly placed outdoor sensor sends bad information. Keep it on a shaded, protected exterior wall, away from vents and sun, as the control maker directs.
  • Confirm that the thermostat or zone calls still work normally with reset.
  • Check that zones, emitters and flow rates can deliver the heat at the lower water temperature.

Steam systems use a related idea, where outdoor temperature sets how long the boiler runs rather than water temperature. Always follow the control manufacturer's setup sequence.

The rule that overrides everything on this page: the manufacturer's instructions for the exact equipment in front of you, and the rules where you work, always govern. This lesson explains concepts. It does not give setpoints or wiring and piping steps.

Field card: Outdoor Reset and Weather Compensation

Mild weather needs cooler water.

  • Outdoor sensor plus reset curve sets boiler water temperature.
  • Lower water in mild weather saves fuel and reduces overshoot.
  • Protect conventional boilers from cold return water.
  • Tune the curve slowly. Wait days between changes.
  • Sensor placement and wiring matter. Follow the manual.
