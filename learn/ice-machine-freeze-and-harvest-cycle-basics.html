Ice Machine Basics: The Freeze and Harvest Cycle

A cube ice machine is a refrigeration system with a very simple job and a very particular rhythm: make a sheet of ice, warm it just enough to let go, drop it in the bin, repeat. Once you know the rhythm, most complaints sort themselves out.

The loop in one paragraph

Manufacturer technician handbooks describe the same two-part loop. During the freeze cycle, a water pump sends water over a cold evaporator plate and ice builds in the cube cells. When the control decides the ice is thick enough, the machine switches to harvest. A hot gas valve routes warm refrigerant gas into the evaporator, the ice loosens and slides into the bin, and the machine returns to freeze. The exact sensors, timers and fan behavior differ by brand and model, so what follows is a general picture built from the handbooks for a few lines of equipment.

The freeze cycle

One brand's handbook for a large modular series says that after startup the machine purges old water, starts the compressor, and prechills the evaporator before the pump begins. It describes a short freeze lock, a few minutes in that example, that keeps the control from starting a harvest too early. In the same handbook, harvest begins once water has touched an ice thickness probe for a set number of seconds. A different brand's under-counter model uses a water level sensor to end the freeze, and checks that reservoir water cools to a preset point within a few minutes, which confirms the refrigeration system is working and the inlet valve is not leaking through. If you want to know why freeze cycles run long, dirty or scaled water-contact parts are on the list, and one retail maintenance guide says a dirty float switch can cause long freeze cycles and low ice production.

The harvest cycle

In harvest the pump stops or changes behavior, water is purged or the reservoir is refilled and rinsed, and the hot gas valve opens. The brand handbook above caps harvest at a few minutes for a single-evaporator model, with a longer cap for a four-evaporator model, and states that if freezing runs a long time without a harvest, the board forces one. Those caps exist so a failed sensor cannot freeze the machine solid. A short, clean sheet of cubes sliding into the bin is what a healthy harvest looks like. Ice that clings, or a sheet that breaks apart, is a clue to look at scale, water flow, the hot gas valve and the refrigerant circuit, in that kind of order.

Bin control and shutoff

The machine needs a way to know the bin is full. One handbook describes a curtain or bin switch: when the sheet of ice cannot clear it, the machine shuts off after a short delay and waits a few minutes before it can restart. Another brand's under-counter model uses two photo-electric eyes between the evaporator plates and shuts down at the end of the next harvest if the beam is blocked for twenty seconds or more. A retail guide for another brand suggests checking the bin sensor for ice buildup or damage twice a year and confirming the machine really shuts off when the bin is full. If a machine seems dead, check whether the bin is simply full or a control is stuck before you suspect the sealed system.

What the refrigeration side needs

The compressor and condenser do the same work as any other refrigeration circuit. Air-cooled models need clean condenser fins and clear airflow, and water-cooled models need a healthy water supply. One under-counter handbook notes that the fans cycle to keep discharge temperature in range, and that the machine restarts on its own after power or water interruptions. Keep the evaporator clean, too, since the slots at the top of the plates must stay clear for water to distribute evenly. A simple way to diagnose

  • Watch a full cycle with the front off only if the manual allows it and you can do so safely.
  • Note which stage hurts: slow freeze, no harvest, short harvest, or no restart.
  • Check the cheap things first: water supply, filters, condenser airflow, scale and bin controls.
  • Compare your observations to the handbook for that exact model before you replace anything.

Never bypass a bin control, high-temperature cutout or safety shutdown to make a machine run. A safety stop is the machine telling you something.

Field card: Ice Machine Basics: The Freeze and Harvest Cycle

Freeze, harvest, bin full, repeat.

  • Freeze: pump moves water over a cold evaporator while ice builds.
  • Harvest: hot gas warms the evaporator so the sheet of ice drops into the bin.
  • Timers cap both stages; a forced harvest points to a sensor or water problem.
  • Bin controls stop the machine when ice blocks them. Check the bin before the sealed system.
  • Do not bypass safety shutdowns. Use the handbook for that model.
