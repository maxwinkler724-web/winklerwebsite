NJ Black Seal and Boilers Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-16

New Jersey licenses the people who operate certain boilers and pressure systems, and it inspects the boilers themselves. People often call the operator credential a Black Seal license. Which license you need depends on the kind of plant and its size, so the first step is to read how the license paths work before choosing study material.

This guide starts with the New Jersey side: the license paths, the study plan, how boiler inspections, certificates and logbooks work for owners, and what post-service testing means. Then it links the technical lessons that operator exams and daily plant work keep returning to: steam fundamentals, steam traps, feedwater and deaerators, water treatment and blowdown, low-water cutoffs, safety valves and flame safeguards.

Rules and exam content are set by the State and change over time. Use these lessons to understand the ideas, and confirm the current requirements with the New Jersey agencies before you apply or sign up for a course. Nothing here replaces the boiler manufacturer instructions or your plant procedures.
