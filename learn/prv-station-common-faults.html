PRV Station Common Faults

When a PRV station works, nobody notices it. When it does not, you get overpressure, noise, water hammer or a cold building. This lesson starts from the symptom and works back to likely causes.

Safety first

Steam is often invisible near a leak, and condensate flashes to steam when pressure drops. Treat leaking joints, packing and relief discharge as hazard zones. Isolate, relieve pressure and follow lockout/tagout before opening anything. Do not gag or adjust a relief valve to make a symptom go away. This page lists things to check, not repair steps.

How the valve works, briefly

A manufacturer learning page describes two common self-acting types. A direct-acting valve uses downstream pressure on a bellows or diaphragm against a spring, and it cannot hold pressure exactly: downstream pressure drifts with load, an effect called droop. A pilot-operated valve has a small pilot and a dedicated sensing pipe that carries downstream pressure back to it, which holds closer to setpoint. Many faults come from that sensing path, from dirt in small passages, or from wet steam.

Symptom: downstream pressure creeps up at no load

This points to a leaking seat. Debris cuts seats, and wet steam erodes them (the manufacturer calls this wiredrawing). Look at the strainer and the separator first, since they exist to protect the seat. A relief valve may start to weep or lift, which is the system doing its job. Do not tamper with it.

Symptom: pressure too low under load

  • Valve undersized, or wide open and still short of capacity.
  • A clogged strainer. Compare upstream and downstream of it.
  • A partly closed isolation valve, or undersized pipework adding pressure drop.
  • Low supply pressure, or a failed pilot.

Symptom: hunting or cycling

An oversized valve running nearly closed is a classic cause, because small plug movements then make large flow changes. Also check that the sensing line is clear and connected to a stable location, whether a pilot sticks, and whether a controller is tuned too aggressively.

Symptom: noise or rumble

Large pressure drops across a single stage create noise and erosion. The manufacturer suggests series (two-stage) stations for high pressure ratios and gives 10:1 as a practical per-valve maximum for self-acting valves, which is that company’s guidance and not a universal rule. Wet steam can also be the cause, so check the separator and its trap.

Symptom: banging or water hammer

Look at drainage first: trapped drip points, working traps, and pitch of the piping. The steam-distribution reference lists poor drainage, sagging pipe, strainers installed with the basket facing down, and opening valves too quickly on cold pipe as typical causes. Slowing the valve does not cure bad drainage. See Steam Traps Explained.

Symptom: relief valve lifts repeatedly

Treat it as real overpressure until proven otherwise: a leaking PRV, a relief set too close to operating pressure, or a worn relief. The manufacturer reminds readers that a safety valve is a protective device and not a pressure limiter. Find the cause. Do not adjust the relief to stop the lifting.

Other findings

  • Steam at a vent or weep hole can mean a failed diaphragm or bellows. Isolate and report.
  • Packing or stem leaks: follow the manufacturer on whether tightening is permitted, and never tighten under pressure beyond what the procedure allows.
  • Gauges that disagree: compare with a known gauge and check the siphon or pigtail.

Printable checklist

  • No visible steam leaks at flanges, packing or relief
  • Relief valve not weeping; discharge pipe clear and routed safely
  • Upstream and downstream pressure recorded; downstream does not creep at no load
  • Pressure drop across strainer noted; strainer cleaned per schedule
  • Separator and drip traps working
  • Sensing line clear and connected (pilot-operated valves)
  • No hammer on load changes
  • Gauges agree with a reference gauge
  • Bypass closed and secured as your site requires
  • Anything unexplained reported and a work order opened

Field card: PRV Station Common Faults

Symptom first, then the likely cause, then ask.

  • Creeping pressure at no load: suspect the seat, check strainer and separator.
  • Low pressure under load: strainer, isolation valves, capacity, pilot.
  • Hunting: oversizing, sensing line, pilot, tuning.
  • Hammer: fix drainage first.
  • Never adjust or gag a relief valve to hide a symptom.
