EPA 608 Type II Practice Test (Free, Explained)

Last updated: 2026-10-16 · 25 questions

Type II covers high-pressure and very-high-pressure appliances: split systems, rooftop units and commercial refrigeration.

The real exam is a multiple-choice test given by an EPA-approved certifying organization. Question counts, passing scores and open- or closed-book rules differ by organization, so confirm them with the organization you plan to test with and on the EPA Section 608 page (checked 2026-10-16).

How to use this test. Read each question, choose your answer, then open “Show answer” to see why and where it comes from. These are original practice questions, not real exam questions. Every explanation links to the source it is based on.

1. Which refrigerant is high-pressure?

  1. R-11
  2. R-123
  3. R-22
  4. R-113
Show answer

Answer: C. R-22

R-22 is a high-pressure refrigerant, while R-11, R-113 and R-123 are low-pressure.

Source: 40 CFR 82.152 (pressure definitions)

2. Fastest way to recover from a large system is usually:

  1. Vapor only
  2. Liquid recovery (push-pull on large systems)
  3. Wait
  4. Nitrogen purge
Show answer

Answer: B. Liquid recovery (push-pull on large systems)

Liquid-phase recovery moves refrigerant much faster than vapor recovery on large systems.

Background source: Contracting Business, Effective Refrigerant Recovery Techniques

3. R-407C should be charged as:

  1. Vapor
  2. Liquid
  3. Either
  4. Neither
Show answer

Answer: B. Liquid

R-407C is a zeotropic blend, so it must be charged as liquid to keep its composition.

Source: Arkema Forane R407C spec sheet

4. Oil used with HFC systems is typically:

  1. Mineral
  2. POE
  3. Water
  4. Alkylbenzene only
Show answer

Answer: B. POE

Check nameplate.

Background source: Arkema Forane R407C spec sheet (POE required with R-407C)

5. A burned-out compressor will likely cause:

  1. Acidic oil
  2. Lower pressure only
  3. Nothing
  4. Quiet operation
Show answer

Answer: A. Acidic oil

Test oil, replace driers, flush if required.

Background source: ACHR News, Performing a clean-up after a burnout

6. You must pressure-test with:

  1. CO2 only
  2. Dry nitrogen via regulator
  3. Air
  4. Oxygen
Show answer

Answer: B. Dry nitrogen via regulator

Dry nitrogen through a regulator is the safe, standard medium for pressure testing.

Source: AirConSpares, Nitrogen pressure testing HVAC systems

7. Before opening a section of a split system:

  1. Recover to the required level
  2. Vent
  3. Start the compressor
  4. Cap the line only
Show answer

Answer: A. Recover to the required level

Refrigerant must be recovered to the required level before opening any section of the system.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(a)

8. A rack system leak check method NOT appropriate:

  1. Electronic detector
  2. Soap bubbles
  3. Open flame leak check near the rack
  4. Ultrasonic
Show answer

Answer: C. Open flame leak check near the rack

Open flame leak checks are unsafe near refrigerants, which can decompose into toxic gases.

Source: Dwyer-Omega, Halogen leak detection (halide torch/phosgene)

9. Rule on leak repair:

  1. Never required
  2. Required for covered appliances over thresholds, with verification tests
  3. Only for CFCs
  4. Only for residential
Show answer

Answer: B. Required for covered appliances over thresholds, with verification tests

Thresholds are 20% commercial refrigeration, 30% industrial process, 10% comfort cooling (40 CFR 82.157 for CFC/HCFC appliances of 50 lb or more). HFC appliances of 15 lb or more follow the AIM Act rule, 40 CFR Part 84, since Jan 1, 2026; confirm details on EPA and eCFR.

Source: 40 CFR 82.157(a),(c),(d),(e) and 40 CFR 84.106

10. Evacuation prior to charge removes:

  1. Oil
  2. Moisture and air
  3. Refrigerant
  4. Dirt
Show answer

Answer: B. Moisture and air

Evacuation removes air and moisture, which would otherwise cause corrosion and non-condensable buildup.

Source: AirConSpares, How to evacuate an AC system

11. A system that has lost most charge: recover remainder to:

  1. Whatever level
  2. Required recovery level
  3. Zero psig
  4. 25 psig
Show answer

Answer: B. Required recovery level

Remaining refrigerant must be recovered to the required level, regardless of how much was lost.

Source: 40 CFR 82.156(a)

12. Type II covers:

  1. Small appliances only
  2. High-pressure and very-high-pressure appliances
  3. Low-pressure only
  4. Automotive
Show answer

Answer: B. High-pressure and very-high-pressure appliances

Automotive MVAC is covered by Section 609, a separate program.

Source: 40 CFR 82.161(a)(1)(ii) / EPA types of technician certification

13. A TXV system is most commonly charged by checking:

  1. Superheat only
  2. Subcooling
  3. Hand feel of the line
  4. Compressor amperage
Show answer

Answer: B. Subcooling

TXV systems are commonly charged by subcooling, since the valve controls superheat.

Source: ACHR News, Charging methods for metering devices

14. A condenser with a fixed-orifice (piston) metering device is commonly checked by:

  1. Subcooling only
  2. Compressor amperage
  3. Superheat
  4. Pressure only
Show answer

Answer: C. Superheat

A fixed device does not regulate superheat, so superheat is the usual check.

Source: ACHR News, Charging methods for metering devices

15. Subcooling is calculated as:

  1. Saturation temperature at liquid-line pressure minus liquid-line temperature
  2. Discharge temperature minus suction temperature
  3. Suction temperature minus ambient temperature
  4. Evaporator temperature minus liquid temperature
Show answer

Answer: A. Saturation temperature at liquid-line pressure minus liquid-line temperature

Subcooling is how far the liquid has cooled below its saturation temperature.

Source: ACHR News, Charging methods for metering devices

16. A dirty air-cooled condenser coil most likely causes:

  1. Low head pressure
  2. Low suction pressure only
  3. No change in pressures
  4. High head pressure
Show answer

Answer: D. High head pressure

Restricted airflow reduces heat rejection, which raises condensing pressure.

Source: ACHR News, Restricted condenser airflow

17. Liquid floodback to a compressor can cause:

  1. Warmer oil
  2. Valve damage or compressor failure from liquid slugging
  3. Better efficiency
  4. Faster cooling
Show answer

Answer: B. Valve damage or compressor failure from liquid slugging

Liquid does not compress, so it can damage valves and bearings.

Source: ACHR News, Role of the suction line accumulator

18. A crankcase heater is used to:

  1. Heat the refrigerant to boil it
  2. Heat the condenser coil
  3. Keep refrigerant from migrating into the oil during off cycles
  4. Run the fan
Show answer

Answer: C. Keep refrigerant from migrating into the oil during off cycles

Warm oil prevents refrigerant from condensing into it while the compressor is off.

Source: HVACR School, Field guide to crankcase heaters

19. Continuous bubbles in a liquid-line sight glass on a running system most often indicate:

  1. Undercharge or a restriction causing flash gas
  2. Normal operation in all systems
  3. A full charge
  4. A failed compressor
Show answer

Answer: A. Undercharge or a restriction causing flash gas

Flash gas in the liquid line signals lost subcooling.

Background source: HVACR School, Flash gas

20. A failed run capacitor can cause a compressor to:

  1. Start quickly
  2. Run at lower amps only
  3. Stop the condenser fan
  4. Hum but fail to start
Show answer

Answer: D. Hum but fail to start

A hum with no start is a classic symptom of a weak or failed capacitor.

Background source: HomeTips, AC capacitor symptoms

21. Flowing dry nitrogen through copper tubing while brazing is used to:

  1. Cool the joint
  2. Prevent internal oxide scale
  3. Pressurize the coil
  4. Remove oil
Show answer

Answer: B. Prevent internal oxide scale

Nitrogen displaces oxygen inside the tube so scale does not form.

Source: HVACR School, Flowing nitrogen purge

22. A thermostatic expansion valve bulb should be:

  1. Clamped to a clean suction line and insulated
  2. Strapped to the liquid line
  3. Left loose in the cabinet
  4. Mounted inside the fins
Show answer

Answer: A. Clamped to a clean suction line and insulated

The bulb must sense suction-line temperature accurately, so it needs good contact and insulation.

Background source: ACHR News, Positioning the TXV sensing bulb

23. Pump-down means:

  1. Evacuating the system to 0 psig
  2. Pumping oil out of the compressor
  3. Running the compressor with the liquid line valve closed to store refrigerant in the condenser or receiver
  4. Recovering with a vacuum pump
Show answer

Answer: C. Running the compressor with the liquid line valve closed to store refrigerant in the condenser or receiver

This isolates most of the charge on the high side for service.

Background source: ACHR News, Migration and pump-down systems

24. A hot gas bypass valve is used to:

  1. Boil the oil
  2. Warm the condenser
  3. Pressurize the receiver
  4. Keep minimum refrigerant flow through the evaporator at low loads
Show answer

Answer: D. Keep minimum refrigerant flow through the evaporator at low loads

Bypassing hot gas maintains evaporator load and helps prevent coil icing.

Source: ACHR News, Understanding hot gas bypass

25. Oil traps in vertical suction risers are placed to:

  1. Stop all refrigerant flow
  2. Help carry oil back up to the compressor
  3. Collect liquid for a recovery machine
  4. Hold the filter-drier
Show answer

Answer: B. Help carry oil back up to the compressor

The trap helps lift oil up the riser so the compressor keeps its lubrication.

Source: HVACR School, Understanding P-traps in refrigerant lines
