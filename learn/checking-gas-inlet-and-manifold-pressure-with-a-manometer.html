Checking Gas Inlet and Manifold Pressure With a Manometer

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A gas furnace needs the right pressure at two places: the inlet of the gas valve and the manifold that feeds the burners. A manometer reads both in inches of water column. This lesson covers how to check each one, what the manufacturers' manuals say the numbers should be, and how to close the job up safely.

Check the model's manual

Check the model's manual. Pressure targets belong to one model and one gas valve. The figures below are examples from the documents named, not settings to copy. Read the rating plate and the installation instructions for the furnace in front of you.

Inlet versus manifold pressure

Inlet (supply) pressure is what the piping delivers to the gas valve. Manifold pressure is what leaves the valve's regulator and reaches the burner orifices. Inlet pressure has to be high enough that the regulator can do its job. Goodman's installation manual for its MES80/CES80 furnaces says the minimum supply pressure prevents unreliable ignition and the maximum prevents over-firing. Manifold pressure, together with the orifice size, sets the firing rate. That is why the manifold reading is the one tied to the rating plate input.

What the manuals list

These are published figures, all in inches of water column (in. w.c.). They differ, which is the point: look up your model.

  • Goodman MES80/CES80 (IOG-2021G). Inlet: natural gas 4.5 minimum, 10.0 maximum; propane 11.0 minimum, 13.0 maximum. Manifold: natural gas 3.2 to 3.8; propane 9.7 to 10.3.
  • Carrier 58SC0A/58SC1A product data. Natural gas inlet 4.5 minimum, 13.6 maximum. The sheet does not list manifold pressure; that comes from the furnace installation instructions.
  • Trane S-series installer's guide (18-CH79D1-5C, propane conversion kit). Inlet to the valve: natural gas 5.0 minimum, 13.8 maximum; propane 11.0 minimum, 13.8 maximum. Manifold, high stage and low stage: S9 natural gas 3.5 and 1.7, propane 10.0 and 6.0. S8 (except the D120) natural gas 3.5 and 1.6. S8-D120 natural gas 3.5 and 1.8, propane 10.0 and 7.5.
  • Trane Precedent staged unit (support article). Two-stage natural gas manifold 3.5 high and 1.8 low; the article says the natural gas low-fire value is not field adjustable.
  • Lennox kit instruction 506924-01. Two-stage furnace with a White-Rodgers valve: natural gas 1.7 low and 3.5 high, propane 4.9 low and 10.0 high. Honeywell ML193/ML195/EL195 valves: natural gas 3.5 ± 0.3, propane 10.0 ± 0.3. This document does not give supply pressure.

Two-stage furnaces have a low-fire and a high-fire value, as the Trane and Lennox figures show. You check both. For how staging works, see Two-Stage and Modulating Gas Furnaces: How Staging Works. Values can also move with altitude and gas heating value: a Rheem replacement-valve guide for Honeywell VR8205S valves lists natural gas manifold pressure of 3.5 at most elevations dropping to 3.0 at 8,000 to 10,000 feet.

Where the taps are

Combination gas valves have an inlet pressure tap and an outlet pressure tap. Emerson's installation instructions for the White-Rodgers 36J22/36J24/36J54/36J55 valves (37-7338B) show an inlet pressure post and an outlet pressure post, and a Trane guide calls the 36J outlet boss "OUT P." Honeywell's document for the VR8205 and VR4205 (68-0049-03) says inlet and outlet taps are standard and accessible from the top of the control. That Honeywell document does not cover the VR9205, so use the VR9205's own sheet. Goodman's manual says supply pressure can be measured at the valve inlet tap, or at a hose fitting in the drip leg.

Manometer types

A U-tube manometer is a tube bent into a U and partly filled with liquid. Pressure on one leg pushes the liquid down on that side and up on the other, and the pressure is the difference in height, per Dwyer's explanation; the scale reads inches of water column directly. A digital manometer shows the number on a screen. Fieldpiece's SDMN6 manual lists inWC among its units, says to zero the meter at ambient pressure with hoses attached, and for readings under 2 inWC to take the reading within a minute of zeroing. It also gives a maximum of 900 inWC (17.4 psi) and says exceeding it will damage the sensors, so never connect a meter to a pressure above its rating.

Reading the pressures

  1. Get the targets first. Fieldpiece's manual says to check the manufacturer's specifications for inlet and outlet pressure before you measure.
  2. Open the tap. Goodman says to back the test screw out no more than one turn, then connect the hose and manometer to the tap.
  3. Read inlet with everything firing. Goodman's manual says the supply pressure must be constant and available with all other household gas appliances operating. Light the furnace and the other appliances on the same piping, read the inlet, and compare to the minimum. A reading that falls below the minimum when the others fire points at the piping or the supply, not at the furnace. See Sizing Natural Gas Piping: The Longest Length Method. Goodman's text says in one place to measure with the unit off and in the procedure with burners firing, so note the condition in which you took each number.
  4. Read manifold with burners firing. Goodman measures manifold pressure at the outlet tap with burners firing. On a two-stage furnace, the Emerson instructions say to use low stage first, then call high stage.
  5. Compare to the rating plate and manual, not to a number from another model.

Adjusting, and when not to

If the manifold reading is off and the inlet is in range, the manual may allow a regulator adjustment. For the White-Rodgers 36J, Emerson says to remove the regulator cover screw, turn the adjusting screw clockwise to increase pressure or counterclockwise to decrease it, and replace the cover screw. A two-stage 36J has separate low and high regulators, and the valve cannot be adjusted outside its range (natural gas high stage 2.0 to 5.0, low stage 1.0 to 4.0, with at least 1.0 between them). Honeywell's 68-0049-03 sheet gives a natural gas regulator range of 3 to 5 and LP of 8 to 12 for the VR8205. Goodman warns that only minor adjustments should be made at the regulator and that you must not derate the furnace by setting manifold pressure lower than specified.

Do not adjust when the inlet pressure is out of range, because you would be hiding a supply problem. Do not adjust if the manual says not to: Lennox says for its SLP98 gas valve, "Do not attempt to make adjustments to the gas valve," and Trane says the Precedent natural gas low-fire value is not field adjustable. Apprentices read and record; adjustments go through your supervisor.

Sealing the taps and leak checking

This step is not optional. Goodman says to turn each test screw back in to seal the port (clockwise, 7 in-lb minimum) and retest for leaks; if bubbles form, shut off the gas and repair the leak. Emerson says the same: restore power, energize the valve and check the pressure post screw with leak detection solution. Trane's guide warns to replace or tighten every plug removed or loosened and leak check the fittings before the furnace goes back into service, and says failure could result in fire, explosion or property damage. Prefer the manual's own leak-check steps. The lesson on nitrogen pressure testing covers a different job, testing new piping, so it is not the procedure here.

Worked example: inches w.c. to psi and ounces

The conversion needs a factor. NIST lists 1 inch of water (39.2 °F) as 249.082 pascals and 1 psi as 6,894.757 pascals. Dividing, 6,894.757 / 249.082 = 27.68, which rounds to 27.7 in. w.c. per psi. A pound is 16 ounces, so 1 psi is 16 ounces per square inch.

Suppose a Goodman MES80 on natural gas reads 3.4 in. w.c. at the manifold with burners firing, and 7.0 in. w.c. at the inlet.

  • Manifold in psi: 3.4 / 27.7 = 0.1227 psi.
  • Manifold in ounces: 0.1227 x 16 = 1.96 ounces per square inch.
  • Inlet in psi: 7.0 / 27.7 = 0.2527 psi.
  • Inlet in ounces: 0.2527 x 16 = 4.04 ounces per square inch.

Now compare to the Goodman ranges. Manifold 3.4 is inside 3.2 to 3.8 (0.2 above the low end, 0.4 below the high end). Inlet 7.0 is inside 4.5 to 10.0 (2.5 above the minimum). Suppose it then reads 6.2 with the other appliances firing: 6.2 - 4.5 = 1.7 above the minimum, so the supply holds up. Had it read 4.0, that is 0.5 below the minimum (4.0 - 4.5 = -0.5), so check the piping.

How this relates to clocking the meter

Pressure and input are linked but not the same thing. Manifold pressure in range with a wrong orifice, or a wrong heating value, can still give the wrong input. Clocking the meter measures actual input; the manometer tells you whether the pressure feeding it is what the manual expects. Use both. The procedure is in Clocking a Gas Meter and Checking Furnace Input. The combustion side is in Combustion and CO Safety Basics.

Field card: Checking Gas Inlet and Manifold Pressure

Inlet proves the supply; manifold proves the regulator.

  • Get the model's inlet and manifold targets from its manual first.
  • Zero a digital meter with the hoses on; back the tap screw out no more than one turn.
  • Inlet: read with other gas appliances firing and compare to the minimum.
  • Manifold: read with burners firing; two-stage furnaces have low and high values.
  • 1 psi = 27.7 in. w.c. = 16 oz per square inch (3.4 in. w.c. is about 1.96 oz).
  • Adjust only as the manual allows. Some valves and low-fire settings are not adjustable.
  • Seal every tap (Goodman: 7 in-lb minimum) and leak check with solution.
