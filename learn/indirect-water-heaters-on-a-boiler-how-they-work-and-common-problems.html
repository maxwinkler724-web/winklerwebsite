Indirect Water Heaters on a Boiler: How They Work and Common Problems

Last updated: 2026-10-16

An indirect water heater is an insulated tank with no flame and no electric element of its own. Your boiler heats the water in it by sending hot boiler water through a coil inside the tank. When the tank runs lukewarm, the cause is usually in the boiler, the pump or valve that serves the tank, or the settings, not in the tank itself.

Check the label and the manual first

Your tank's label and manual rule over this page. Hot water can scald and a relief valve that is blocked can be dangerous. Gas, oil and electrical work on the boiler belongs to a licensed professional where you live. This lesson explains what is going on so you can describe the problem accurately and ask good questions.

How it works

Weil-McLain's Aqua Plus manual and the Amtrol BoilerMate manual both describe the same basic idea: hot water from the boiler circulates through a heat exchanger coil inside the tank and gives up its heat to the stored drinking water. The two waters never mix. The boiler loop is a closed heating loop. The drinking water is the same supply that feeds your faucets.

The tank has its own thermostat or aquastat. The AllianceSL manual (U.S. Boiler Company, sold under the Burnham name) says a thermostat on the tank calls for heat when the stored water falls below its setpoint. That call starts a pump or opens a valve so boiler water flows through the coil, and it tells the boiler to fire if the boiler is cool.

The pieces that make it work

  • The tank thermostat. This is the "call." Weil-McLain says the thermostat connects at 24 volts AC to a two-pole terminal block. Its setting is the temperature you get. The HTP SuperStor Ultra manual recommends 120 F for the tank, with a 10 to 15 F differential.
  • A circulator or a zone valve. Amtrol says flow can be controlled with either a motorized zone valve or a circulator, and recommends a dedicated circulator with at least 5 gpm. See Zone Valves vs. Zone Circulators for how each one works.
  • Priority. Weil-McLain explains domestic priority: on a hot water call, the control switches off space heating and sends the boiler's output to the tank only, which gives the fastest recovery. HTP says that if the tank call and the heating call overlap and the tank runs short, check the wiring or set the tank as the priority. The AllianceSL manual shows a priority relay wiring diagram but does not explain priority in words.
  • The boiler's own limits. The boiler must be hot enough to push heat into the tank. HTP says to set the boiler high limit at least 20 F above the tank setpoint, and Weil-McLain says the same. See Boiler Controls: Aquastats and Cutoffs for how a high limit and low limit differ.

"Indirect water heater not getting hot": what to check

The manufacturers' lists overlap a lot. Here they are in a sensible order.

  1. Is the boiler running? If it is off or locked out, the tank has nothing to draw from. If you have no heat either, see Hot Water, No Heat: Circulator, Zone Valve and Air Diagnosis.
  2. Tank setpoint too low. HTP and Amtrol both list a setpoint that is too low. A tank set to 120 F will not feel as hot as one set to 140 F.
  3. Boiler setpoint too low. HTP lists "boiler temperature too low" and Weil-McLain says to raise the boiler operating temperature. Weil-McLain gives 200 F as the ceiling for boiler supply, and the AllianceSL manual says the high limit must hold the boiler at or below 200 F.
  4. No flow through the coil. Both Amtrol and HTP name these: a stuck or undersized zone valve, a circulator that is weak, failed or installed backward, closed shutoff valves, a stuck flow check, and trapped air.
  5. Calls overlapping. If the house is calling for heat at the same time as the tank, the boiler output is shared. This is the priority issue above.
  6. Demand higher than capacity. HTP and Amtrol list an undersized tank or boiler, or a flow that is simply too high. More on this in the example below.
  7. A dirty coil. Weil-McLain and Amtrol both say scale or deposits on the coil cut heat transfer. HTP notes that water above 135 F speeds up lime scale.
  8. A failed tank thermostat. Weil-McLain lists checking the thermostat for continuity and for a high enough setting. The AllianceSL thermostat has an indicator light: a flashing red light means a failure was detected and the thermostat needs to be replaced.

A homeowner can look at the thermostat setting, check whether the boiler is on, and note whether pipes to the tank are warm. Opening a zone valve, bleeding air or changing a boiler limit belongs to a service technician.

Scalding and the mixing valve

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's tap water scald alert gives these exposure times for severe burns on adults: about 2 seconds at 150 F, 6 seconds at 140 F, 30 seconds at 130 F and 5 minutes at 120 F. It urges people to set their water heaters to 120 F.

That matters more on a boiler than on a plain tank. A tank on a hot boiler can reach a high temperature fast, and Weil-McLain warns about "stacking," where the top of the tank is far hotter than the bottom. The AllianceSL manual says the factory setpoint is 120 F and the temperature should not go above 140 F without a properly installed mixing valve. HTP goes further and says that above 120 F you must use an ASSE 1017 rated mixing valve, and also says a mixing valve reduces scald risk but does not remove it.

HTP's own troubleshooting list suggests a mixing valve plus a higher tank temperature when demand flow is too high: store hotter, blend down. Do not confuse this domestic valve with the heating-loop valves in Mixing Valves and Injection Mixing Basics.

The relief valve

Every tank has a temperature and pressure (T&P) relief valve. HTP says one is required on all models and lists three reasons it might discharge: tank temperature too high, thermal expansion (add an expansion tank), or high line pressure (add a pressure-reducing valve). Weil-McLain and HTP both say the valve should be lifted by hand once a year, with nobody near the discharge, and that nothing may be placed between the valve and the tank or in the discharge line. Never plug or cap it. For a dripping valve, see Water Heater Leaking: Relief Valve, Drain Valve or Tank.

Worked example: does the boiler have enough output?

HTP's SuperStor Ultra manual lists the SSU-45 (a 45 gallon tank, going by the model name) with 141,000 Btu/hr gross boiler output and a first-hour rating of 212 gallons at a 140 F outlet with a 90 F rise. It also gives the continuous draw rule: first-hour rating minus 0.75 times the tank volume.

  1. Continuous draw = 212 - (0.75 x 45) = 212 - 33.75 = 178.25 gallons per hour.
  2. Heat needed for that flow: 178.25 gal/hr x 8.33 lb/gal x 90 F = 133,637 Btu/hr. (Water weighs about 8.33 lb per gallon, and one Btu raises one pound by one degree.)
  3. The listed 141,000 Btu/hr covers this with a small margin. The rating assumes the boiler can deliver that output and the circulator can push the required flow through the coil, as the manual states.
  4. Now suppose the boiler is limited to 100,000 Btu/hr for the tank. Even with perfect heat transfer, the most it could deliver is 100,000 / (8.33 x 90) = 133 gallons per hour, about 25 percent below the rating. A long shower plus a dishwasher will feel like it ran out.

That is the sizing logic behind "boiler output is insufficient" in the Amtrol list. The fix may be priority wiring, a larger tank, or a bigger boiler.

For technicians: quick bench checks

  • Confirm the tank call reaches the control (24 V at the thermostat, relay or zone valve end switch), then confirm the circulator runs or the zone valve opens.
  • Check the circulator arrow, the flow check, and the zone valve size. HTP calls for a 1 inch full-bore valve and at least 1 inch pipe.
  • Compare the boiler high limit to the tank setpoint (at least 20 F apart) and check for boiler low-limit or outdoor reset settings that hold the boiler cool.
  • Look up the model's first-hour and continuous ratings at your boiler supply temperature. Weil-McLain's ratings rise with boiler supply temperature, so a boiler running cooler than the rating table will fall short.

Field card: Indirect Water Heaters on a Boiler

The tank is only storage. The heat comes through the boiler, the pump or valve, and the tank call.

  • Boiler water runs through a coil in the tank; it never mixes with drinking water.
  • The tank thermostat makes the call; a circulator or zone valve moves the heat.
  • Boiler high limit at least 20 F above the tank setpoint (HTP, Weil-McLain).
  • No hot water: check setpoints, flow (valve, pump, air), overlapping calls, sizing, scale.
  • Scald: 150 F burns in about 2 seconds, 120 F in about 5 minutes (CPSC). Above 120 F, use a mixing valve per the maker.
  • Continuous draw = first-hour rating minus 0.75 x tank gallons (HTP).
  • Test the T&P valve yearly, never cap or plug the discharge.
