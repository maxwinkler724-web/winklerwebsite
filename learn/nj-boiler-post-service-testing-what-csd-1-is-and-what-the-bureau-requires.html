NJ Boiler Post-Service Testing: What CSD-1 Is and What the Bureau Requires

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the NJ Black Seal and Boilers Guide

After a repair, a boiler is not “done” until its safeties have been checked and the results written down. Here is what the standard is and what New Jersey's boiler bureau asked for.

What CSD-1 is

CSD-1 is the ASME standard for Controls and Safety Devices for Automatically Fired Boilers. It covers the safety side of a boiler that lights and runs itself: the low-water cutoff, the pressure and temperature limits, the relief valves, and the fuel and flame safeguards. The standard itself is sold, not free, and this lesson is based on its public table of contents, state forms, maker bulletins and the state advisory, not on the text. It does not claim anything is “compliant with CSD-1.”

The public contents page shows how it is laid out. General rules (testing and upkeep, certification and reporting, operating rules) come first. Then come sections for electrical, for the steam and water side (low-water cutoff and feedwater, pressure controls, temperature controls, relief valves), and for the combustion side (gas, purge, gas safety controls, oil, low-fire start, electric). Two appendices matter to field people: a manufacturer's or installing contractor's report form, and a recommended preventive maintenance schedule that is not mandatory. A section called CM-100 covers routine periodic testing and upkeep, and that is the one New Jersey points to.

What the NJ Bureau said in September 2023

The New Jersey Bureau of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Compliance issued an advisory dated September 6, 2023, under N.J.A.C. 12:90. In plain words:

  • Who: owners, maintenance contractors and repair contractors.
  • What: boilers must be tested after maintenance or repair, following Part CM-100 of the 2012 edition of CSD-1 as named in the advisory.
  • When: maintenance and inspection must happen on a periodic schedule at least as often as the equipment maker requires.
  • Records: device operability and set points must be written down in the boiler log, a maintenance record, the service invoice or another written record.
  • Defects: problems found go to the owner and are to be corrected immediately.

The advisory lists penalties under N.J.A.C. 12:90-9.1 of $500 to $10,000 for a first offense and $500 to $25,000 for later ones. Confirm current amounts with the Bureau before quoting them.

Under review: which edition, and how often

The advisory names the 2012 edition, while ASME has published newer ones. Which edition New Jersey has adopted by reference is not confirmed here and should be checked with the Bureau. Likewise, this lesson does not say that New Jersey requires CSD-1 testing every year apart from repairs; the advisory as summarized ties the test to maintenance and repair, and the schedule rule points back to the maker. Record-retention length is also unconfirmed.

Which devices are on the list

State report forms from Iowa and Arizona, which cite CSD-1 paragraphs, show the typical roster. Waterside: low-water fuel cutoff, forced-circulation safety, high steam pressure limit, high water temperature limit, plus the operating controls for pressure and temperature. Fuel and combustion side: main and pilot safety shutoff valves, combustion air switch, high and low gas pressure switches, low oil pressure and oil temperature switches, atomizing medium switch, purge air flow, flame safeguard and detector, and the low-fire start switch. Relief: safety or safety relief valves, with maker, model, size and capacity recorded. Paragraph numbers differ a little between forms, so use the printed form for the edition in force. A maker bulletin from Burnham adds that a low-water cutoff should be testable without draining the system.

Why the paperwork matters

Think of the record as proof of the test, not a receipt for the visit. An invoice that says “serviced boiler” shows nothing. One that names the device and its set point shows that someone checked it. The next lesson, what to write down, covers a sample record layout.

Field card: NJ post-service boiler testing

The test after service is part of the repair, and the record is part of the test.

  • CSD-1 = ASME controls and safety devices standard for automatically fired boilers. Text is paywalled; this lesson did not use it.
  • NJ Bureau advisory, Sept 6, 2023 (N.J.A.C. 12:90): test after maintenance or repair per CM-100, record operability and set points.
  • Owners, maintenance contractors and repair contractors are all named.
  • Defects go to the owner and are corrected immediately.
  • Penalties listed: $500 to $10,000 first offense, $500 to $25,000 after. Confirm current amounts.
  • Edition adopted, annual-test question and retention period: check with the Bureau.
