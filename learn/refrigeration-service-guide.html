Refrigeration Service Guide

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Most cooling and refrigeration calls come down to the same loop: compressor, condenser, metering device and evaporator, with the right charge, clean oil and dry refrigerant moving through it. This guide collects the free lessons a service technician uses once the equipment is open, in an order that builds from the cycle to the diagnosis.

Start with the cycle and the readings that describe it: pressures, temperatures, superheat and subcooling. Then the parts that protect the circuit and the compressor: filter driers and sight glasses, refrigerant oils, evacuation, and the starting components that decide whether a compressor runs at all. The last group ties symptoms to causes, such as undercharge versus overcharge and dirty condensers.

Handling refrigerant is regulated. Certification, recovery and leak rules are covered in the EPA 608 guide, and newer mildly flammable refrigerants are in the A2L guide. Targets and procedures come from the equipment maker, so use these lessons for understanding and the nameplate and manual for numbers.

  • Charging Methods Overview: A general overview of refrigerant charging methods (weigh-in, superheat, subcooling and approach) and why the manufacturer's instructions always decide.
