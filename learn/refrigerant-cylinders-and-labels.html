Refrigerant Cylinders and Labels

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the EPA 608 Guide

A refrigerant cylinder is a pressure vessel with rules attached. This lesson covers the difference between new and recovery cylinders, what labels tell you, and the basic habits of safe handling.

Two kinds of cylinders

You will meet two broad kinds of refrigerant containers.

  • Virgin (new product) cylinders hold new refrigerant from a producer. Many are disposable, meant to be used once and never refilled.
  • Recovery cylinders are built to be refilled. They are used to hold refrigerant taken out of equipment. They are a different design from disposable cylinders, often with separate liquid and vapor connections, and they carry required markings.

Never refill a disposable cylinder. Never use a recovery cylinder for anything the markings and rules do not allow.

Why labels matter

A label tells you what is inside, and what is inside decides how you can use it. Look for the refrigerant name or number, the hazard information, and the manufacturer's details. Federal hazard communication rules require chemical containers to carry standard label elements and require the maker to supply a safety data sheet. The data sheet is where you learn about hazards, first aid and handling for that specific refrigerant.

If a label is missing, torn or unreadable, treat the contents as unknown. Do not guess, and do not connect it to equipment. Follow your employer's procedure for unidentified containers.

Colors are a hint, not an answer

Many refrigerants follow a color convention, and recovery cylinders are often gray with yellow on the top or collar. Industry guidance (AHRI Guideline N) also recommends moving to a uniform light green-gray for new refrigerant containers, so older color charts may not match new cylinders. Colors fade, get painted over and vary between makers; an industry article quotes a refrigerant maker saying color should never be relied on to identify the contents. Use color to get your attention, then confirm with the label.

Do not mix refrigerants

Mixing different refrigerants in one container creates a contaminated blend that may be costly or impossible to reuse. It can also be unsafe. Keep each refrigerant in its own properly marked container, and follow the rules that apply to how recovered refrigerant is stored and sent for reuse or reprocessing.

Safe handling habits

  • Respect the fill limit. Liquid expands when it warms, so cylinders must keep empty space. A scale is the usual way to track what goes in. A common industry rule of thumb for recovery cylinders is no more than about 80 percent liquid full at 77 °F, and never past the gross weight on the markings. That is trade guidance, not a quoted DOT rule. The federal text we read (49 CFR 173.304) says the liquid may not completely fill a liquefied-gas cylinder at 131 °F and gives no percentage. So follow the cylinder markings and your supplier or reclaimer.
  • Never heat a cylinder with a flame or another uncontrolled source.
  • Protect the valve. Keep valve caps on when not in use. A broken valve can turn a cylinder into a hazard.
  • Secure it. Keep cylinders from tipping or rolling, indoors and in vehicles.
  • Inspect before use. Look for dents, rust, damage and expired test dates. A damaged cylinder should be taken out of service and reported.

Transport basics

Moving cylinders on public roads is covered by hazardous materials transportation rules from the U.S. Department of Transportation. The details depend on the contents, the quantity and your role, so they are beyond a beginner lesson. The basic habits are to keep cylinders secured and upright where required, keep them out of the passenger area, keep the valve protected, ventilate the vehicle, and never leave cylinders in a hot closed vehicle. Your employer should train you on the specific requirements that apply to your work.

For small loads, the DOT “materials of trade” exception (49 CFR 173.6) is the usual reference: it caps the total gross weight of such materials on one vehicle at 440 lb (200 kg) and a single flammable or non-flammable gas cylinder at 220 lb (100 kg) gross, with securing and marking conditions. Carrying more, or other cases, brings extra requirements, and state rules and your employer’s policy also apply.

Flammable refrigerants

Cylinders of flammable refrigerants have their own labeling and handling rules. If you are not trained for them, do not handle them. When you are trained, treat storage and transport with extra care.

Field card: Refrigerant Cylinders and Labels

Know what is in the cylinder and what the cylinder is built for.

  • Disposable cylinders are never refilled.
  • Recovery cylinders are made for refilling and carry required markings.
  • Read the label. Colors are only a hint.
  • Never mix refrigerants or heat a cylinder.
  • Secure cylinders and protect valves in storage and transport.
