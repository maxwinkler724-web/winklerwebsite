EPA 608 Records: What Technicians Keep

Last updated: 2026-10-16

Part of the EPA 608 Guide

Recordkeeping is the least exciting part of Section 608 and one of the easiest things for an inspector to ask about. Here is a plain summary of what EPA's own pages say technicians must keep, and for how long.

Your certification

EPA says to keep a copy of your certification at your place of business, until three years after you are no longer operating as a technician. You do not have to carry the card on every job, but a photo on your phone can help if a customer asks. If you lose a card, contact the organization that certified you; if that organization is gone, EPA lists other approved programs that can issue replacements. Certification types are in Type I, II, III Explained.

Disposal records for mid-size equipment

For appliances holding 5 to 50 pounds of refrigerant, such as many residential split systems, technicians who dispose of them keep records. EPA lists these items:

  • the location of the appliance, the date of recovery, and the type of refrigerant
  • the total amount recovered each calendar month, by refrigerant type
  • for refrigerant sent for reclamation or destruction, the quantity and type, who received it and the transfer date

EPA's technician Q&A page states these records are kept for three years, in any format, paper or electronic. An employer can keep them, but the technician remains responsible for making sure they are kept. See Refrigerant Recovery, Recycle and Reclaim.

Large equipment

For appliances with 50 or more pounds of ozone-depleting refrigerant, EPA says technicians provide the owner with an invoice showing refrigerant added and records of leak inspections and repair verification tests. Owners keep servicing records showing the date, type of service and amount of refrigerant added. These leak repair rules in 40 CFR 82.157 cover ozone-depleting refrigerant (CFC and HCFC), not appliances using only HFCs; EPA's 2020 rule removed the HFC extension. HFC appliances with 15 pounds or more of a refrigerant with GWP above 53 follow the AIM Act Emissions Reduction and Reclamation rule, 40 CFR Part 84, with leak repair starting January 1, 2026. Law-firm summaries describe thresholds of 20, 30 and 10 percent by appliance type and a 30-day repair window, with a narrow exemption for residential and light commercial air conditioning (check the rule for its scope). We did not read the HFC rule on an EPA page, and recordkeeping details for it were not in those summaries, so confirm on the EPA and eCFR pages. A broader view is in Leaks and Leak Repair Basics.

Who else keeps records

  • Certifying organizations keep test-taker records, scores, dates and locations indefinitely unless transferred, post certified-technician information online, and send EPA activity reports twice a year.
  • Owners of large appliances that leak more than a set amount in a year report to EPA.
  • Reclaimers keep records of who sent them material and report totals yearly.

What the pages do not say

EPA's recordkeeping page does not list retention periods for every record type, and refers you to the regulation (40 CFR Part 82, Subpart F). The regulation text (40 CFR 82.157(l), read through Cornell's law library copy) says leak inspection, service and disposal records are kept at least 3 years, and full-charge records until 3 years after the appliance is retired. That copy can lag behind changes, so confirm on EPA's current eCFR page or with your employer's compliance lead. Also note that EPA can inspect and fine for violations, so keep tidy, dated records. To practice the rules, use the free 608 course and quiz. For a start on background, see EPA 608 Basics.

Field card: EPA 608 Records: What Technicians Keep

If it is not written down, it did not happen.

  • Keep a copy of your certification at your business for 3 years after you stop working as a technician.
  • Mid-size disposals (5 to 50 lb): record location, date, refrigerant type, monthly totals and reclaim transfers.
  • Disposal records are kept 3 years, paper or electronic.
  • Large equipment (50 lb+ ODS): invoices and leak inspection / verification records go to the owner.
  • Leak inspection, service and disposal records: at least 3 years (40 CFR 82.157(l)); confirm on current eCFR.
  • HFC systems at 15+ lb follow the AIM Act rule (40 CFR Part 84) instead of 82.157; confirm on EPA/eCFR.
  • Check 40 CFR Part 82 Subpart F for details this page does not give.
