Combi Boiler: No Hot Water or No Heat, Common Causes

Last updated: 2026-10-16

A combi boiler heats your house and makes hot water for the tap with one burner and no storage tank. That is why one problem can take out both, and why "no hot water" often has a different cause than "no heat." This lesson explains how the hot water side works, then walks the common causes a homeowner can recognize.

Start here: what you can safely check

Look at the pressure gauge, check whether the display shows a message, and try one hot tap at a steady, moderate flow. Write down any message exactly as shown. Do not open the cabinet, bypass a safety, or relight anything that smells of gas. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility from outside. Everything past the gauge, the display and the taps belongs to a licensed technician.

How a combi makes hot water on demand

Inside the boiler is a small plate heat exchanger: a stack of thin stainless plates where boiler water runs on one side of each plate and your potable water runs on the other. The two never mix. Viessmann's Vitodens 100-W manual lists a plate-type heat exchanger, a flow sensor, a pump, a diverting valve and an outlet temperature sensor as the parts of its on-demand hot water kit. Navien's combi installation manual likewise lists a DHW heat exchanger, a DHW flow sensor, a motorized three-way valve and a circulation pump as key components. Lochinvar describes the plate exchanger in its Noble combi as stainless and brazed.

Here is the sequence in plain terms. You open a tap. Water moving through the unit trips the flow sensor. The control sees that call, sends boiler water through the plate exchanger instead of the heating loop, and fires the burner. When you close the tap, the call ends and the boiler returns to heating. The Viessmann manual states that DHW has priority over the heating circuit in its layout, and also notes a short pump post-purge after the call ends. Because priority is a design choice, read your own manual for how your model behaves.

No hot water: the common causes

Flow too low to start

The burner only starts when the sensor sees enough flow. Navien's NCB-E spec sheet lists a minimum DHW flow of 0.5 GPM, and Triangle Tube lists 0.5 GPM for its Combi models. A barely open tap, a clogged aerator or a partly closed valve can stay under that line and give you cold water. Try a tap at a stronger flow. A clogged inlet filter screen can also cut flow; Navien's manual checklist asks whether the cold inlet water filter has been cleaned. See Tankless Gas Water Heater Problems for the same idea in a stand-alone unit.

Low system pressure

A combi is also a boiler with a closed heating loop, and that loop needs pressure. Viessmann's manual gives a minimum boiler operating pressure of 14 to 23 psig for the Vitodens 100-W. Navien's CH manual says its pressure-type low water cutoff allows a minimum system pressure of 7 psig. Different makers, different numbers. If the gauge has dropped, the boiler may stop firing for both heat and hot water. Do not keep refilling without finding out why it fell; Boiler Pressure Too High or Too Low covers what to check and when to call.

Scale inside the plate exchanger

Hot, hard water leaves mineral deposits. The passages in a plate exchanger are narrow, so scale cuts heat transfer and flow. Navien's CH manual recommends water treatment such as a softener for highly acidic or extremely hard water to keep the full warranty, and tells you to consult the local water authority. Symptoms are a slow drift to lukewarm water or a drop in flow over months. Descaling is a maker-specific job with the right isolation valves and fluid; leave it to a technician or follow your manual exactly.

Condensate trap or line blocked or frozen

A condensing boiler makes acidic water as it cools the flue gas; see Boiler Return Temperature and Condensing. It leaves through a trap and a small drain line. Navien's CH manual says to confirm the condensate flows freely and to pull off the condensate hose every 6 months to check for debris. Its NCB-E guide says the trap must be filled with water at setup. Weil-McLain's AquaBalance manual warns to prevent boiler water and condensate from freezing. A line that runs through a cold space can freeze and back up. What your boiler does when the trap backs up is in its own troubleshooting table, so do not guess. If you use a pump, see Condensate Pump Not Working.

Power or freeze protection

Navien's CH manual warns that during a power failure in cold weather its freeze prevention system will not operate and may result in the heat exchanger freezing, and that freeze damage is not covered by the warranty. A dead display after a power outage is worth noting for your technician.

Error codes belong to the maker

There is no universal code list. A number or letter on one brand means something else on another. Navien's quick guide says only that you can try resetting the boiler if an error appears. Lochinvar says its Noble display shows diagnostic information in words rather than codes and logs the last 10 lockouts. Viessmann's manual mentions a fault F4 at start-up from air in the gas line, and points to a diagnostics table for the rest. Write down the code and look it up in your model's manual. One reset is reasonable; repeated resets on a lockout are not, so call for service.

Worked example: flow versus temperature rise

Hot water output is limited by burner capacity, so flow and temperature rise trade off. Triangle Tube's published table for its Combi 155 lists 4.0 GPM at a 70 degree F rise, 3.7 GPM at 75 and 3.5 GPM at 80. Water carries about 500 Btu/h for each GPM per degree F (8.33 lb per gallon x 60 minutes per hour x 1.0 Btu per lb-degree F, rounded). Check the table against that rule.

  1. At 70 degrees: 500 x 4.0 x 70 = 140,000 Btu/h.
  2. At 80 degrees: 500 x 3.5 x 80 = 140,000 Btu/h. The table agrees: the capacity is constant, and flow trades for rise.

Now put it in a house. Say winter water enters at 45 degrees F and the shower needs about 115, a 70 degree rise. The unit can deliver 4.0 GPM at that rise. If a second person runs a sink so total hot demand is 5.0 GPM, the rise drops to 140,000 / (500 x 5.0) = 56 degrees F. Leaving water is then about 45 + 56 = 101 degrees F, which feels lukewarm. In colder incoming water the effect is bigger. That is the combi's normal limit, not a defect. The Combi 199 table shows 5.3 GPM at 70 degrees, so a bigger burner means more flow at the same rise. This arithmetic ignores mixing and heat loss, so treat the result as an estimate.

No heat but hot water works

If the display is dead or the boiler will not fire for heat, check the thermostat call and the pressure gauge first, then note any message and call a technician. A working hot tap does not clear the heating side, because the two run through different paths in the boiler.

Tech section

For technicians: confirm cold inlet pressure and flow at the unit against the spec (Navien lists 15 to 150 PSI DHW on the NCB-E sheet, and a supply pressure greater than 40 psi on the CH checklist). Check the flow sensor reading, three-way valve travel, outlet temperature sensor and circulator. Compare plate exchanger temperature rise to the maker's table before blaming the burner. Verify trap fill (Viessmann specifies at least 10 fl. oz. of water) and hose cleanliness. Follow the maker's flush and descale method; do not substitute a generic acid.

Field card: Combi Boiler No Hot Water or No Heat

Flow, pressure, scale, condensate, then the manual.

  • Tap flow must pass the minimum to start the burner (0.5 GPM on several makers).
  • Check the pressure gauge; low pressure can stop heat and hot water.
  • Hard water scales the plate exchanger; follow the maker for treatment.
  • Keep the condensate trap and line clear and unfrozen.
  • Codes are maker-specific; look up your model.
  • Capacity: Btu/h = 500 x GPM x rise.
  • Gas smell: leave and call from outside.
