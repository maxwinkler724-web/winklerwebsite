Cooling Tower Fan, Fill, Drift and Basin Checks

A cooling tower has four parts that do most of the work: the fan and drive, the fill, the drift eliminators and the basin. Each fails in its own way, and each is easier to check than to repair.

Safety before you touch anything

Fans, gearboxes, drives and pumps can start without warning, even from a remote command. Before reaching into a fan stack or opening a drive guard, follow your site’s lockout/tagout procedure, and remember that a wind-milling fan is still stored energy. Towers sit on roofs and platforms, so use the fall protection your site requires. Basins and sumps may be confined spaces that need a permit and testing. Read the safety data sheet before any chemical task. Hosing and cleaning create mist, so ask what respiratory protection your site requires.

How to use the intervals

The frequencies below come from a maintenance checklist page and are examples of typical intervals. The manufacturer’s manual and your site plan take priority.

Fans, gearbox and drive

  • Walk-around: listen for noise, feel for vibration, and look for leaks. The example page suggests this daily.
  • Blades: condition, pitch and tip clearance (example: quarterly).
  • Fasteners and mounting bolts: tightness (example: monthly).
  • Gearbox oil: level, color and leaks (example: monthly). Milky or low oil can mean water ingress or a seal leak. Report it and do not run the unit to see.
  • Couplings, shafts and belts: inspect (example: monthly).
  • Vibration cutout switch: test per the manufacturer (example: quarterly). Never bypass it.

Fill and nozzles

Fill is the surface that spreads water so air can evaporate it. Look for scale, algae and debris (example: monthly) and for sagging, breaks or collapse (example: quarterly). Spray nozzles should be clear and give an even pattern. Uneven distribution leaves part of the fill dry and the leaving water warm.

Drift eliminators

These catch droplets that would otherwise leave the tower, and CDC’s toolkit recommends high-efficiency ones as part of Legionella control. Confirm they are in place, seated and undamaged (the example page suggests quarterly and marks it critical). Missing sections are worth reporting even if everything else looks fine. See Cooling Tower Water Management and Legionella Awareness.

Basin and strainers

  • Level and make-up valve operation: is the float or level control working, and is the valve passing?
  • Sludge, silt and biofilm (example: monthly).
  • Suction and basin strainers cleaned (example: monthly).
  • Seasonal drain, clean and disinfect, following your plan.

Structure and access

Check casing and fasteners for corrosion, and ladders, handrails and access doors for safety (example: quarterly). In cold weather, add freeze checks: basin temperature, heaters and heat trace, and ice on louvers or fill. Heater maker Indeeco says basin freeze-protection thermostats are often set near 45 °F, with heater sizing based on about 40 °F. That is one example; use your site’s setpoints and the tower and heater makers’ instructions.

Simple things first

If leaving water is warmer than usual, check the simple causes before blaming the equipment: is the fan running at the commanded speed, is the basin level right, are strainers clean, are nozzles and fill in good condition, and is flow normal? Write down what you found before changing anything. See Cooling Tower Basics for how the tower works.

Printable checklist

  • Lockout/tagout before any fan or drive work
  • Fall protection and confined-space rules followed
  • Fans: noise, vibration, leaks checked
  • Gearbox oil level and color checked
  • Drive guards, couplings and belts in place and sound
  • Vibration cutout switch tested per schedule
  • Fill: no scale build-up, algae, sagging or collapse
  • Nozzles clear, even distribution
  • Drift eliminators intact and seated
  • Basin: level, make-up valve, sludge, strainers
  • Freeze protection working (cold weather)
  • Findings written down and reported
