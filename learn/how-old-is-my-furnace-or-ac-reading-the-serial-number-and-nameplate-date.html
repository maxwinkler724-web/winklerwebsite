How Old Is My Furnace or AC? Serial Number and Nameplate Date

Last updated: 2026-10-16

The age of your equipment is stamped on it, but not always in plain numbers. This lesson shows where to find the data plate on each kind of unit, what we could and could not confirm about decoding serial numbers, and how to turn a date into an age.

Check the maker

Check the maker. Serial number formats change over the years and between product lines. This page uses only rules that a manufacturer published on its own web pages, which we read in 2026. Where a maker did not publish a rule, we say so instead of guessing. For a definite answer on your unit, give the maker or your dealer the full model and serial number.

Step 1: find the data plate

The age clue lives on the rating plate, also called the nameplate or data plate. It is a label or metal tag with the model number and the serial number. Where it sits depends on the equipment, and the makers say so:

  • Outdoor air conditioner or heat pump. Trane says it is on the top, right-rear of the outdoor unit. Carrier says it is on the back near the service valve connections. Goodman describes the rating plate as slightly above the refrigerant valves on the back side. Plates move around between makers, so check the back and the top.
  • Furnace. Carrier says it is inside the front door panel. Trane says the right or left side panel. Lennox tells owners to remove the top-front service panel and look on the left side of the interior cabinet or on top of the blower deck, and its furnace manual places the nameplate on the blower deck below the combustion air inducer. Shut the furnace off at the switch before you open a panel, and put it back before you restart it.
  • Air handler or indoor coil. Trane says the front panel of the indoor unit, which may be in a closet, attic, basement or garage.
  • Packaged unit. Trane says the rear panel of the outdoor unit. Rheem notes that package equipment carries one model and serial tag, while a split system has two, one on the outdoor unit and one indoors. Match the tags to the right piece of equipment.
  • Boiler. Weil-McLain says its CP number (its name for the serial-style number) is on a small barcode sticker on either side of the boiler jacket, and on some models, such as the Ultra, inside the jacket near the heat exchanger.

Photograph the plate with your phone. It is easier than reading it upside down behind a furnace, and you will want the full numbers if you call the maker.

Step 2: what the makers publish

Here is the honest picture. We read the owner-support pages of Trane, Carrier and Bryant, Lennox, Goodman and Rheem. They tell you where the plate is, but none of the pages we read spells out how to turn a furnace, air conditioner or heat pump serial number into a build date. Trane's page, for example, says only "Contact your installing dealer" for help decoding the number, and Goodman says the serial number is a ten digit code that helps identify when the unit was produced without giving the format. Carrier's furnace warranty page lists an eligible serial range, but it does not explain the digits. Lennox's model and serial nomenclature document has a serial numbering heading with no format under it.

That is why lists of decoding rules on the internet are risky. Many come from repair blogs and forums, and a rule that fits one product line can be wrong for another. We left them out. If you want one, treat it as a secondary guess and confirm it with the maker.

Boiler makers are more open. Two published rules we could confirm:

  • Weil-McLain. The maker posts a CP date range chart. Find the range your boiler's seven-digit CP number falls in, then read the year in the left column. The chart calls the result an approximate date of manufacture and warns that AquaBalance CP numbers are not sequential, so the chart may not work for that model.
  • Peerless (PB Heat). The maker's FAQ says the numbers after the hyphen in the serial number indicate the manufacture date. Its example is a serial ending in March 2010. One page of its FAQ shows that as 123456-0310, and a second page shows it as 123456-201003. Both give March 2010, but the two formats differ, so confirm yours with PB Heat.

Worked example: two boilers, step by step

Weil-McLain boiler. Suppose the sticker on the jacket reads CP 3900000. This number is made up to show the method; the range comes from the maker's chart.

  1. Find the CP number: 3,900,000.
  2. Scan the chart for the row whose range contains it. The row for 2000 runs from 3,810,001 to 4,055,000, and 3,900,000 falls inside it.
  3. Read the year in the left column: 2000.
  4. Age as of 2026: 2026 minus 2000 = 26 years, approximate, because the chart gives a year range.

Peerless boiler. Use the maker's own example, serial 123456-0310.

  1. Look only at the numbers after the hyphen: 0310.
  2. Under the maker's rule, 0310 means March 2010. (In the six-digit form, 201003 reads the same way: 2010, month 03.)
  3. Age as of 2026: 2026 minus 2010 = 16 years. Counting from March 2010 to October 2026, it is about 16 and a half.

For both, the arithmetic is simple. The hard part is knowing the rule is real for your product, which is why we only used published ones.

Does the plate print a date?

Some plates have a printed manufacture date or a "date of manufacture" field. None of the maker pages we read describe one, so we cannot say how common that is. If you see an actual date on the plate, use it instead of decoding the serial number. Be careful not to confuse it with a certification date, a warranty date or the date the unit was installed.

Why age alone does not decide replacement

ENERGY STAR says to consider replacing a heat pump or air conditioner that is more than 10 years old, or a furnace or boiler more than 15 years old. It also lists frequent repairs with rising bills, uneven rooms, humidity, dust and noise as other signs. Those are prompts to compare options, not expiration dates. A 26-year-old boiler that runs safely and gets its yearly service is a different decision than a 9-year-old air conditioner with a failed compressor. Our lesson How Long Do HVAC Systems Last? Repair or Replace walks through the repair-or-replace questions, and the age you found here is one input.

What else the plate tells you

  • Refrigerant. EPA says most air conditioners have a nameplate that identifies the refrigerant and also lists safety certifications and electrical ratings. An older unit marked R-22 raises different questions; see My AC Uses R-22: Repair or Replace?
  • Electrical ratings. MCA and MOCP size the wire and the breaker. Read them in Reading an HVAC Nameplate, and leave circuit changes to a licensed electrician.
  • Model number. It points to the product line and size. For what efficiency ratings mean, see What Does a SEER2 Rating Mean?

Field card: How Old Is My Furnace or AC?

Find the plate, photograph it, ask the maker.

  • Outdoor unit: look on the back or top. Furnace: inside the front panel or on a side. Boiler: sticker on the jacket.
  • Photograph the full model and serial number.
  • Decode only with a rule the maker published, or ask the maker or dealer.
  • Weil-McLain: find the CP range in the maker's chart, read the year.
  • Age = 2026 minus build year. Treat it as approximate.
  • Age is one input. Repairs, bills, safety and refrigerant matter too.
