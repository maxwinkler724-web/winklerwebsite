Fix-It Van: guide for parents and teachers

A short, cheerful game for roughly ages 7 to 12. Players drive a service van around a small made-up town and fix twenty-five home calls. Each step asks a multiple-choice question, and a helper explains why the right answer is right. Wrong answers never end the game; they just explain and let the player try again.

Using it

  • Play time: 3 to 6 minutes per job. Stars (1 to 3) depend on how many wrong picks a player made, and progress is saved on that device only.
  • Accessibility: everything works from the keyboard, the "Call board" lists every job so nobody has to drive, sound has an on/off button, and animation respects the reduced-motion setting.
  • Finish all twenty-five jobs to see a printable on-screen certificate. The name is typed on that screen only and is never saved or sent.
  • Controls: arrow keys or WASD, or the on-screen buttons on phones and tablets. Space or the Knock button opens a job. Number keys 1 to 3 pick answers.
  • Classroom idea: play one job together on a projector, ask "why do you think that comes first?" before revealing the answer, then let pairs try another.

What each job teaches

JobReal-world ideaSafety habit
Furnace igniter (Maple Street)A furnace lights its gas with an electric igniter that can crack. A meter shows whether electricity passes through a part.Power off at the service switch before opening a panel; handle parts by the base; test the full cycle.
Dirty filter (Oak Avenue)Airflow matters. Filters have an arrow showing air direction.Turn the thermostat off while swapping; change filters on a schedule.
CO alarm (Pine Court)Carbon monoxide is invisible and odorless. Fuel-burning appliances can make it when vents or burners have problems.Get everyone out and call 911; never disable an alarm or bypass a safety device.
Water heater (Cedar Lane)An electric heater warms water with elements inside the tank.Prove power is off with a meter; drain below the element; fill the tank before power on; 120 degrees F avoids scalds.
Thermostat (Birch Road)A thermostat sends a low-voltage signal that tells the furnace to run. Wire terminals have letters.Power off, photograph and label wires by terminal letter, then test.
Mini-split (Elm Street)Dirty filters and blocked outdoor units weaken cooling.Use the disconnect; only EPA 608 certified techs open refrigerant lines.
Condensate drain (Spruce Drive)Cooling removes water from the air, and a drain carries it away.Never bypass a float switch; power off near water.
Outdoor unit (Willow Way)The outdoor unit sheds heat and needs clear airflow.Power off before reaching in; wear gloves; rinse gently, never with a pressure washer.
Smoke alarm chirp (Aspen Alley)A chirp means low battery; alarms expire after about 10 years.Use a stable ladder; test monthly; get out and call 911 if it sounds for smoke.
Dryer vent (Linden Lane)Lint blocks airflow, wastes energy and can burn.Unplug (and shut gas) first; metal duct; clean the lint trap every load; never use flame.
Gas smell (Hazel Hill)Natural gas has an added rotten-egg smell so leaks get noticed.Leave right away, avoid sparks and switches, call from outside, wait for the pros.
Space heater (Cottonwood Ct)Heaters can ignite cloth and overload cords.Three feet of clear space, wall outlet only, off when away; tell a grown-up about hot cords.
Frozen faucet (Frostwood Dr)Water expands when it freezes and can crack pipes.Never use open flame; stay away from wet outlets; tell a grown-up to shut the main valve and call a licensed plumber.
Window AC drip (Breezy Blvd)Cooling removes water from the air and it must drain outside.Stay out of water near cords; a grown-up unplugs only when safe; do not open the case.
Generator (Alder Ave)Portable generators make carbon monoxide and need dry, open-air use.Outdoors only, about 20 feet from the house, CO alarms inside, never plug into a wall outlet, cool before refueling; tell a grown-up.
Power strip (Birchwood Pl)Crowded or high-power loads can overheat strips and cords.No heaters on strips, no daisy-chaining, tell a grown-up about warm plugs or frayed cords.
Drafty bedroom (Chestnut St)Air leaks make rooms feel cold and waste energy; vents need clear space.Test drafts with tissue never flame, never cover vents, a grown-up does caulking and ladders.
Hot radiator (Hawthorn Way)Hot surfaces burn; keep cloth and paper away; knocking pipes are a job for a tech.Look, don't touch; leave space around heaters; tell a grown-up about banging or leaks.
Steamy bathroom (Sycamore St)Moisture and mold; exhaust fans pull damp air out.Use the fan, a grown-up turns power off before any fan repair, tell a grown-up about mold.
Fireplace (Juniper Lane)Fire safety and carbon monoxide.Grown-ups light fires, use a screen, keep alarms working, make sure it is out before bed.
Winter no-heat (Snowberry Lane)Check the thermostat first; stay warm safely; check the CO alarm.Never use ovens, stoves or grills for heat; power off before opening a furnace; tell a grown-up.
Spring filter change (Tulip Terrace)What an air filter does, how to tell it is dirty, and the airflow arrow.Power off first; only open the filter door; a grown-up changes it about monthly.
Alarm battery check (Cardinal Court)Smoke and CO alarm chirps, test buttons, end-of-life dates.Grown-ups use step stools; never test with flames; get outside if a CO alarm sounds.
Outdoor unit cleanup (Maple Hollow)Why an outdoor unit needs airflow and clear space.Power off, gloves, never poke the fins; a grown-up restores power.
Vacation mode puzzle (Seashell Street)Away mode saves energy; match the mode to who is home.Use buttons not tools; sensible settings; grown-ups set schedules.

Important notes

  • The game teaches ideas, not hands-on repair. Children should never open furnaces, water heaters, outdoor units or electrical panels. Real work is for trained, licensed adults. Rules and licensing vary by state.
  • Details such as the 120 degree F setting, clearances and filter schedules are common guidance. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions and local codes.
  • Characters and houses are made up. Nothing in the game refers to real people.

Privacy (COPPA)

The game collects no personal information, has no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and makes no requests that send data anywhere. After the first visit the game also opens without a connection, because the browser keeps the game's own files on the device. Stars are stored only in the browser on the player's own device and can be erased by clearing site data. A page that sells or shares children's data would break the promise made to families, so none exists.
