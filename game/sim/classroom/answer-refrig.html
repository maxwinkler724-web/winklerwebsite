Answer key: Commercial refrigeration

Teacher copy. Student worksheet | Open the simulator module

Learning goals
  • Explain defrost (timer, termination, fail-safe) and fan delay for a walk-in.
  • State the cold-holding limit (41 F or below) and why product held above it is evaluated, not served.
  • Follow an ice machine through fill, freeze and harvest and test the controls before the board.
Safety note from the module. Educational simulation only. Lock out before touching heaters, fans or the ice machine. Never serve product that has been held above 41 F without following the food safety plan. Refrigerant work requires EPA 608 certification. Never bypass defrost or safety controls.

Answers are the simulator's own explanations, so wording in a student's own words will differ. Accept any answer that names the cause and a repair that matches. Scores and numbers in the sim are practice examples.

Call 1: Walk-in freezer coil is a block of ice

Kitchen manager Mr. Dalton
Cause and explanation: The timer fired and the termination sensor was sound, but the cycle ended only on the fail-safe timer: one defrost heater element was open and could not melt the coil. Measure the heaters before blaming the compressor. Replace the open element with the coil's own part, hand-defrost the coil with the unit off, and confirm defrost now ends on temperature. Do not lift the fan delay shorter than the drip time.
Repairs needed: Replace the open defrost heater element (match the coil's part).
Also acceptable (no penalty): Hand-defrost the coil with the unit off (no sharp tools); Check the termination sensor and timer settings.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Is defrost starting? Is it ending on temperature or on the fail-safe? Measure the heaters. / The sensor and timer behave. What heats the coil?
Source basis: ACHR News, Troubleshooting Defrost Problems; ACHR News, The Professor (defrost termination and fan delay); Copeland defrost board sheet. Numbers are examples.

Call 2: Reach-in runs warm after the lunch rush

Cafe owner Ms. Greco
Cause and explanation: The condenser coil was matted with grease and lint, so head pressure and amps climbed, and the torn gasket let warm air in. The shelf air read 46 F and the product 44 F. Clean the coil, replace the gasket, recheck the temperature, and tell the manager that product held above 41 F has to be evaluated under the food safety plan, not served. FDA Food Code cold holding is 41 F or below.
Repairs needed: Clean the condenser coil; Replace the door gasket; Tell the manager: product held above 41 F must be evaluated, not served.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Look at the condenser coil, the gasket and the real temperatures. / What happens to food that sat above 41 F?
Source basis: FDA Food Code, cold holding at 41 F or below (section 3-501.16); state inspection guidance. Box targets are practice.

Call 3: Ice machine makes small cloudy cubes

Bar manager Ms. Tran
Cause and explanation: Hard water had scaled the evaporator plate and a small, low-flow filter starved the machine, so freezes ran long and the freeze-timer alarm sounded (3 beeps on this maker's example). Descale and sanitize following the maker's instructions, fit a properly sized filter, then clear the alarm. Replacing the board or compressor would have fixed nothing.
Repairs needed: Descale and sanitize per the maker's instructions; Fit a properly sized water filter.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Clear the alarm and restart.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Read the control and look at the water supply. / Look at the evaporator plate.
Source basis: Hoshizaki KM-201/260 service manual (one maker's example: other makers use different timers, codes and cleaning intervals).

Call 4: Ice machine hums but makes no ice

Hotel engineer Mr. Bell
Cause and explanation: The bin switch worked, but the harvest never ended: the harvest thermistor read open, so the control never saw the evaporator warm, and the long-harvest alarm sounded after about 20 minutes (one maker's example). Test the bin switch and read the thermistor in ice water before touching the board. Replace the thermistor and clear the alarm.
Repairs needed: Replace the harvest thermistor.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Clear the alarm and restart.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Test the simple controls before the board. Which one tells the machine harvest is done? / Measure the thermistor in ice water.
Source basis: Hoshizaki KM-201/260 service manual (one maker's example).

Call 5: Walk-in cooler warm after defrost, compressor trips

Grocery manager Ms. Abara
Cause and explanation: A jumper bypassed the fan-delay sensor, so the evaporator fans started the instant defrost ended. They blew warm wet air on the product and pushed dense suction vapor to the compressor, which spiked the amps and tripped the overload. Remove the jumper, restore the fan-delay control (fans wait until the coil is about 20 to 30 F, adjustable) and confirm the termination sensor is sound. Resetting the overload alone would have repeated the trip.
Repairs needed: Remove the jumper and restore the fan-delay control.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Check the termination sensor and timer settings.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): What do the fans do right after defrost? / Look at the fan-delay wiring.
Source basis: ACHR News, The Professor (defrost termination and fan delay). Numbers are examples.
Wrap-up answers. Look for a specific safety rule from this module (de-energize and verify dead, PPE, no jumpers on safeties, recover refrigerant, never cap a relief valve, or the module's own). Look for a reading tied to a decision, and a concrete mistake to avoid, such as replacing a part before testing.

How numbers are labeled. Readings, prices, set points, timers, blink codes and trip values in the calls are practice examples made up for the game unless a call says a source gives them. Words like about, example and practice mark them. Where a number comes from one maker's document (for example Hoshizaki timers or Trane flash codes), it is that maker's example and other makers differ. Rules of thumb (such as coolers about 35 to 38 F) are common practice, not rules. Laws and codes are cited by name: confirm the current text. Always follow the equipment maker's instructions and local code.
