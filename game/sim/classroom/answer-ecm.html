Answer key: ECM blower

Teacher copy. Student worksheet | Open the simulator module

Learning goals
  • Tell a failed ECM motor from a failed control, a loose pin or a restriction by testing in order.
  • Explain why a short wait is needed before opening an ECM motor, and why the control and harness are checked before the motor is condemned.
  • Describe how a thick filter or duct restriction makes the blower hunt.
Safety note from the module. Educational simulation only. Disconnect power and wait about five minutes before opening an ECM motor. The power connector carries line voltage. Never work on the motor with AC applied. Never leave a system running without a filter.

Answers are the simulator's own explanations, so wording in a student's own words will differ. Accept any answer that names the cause and a repair that matches. Scores and numbers in the sim are practice examples.

Call 1: Blower dead after the power surge

Mr. Lee
Cause and explanation: With 24 V present, the call answered and line voltage at the motor, the motor itself failed: its leads leaked to the shell at about 40 kohm, which on one maker's rule (under about 100 kohm) means a bad motor that a new control will not cure. Install the correct matched motor and module. A universal module will not run the motor even if it looks identical.
Repairs needed: Replace the motor with the correct matched motor and module.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Check 24 V, the call, the harness and line voltage before the motor. / Test the motor to its shell with the control removed and power off.
Source basis: ClimateMaster ECM blower motor replacement guide.

Call 2: Blower stops and starts on its own

Ms. Costa
Cause and explanation: An intermittent blower after someone worked in the cabinet is very often a pin pushed back in a harness plug. Reseat the plug and check the pins before condemning a motor or module. Disconnect power and wait before opening an ECM motor, and keep the line-voltage power connector correctly oriented.
Repairs needed: Reseat and secure the harness pins.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): The motor is sometimes fine. Check connections. / Inspect the harness plug pins.
Source basis: ClimateMaster ECM blower motor replacement guide.

Call 3: Surges and whistles after the new filter

The Marchettis
Cause and explanation: A high-MERV filter in a slot built for a thin one, a dirty coil or closed registers push static pressure up and make the motor hunt. Cut the restriction with a larger filter area or a cleaner coil, and lower the maximum airflow only if the system allows. Never leave the filter out: the coil can freeze or a furnace can overheat and trip its limit.
Repairs needed: Fit a larger-area filter or clean the coil to cut restriction.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Clean the evaporator coil; Lower the maximum airflow setting.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Does the puffing stop with the filter removed? Measure static pressure. / A thick filter in a thin slot restricts the system.
Source basis: ClimateMaster ECM blower motor replacement guide.

How numbers are labeled. Readings, prices, set points, timers, blink codes and trip values in the calls are practice examples made up for the game unless a call says a source gives them. Words like about, example and practice mark them. Where a number comes from one maker's document (for example Hoshizaki timers or Trane flash codes), it is that maker's example and other makers differ. Rules of thumb (such as coolers about 35 to 38 F) are common practice, not rules. Laws and codes are cited by name: confirm the current text. Always follow the equipment maker's instructions and local code.
