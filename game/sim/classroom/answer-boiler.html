Answer key: Hot-water boiler

Teacher copy. Student worksheet | Open the simulator module

Learning goals
  • Trace water flow: boiler, circulator, zone valves, piping and radiation, and use pressure and temperature readings to find where it stops.
  • Explain what the expansion tank, fill valve, air vent and relief valve each do, including where the tank connects.
  • Run the post-service safety test (low-water cutoff and high limit) and record the set point and trip value.

Answers are the simulator's own explanations, so wording in a student's own words will differ. Accept any answer that names the cause and a repair that matches. Scores and numbers in the sim are practice examples.

Call 1: Boiler hot, no heat in the house

Mrs. Delgado
Cause and explanation: A hot boiler with a cold system and a very large supply-to-return difference (about 77 F here against a normal 20 F) means the water is not circulating. The circulator had 120 V at its terminals but the rotor was seized. With power off and the isolation flanges closed, replace the circulator, purge the air and verify normal flow.
Repairs needed: Replace circulator.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Compare supply and return temperatures after a few minutes, then check the circulator. / The supply is far hotter than the return, so water is not moving. The circulator has power but the shaft may be stuck.

Call 2: Cold upstairs radiators and gurgling

Mr. Park
Cause and explanation: Normal pressure and a running circulator with one cold upper zone and gurgling point to trapped air. The automatic air vent at the high point was stuck closed. Replace the vent and purge the zone by flushing water through the bleeder until the air stops. Do not just bleed it repeatedly without fixing the vent.
Repairs needed: Purge air from the zone; Replace automatic air vent.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Check pressure, then feel the first-floor and second-floor piping. / System pressure is normal. The circulator runs. Air collects at the high point.

Call 3: Relief valve dripping

The Haddads
Cause and explanation: Heated water expands. The expansion tank's air cushion absorbs it. This tank had lost its air (the bladder failed and water came from the air valve), so pressure rose to the 30 psi relief setting. Replacing the relief valve would not fix the cause. Replace the tank and set its precharge to match the fill pressure (12 psi) before it goes on the system.
Repairs needed: Replace expansion tank, precharge to 12 psi.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Read the pressure cold and hot. Then check the expansion tank. / Pressure climbs from 14 to 30 psi. Press the air valve on the tank.

Call 4: Low pressure and noise

Ms. Romano
Cause and explanation: A hydronic system needs about 12 psi cold. The pressure-reducing fill valve was clogged with debris and not passing water. Replace the fill valve and set it to 12 psi cold. Do not hand-fill a hot, nearly dry boiler: cold water on hot cast iron can crack the sections. Burnham manuals say not to introduce return water more than 40 F colder than the idle boiler, and to keep return water at about 135 F or higher.
Repairs needed: Replace fill valve, set to 12 psi cold.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Compare the pressure gauge with the fill valve inlet pressure. / The house supply is 60 psi but the system sits near 3 psi. Water is not getting through the fill valve.

Call 5: One zone never heats

Mr. Sullivan
Cause and explanation: The upstairs thermostat called and the zone valve motor had its 24 V, but it never drove the valve open. The valve body moved freely by hand, so the motor head was the failed part. Power off, replace the motor, and check that the end switch starts the circulator when the valve opens.
Repairs needed: Replace zone valve motor.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Is the upstairs zone valve getting 24 V when its thermostat calls? / The valve has 24 V but never opens, and the body opens fine by hand.

Call 6: Air in the pipes every week, circulator hums

Mrs. Okafor
Cause and explanation: The expansion tank was piped on the circulator's discharge side, and the fill valve was set low. With the circulator running, parts of the system dropped below atmospheric pressure, so the float vent and fittings pulled air in. In this example the top of the system (about 15 ft above the gauge) saw only about 1.5 psi, below the 5 psi minimum often used as a rule of thumb. Fix: move the tank to the circulator inlet side, set the feed valve so the top of the system holds at least 5 psi (about 12 psi at the gauge for 15 ft), then purge the air and vent the circulator. Bleeding every week treats the symptom only. Source: Caleffi Idronics 2 (maker publication); the numbers here are practice examples.
Repairs needed: Move the expansion tank to the circulator inlet side; Set the feed valve so the top of the system holds 5 psi or more; Purge air from the zone.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Read the cold pressure, then work out the pressure at the top of the system. / Look at where the expansion tank is piped compared with the circulator.

Call 7: Supply temperature drops when the second zone starts

Mr. Brennan
Cause and explanation: Closely spaced tees let the boiler loop and the zone loop share a very short common pipe, so each pump does its own job. The rework added elbows and an offset between the tees, so the zone circulator pulled flow through the boiler loop and mixed cooler water into the supply. Fix: restore a short, straight common section (tees close together, no fittings between them), or install a hydraulic separator. Caleffi notes that field offsets and bends often break the principle. No exact spacing figure is stated here: follow the maker's instructions. Numbers are practice examples.
Repairs needed: Re-pipe the tees close together with a short straight common section or Install a hydraulic separator.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Watch the supply temperature as the zone starts, then look closely at the piping where the boiler loop meets the zone loop. / The two tees are no longer close together. Look for bends and offsets between them.
Wrap-up answers. Look for a specific safety rule from this module (de-energize and verify dead, PPE, no jumpers on safeties, recover refrigerant, never cap a relief valve, or the module's own). Look for a reading tied to a decision, and a concrete mistake to avoid, such as replacing a part before testing.

How numbers are labeled. Readings, prices, set points, timers, blink codes and trip values in the calls are practice examples made up for the game unless a call says a source gives them. Words like about, example and practice mark them. Where a number comes from one maker's document (for example Hoshizaki timers or Trane flash codes), it is that maker's example and other makers differ. Rules of thumb (such as coolers about 35 to 38 F) are common practice, not rules. Laws and codes are cited by name: confirm the current text. Always follow the equipment maker's instructions and local code.
