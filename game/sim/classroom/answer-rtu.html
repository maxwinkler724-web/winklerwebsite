Answer key: Rooftop economizer

Teacher copy. Student worksheet | Open the simulator module

Learning goals
  • Explain what an air-side economizer does and what the changeover setpoint and minimum position are for.
  • Use controller alarms and test menus before touching linkage, and record as-found and final settings.
  • Practice rooftop safety: fall protection, lockout and pinch points.
Safety note from the module. Educational simulation only. Roof work needs fall protection. Lock out before reaching into damper linkage (pinch points). Keep the minimum ventilation position set. Record as-found and final settings.

Answers are the simulator's own explanations, so wording in a student's own words will differ. Accept any answer that names the cause and a repair that matches. Scores and numbers in the sim are practice examples.

Call 1: Compressors run at 55 F outside

Building manager Ms. Park
Cause and explanation: Outdoor air was below the changeover (about 63 F on one maker's controller) yet the damper stayed nearly closed: command and feedback disagreed. The test mode showed something binding, and the linkage was rusted and slipped on the actuator shaft. Free and reconnect the linkage, confirm the damper follows its command, and record as-found and final settings. Replacing a good actuator or controller would not have fixed it.
Repairs needed: Free and reconnect the damper linkage.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Record as-found and final settings.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Run the controller's test mode and compare command with feedback. / The sensors read fine. Look at the linkage.
Source basis: Lennox economizer sheet and PNNL HVAC Economizers 101 (setpoints marked about, one maker's example).

Call 2: Cool but clammy humid July day

Store manager Mr. Hayes
Cause and explanation: The economizer was set to open below about 85 F dry bulb, a dry-climate setting, and the enthalpy sensor was not connected, so humid outside air was admitted as free cooling. Reset the changeover for a humid climate (about 55 F dry bulb or an enthalpy limit near 20 to 22 Btu per pound, as one source's guide), connect the sensor, set the minimum position, and record as-found and final settings.
Repairs needed: Reset changeover for the humid climate (enthalpy limit).
Also acceptable (no penalty): Connect the enthalpy sensor; Record as-found and final settings.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Check the controller's changeover and whether an enthalpy sensor is used. / The setting suits a dry climate.
Source basis: PNNL HVAC Economizers 101; Lennox economizer sheet.

Call 3: Always stuffy, damper never opens

Office manager Ms. Reyes
Cause and explanation: The actuator and linkage were fine: the damper moved through full travel in test mode. The minimum position had been turned to 0 percent, so no outdoor air came in during occupied hours and CO2 climbed. Set the minimum position, then verify by measuring the actual outdoor-air percentage from return, mixed and outdoor temperatures. Replacing parts would not have fixed it. No single minimum fits every building, so follow the ventilation design.
Repairs needed: Set the minimum position, then verify the outdoor-air percentage.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Record as-found and final settings.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): The actuator moves freely. What setting decides how far the damper sits when ventilation is the only reason to open it? / Measure the actual outdoor-air percentage instead of guessing.
Source basis: PNNL HVAC Economizers 101 (minimum position, measuring outdoor-air percentage).

Call 4: Controller flashing a sensor code

Facilities tech Mr. Obi
Cause and explanation: Eight flashes on one maker's controller means a mixed-air sensor error (four means return-air). The input voltage sat at the open-circuit reference, the wiring measured a few ohms end to end, and the sensor itself read open. Replace the mixed-air sensor, confirm the code clears and record the settings. Replacing the controller would have cost more and left the fault.
Repairs needed: Replace mixed-air sensor.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Record as-found and final settings.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Which sensor does eight flashes mean on one maker's controller? / Follow the order: voltage with the sensor connected, wiring resistance, then the sensor itself.
Source basis: Trane ReliaTel MAT/RAT troubleshooting (one maker's example; flash counts differ by brand).

How numbers are labeled. Readings, prices, set points, timers, blink codes and trip values in the calls are practice examples made up for the game unless a call says a source gives them. Words like about, example and practice mark them. Where a number comes from one maker's document (for example Hoshizaki timers or Trane flash codes), it is that maker's example and other makers differ. Rules of thumb (such as coolers about 35 to 38 F) are common practice, not rules. Laws and codes are cited by name: confirm the current text. Always follow the equipment maker's instructions and local code.
