Answer key: Condensing boiler

Teacher copy. Student worksheet | Open the simulator module

Learning goals
  • Explain why a condensing boiler needs a drained condensate path, a sealed vent and a clean intake.
  • Work a lockout in order (gas, electrodes and ground, valve power, drain, combustion) instead of resetting it again and again.
  • Explain why a proving switch is never jumpered and why a blocked flue can mean carbon monoxide.
Safety note from the module. Educational simulation only. A gas smell means stop, ventilate, no switches, leave and call. A blocked flue can mean carbon monoxide. Never jumper a proving switch. Combustion checks need an analyzer. Condensate is acidic.

Answers are the simulator's own explanations, so wording in a student's own words will differ. Accept any answer that names the cause and a repair that matches. Scores and numbers in the sim are practice examples.

Call 1: Quits, comes back, worse when cold

Mrs. Dubois
Cause and explanation: A blocked condensate trap or sagging, partly frozen drain lets water rise to the flame-rod circuit, and the control reads it as loss of flame. Clear and refill the trap, then level and insulate the pipe. Replacing the flame rod first costs money and fails. Condensate is acidic: wear gloves and glasses.
Repairs needed: Clear and refill the condensate trap; Level, pitch and insulate the condensate pipe.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): The code is about flame signal. What can make the control read loss of flame without a bad rod? / Look at the condensate trap and the drain pipe.
Source basis: US Boiler Company condensing boiler troubleshooting document; one maker's example flame-signal code only.

Call 2: Lockout after three resets

Mr. Nowak
Cause and explanation: Work the order: confirm gas and purge air, inspect the ignition and flame electrodes and the burner ground, check 24 V at the valve during trial, check the condensate drain, then set the air-fuel ratio with an analyzer. Here the electrodes were dirty and a ground was loose. Never keep resetting a lockout, and a gas smell means leave and call from outside.
Repairs needed: Clean or replace the ignition electrodes; Restore the burner ground.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Check the order: gas supply, electrodes, ground, valve power, then combustion. / Look at the electrodes and the ground connection.
Source basis: US Boiler Company condensing boiler troubleshooting document.

Call 3: Shuts down every few minutes in the ice storm

Ms. Brooks
Cause and explanation: A blocked vent or air intake keeps the air-proving switch from closing, so the control shuts the burner down. That is the safety working. Clear the terminals, then check the hose, pitch and condensate return. A blocked flue can mean carbon monoxide, so never jumper the switch. A customer with a CO alarm sounding gets a different call.
Repairs needed: Clear the vent and intake terminals.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): What proves the vent is open? Look outside. / The air-proving switch is doing its job.
Source basis: US Boiler Company condensing boiler troubleshooting document; HVAC School pressure switch article.

How numbers are labeled. Readings, prices, set points, timers, blink codes and trip values in the calls are practice examples made up for the game unless a call says a source gives them. Words like about, example and practice mark them. Where a number comes from one maker's document (for example Hoshizaki timers or Trane flash codes), it is that maker's example and other makers differ. Rules of thumb (such as coolers about 35 to 38 F) are common practice, not rules. Laws and codes are cited by name: confirm the current text. Always follow the equipment maker's instructions and local code.
