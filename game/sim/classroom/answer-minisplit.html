Answer key: Mini-split

Teacher copy. Student worksheet | Open the simulator module

Learning goals
  • Use fault history and communication checks to separate wiring faults from a failed board.
  • Explain why filters, coils and clearances cause icing and overload trips.
  • Describe why ice is thawed and never chipped, and why an overload is never bypassed.
Safety note from the module. Educational simulation only. Lock out power and wait for the outdoor board to discharge. Never bypass an overload protector. Refrigerant work requires EPA 608 certification.

Answers are the simulator's own explanations, so wording in a student's own words will differ. Accept any answer that names the cause and a repair that matches. Scores and numbers in the sim are practice examples.

Call 1: Head unit blinks after the storm

Mr. Alvarado
Cause and explanation: A communication fault after a storm is often the interconnect cable: a corroded terminal or chafed conductor, or reversed polarity from an earlier repair. Test wiring end to end and read the signal voltage pattern before blaming a board. The outdoor board holds a charge after power-off, so wait before touching the inverter section.
Repairs needed: Repair or replace interconnect cable and terminal; restore polarity.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Dry and reseat the connector.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Read the code, then check the wiring before any board. / The signal wire may be damaged. Test it end to end with power off, and wait for the board to bleed first.
Source basis: Lennox ductless mini-split service manual; ServiceMag mini-split guide (the same blink count means different faults on different brands).

Call 2: Block of ice on the indoor unit

Ms. Okoro
Cause and explanation: Check in order: filter and airflow, then low refrigerant (leaks at flares, service valves and the outdoor coil), then low outdoor temperature, then the indoor fan. Here the filter was packed with lint. Thaw with cooling off and the fan running, clean the filter and coil, and recheck. Adding refrigerant without finding a leak would have been the wrong fix.
Repairs needed: Clean the indoor filter and coil.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Turn cooling off, run fan only to thaw.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Look at the filter and airflow first. / Low fan current and a packed filter. Thaw it, do not chip it.
Source basis: ServiceMag mini-split guide; Lennox mini-split manual (low-refrigerant protection).

Call 3: Error only on hot afternoons

Mrs. Fischer
Cause and explanation: A sensor out of range usually means the thermistor or its connector, not the board. Reseat and dry the connector, measure the sensor against the maker's chart at the measured temperature, and replace the sensor. The board is last. Thermistors read wrong when wet.
Repairs needed: Replace the sensor.
Also acceptable (no penalty): Dry and reseat the connector.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Is the code about a sensor? Check the connector and measure the sensor. / Compare resistance with the maker's chart at the measured temperature.
Source basis: Lennox mini-split manual; ServiceMag mini-split guide.

Call 4: Quits every hot afternoon

The Garcias
Cause and explanation: A matted outdoor coil and almost no clearance in a hot ambient pushes current and discharge temperature toward the protection limits, so the unit trips and later restarts. Wash the coil and restore clearance. Never bypass an overload protector.
Repairs needed: Wash the outdoor coil; Restore clearance around the unit.
Teacher prompts (the sim hints): Look at the outdoor unit and the clearance around it. / Check the coil and the current.
Source basis: Lennox mini-split manual (overload, overheat and high discharge temperature protection).

How numbers are labeled. Readings, prices, set points, timers, blink codes and trip values in the calls are practice examples made up for the game unless a call says a source gives them. Words like about, example and practice mark them. Where a number comes from one maker's document (for example Hoshizaki timers or Trane flash codes), it is that maker's example and other makers differ. Rules of thumb (such as coolers about 35 to 38 F) are common practice, not rules. Laws and codes are cited by name: confirm the current text. Always follow the equipment maker's instructions and local code.

Educational simulation and study aid. Not a certification, not a substitute for hands-on training, manufacturer instructions or local code. Nothing in this kit collects or sends data.
