    Sources and how numbers are labeled

    Where the facts in these calls come from. Links open other sites; the simulator itself makes no network calls. All sources

    • ClimateMaster: TXV diagnostic and replacement guidelines. MFR. TXV bulb placement and diagnosis.
    • 40 CFR 82.156: required evacuation levels (Cornell LII). REG. Evacuate before opening; confirm the current text.
    • 40 CFR 82.158: standards for recovery and recycling equipment (Cornell LII). REG. Confirm the current text.
    • EPA: Section 608 technician certification requirements. GOV. Who may handle refrigerant.
    • HVAC School: how to evacuate an air conditioning system fast. TRADE. Evacuation practice (a presenter's target of about 300 microns, not a rule).
    • HVAC contractor-supplier blog: capacitor and contactor diagnostics. TRADE. Capacitor and contactor checks.

    How numbers are labeled. Readings, prices, set points, timers, blink codes and trip values in the calls are practice examples made up for the game unless a call says a source gives them. Words like about, example and practice mark them. Where a number comes from one maker's document (for example Hoshizaki timers or Trane flash codes), it is that maker's example and other makers differ. Rules of thumb (such as coolers about 35 to 38 F) are common practice, not rules. Laws and codes are cited by name: confirm the current text. Always follow the equipment maker's instructions and local code.
