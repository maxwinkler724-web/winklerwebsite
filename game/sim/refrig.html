Reference: defrost, cold holding, ice machines and leak rules

Walk-in defrost

  • A timer starts defrost, from once a day to several times a day. Walk-ins usually defrost once or twice daily, more in humid places (add one period at a time).
  • Heaters are rated in watts per foot and are usually coil-specific: use the maker's part.
  • Termination is most often by temperature, from a sensor high on the coil where frost clears last (one example: about 60 F; check the maker). A fail-safe timer ends defrost if the sensor never terminates it (usually about 35 to 45 minutes).
  • Fan delay holds the evaporator fans until the coil is about 20 to 30 F (adjustable). Starting fans right after defrost blows warm, wet air on the product and sends dense suction vapor to the compressor, which raises amps and trips overloads.

Cold holding

The FDA Food Code holds time/temperature-controlled food at 41 F or below. Boxes run colder than that (coolers about 35 to 38 F, freezers about 0 F are common practice, not a rule) so the product meets 41 F.

Ice machine cycle (one maker's example: Hoshizaki crescent cuber)

Fill, harvest (hot-gas valve open, water valve open), freeze (compressor, pump and fan run), pump-out at a set frequency, then repeat until the bin control stops the machine. Example numbers: freeze about 20 to 40 minutes, harvest about 2 to 3 minutes, long-harvest alarm past 20 minutes, thermistor about 4.7 to 6.2 kohm in ice water, alarm beeps 1 high evaporator temperature, 2 long harvest, 3 freeze timer. Other makers use different timers and codes.

Leak rules (check the current rule text)

The older leak-repair thresholds in 40 CFR 82.157 now apply to ozone-depleting refrigerants (CFC and HCFC) only. HFC systems of 15 lb or more (refrigerants with GWP above 53) follow the AIM Act rule, 40 CFR Part 84, in effect from January 1, 2026: a 20 percent leak-rate threshold for commercial refrigeration, with repair within 30 days of adding refrigerant. There is a narrow exemption for residential and light commercial air conditioning and heat pumps. These details come from law-firm summaries and EPA pages: confirm them on eCFR before relying on them.
