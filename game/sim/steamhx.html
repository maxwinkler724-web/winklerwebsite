Reference: stall, vacuum breakers, traps and steam hammer

Stall

A small heat exchanger can stall when its steam pressure falls to or below the pressure the trap must discharge into after a load drop. Condensate then stands in the steam space and floods the coil. A vacuum breaker on the steam side lets air in so condensate drains by gravity to the trap. It does not help if condensate is lifted after the trap (a pump trap is the usual answer). Larger units usually avoid breakers because air in the steam space lowers heat transfer. Follow the maker: no set pressures are given in the sources used here.

Steam hammer

Common causes: condensate pockets, sagging pipe or wrong pitch, failed or undersized traps, opening valves quickly, and cold start-ups. Prevent it with drip legs and traps, dry steam, gradual valve opening and warm-up bypasses.

Failed traps

A trap that fails closed can be cleared at the strainer blowdown. A trap that fails open should be repaired or replaced, not valved off.
